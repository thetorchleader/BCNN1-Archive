National Weather Service Says At Least 8 Tornadoes Touched Down in Texas on Sunday

April 27, 2015 National

Patrick Pace (left) and Federico Monsivas-Rodriguez removed wet carpet Monday from Brazos Restoration & Waterproofing in downtown Waxahachie. Several buildings filled with water after Sunday night’s storms. (PHOTO CREDIT: Ron Baselice/Staff Photographer)

At least eight tornadoes touched down Sunday in rural areas south of Dallas and Fort Worth, the National Weather Service confirmed Monday evening.

The band of tornadoes flattened buildings, tore the roofs off other structures and forced people to rush for shelter. No injuries were reported.

Johnson County took the brunt of the storms. Survey teams from the Weather Service confirmed that four tornadoes touched down in the Rio Vista area, south of Cleburne.

The damage suggests that all were EF-0 twisters, with winds up to 85 mph.

Tornadoes also touched down in Glen Rose, in Somervell County, and in Comanche and Erath counties.

A 50-mile path of damage could be seen across Johnson County — from near Cleburne State Park to Grandview, said Jamie Moore, Johnson County Emergency Management coordinator.

“Based on what I was expecting to see when we had reports of half-mile wide tornadoes across Johnson County last night, we were very fortunate,” said Moore, who flew over the area Monday. “There is damage, ranging from fairly minor to very serious, but it’s not as bad as I expected it would be.”

The tornado tally could change if surveyors make more assessments, Weather Service meteorologist Jesse Moore said. Fourteen tornadoes had been reported Sunday.

Aside from wind damage, flooding swamped Johnson, Somervell, Hood and Ellis counties. Rainfall totals up to 6 inches were reported in some areas, leading to several high-water rescues.

Part of the Waxahachie police headquarters flooded as water several inches deep rushed into the building.

“The storms were moving slowly, and they were repeat offenders,” Weather Service meteorologist Tom Bradshaw said. “This caused several places to experience copious amounts of rainfall.”

Storms were back Monday evening as showers and thunderstorms moved across the Dallas-Fort Worth area with scattered pea-size hail.

There was a report of a possible tornado near Kaufman that damaged part of a car dealership and one high-water rescue in Fort Worth, said KXAS-TV (NBC5) meteorologist Rick Mitchell.

Tuesday is expected to be “an indoor day or a jacket day,” with a high of 60, a low of 53, and occasional showers. But then the sun will come out Wednesday, Mitchell said.

Temperatures Wednesday through Saturday are expected to reach highs in the lower 70s and 80s and lows between 49 and 60 degrees.

Storms are expected to return Monday, Mitchell said. But in the meantime, he said, “we don’t have to worry about tornado warnings.”

SOURCE: The Dallas Morning News, The Associated Press, The Fort Worth Star-Telegram
