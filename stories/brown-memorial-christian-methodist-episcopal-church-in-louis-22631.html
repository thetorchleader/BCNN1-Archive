Brown Memorial Christian Methodist Episcopal Church in Louisville, KY Celebrates 160th Anniversary

May 17, 2014 African-American

Bishop Sylvester Williams, left, and the Rev. Janci Finkley at Brown Memorial Christian Methodist Episcopal Church, among the ealiest African-American churches in Louisville.
(Photo: Yi-Ke Peng/The Courier-Journal )

Housed in a distinctive brick building that’s thought to be the last work of Louisville architect Gideon Shryock (1802-1880), Brown Memorial Christian Methodist Episcopal Church on West Chestnut Street downtown will mark its 160th anniversary with a three-day celebration May 23-25.

Events include a church and community parade starting at 8 a.m. May 24 at Central High School’s football stadium, 1500 W. Muhammad Ali Blvd., and ending at the church, 809 W. Chestnut, followed by a concert.

A state historical marker in front of the church tells about its prior history as Center Street Church and then Chestnut Street CME Church, its new name after it moved in 1907 to its present site.

The Rev. Janci Finkley, the church’s pastor for four years, said it’s among the earliest African-American churches in Louisville — also including the older Green Street Baptist and Fifth Street Baptist — and predates the founding of the historically black CME denomination in 1870.

Its history

The church had its beginnings in the Eight (or possibly Eighth) Street Methodist Episcopal Church South, when a sub-group of the church with 465 black members and four local preachers was officially recognized as Center Street Church in 1854, according to a written church history.

The church’s “birth occurred in the midst of slavery,” the history notes. But the church “witnessed the emancipation of brothers and sisters from legal slavery in 1865.”

In 1870, Center Street’s pastor, the Rev. William Henry Miles, was elected the first bishop of the CME denomination, and in 1907, Center Street pastor the Rev. L.H. Brown bought the current property for $17,000, the history says.

Center Street Church was located on Center Street (Armory Place), between Green (Liberty) and Walnut (Muhammad Ali Blvd.) streets, the Encyclopedia of Louisville says. The Jefferson County Jail had been built in front of the church on Green at Sixth Street, and Brown moved the church from this “undesirable location” to the West Chestnut Street site and changed the name to Chestnut Street CME Church, the history and encyclopedia say.

The church there had been built as Chestnut Street Methodist Church, which was dedicated in 1865. The church was renamed for Brown in 1954, at the time of its 100th anniversary.

Its future

The church has about 236 members and is working on building up its membership again, especially targeting young adults, Finkley said. It’s staying engaged in the community through prayer walks and other activities, and continues to encourage participation in Project One, a community program with adult mentors focusing on job skills and placement that originated at the church.

The church also provides scholarships to youths to go to the CME denomination’s five colleges throughout the United States, including Miles College in Birmingham, Ala., and has encouraged students to attend Simmons College in Louisville, Finkley said.
