Jameis Winston’s Adviser Wants to Know Why, All of a Sudden, Florida State Wants to Do This Late Hearing

October 14, 2014 Sports

Jameis Winston (Photo: Rich Barnes, USA TODAY Sports)

Representatives for Florida State quarterback Jameis Winston and the woman who said he raped her in 2012 have agreed on little, but as the case heads toward a disciplinary hearing by an outside third party, neither thinks the school has complied with Title IX.

In a letter to Florida State on Tuesday, David Cornwell, an attorney advising Winston, argues the disciplinary proceedings the school outlined to Winston in a letter last week are “untimely.”

FSU informed Winston in a letter on Friday that it would use a hearing officer from outside of the university to conduct an investigative hearing to determine if the Student Conduct Code had been violated and, if appropriate, decide on sanctions.

Cornwell questioned why the school was proceeding with the hearing nearly two years after the woman first reported to FSU police on Dec. 7, 2012. He argued that the decision “seems motivated to protect FSU’s interests and respond to media pressure.”

In a timeline of its response in the case released last week, FSU acknowledged that its Title IX coordinator did not become aware of the allegation until November 2013. That was after the woman made her to report to campus police 11 months earlier and 10 months after the athletic department became aware that Winston had been identified as a suspect.

“The United States Department of Education imposed a duty on FSU to promptly investigate the allegations against Mr. Winston in or around December 2012,” Cornwell wrote in the letter, which was obtained by USA TODAY Sports.

“Based upon both the FSU’s student conduct policy and the (Department of Education’s Office for Civil Rights), the investigation is untimely.”

FSU spokeswoman Browning Brooks did not have comment beyond a letter the school sent to supporters on Friday detailing its actions in the case.

Cornwell contends that FSU is not following its own sexual harassment policy, which says that complaints will “generally not be investigated” after one year.
