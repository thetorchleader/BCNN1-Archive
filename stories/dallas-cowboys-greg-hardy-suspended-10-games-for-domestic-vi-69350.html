Gary Payton Is Being Sued by a Woman Who Accuses Him of Assaulting Her 3 Times; Fox Sports 1 Suspends Him

April 23, 2015 Sports

The N.F.L. found in an extensive two-month investigation that Greg Hardy had used physical force against his former girlfriend in at least four instances. Credit Chris Keane/Associated Press

The N.F.L. on Wednesday suspended Greg Hardy of the Dallas Cowboys for 10 games for his role in a domestic-violence case, the stiffest penalty handed out under the league’s new and stronger personal-conduct policy.

Hardy, a defensive end who previously played for the Carolina Panthers, was convicted in North Carolina of domestic abuse last year. The decision was overturned on appeal because his former girlfriend Nicole Holder refused to testify against him. But in a two-month investigation, the N.F.L. found that Hardy had used physical force against Holder in at least four instances.

“The net effect of these acts was that Ms. Holder was severely traumatized and sustained a range of injuries, including bruises and scratches on her neck, shoulders, upper chest, back, arms and feet,” Commissioner Roger Goodell wrote to Hardy. “The use of physical force under the circumstances present here, against a woman substantially smaller than you, and in the presence of powerful, military-style assault weapons, constitutes a significant act of violence in violation of the personal-conduct policy.”

Hardy was placed on paid leave last season after his conviction. Dallas signed Hardy in March knowing that he could be penalized.

At league meetings last month, Cowboys Coach Jason Garrett was asked why his team had signed Hardy. Garrett said that the Cowboys had done their due diligence and that they had structured his contract to reduce the team’s exposure if he were suspended.

The Cowboys’ owner, Jerry Jones, said in a statement: “Our organization understands the very serious nature of this matter. We will use our resources — work closely with Greg and with the league — to ensure a positive outcome.”
