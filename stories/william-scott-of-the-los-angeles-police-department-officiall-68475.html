William Scott of the Los Angeles Police Department Officially Takes Role as Deputy Chief

April 19, 2015 Black

William Scott of the Los Angeles Police Department was recently promoted from the ranks of commander to the ranks of deputy chief, calling the promotion “an honor and a huge responsibility.”

“It’s a tremendous responsibility in that the bureau that I oversee, which is south bureau, has approximately 650,000 citizens and over 1700  employees,” Scott told the Sentinel in a recent interview.

“I don’t take that lightly.”

Scott, 50, has been with the department since 1990, having forgone an intended stint in the military after college graduation.

“My cousin, who recently retired from LAPD, had been a new officer at the time,” Scott recalled.

“He was very excited about his job in the police department and he started talking to me about what a great opportunity it was… making a difference with a job that has meaning and purpose.”

His “meaning and purpose,” he said, lies within leaving the department a little better than he found it by the time his job is done.

“I hope that I can make a difference,” Scott explained.

“South LA still has high levels of violence. And, there’s a lot of gang related violence and a lot of homicide. We typically lead the city in homicides and most of those are gang related. A lot of those are same ethnicity type homicides, whether it be black on black or brown on brown.

“And, that doesn’t really matter. I point that out because it’s a huge challenge to try to delve into the culture that drives those homicides and turn that around. And I think that the police department over the last ten years… we’ve done a good job of that but we still have a lot of work to do…”

The work includes fostering trusting relationships between the department and the communities it serves.
