Daughters of Slain Black Leaders, Malcolm X and Medgar Evers, Find Themselves Bound by Tragedy and Purpose 50 Years Later

February 17, 2015 Black

Malcolm X’s daughter, Ilyasah Shabazz, left, and Reena Evers-Everett, daughter of Medgar Evers and executive director of the Medgar and Myrlie Evers Institute, share a lighthearted moment Monday before Jackson State University’s Two Daughters of Two Martyrs program.
(Photo: Greg Jenson/The Clarion-Ledger)

As the 50th anniversary of Malcolm X’s assassination nears, the daughters of two civil rights martyrs discussed the horrors of those days and their hopes for the future.

Ilyasah Shabazz was about 3 years old when her father, Malcolm X, was assassinated on Feb. 21, 1965, while speaking to his Organization of Afro-American Unity at the Audubon Ballroom in Washington Heights, New York.

Reena Evers-Everette was 8 years old when her father, Medgar Evers, was assassinated on June 12, 1963, in the driveway of their home in Jackson.

Meeting Monday at Jackson State University, the two women talked together publicly for the first time.

Evers-Everette, executive director of the Medgar and Myrlie Evers Institute, said their mothers, Myrlie Evers and Betty Shabazz, along with Coretta Scott King, became close friends, and the children in turn became friends.

“We belonged to a club that nobody should want to join, a club we have suffered through pain, grief and tragedy,” recalled Evers-Everette. “Mama Betty was truly another mother to me.”

Ilyasah Shabazz, author of the memoir, “Growing Up X,” recalled the days when “Aunt Coretta” and “Aunt Myrlie” became close friends with the family.
