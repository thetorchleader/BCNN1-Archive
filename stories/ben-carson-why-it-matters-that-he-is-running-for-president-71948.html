Ben Carson – Why It Matters that he Is Running for President

May 5, 2015 Black, Opinion

Let’s acknowledge that Ben Carson is an unlikely Republican presidential nominee in 2016. 

But even contenders who don’t win the nomination can have a effect on the course of the debate. That’s especially true when they’re dynamic speakers who can stand out in debates and command even a narrow band of avid supporters.

That’s Ben Carson.

The conservative commentator and celebrated neurosurgeon has an up-from-the-bootstraps story that resonates with audiences. Born in poverty, raised by a single mother who never finished elementary school — at 33 he became the groundbreaking director of the pediatric neurosurgery program at Johns Hopkins Children’s Center.

He provides a voice for the GOP’s hard right. He has argued that Obamacare is “the worst thing that has happened in this nation since slavery.” He once said President Obama might declare martial law and cancel the 2016 elections. Some analysts say that, as the only African American in the GOP field, Carson may be best able to press the case against Obama’s policies and his legacy without being accused of racism.

He also may spotlight urban issues that are sometimes overlooked. He formally announced his campaign in Detroit, where he grew up. And he worked for years in Baltimore, which has become a focus for the debate over police treatment of black men.
