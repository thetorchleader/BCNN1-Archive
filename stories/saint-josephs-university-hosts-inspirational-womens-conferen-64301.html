Saint Joseph’s University Hosts Inspirational Women’s Conference

March 29, 2015 Black

Facilitators and speakers of Saint Joseph University’s Women’s Conference encourage women to live their own truths and be their own champions. — Tribune Photo by Nathaniel Lee

 

Women from around the city gathered at Saint Joseph’s University to attend the Saint Joseph’s University Women’s Conference on Saturday.

The event was sponsored by the university’s Office of Inclusion and Diversity directed by Monica M. Smith, and its theme was “Live Your Truth, Be Your Own Champion.”

The day-long conference consisted of several workshops on such topics as “Gender, Race and Age: What the Media Has to Say About Us,” “Mindfulness” and “Creating Your Personal Brand,” among others.

“It’s a conference about affirming, inspiring and empowering women of all diverse identities to live the best lives they can,” Smith said. “We have something for everyone so the audience we are targeting [is] from high school and above.”

“All of the workshops are really exciting — there’s a particular workshop by the author Rayna Gray, who wrote the book ‘7:17.’ She tells her story of triumph over tragedy and how she overcame many things in her life,” Smith added.

She hoped women would come together as a collective body and embrace their uniqueness and diversity across identities and roles.

“I want people to walk away with the sense of being affirmed and knowing that where they are isn’t where they have to stay and I also want women to feel that they are where they are supposed to be when they are supposed to be there,” Smith said.

Author Rayna Gray said she was abandoned by her mother, who was addicted to crack cocaine.

“I was left to take care of my little brother and sister at the age of 10,” she said. “After that I was a teen mom and then found myself in a domestic violence relationship where I almost lost my life several times.”

Gray said through God, faith and prayer she survived.

Her message to the women attending the conference and going through their own difficulties and challenging times?

“I would tell them that despite what you went through, you can persevere and you can live the life that God meant for you to live,” Gray said.
