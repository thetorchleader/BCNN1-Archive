How Wise Planning Can Help Avoid College Debt

June 8, 2013 Uncategorized

A two-year or four-year college degree is considered essential today in order to achieve economic success. In fact, many companies require new employees to hold at least a Master’s degree with some emphasis also placed on Doctoral candidates. By considering your overall interests, aptitude, and economic situation, you can find a college that best fits your career objective. If you are flexible in choosing a college, you can save thousands of dollars in government loans.

Regardless of your academic prowess, there will always be the question of how to pay for the education. As a result, students often resort to borrowing most of the funds from the government in the form of student loans. “Student loans themselves are not the problem,” said the late Larry Burkett, co-founder of Crown Financial Ministries. “The problem is that students often borrow in excess of what they need to get a college education. A student loan should only be considered as a ‘last resort.'”
If you are already in college, the key to survival is money management. As the role shifts to the student to pay for college, some analysts predict the cost of attending a state sponsored college will soar to $120,000 by 2015, which only magnifies the need for wise money management.
Financial Aid
Who qualifies for college financial aid? Categorically, everyone qualifies. The Federal Stafford Loan, for example, is available to every student regardless of family income. Frequently the Stafford Loan is promoted as a low-cost government student loan, but it is anything but “low-cost.” In fact, it is outrageously expensive and can lead students from financial euphoria into financial bondage.
Unlike the Stafford Loan, a Federal Perkins Loan is one of the most sought-after financial aid programs available. If you qualify, you may borrow up to $20,000 as an undergraduate and another $20,000 for grad school. Best of all, there is no origination fee and Uncle Sam pays the interest while you’re in school.
What makes the Perkins Loan so popular is the low interest rate of only 5% compared to over 6.8% for the Stafford Loan and 8.5% for the Parent Loan, plus the shameful 3-4% origination fee Uncle Sam charges for every dollar borrowed. What’s more, the Perkins Loan may be service-cancelable, in effect becoming a cash grant. Sounds good, doesn’t it?
Some years ago, two sisters each attended the same college, one majoring in communications, the other in education. Both sisters qualified for a Federal Perkins Loan. The older sister graduated and became a flight attendant for Delta Air Lines. After a nine-month grace period, her Perkins Loan became due, and she dutifully paid the lender fifty dollars each month year after year.
Her sister, on the other hand, graduated with a teaching credential that allowed her to work with students diagnosed with severe behavior problems. Nine months later, her Perkins Loan also became due, but 100% of her student loan canceled during the five years she was teaching, including all finance charges because she taught special education in private and public schools.
