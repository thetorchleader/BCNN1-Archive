Liberian Baptist Leader, Emile Sam-Peal, Eager to Begin Recovery Work In Ebola-Devastated Country

December 5, 2014 Christian

Emile Sam-Peal says he eager to get back to Liberia and help his nation recover from the Ebola crisis.

When Liberian Baptist leader Emile Sam-Peal traveled to the United States last July to touch base with American mission partners, he expected to stay six weeks. Instead, as flights to the Ebola-ravaged country were cancelled, Sam-Peal and his family found themselves stranded for five months.

They finally are returning home today, having secured tickets on one of the few carriers still flying to the West African nation.

“I’m thankful that we have been safe while here, and it is now time to return home and back to ministering, providing leadership, assistance and hope to folks who are going through a very, very difficult experience resulting from this Ebola crisis,” said Sam-Peal, who is superintendent and principal of the Lott Carey Mission School in Monrovia, the country’s capital.

Sam-Peal, his wife and two daughters, were scheduled to return in August. But as concerns about the spread of the deadly disease grew, airlines from the U.S. and Europe suspended service to affected counties, including Liberia. Sam-Peal’s airline told him it could not extend a refund for at least three months because of a heavy backlog of stranded passengers.

Since then, the family has lived in Richmond, Va., a city familiar to Sam-Peal, who graduated from Baptist Theological Seminary at Richmond in 2000. He also maintains ties with churches of the Baptist General Association of Virginia, which works closely with Baptists in Liberia.

Though he has been in Richmond as the disease spread in Liberia, Sam-Peal wasn’t unscathed by the crisis. A pastor and close friend of Sam-Peal’s has died, along with the pastor’s daughter, who was caring for him, and the pastor’s physician.

In addition, one Lott Carey student and one graduate also have died from Ebola.

What’s more, Sam-Peal’s 17-year-old son remained in Liberia — something never far from the father’s mind, though he has spoken to him almost every day.
