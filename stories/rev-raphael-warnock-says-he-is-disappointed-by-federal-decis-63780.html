Rev. Raphael Warnock says he Is ‘Disappointed’ by Federal Decision on Ferguson

March 26, 2015 Black

Rev. Raphael Warnock knew it would be tough to prove Officer Darren Wilson violated Michael Brown’s civil rights.

“I was not surprised by the decision,” said Warnock, pastor of Ebenezer Baptist Church in Atlanta.

The U.S. Justice Department said it won’t prosecute Wilson, a white police officer who shot and killed Brown, an unarmed African American teenager.

“The threshold for an indictment at the federal level is exceedingly high,” Warnock said.

Wilson was cleared of criminal charges in the fall of 2014, but later faced federal civil rights charges.

Warnock said it’s tough to know if Wilson intentionally shot Brown because he was African American.

“It’s difficult to prove what’s going on in the heart of a person,” Warnock said.

Federal prosecutors said they interviewed more than 200 people and combed cell-phone and surveillance video, but found no evidence to show Wilson was lying when he said he feared for his safety. Investigators also said there was no reliable evidence to show Brown had his hands up when he was shot.

Investigators did say they uncovered racist emails written by Ferguson police and city officials that show deep patterns of prejudice.
