Prayer Vigil Held In Lynchburg, Virginia, to Remember 34-Year-Old Shawn Wendale Moss

August 29, 2014 Black

Pastor James Coleman (second from left) of Providence Place Church, leads a prayer during a vigil in honor of Shawn Moss at Hunton Randolph Community Center. (Photo by Max Oden / The News & Advance)

Community members gathered to hold hands Thursday evening and share cherished memories of Shawn Moss.

About a hundred people lined the street, stairwell, and lawn behind the Hunton Randolph Community Center, gathering to remember the 34 year old who was shot to death Tuesday evening on Polk Street.

“We are here for his family and all other families…to tell someone they are not alone,” Dr. James Coleman said as he led a prayer to start the evening.

Pastor Walter Fore followed Coleman’s lead and said “Lynchburg is like a village here, when we lose one of our own, there is hurt in our hearts.” Another pastor stepped up to say “we are a part of the solution and life is too short.”

Moss’ aunt, Sonya Jones, remembered one day when Moss came up to her and said “we got to reach the masses” referencing a choir song entitled “How to reach the masses” to let everyone know about Jesus Christ.

Jones said the community should not let Moss’ name fade with time. His death should emphasize his message, she said.

Soon after, attendees held their neighbors’ hand to show solidarity for the community.

“No one wants to be here for the reason we are…if we want to be a community we need to realize, our blood is the same, our lives matter,” Coleman said.
