The National Black Church Initiative Tells Its Members Not to Donate to NPR After NPR Cancels ‘Tell Me More’ – Its Only Program Featuring a Black Host

May 31, 2014 Black

A black church group is telling its members to keep their money.

NPR recently canceled “Tell Me More,” its only program featuring an African-American host, and now the National Black Church Initiative, a faith-based coalition of 34,000 churches, is calling on all African Americans not to donate to National Public Radio. The show, which was hosted by veteran journalist Michel Martin, focused regularly on issues and concerns of the black community.

The loss of  “Tell Me More” was part of series of cuts to address the NPR’s budget deficit, reports Target Market News.

In a letter to NPR CEO, Jarl Mohn, NBCI president, Rev. Anthony Evans, conveyed the group’s concern. “The weekday NPR program was a strong voice on issues of race, identity and faith, and host Michel Martin was a crucial voice for the Black Church community… The show represents a shining light for African American broadcasters, and serves a much-needed role of minority voices in the media. NPR has abandoned the African American community, and we must turn a deaf ear to you,” said the letter.

The letter continued “as a result of this cancellation, NBCI has urged its 15.7 million members to not consider donations to NPR, and to stop donations that are currently underway. The Black Church cannot be expected to donate to a station who has failed minority voices in America.”
