Lame Duck Session Begins Today

November 12, 2014 Politics

President Obama and congressional leaders have a full plate of issues to handle in the lame-duck session. (Dennis Brack-Pool/Getty Images)

Congress returns from its election recess on Wednesday to begin what is expected to be a six-week lame duck session before new lawmakers are seated in January.

On the larger agenda are plans for a budget extension past Dec. 11, a new authorization of military force for operations against the Islamic State, and the completion of the annual defense authorization bill. But the first week will start off slow, with just a few oversight hearings on the White House’s Ebola response, Veterans Affairs Department reforms and military operations in Iraq and Syria.

Hearing times and locations are subject to change.

Wednesday

Senate Appropriations — 2 p.m. — Dirksen Senate Office Building, G50

U.S. Government Response to the Ebola Outbreak

The hearing will feature testimony from Assistant Secretary of Defense for Special Operations Michael Lumpkin, Department of Homeland Security Secretary Jeh Johnson, and a host of Health and Human Services officials.
