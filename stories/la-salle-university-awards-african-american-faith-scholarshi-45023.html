La Salle University Awards African-American Faith Scholarships

November 13, 2014 Black

Dr. Robin Smith; RosaLee Smith, wife of the late Dr. Warren E. Smith; and award recipients Safiya Lynch, ’15, Nursing; Daniel Smith, ’17, Business Administration; Ida Teklu, ’16, Political Science; and JaQuay Whatley, ’17, Biology.

The La Salle University African American Alumni Association Awards Dinner and the Charles Crumbley Sr. Service Awards showed how African-American faith translates into dollars and cents to support the community. The former was held at La Salle University, a Catholic University in the tradition of the Brothers of the Christian Schools, on Nov. 7. The latter was hosted by the Philadelphia Club Frontiers International at the PECO Building on Sunday.

At La Salle the Dr. Warren E. Smith, M.D. Award recognizes an outstanding African-American leader serving the community. The Smith family presents this award to someone who demonstrates a commitment to traditional Lasallian values. These include excellence in teaching, fostering communities working collaboratively, direct service to the poor and social justice, and an education that fosters faith development. Dr. Smith is a 1954 La Salle alumnus.

This year’s Dr. Warren E. Smith Award recipient was Susan L. Taylor, founder of the National CARES Mentoring Movement. Taylor, a fourth generation entrepreneur, is editor-in-chief emeritus of Essence magazine. National CARES is committed to breaking the cycle of intergenerational poverty among African Americans.

“Linking arms and aims, we fortunate ones can end the dream-crushing pain that is limiting the lives of millions of our nation’s children trapped in unstable communities, fractured families and under-resourced schools,” Taylor said. “The Rising [initiatives] are designed to re-culture our young to help struggling students and youngsters returning from incarceration to rediscover the innate genius that is inherent in all.”
