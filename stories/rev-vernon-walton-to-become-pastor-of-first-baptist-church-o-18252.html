Rev. Vernon Walton to become Pastor of First Baptist Church of Vienna in Northern Virginia

April 16, 2014 Uncategorized

BERNADETTE MARCINIAK/STAFF PHOTOGRAPHER
Over the years, the Rev. Vernon Walton of Englewood acted as a community leader, serving as a pastor, councilman and freeholder.

The Rev. Vernon Walton – Bergen County’s first black freeholder – has moved to northern Virginia to take on a new assignment after 16 years as pastor of the Mount Calvary Baptist Church in Englewood.

Walton will be installed next month as senior pastor of the First Baptist Church of Vienna, a 146-year-old church with a congregation of 1,100 people that draws from Washington, D.C, Maryland and Virginia.

Leaving Mount Calvary and Bergen County was not an easy decision, Walton said Tuesday.

“It was extremely difficult,” Walton said, citing friendships and ties both to his church and to Englewood, where he served one term as an at-large city councilman.

He is nearly done cutting those ties. On Friday, he resigned from his part-time $17,600-a-year job as one of the two Democratic commissioners on the County Board of Elections. Democratic County Chairman Lou Stellato is expected to appoint a replacement later this week.

He also resigned from the Englewood Housing Authority board and will soon resign from the board of trustees at Ramapo College, where he was appointed by former Gov. James McGreevy in 2006.

While he called his time on the Ramapo board “a joy,” Walton said he is concerned that the all-white, all-male board he leaves behind doesn’t reflect the diversity of the student body.

Board Chairman George Ruotolo Jr. said he shares that concern and is working to diversify the board.

Ruotolo described Walton as “an inspiration.”

“He has helped me look at things in a different way and from a different perspective. I’m going to miss him greatly.”

Walton said he always has seen his faith and social justice as inseparable. “I really feel I am called to serve the church, but I feel very strongly that the church cannot neglect its community responsibility,” he said.

The 42-year-old Jersey City native first came to Bergen County at the urging of Pastor Gregory Jackson, whom he had gotten to know through their church work while they were both in Hudson County.

After Jackson became pastor of Mount Olive Baptist Church in Hackensack, he hired Walton first as a part-time youth minister and then an assistant pastor before recommending him for the post at Mount Calvary. Jackson also alerted Walton to the opening for a pastor in Vienna.
