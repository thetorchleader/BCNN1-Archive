Fundraiser in North Jersey Focuses on Black Women’s Stress and Health

March 31, 2014 Uncategorized

TIM FARRELL / SPECIAL TO THE RECORD
Minnie Adams of Glen Rock, Brenda White of Montclair and Alicia Henderson of Closter chat at the Jack and Jill of America, Inc., Bergen-Passaic Chapter, annual Literary Luncheon Fundraiser.

Several African-American authors told a group of black women Sunday that if they continue to selflessly take on too many burdens in their lives — at both home and work — they can risk life-threatening stress and other problems.

An audience of nearly 200 women heard the warnings at the second Literary Luncheon Fundraiser held by the Bergen-Passaic Chapter of Jack and Jill of America Inc., a national organization devoted to supporting cultural, civic, social and educational opportunities for children.

The event, held at the Knickerbocker Country Club in Tenafly, raised money for Jack and Jill’s partnership with the Boys & Girls Club of Paterson and Passaic, which serves 6,500 children and teenagers annually, said its executive director, Peter Thornton. The luncheon’s goal was to raise about $5,000 for programs with the club, said Shirell Gross, president of the Bergen-Passaic Jack and Jill chapter. It has 55 mothers as members, and their teenagers also volunteer and work with youths at the Boys & Girls Club.

This year’s literary speakers were Dr. Jeffrey Gardere, an author known as “America’s Psychologist” who often appears on such TV outlets as CNN and “The Today Show”; Carol M. Mackey, writer of “Sistergirl Devotions: Keeping Jesus in the Mix on the Job”; and Dr. Theda Palmer Saxton, author of “The Seasoned Woman Speaks,” about empowerment for mature females.

Gardere discussed the havoc that stress — with its links to cancer, high blood pressure, kidney stones, acid reflux, headaches — plays on a person’s health and how black women are more susceptible to its impact.

“A lot of the research that I’ve done shows that … women of color, African-American women, this is the group that if nothing else, for some reason tends to sit on that stress, tend not to talk about the stress,” Gardere said.

African-American women are sometimes the single figure in one-parent homes and subject to racism, he said. Those are stresses that lead to high blood pressure rates among black women, contributing to strokes, he added. Gardere advised women to talk about stress, seek help and develop ways to cope with it.

Mackey, a born-again Christian, is editor-in-chief of the Black Expressions Book Club and a publishing industry veteran. Her book, “Sistergirl Devotions,” is aimed at black women in all walks of life, she said.
