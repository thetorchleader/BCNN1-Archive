Has Herman Cain Hurt Black Conservatives?

December 9, 2011 Uncategorized

What does the fiasco of Herman Cain’s now suspended GOP presidential candidacy do for the future prospects of black conservatives who want to run for high national office? The short answer: It doesn’t help. For one, Cain’s actions magnify the stereotype that black males are smooth operators who can’t be faithful to their wives.
Sure, Cain is a successful businessman, and he isn’t the first black conservative to have his public image tarnished. Michael Steele had a bumpy ride as chairman of the Republican National Committee. Alan Keyes was probably better known for his radio and TV commentary than his multiple unsuccessful campaigns for the presidency and U.S. Senate.
In their defense, black conservatives always seem to be caught in the political crossfire of race and loyalty to “the race.” Still, despite conservative Ann Coulter’s smug proclamation on national TV last month that “our blacks are so much better than their blacks,” I’d beg to disagree.
Lest we forget, Cain exhibited an embarrassing lack of knowledge — or, at least, a lack of preparation — on serious foreign policy matters such as Libya and China. Those images, coupled with a recent comment by the conservative NationalReview columnist Victor Davis Hanson that “black authenticity, as defined by Southern mannerisms and darker complexion, amplified by conservatism or traditionalism, earns liberal unease,” make me wonder why any black conservative in his or her right mind would want to be one.
