Democratic Missouri Representative William Lacy Clay says Voters in Ferguson Need to ‘Clean House’ on Local Police Force

March 15, 2015 Black

Rep. William Lacy Clay (D-Mo.) on Sunday said voters in Ferguson, Mo. should replace city officials in the wake of a scathing Department of Justice (DOJ) report that found systemic racial discrimination. 

“You know, out of this practices and patterns report from the DOJ, you can tell it was system-wide, the way they targeted the people of that community to – as really an ATM machine to derive revenue in order for the city government to function,” he said on ABC’s “This Week.” “They need to clean house from top to bottom.”

Clay cited Ferguson’s upcoming municipal elections on April 7 as an opportunity for citizens to start fresh with the government there. The city has roiled in turmoil since last year, when Darren Wilson, a white police officer, shot Michael Brown, an unarmed black teen.

“It – the real test for the voters of Ferguson will be April the seventh in their municipal election and in the surrounding communities, George,” he told “This Week” host George Stephanopoulos. “If they were – get enough signatures, on a petition to recall the mayor, then another election will be set and I will go about educating the voters of Ferguson on what their rights are and just how they can change their city government for the better.”

Clay said he would dismantle the entire Ferguson police force if its discriminatory practices against minorities there warranted it.

“If that’s what justified the existence of the police force, then, ‘yes,’ ” he said when Stephanopoulos asked him about the Department of Justice’s report on racial discrimination by Ferguson’s law enforcement.

“The police force should probably be dismantled and allow the St. Louis County Police, a better trained force, a more qualified force, a larger force, to actually come in and conduct policing in a totally different way that restores the trust of the people, that shows respect for the people who pays their salaries and to start and to stop judging people by the color of their skins and criminalizing it because of what color they are, which is evident – is what was happening up to this point in Ferguson and surrounding communities,” Clay said.
