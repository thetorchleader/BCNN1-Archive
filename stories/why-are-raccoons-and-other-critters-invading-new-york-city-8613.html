Why are Raccoons and Other Critters Invading New York City?

October 8, 2012 Uncategorized

First came reports of alligators at area supermarkets–and not in the food section, either, but in the parking lot. Now come the stories of marauding raccoons, tipping over garbage cans and even breaking into homes.
It may be premature to declare that New York is under siege by amphibians and rodents, but residents are certainly having to cope with unexpected intruders in their day-to-day affairs.
The gators, Yahoo News reports, turned up last week in the parking lot of a Pathmark supermarket in Nassau County on Long Island. Customers entering and leaving the store were taken aback at the sight of the swamp creatures, one measuring as much as 4 feet in length.
The gators were captured by agents of the Nassau County Emergency Services Unit and will be shipped off to a reptile sanctuary in Florida. Authorities believe the reptiles were dumped at the location.
The raccoons, meanwhile, seem intent on holding their ground. CBS New York reports that the creatures are brazenly confronting residents of Clinton Hill, Brooklyn. A resident told reporters that that one of the masked critters tried to gain entrance to her home, and another said that a coon stood up on its hind legs and hissed at her.
To add insult to injury, the raccoons are evading capture by the most determined of trappers. Rich Weddle of the Animal Husbandry Department at Liberty Science Center explains. Raccoons, he says, “are extremely intelligent animals. In terms of trying to trap them, once they’ve been trapped once they’ll know not to go in that trap again.”
Residents of the community have called 3-1-1 but have been told that the city will not intervene on their behalf unless the raccoons appear disoriented and potentially rabid. And that is a risk, says Weddle, who notes that raccoons can also spread distemper and parasites, such as fleas and ticks, which is a concern for dog owners.
