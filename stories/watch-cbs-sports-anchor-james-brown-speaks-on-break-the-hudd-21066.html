WATCH: CBS Sports Anchor James Brown Speaks on “Break the Huddle and Run the Play” at Houston Church; Discusses Donald Sterling Controversy on “Face the Nation”

May 5, 2014 Christian

With less than a week remaining before the NFL Draft, CBS Sports anchor James Brown made a quick visit Sunday to Houston for purposes other than football.

Brown, host of “The NFL Today” and an ordained minister since 2009, delivered two sermons at First Baptist Church in Pasadena, speaking on the topic “Break the Huddle and Run the Play.” Brown was invited to speak to the Pasadena church after meeting Dr. Charles Redmond and Dr. Jon Redmond, the church’s pastor and assistant pastor, last year at the Billy Graham Evangelistic Association’s training center in North Carolina.

Brown’s sermon text was the familiar passage from the book of James that includes the phrase “faith without works (for which Brown substituted the word “action” in his sermon) is dead.” He also spoke of his own religious faith, his family, the occasions when bystanders have confused him with the late James Brown, the Godfather of Soul, while citing a number of football analogies, including his conviction that men and women have only a limited amount of time to take on the tasks given them by the Almighty and the play clock, in a manner of speaking, is ticking for everyone.

His appearance in Houston came the same day that CBS News, for which he is a contributor, aired his interview with Bob Schieffer on “Face the Nation” regarding the Donald Sterling controversy. In fact, Brown cited a Bible verse during that interview to drive home his thoughts about the manner in which Sterling’s opinions about race may have influenced his actions as owner of the Clippers.

“I firmly believe that out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks (a reference to Luke 6:45), and what Mr. Sterling spoke really colored and influenced the decisions that he’s made with his organization and reflects across a number of sports organizations,” he said.

Brown said he hopes the furor surrounding Sterling’s remarks will “force meaningful conversation, not in homogeneous groups, but with inclusive and diverse groups to really get the core of this.”

“This really continues to be the third rail of American politics from my humble perspective and it’s sickening that this has been going on this long and prudent folks, intelligent folks, mature folks, can’t have a meaningful dialogue objective,” he added.
