Apple to Reportedly Add Security Alerts for iCloud Users

September 5, 2014 Technology

Tim Cook, chief executive officer of Apple Inc., attends the Allen & Company Sun Valley Conference on July 9, 2014, in Sun Valley, Idaho.
(Photo: Scott Olson, Getty Images)

Apple plans to launch security alerts for iCloud users in the wake of a recent scandal in which someone hacked celebrity accounts and posted their nude photos on the Internet, according to a new report.

Apple CEO Tim Cook has said in his first interview since the dust-up this week that the technology giant will alert users via e-mail and push notifications if someone tries to change a password, move iCloud data to a new device or if a device logs into an account for the first time. Cook made the comments to the Wall Street Journal.

Up until now, iCloud users would receive e-mails if someone tried to change a password or tried to log in for the first time from a new device, but the notifications for restoring iCloud data to a new device are new, according to the news organization.

Earlier this week, Apple announced that after an investigation, it discovered that someone infiltrated “certain” celebrity accounts and stole photos in a “very targeted” plot that involved user names, passwords and security questions.

Cook also told the Journal that Apple will increase its use of “two-factor authentication,” which requires any user to have two of three pieces of intricate information before being able to log in.

Actress Jennifer Lawrence and model Kate Upton were among those whose accounts were hacked.
