All My Children (Part 5)

November 28, 2011 Uncategorized

by Joey Johnson 
November 28, 2011
Click here to read previous articles by Joey Johnson

This story doesn’t show us only Joseph’s family
mess–it shows us the mess in his brothers, too. When his brothers
heard the dream, they hated him even more. Family mess! They hated
their own brother. How many of us hate our own relatives? Maybe
you’d better not answer that.

Then Joseph had another dream. This time the dream seemed to indicate
that not only his brothers but his father and mother would one day have
to bow down before him. He again shared it with his brothers, and they
hated him even more. More family mess!

Finally Joseph decided to tell his parents about this dream. Now it
wasn’t only his brothers hating him, now his father had to rebuke
him. The prophecy, which told of the destiny God had for Joseph, did
not bring encouragement, happiness, or affirmation–at first. The
dream brought him loneliness, misunderstanding, hatred, mistreatment,
rebuke, and jealousy from his family members.

Likewise, Satan uses family mess, loneliness, misunderstanding, hatred,
mistreatment, rebuke, and jealousy to discourage the prophecy, promise,
and destiny of God in our lives. He has us think: “If my own
mother doesn’t like me, then who could love me? If my own father
won’t take time with me, then who can love me? If my own people
think this way about me, then who can love me?”

The question you need to take into consideration is not, “What do
people think about me?” but “What does God think about
me?” In order to know what God thinks about you, you’ve got
to read some of what God thinks so you can find out what He thinks
about you. You’ll find it in His Word. What “they”
think about you will not determine your destiny–unless you let it.

But even though Jacob, now called Israel, was upset by the dream, the
Bible tells us that he kept it in mind. He thought about it. He
didn’t altogether dismiss it. His age and experience with God
would not let him completely discount it.

When your children tell you something that God is saying to them,
listen and encourage them, even if you don’t like the sound of it
right away.
