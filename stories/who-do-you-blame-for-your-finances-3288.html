Who Do You Blame for Your Finances?

January 24, 2012 Uncategorized

“The way that I was raised, the wife would take care of the bills, and the husband would go out and make the money,” says Dexter Brown.
Wife Cynthia agrees. “I thought that the husband makes sure the bills get paid and makes sure the family is well taken care of.”
Neither Cynthia nor Dexter Brown’s expectations were met. No one made a household budget or monitored the spending, and no one kept up with the bills.
“The utility bills, the regular bills – we would put that on the back burner and spend the money on things we wanted,” says Dexter.
“I couldn’t understand how he could be a budget analyst and handle so much money throughout the day, and then come home and everything was a mess,” says Cynthia. 
  
This included the Brown’s marriage. 
Dexter remembers, “I started blaming my wife. My wife started blaming me.”
“To me as a woman, I need to know. I need to feel safe, and I didn’t feel safe,” she says.
That’s because Cynthia and Dexter were $15,000 in debt. Their electricity and cable were cut off, and all four of their cars were repossessed. When the Browns faced foreclosure on their home, they filed for bankruptcy. Then things got worse. The couple received a second foreclosure notice that said if they did not pay $2,000 immediately, they would be evicted from their home.
Cynthia turned to God and said, “If You’ll help me get back on track, I ‘m going to do the right thing.” 
And just one day before they were to be evicted, the Browns sold a van they’d received as a gift, for – you guessed it – $2,000, so they didn’t have to leave their home. Now it was time to make good on that promise to God.
“I had to swallow my pride and ask for help,” says Dexter.
