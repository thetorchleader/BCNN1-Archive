3D Print Your Meals With Foodini

November 7, 2014 Technology

After a long day at the office, the last thing you want to do is tie on an apron and cook a meal. How about just printing one?

Startup Natural Machines has introduced a 3D printer for food, dubbed Foodini, which creates food with stainless steel capsules and edible ingredients.

“It’s the same technology” as regular 3D printers, co-founder Lynette Kucsma told CNN. “But with plastics there’s just one melting point, whereas with food it’s different temperatures, consistencies, and textures.”

Introduced during the Web Summit technology conference in Dublin, the Foodini serves as a sort of mini food manufacturing plant, about the size of a microwave oven, which can print a range of dishes, from sweet to savory.

While the thought of having a steaming plate of melted capsules for dinner might not sound super appetizing, Natural Machines says that its printers will utilize fresh ingredients.

“The food is real food, made from fresh ingredients prepared before printing,” the company says. “Promoting cooking with fresh ingredients, Foodini manages the difficult and time-consuming parts of food preparation that often discourage people from creating homemade food.”

That means you’ll still have to bake or boil the food before consuming it.

Still, pre-packaged plastic capsules for quick loading and printing is in the works, the company told CNN, though they would probably expire after about five days, so no stockpiling your Foodini capsules.

Initially, Natural Machines is marketing the Foodini to professionals, but a consumer version is also expected – for about $1,000.
