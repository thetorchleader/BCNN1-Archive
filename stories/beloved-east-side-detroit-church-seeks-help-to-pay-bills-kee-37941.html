Beloved East Side Detroit Church Seeks Help to Pay Bills, Keep Programs Running

October 7, 2014 Black

The Church of the Messiah of Detroit’s east side is a different kind of church. It’s a place where drug dealers, criminals and the poor find hope and opportunity. Despite its success, the church is struggling to pay the bills.

Dwight Roston was on probation when he first came to the Church of the Messiah. He is no longer breaking the law. He is making music in the church basement, in what’s known as the Makerspace.

“I ended up recording my first two CD’s down here,” said Roston.

The 20-year-old recently opened up for award winning singer Kem and was a model in Detroit’s Fashion Week. Those are experiences he had because of the church.  Without it, he believes he would still be in trouble with the law because before he found new opportunities through the church, his life was about survival.

Through the years, the church has been recognized world-wide for its programs and for helping Detroit’s inner city kids. Seventeen of whom are in college right now.

“The White House has been here twice, Popular Mechanics, Crains Detroit has featured the church and we are part of three documentary films as we speak,” said Pastor Barry Randolph.

Despite the glowing reviews, it could all go away. Programs take money and the coffers at the church are just about dry.
