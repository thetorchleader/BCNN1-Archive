U.S. Women Beat Mexico to Qualify for World Cup

October 26, 2014 Sports

United States midfielder Carli Lloyd (10) is congratulated by teammates after scoring against Mexico on a penalty kick during the first half of a women’s World Cup Qualifier soccer match at PPL Park. Mandatory Credit: Bill Streicher-USA TODAY Sports

The U.S. women are in.

Two first-half goals from Carli Lloyd set the tempo and Team USA clinched a spot in the 2015 World Cup with a 3-0 win over Mexico on Friday night at PPL Park.

It was a much different atmosphere and outcome from the last time these teams played in a qualifying match four years ago. The game was in Cancun, Mexico won, and the U.S. was sent down a more difficult path in order to make the 2011 tournament.

The Americans started off fast like they wanted with Lloyd scoring in the sixth minute. Tobin Heath sent a perfect cross into the middle of the box and Lloyd headed it in to put her team up 1-0.

Coach Jill Ellis said after the game getting Lloyd in position to score was part of their plan and will be at times moving forward.

“We try to get her closest to the goal in areas we think she’d be more dangerous, and obviously she’s producing,” Ellis said.

After the goal, Mexico began to sit back and the U.S. controlled the pace and created more chances, which is something Ellis predicted would happen. At one point in the first half, the Americans had three shots on goal in 30 seconds, but none went in. Christen Press had hers blocked, her rebound crossed by Ali Krieger was cleared out, and that was followed by two more shots by Lauren Holiday and Heath.

But in the 30th minute, Heath streaked down the left flank and was fouled in the box. Lloyd took the penalty kick to put the U.S. up 2-0.

After that, Mexico got antsy, fouling two more times in the next four minutes. Their game plan of trying to create traffic and disturb U.S. combinations wasn’t panning out. But coach Leonardo Cuellar said after the game he held out several skill players so they could be fresh for Sunday’s game against Trinidad and Tobago. A win will qualify them for the World Cup.

The U.S. had more chances in the second half—hitting the crossbar in the 49th minute, the right post in the 61st and the left in the 84th.

Press — who had multiple chances in the first half, hitting the outside netting twice and going over the top of the goal once — finally got herself a goal in the 57th minute. The first-year national player is proving to be a dominant hustler up top, which is key without Alex Morgan. Her performance in this tournament could see her become a permanent fixture in the starting lineup even when Morgan returns.

“She’s got a lot of versatility and I think it’s just continuing to demand more form her,” Ellis said. “I think she’s got a lot in her locker and we’re just scratching the surface.”

The U.S. played most of the game without Abby Wambach, as the star forward didn’t come off the bench until the 62nd minute. No reason other than the U.S. has an incredible amount of depth at forward with six players or so who can be rotated. But Ellis doesn’t put much into who starts versus who finishes games.

“I’ve said from day one we have a lot of depth and so I think I can look at certain games and select tactically and personnel based on how I see the game,” Ellis said. “So I mean yeah, based on what I thought and what I felt, we wanted her to come off the bench. And she’s been fantastic. Her role on and off the field. She’s been superb.”

The U.S. will play Costa Rica in the CONCACAF final on Sunday at 6 p.m. ET, and both teams are already guaranteed spots in the World Cup. Ellis has watched Costa Rica play and sees similarities to Mexico.

“As far as technical players that like to counter against you and sit deeper, so I think that’s given us a bigger picture of what to expect,” she said. “More importantly, and I’ve said this from day one, we’re focusing on us.”

Though the game is meaningless for both teams in terms of spending next summer in Canada, the Americans don’t want to lose.

“For me, it’s a final and a final, we’re going to win,” Lloyd said. “We want to win decisively. This was a great win, we’re heading to Canada, but we got one more and we want to win.”
