Jawbone Fitness Band Finds That People Workout Most on Mondays

October 25, 2014 Technology

The Jawbone UP computer or smartphone-connected fitness band. (PHOTO CREDIT: Jawbone)

Oh, optimism.

After falling off diets and exercise regimes all weekend, people start the new week with a flourish of exercise, running, walking or hitting the gym. And then their habits gradually deteriorate as the week progresses, a new data analysis shows.

Jawbone, the maker of computer-connected fitness bands, collected data from a sample of more than 100,000 of its U.S. users in a 12-month period ending in August. Users of the Jawbone UP band automatically log sleep, inactivity and walking just by wearing the band. They also can record their type of workouts — say taking a yoga class or playing basketball — plus food intake into a connected app.

The sheer number of users allows for very big data sets, which the company’s vice president of data, Monica Rogati, said should allow it to draw some conclusions about exercise habits that smaller studies can’t.

What Jawbone found in terms of exercising isn’t exactly shocking, but it puts some numbers on what many might suspect from their own habits. Similar to the surge of gym memberships sold during the start of the year, overall workouts peak on Monday, are fairly steady Tuesday and Wednesday, and then fall apart.
