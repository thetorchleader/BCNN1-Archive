E-Book Software Company Trajectory Signs Deal With Tencent

March 16, 2015 Books

It’s a fact: even though printed books are not yet dead, there’s an increasing number of people who are turning to e-books to read their favorite book.

Trajectory, a Marblehead-based company launched three years ago by two technology veterans, seeks to not only help publishers distribute their e-books to online retailers around the world but also help them categorize those e-books.

Trajectory co-founders Jim Bryant and Scott Beatty launched the self-funded company out of the Cambridge Innovation Center in 2012 with the aim of helping publishers and retailers connect.

The business of distributing those e-books has become complicated with multiple e-readers such as Amazon’s Kindle and Barnes and Noble’s Nook on the market. Add to that a growing number of retailers that are selling ebooks and a surge in the number of publishers providing those e-books (there are about 1 million English-language ebooks on the market today).

Trajectory’s software works by scanning entire e-books and then, using its proprietary software, spitting out various pieces of data that can help retailers and libraries better categorize their ebook collection.

“We are able to curate and create new genres, micro-categories on the fly,” said Beatty. “It’s really akin to Netflix in how they categorize and tag and really create new categories of book.”

For example, Beatty said, “a big hot genre is apocalyptic fiction, but they don’t have a category for that. Everything can get carved up.”

The company’s software then distributes e-books from publishers to various retailers.

Bryant said the company currently has 100 publishers as customers and is working with over 350 e-book and library distributors. While Bryant declined to disclose revenue for the company, Trajectory makes its money when a book is sold (it receives a percentage of the sales).
