WATCH: Rev. LuTimothy May and Other Pensacola, Florida Pastors and Leaders Call for an End to Gun Violence

June 17, 2014 Black

Robert Gross, the father of slain Shaquille Purifoy, speaks during a press conference Monday morning at St. John The Divine Baptist Church.
(Photo: Tony Giberson/tgiberson@pnj.com )

Frustration crept into the Rev. LuTimothy May’s voice as he found himself addressing another group of concerned citizens about another homicide at another “Stop the Violence” news conference in Pensacola.

“After doing two or three of these per year, per church, you start to wonder — when is it going to stop?” he said.

May was one of about three dozen pastors, elected officials and area residents who gathered at St. John Divine Missionary Baptist Church on Monday morning following the fatal shooting of 20-year-old Shaquille Purifoy.

Shaquille Purifoy dead of gunshot wound

Arrest made in Purifoy killing

Purifoy, who died in the hospital after being shot in the head Friday, is the sixth homicide victim in the county this year. He is among more than a dozen victims of shootings this year, most of whom have been young black males.

On Monday, Purifoy’s father, Robert Gross, said it was up to each government organization, each church and each family to take a role in ending the violence, starting with himself.

“I don’t want retaliation. I don’t want revenge. I want to cease fire,” Gross said.

Xavier Tyrone Moore, 39, is charged with third degree murder in Purifoy’s death.

The 20-year-old was found in the 8400 block of Country Walk Drive, north of East Olive Road, with a gunshot wound to the head. He died a few hours later, and Moore reportedly went to the Escambia County Sheriff’s Office and confessed to the shooting.
