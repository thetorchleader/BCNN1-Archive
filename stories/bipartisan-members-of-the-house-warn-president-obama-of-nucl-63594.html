Bipartisan Members of the House Warn President Obama of Nuclear Iran

March 25, 2015 Politics

U.S. President Barack Obama answers a question while holding a joint press conference with President of Afghanistan Ashraf Ghani in the East Room of the White House March 24, 2015 in Washington, DC.
Win McNamee/Getty Images North America

Last Friday, a bipartisan group of 367 Members of Congress sent a letter to President Obama emphasizing that any agreement with Iran involving the sanctions passed by the United States Congress would require new legislation: “In reviewing such an agreement, Congress must be convinced that its terms foreclose any pathway to a bomb, and only then will Congress be able to consider permanent sanctions relief.”

“Resolving the nuclear crisis with Iran remains of grave importance to our nation’s security,” the group of Congressmen wrote. “As the Administration continues to negotiate with Iran, we are prepared to evaluate any agreement to determine its long-term impact on the United States and our allies.”

“The Obama administration’s brokered deal with Iran is misguided and a danger to our own national security, the security of Israel, and the world at large,” said Mat Staver, Founder and Chairman of Liberty Counsel. “Iran is building a nuclear program capable of destroying Israel and reaching the United States. President Obama and Secretary John Kerry need to stop pandering to Iran and need to play hardball. Iran is a terrorist regime, and we should never allow terrorists to have a nuclear bomb,” said Staver.
