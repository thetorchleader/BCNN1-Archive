Duke and Wisconsin to Play in Championship Game Tonight

April 6, 2015 Sports

© Mary Langenfeld-USA TODAY Sports Mary Langenfeld-USA TODAY Sports

When the coaching staffs from Duke and Wisconsin cued up clips from their Dec. 3 matchup, they watched the Blue Devils shoot 65% from field, they watched a hobbled Sam Dekker struggle and they watched Wisconsin allow an uncharacteristic 80 points on its home court.

But the biggest takeaway from reexamining the first meeting between Monday night’s NCAA championship participants is how much these teams have changed, evolved and improved in four months.

They won’t be playing for the national championship because they are replicas of those teams. They are playing for the title because they aren’t.

“I watched part of that game, and I just looked different too,” said Dekker, who was nursing an ankle injury and scored five points during Duke’s 80-70 victory. “I looked real slow.”

Both teams needed to improve defensively, and both have. Duke’s trio of freshmen – Jahlil Okafor, Tyus Jones and Justise Winslow – have continued to blossom. Wisconsin’s Frank Kaminsky said his team looked “lost” offensively at times against Duke. Now they are an historically efficient offensive team.

Duke dismissed Rasheed Sulaimon, who had 14 points in the game. Wisconsin saw Traevon Jackson, who scored 25 points in the game, miss 19 games because of injury. And the Badgers have seen Bronson Koenig emerge as a big-game difference maker.

“We have each grown and have taken steps forward, and you have to,” Wisconsin assistant coach Greg Gard said. “You can’t be the same as you were Dec. 3 and still be playing here in April. Both teams’ young guys have grown up. The stars have cemented themselves as who they are. But neither team has gotten out of their character of who they are.”

Duke coach Mike Krzyzewski said the most striking aspect of that first meeting was that his freshmen players did not appear nervous even in the first true road game of their college careers, one played inside the hostile Kohl Center. Jones, who called it his best game of the season, scored 22 points, while Okafor made 6 of his 8 field goal attempts.

“I mean, you can’t teach that,” Krzyzewski said of their lack of nerves. “They had it. Win or lose, I was going to come away from that game knowing that this would be a team that wouldn’t be afraid of the bright lights and the big stage.”

As senior Quinn Cook said, “It was shocking to me how we performed, especially our younger guys.”

Winslow has made such huge strides since early in the season that he’s arguably Duke’s most important freshman. During the tournament, he has averaged 15 points and 9.4 rebounds, while shooting 58.3% from three-point range.

He scored just five points in the first meeting and recalls guarding Dekker and how both players struggled offensively.
