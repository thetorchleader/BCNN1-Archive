Church Members from the Shiloh Missionary Baptist in Milwaukee Seek Restraining Order Against Pastor Over Church Property

November 6, 2011 Uncategorized
For the third time in three years, members of a north side church have turned to the courts to mediate a dispute with their pastor.
Leaders of Shiloh Missionary Baptist Church, 4801 W. Capitol Drive, will be in court Monday seeking a temporary restraining order requiring the Rev. Carl W. Griffin to turn over all church property and vacate the building while the dispute is pending.
The members sued Griffin in October, saying they voted to dismiss him as their pastor in April but he refuses to leave.
Shiloh’s board chairman, Dorothy Phillips, declined to comment on the underlying issues, and the lawsuit says the church has “no intention of . . . airing them unnecessarily in public.”
But the lawsuit adds – and Griffin agrees – that there are fundamental “disagreements about the proper relationship among the pastor, the governing bodies of the church and members.”
Board members say Shiloh is a congregational-rule church in which the pastor and governing board are accountable to members. Griffin said in an interview that the pastor and deacons govern the church as long as they follow the Bible and bylaws, and that he has the overwhelming support of the congregation.
