Crane Collapses in Manhattan, Injuring 7 People and Damaging Buildings

May 31, 2015 National

A crane that was lifting a large piece of equipment to the top of 261 Madison Avenue in Manhattan damaged nearby buildings on Sunday. (Robert Stolarik for The New York Times)

A crane lifting a heavy piece of equipment to the roof of an office tower in Midtown Manhattan struck the building and dropped its load on Sunday morning, scraping several buildings and injuring seven people, city officials said.

The crane was operating at 261 Madison Avenue when it struck the building around 10:43 a.m., officials said. The equipment fell 30 stories to the street below.

The Fire Department said two construction workers and five motorists sustained minor injuries. The construction workers were taken to Bellevue Hospital Center and two others were taken to NYU Langone Medical Center. Three people refused medical attention.

At a news conference, Mayor Bill de Blasio said the incident was “very serious.” He said 10 people were injured, but the Fire Department later said that was a miscount.

Officials said it was unclear what caused the crane to drop the equipment.

Rick D. Chandler, the commissioner of New York City Department of Buildings, said the device was in good shape. The site has had active construction permits since February, and there were no open complaints for the site, he added.

He said his agency was conducting a full investigation.
