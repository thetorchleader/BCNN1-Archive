WATCH: As Community Awaited Decision, Many in Ferguson Focused on Prayer, Not Protests; Bishop Harry Jackson says ‘God is Giving St. Louis an Invitation to Become a Place of Healing for the Nation’

November 25, 2014 Christian, Slider

Harry R. Jackson Jr.

Heavy rain did not stop large groups of people from gathering in churches for prayer, and in street protests across the St. Louis area Sunday. Many of those community members were focused on praying for Ferguson, or asking for peaceful resolve as a grand jury continues to meet.

It was an evening of prayer at Metro Christian Worship Center of St. Louis Sunday, as hundreds from all denominations gathered to pray for Ferguson and beyond. The night time service was part of an annual Urban World Summit, an event planned prior to the August unrest in Ferguson.

“I believe that what God is giving St. Louis an invitation to become is a healing center for the nation,” Bishop Harry Jackson, Jr. said.

A few miles away, there was a large gathering of a different kind.

Nearly 100 protesters shut down Shaw Street momentarily. They were marching from the corner where armed 18-year-old Vonderrit Myers was shot by a police officer early last month.

One protester from St. Louis, who only identified herself as “Emily,” said she has participated in several recent protests.

“I think it’s important that we show the country and the world that the status quo is not acceptable and we have to change things,” Emily said.

The protesters, many of whom traveled into the area from cities across the country, said they were marching for Myers, Brown, and those who were tired of being unfairly targeted in their communities.

“You know we drive a certain car, we live in a certain area, you know we’ve got to be a criminal,” Miss Tay, a protester from Ferguson speaking out about frustrations between police and community relations, said. “You know, so, It’s not just about Michael Brown, it’s about us being treated fair and us being able to do what everybody else can do as a citizen of the United States.”

As groups continue waiting and watching to see what the 12 members of the Grand Jury determining Wilson’s fate may decide, some community members say they’re remaining committed to prayer. While some others say they will remain passionate about making their voices heard.
