Sarah Collins Rudolph, Birmingham Church Bombing Survivor, to Visit Buffalo for Premiere of New Play, “Somebody Done Bombed the 16 Street Baptist Church”

February 17, 2015 Black

Sarah Collins Rudolph, the only survivor of the 1963 bombing of the 16th Street Baptist Church in Birmingham, Ala., that killed her older sister and three other girls, will visit Buffalo’s Mount Olive Baptist Church on Feb. 28 to attend the premiere of a new play about the bombing by Buffalo playwright Marie Mullen.

The play, “Somebody Done Bombed the 16 Street Baptist Church,” has been a yearlong labor of love for Mullen, a native of Birmingham and friend of Rudolph’s slain older sister who moved to Buffalo in 1968, in part to escape the oppressive atmosphere of the Jim Crow South.

Much of her writing and creative work with her church’s theater workshop ministry has been preoccupied with the bombing, the defining event of Mullen’s youth and a major flashpoint in the civil rights movement. Mullen said she’d planned to tag along to the 16th Street Baptist Church with her friend Addie Mae Collins on that Sunday morning in September, but decided at the last minute not to go.

Memories of that time have haunted Mullen ever since, and she’s been working on translating those memories to the stage on and off for the past 15 years. Because of her connection to Addie Mae Collins, who was 14 years old when she was killed in the ladies’ lounge of the 16th Street Baptist Church, Mullen was able to convince Rudolph to open up about her experience and grant permission for it to be used in her play.

“I was able, thank God, to win her over and build her confidence with me,” Mullen said. “She talked about how many people had hurt her after this and they had made money. They left her out. She really didn’t want to talk to me at first. But now we’re friends and they” – Rudoph and her husband, George – “can’t wait to get here to see this play.”
