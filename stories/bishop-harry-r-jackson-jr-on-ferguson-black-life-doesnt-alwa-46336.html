Bishop Harry R. Jackson Jr. on Ferguson: Black Life Doesn’t Always Seem to Count; This Is No Time for the Church to Be Silent

November 22, 2014 Christian

Harry R. Jackson Jr.

As events in Ferguson, Missouri unfold, prominent officials black and white are meeting in St. Louis with a plan to reduce racial divide.

The 28th Urban World Summit is being held at Metro Christian Worship Center in St. Louis, near the town where a police officer shot a teenager, and a grand jury decision is pending any day.

Bishop Harry Jackson of Hope Christian Church, located in the Washington, D.C. area, is there and is frank about his assessment.

“We’re not in a post-racial era but we have a problem here though that black life doesn’t always seem to count,” he tells OneNewsNow.

But he stresses that it should because all lives have value, especially in the eyes of God.

At the Summit, leaders are unveiling a long-term plan to reduce and eliminate tension and conflict on the basis of race, and to be fair to all entities including police.

Jackson also believes this is no time for the church to be silent.
