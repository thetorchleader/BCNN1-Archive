Pastor Mark Whitlock of Christ Our Redeemer AME in Irvine, CA Preaches a Debt-Free Lifestyle, Encourages Church Members to Cut Up Their Credit Cards

March 31, 2014 Uncategorized

Members of Christ Our Redeemer Church in Irvine rejoice after cutting up their credit cards. Pastor Mark Whitlock, a former banker, is preaching a debt-free lifestyle.
MINDY SCHAUER, STAFF PHOTOGRAPHER

Congregants at Christ Our Redeemer AME were in for a unique sermon Sunday, as members were urged to take scissors to their credit cards – all in the name of financial health.

By the end of the service, a few dozen of the 200-plus in attendance at Orange County’s largest African Methodist Episcopal Church had chopped up their Visas, American Expresses and MasterCards in a symbolic show to distance church members from credit card debt.

Andrea Robinson, 48, did it because it had been in her heart even before she started attending the church earlier this year.

“I’ve been seeking counsel and praying about it, and this was exactly what I needed,” the Costa Mesa resident said.

Jacquie Gray, a 13-year church member, said the act was more of a symbolic gesture, and a way to get people talking.

“I’m fortunate to not have much credit card debt, but if holding it up and cutting a card gets people thinking about money management, then I’m all for it,” Gray said.

During his sermon, the Rev. Mark Whitlock said the average African American has a credit score between 450 and 600, and the average American carries $15,000 in credit card debt.

“We want to help decrease the debt in communities of color,” Whitlock said.

A former banker and venture capitalist, Whitlock has been leading the county’s largest black church through a month’s worth of money management-themed sermons, and Sunday’s was the finale. Prior to preaching, Whitlock brought the congregation’s youth to the front row because he wanted them to hear the message loud and clear.

“You get to college, and the first thing you get handed is a credit card,” Whitlock said. “They’re quick to sell you that, but not the financial literacy.”

Gray said her 22-year-old son Derrick was participating in the church’s financial workshops held during the week.

“He’s learning Japanese in college, but he needs to learn the language of finance, as well,” she said.

While the credit card cutting lined up well with Whitlock’s sermon, does it line up with sound financial advice?

Natalie Lohrenz, a director of counseling at Orange County’s Consumer Credit Counseling Service, said the credit card-cutting gesture is a good start.

“If someone is in danger of credit card debt, the first thing to do is stop digging,” Lohrenz said. But she also warned that today, with online companies storing credit information, cutting the card isn’t enough. “There’s ways around it, and you have to be devoted to do it.”
