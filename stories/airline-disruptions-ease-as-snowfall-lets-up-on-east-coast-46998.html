Airline Disruptions Ease as Snowfall Lets Up on East Coast

November 27, 2014 National

Vehicles move through passenger pick-up lanes at Ronald Reagan National Airport (DCA), yesterday in Washington, D.C. (Photographer: Andrew Harrer/Bloomberg)

The number of flights delayed or canceled in the U.S. shrank as snowfall eased across the East Coast, providing relief to Thanksgiving passengers on one of the year’s busiest travel days.

Airlines canceled 67 flights within, into or out of the U.S. by 10 a.m. New York time today, compared with 750 yesterday, according to FlightAware, an online tracker. A total of 549 were delayed today, versus 4,814 yesterday. A Federal Aviation Administration website indicated delays today at major U.S. airports of 15 minutes or less.

Conditions will continue to improve throughout Thanksgiving Day across New England as the storm moves away from the coast, the National Weather Service said on its website today.

United Continental Holdings Inc. (UAL), American Airlines Group Inc. (AAL) and Delta Air Lines Inc. (DAL) this week waived their customary fees for flight changes for travel yesterday through about two dozen northeastern airports, allowing passengers to rebook flights for the previous day or today, in an effort to ease congestion before projected storms.

The Thanksgiving holiday period, which runs through Nov. 30, is the among the busiest travel times in the U.S. Air passenger numbers are projected to rise to the highest level since 2007, totaling 3.55 million, a 3 percent increase from a year earlier, according to the AAA auto club.

The eastern U.S. will remain colder than average with highs in the 30s and 40s degrees Fahrenheit. More moderate temperatures are likely by the weekend, the service said.

In the U.S. West, snow is forecast in the higher regions of the northern Rockies and rain in the lower valley areas of the Pacific Northwest and northern Great Basin. The greatest concentration of heavy snow will remain in Canada’s Alberta and British Columbia.

Cold weather will continue for the northern Plains and Upper Midwest, according to the service.
