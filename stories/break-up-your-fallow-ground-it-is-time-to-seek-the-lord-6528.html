Break Up Your Fallow Ground: It is Time to Seek the Lord

June 10, 2012 Uncategorized
Please turn in your Bibles to Hosea 10:9-13: O Israel, thou hast sinned from the days of Gibeah: there they stood: the battle in Gibeah against the children of iniquity did not overtake them. It is in my desire that I should chastise them; and the people shall be gathered against them, when they shall bind themselves in their two furrows. And Ephraim is as an heifer that is taught, and loveth to tread out the corn; but I passed over upon her fair neck: I will make Ephraim to ride; Judah shall plow, and Jacob shall break his clods. Sow to yourselves in righteousness, reap in mercy; break up your fallow ground: for it is time to seek the Lord, till he come and rain righteousness upon you. Ye have plowed wickedness, ye have reaped iniquity; ye have eaten the fruit of lies: because thou didst trust in thy way, in the multitude of thy mighty men. 
If we take a look around at our world today, we can easily see where our communities and our culture are mired in sin. Sin is a disease that has crippled our society. It is easy for us to point at the world and say it needs to repent. But I believe that before we point at the world and call for repentance, we need to look at ourselves — those of us who claim to be followers of Jesus Christ. As I Peter 4:17 says, “the time is come that judgment must begin at the house of God.” 
Lord willing, I will be addressing the topic of repentance in the church today and for the next two weeks because I believe that when God’s people get themselves together and repent of our sins, we will have the moral authority to tell the world that it needs to repent. Not only that, but God’s abundant blessings will be poured out on us if we are following Him without any sin hidden away in our hearts. 
In our passage today, we read a message which God gave to the Prophet Hosea to give to the children of Israel. As you know, after the reign of King Solomon, Israel began to slide into sin, idolatry and rebellion.
I. SINS AMONG GOD’S PEOPLE
Let’s take a look at Hosea’s first proclamation. He tells the children of Israel: “thou hast sinned from the days of Gibeah.” Basically, he is saying, Israel your sin has been going on for a long time. The time Hosea refers to ‘in the days of Gibeah’ is recorded in Judges chapters 19 – 21. We don’t have time to read those chapters today, but the story is of a Levite — a priestly man — who was traveling through Israel with his concubine. He stopped in a city called Gibeah to stay the night and was invited into the house of a farmer. While this Levite was in this farmer’s house, the Bible tells us that “the men of the city, sons of Belial, beset the house round about, and beat at the door, and spake to the master of the house, the old man, saying, Bring forth the man that came into thine house, that we may know him.” These men of Gibeah wanted to have homosexual relations with this man who was staying with the farmer. When these men persisted in their demands, the farmer gave his daughter and the Levite gave his concubine to these wicked men and the Bible says they ‘abused her all night.’
Later on when this wickedness was made known throughout Israel, the leaders of the other eleven tribes went to the leaders of the tribe of Benjamin and demanded that the men of Gibeah be handed over for punishment. The Bible tells us that they said, “Now therefore deliver us the men, the children of Belial, which are in Gibeah, that we may put them to death, and put away evil from Israel. But the children of Benjamin would not hearken to the voice of their brethren the children of Israel.” Instead, “the children of Benjamin gathered themselves together out of the cities unto Gibeah, to go out to battle against the children of Israel.” 
This is the situation that Hosea is referring to when he says that Israel has been sinning since the days of Gibeah. 
I want you to notice, very briefly, two things about that situation in Gibeah: 
First, this is similar to what the Bible tells us happened to Lot in the city of Sodom with one difference. The difference here is that this did not happen in a heathen city like Sodom and Gomorrah, but this was happening in a Hebrew city — a city inhabited by God’s people. 
The second thing I want you to notice is that the leaders of the tribe of Benjamin actually tried to defend the sons of Belial. They tried to prevent the men of Gibeah from being punished by God. 
So, here we have sin happening among the children of God and the leaders of God’s people actually defending those who are sinning. It is sad to say, but many of our churches have gotten to this point — where we have open sin in the church and we have people in the church defending those who are sinning. Because of this sin among the children of Israel, Hosea tells them it is time to ‘break up their fallow ground.’ God is telling the church today to break up its fallow ground — to repent of its sins and “seek the Lord.”
II. WHAT IT MEANS TO BREAK UP YOUR FALLOW GROUND
The word ‘fallow’ means land that is unused and inactive — land that is dead and is not bringing forth any plants or crops. The Jews were a nation of farmers, and it is common in the scriptures to refer to their occupation, and to the scenes with which farmers and shepherds are familiar. The prophet Hosea addresses them as a nation of backsliders; he reproves them for their idolatry, and threatens them with the judgments of God. 
Fallow ground is ground which has once been tilled, but which now lies waste, and needs to be broken up and mellowed, before it is it is ready to receive grain. When a farmer broke up his fallow ground, that meant he went and plowed it, fertilized it and got it ready to be used. 
To break up the fallow ground, is to break up your heart, to prepare your mind to bring forth fruit unto God. The mind of man is often compared to the ground in the Bible. The word of God is the seed sown there and the fruit represents the actions and thoughts of those who receive it. To break up the fallow ground therefore, is to bring your life and heart to the place where it is ready to receive God’s word and obey it.  Sometimes our hearts get hard and dry, until there is no fruit coming from our lives even though we claim to be Christians. It is then that we need to break up our fallow ground — that is repent of our sins and begin to obey God’s Word.
III. HOW TO BREAK UP YOUR FALLOW GROUND
When we begin to break up the fallow ground in our hearts, it is not an action which is based on our feelings. People talk about religious feeling as if they could by direct effort, call forth righteous action. But this is not the way the mind acts. No man can make himself feel in this way, simply by trying to feel. The feelings of the mind are not directly under our control. 
If we are going to break up the fallow ground in our lives, we must begin by looking at our hearts. We must examine the state of our minds, and see where we stand with God. The Bible says in 2 Corinthians 13:5 to “Examine yourselves, and see whether ye be in the faith; prove your own selves…” That is the first step to breaking up our fallow ground and repenting of our sin.
Many people pay no attention to the condition of their hearts, and never know whether they are following Christ as they should. They don’t know whether they are gaining ground or backsliding; whether they are being fruitful, or lying waste. Now you must be serious about examining your heart. Make a business of it. Do not be in a hurry. Examine thoroughly the state of your heart, and see where you are: whether you are walking with God every day, or with the devil; whether you are serving God or serving the devil; whether you are under the dominion of the prince of darkness, or of the Lord Jesus Christ. Jeremiah 17:9 says, “The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it?”
To do all this, you must begin to consider your sins. Even the little things you do that you know are not right but you do not consider a sin. You must examine yourselves. Ask God to reveal any hidden sin you have in your heart. Self-examination consists in looking at your life, in considering your actions, in calling up the past, and learning its true character. Look back over your past history. Take up your individual sins one by one, and look at them. Do not just cast a glance at your past life, and see that it has been full
of sins, and then go to God and make a sort of general confession, and ask for pardon. Be specific. 
This is an exercise that every follower of Christ should engage in. Over the next two weeks, we will take a look at sins that oftentimes go unmentioned and overlooked in the church. We will confess those sins together and ask God to forgive us. I hope that you will be with us for those messages. 
__________________
Now, if you do not know Jesus Christ as your Saviour, allow me to show you how you can be saved from sin and the punishment of sin.
First, please understand that you are a sinner and that you have broken God’s laws. The Bible says in Romans 3:23: “For all have sinned and come short of the glory of God.” Please understand that because of your sins, you deserve punishment in hell. Romans 6:23 says “the wages of sin is death…” This is both physical death and spiritual death in hell.
But here is the good news. John 3:16 reads, “For God so loved the world, that He gave His only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in Him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”
“For God so loved the world” means that if you are in the world, God loves you. No matter who you are, where you are, or what you have done, God loves you. 
God loves you more than you love yourself. He loves you so much so “that He gave His only begotten Son.” His only begotten son is Jesus Christ. Jesus Christ died on the cross for your sins and for mine, He was buried, and on the third day, He rose from the dead.
“that whosoever believeth in Him”, whosoever means anybody of any race, any color, or any socio-economic status can believe on Jesus Christ.
“should not perish, but have everlasting life.” if you believe on Jesus Christ as your Saviour, you will not perish in hell, instead you will have everlasting life in heaven with God. 
The Bible also says in Romans 10:9 and 13: “That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved…. For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.” 
Dear friend, if you are willing to believe on the Lord Jesus Christ for salvation, please pray with me this simple prayer: Heavenly Father, I realize that I am a sinner and that I have done some bad things in my life. For Jesus Christ sake, please forgive me of my sins. I now believe with all of my heart that Jesus Christ died for me, was buried, and rose again. Lord Jesus, please come into my heart and save my soul and change my life today. Amen.
