Dr. Candace White Seeks to Inspire Young People to Follow Their Dreams

January 10, 2015 Black

Dr. Candace White speaks to sixth-graders at Lincoln Middle School Wednesday, January 7, 2015. Dr. White spoke about following your dreams.
Doug Finger/The Gainesville Sun

From the time she was 5 years old, Candace White wanted to be a doctor. But when she was in school, she said, few role models looked like her.

“I didn’t see many African-American doctors,” said White, now 37 and in her second year of residency as a doctor of family medicine.

So this week, with the help of Alachua County Public Schools Superintendent Owen Roberts and School Board member Leanetta McNealy, White took a couple of days out of her residency rotation in South Florida to visit east Gainesville schools to talk to students about how to set and achieve goals.

“Now it’s come full circle,” said White, a Gainesville native. “I’m not sure how many times they’ve heard they can do it, and what it takes to do it.”

Thursday morning, the second day of her tour, White stopped at Lincoln Middle with her parents Albert and Ora White in tow.

“Each one of you matters,” she told a group of about 80 eighth-graders. You can live the life that you want to live, she said, but it’s all about setting goals.

Before she started ninth grade at Buchholz High School in the early ’90s, White said she made a list of five goals and stuck them on the refrigerator.

They were: become Buchholz’s first black homecoming queen, graduate with honors, speak at her high school graduation, earn a full scholarship to college and win her event in a state track meet.

She accomplished all but one; White came down with the flu a week before the race.

She made a new list of goals before her freshman year at Florida A&M University, with a plan for how to execute each one. That’s the important part, she told the group.
