WATCH: Ohio Black Pastors Meet with State Attorney General Mike DeWine Over Shooting Death of Unarmed Black Man in Wal-mart

September 2, 2014 Black

Ohio Attorney General Mike DeWine met Monday with dozens of area black pastors about the Aug. 5 police-involved shooting death of John Crawford III in Beavercreek’s Walmart. 

“Attorney General DeWine talked about the possibility of us looking at getting reactionary teams together for any kind of uprising or getting us prepared in the event we should have something where the community becomes disturbed or where we have violence and we need to have a faith-based reactionary group,” said Rev. Dr. Wilburt O. Shanklin, senior pastor at The Living Word of Faith church at 2412 W. Third St.

Shanklin said the meeting was set up by Montgomery County Sheriff Phil Plummer, who talked to DeWine about meeting with area religious leaders. “You have to be prepared to make sure that you don’t bias a community or a certain group before there’s an opportunity to have a realistic, judicial approach and resolution to the matter,” Shanklin said.

“We need to get the faith-based community together and get prepared in case we have a riot or civil disorder, something we don’t want here, and we don’t want our town tore up,” Plummer said. “We just need to work together and communicate. There’s always two sides to every story. We want to listen to what the faith-based community has to say and then we want to explain how the police operate and some of our tactics, so we can work together through these issues.”
