African-Americans Key for Obama in Battleground States

December 29, 2011 Uncategorized

As President Barack Obama’s approval rating has bumped along, mostly sliding through his first three years in office, the support of one group of voters has been remarkably buoyant: African-Americans.
In the last Wall Street Journal/NBC poll, 46% of voters on the whole said they approved of the job Mr. Obama was doing as president. Among African-American voters, the approval rate was 92%. That’s an extraordinary disparity – some 46 percentage points.
There is almost always a difference in the way blacks and whites perceive politicians and issues, and for the most part African American voters tend to side with Democrats. But the size of the discrepancy when it comes to Mr. Obama is something special – and it could play a big role in the coming election year.
Looking at the country through Patchwork Nation, our demographic/geographic breakdown of American counties, explains how significant that massive edge in the African-American vote may be when all the votes are tallied.
African-Americans make up a larger-than-average part of the population in two of Patchwork Nation’s 12 county types – the big city Industrial Metropolis counties and the counties we call Minority Central. And those Minority Central counties, in particular, could play a major role in two key states: Virginia and North Carolina.
In Virginia some 9% of the population lives in the state’s Minority Central counties. In North Carolina, it’s 12%. And in 2008, those places were critical to Mr. Obama. He won both states and did much better in those counties than he did in the states at large. In Virginia, Mr. Obama captured 61% of the Minority Central vote versus about 53% statewide. In North Carolina, he won 54% of the Minority Central vote compared with about 50% statewide.
Generally, those Minority Central counties are not critical to the presidential electoral calculus. They are, for the most part, located in reliably Republican states like Mississippi, Alabama, South Carolina and Georgia (see the orange counties on the map below).
