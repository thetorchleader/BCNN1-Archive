Ebola to Be the Focus of the Sunday Network Shows

October 18, 2014 Media

Liberian health workers are seen at the Doctors Without Borders’ Ebola treatment center in Monrovia on Oct. 18, 2014.
(Photo: Zoom Dosso, AFP/Getty Images)

The Sunday network shows will focus on Ebola with Anthony Fauci, who leads the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases appearing on all the major talk fests.

The deadly virus has unnerved the nation with the infection of two nurses who cared for the first confirmed case in the United States. Thomas Duncan died of Ebola, which he contracted in Liberia, on Oct. 8. False alarms, including one that forced the Pentagon to cordon off a parking lot Friday, show just how jittery the country has become. The disease has killed nearly 4,500 people in West Africa.

President Obama on Friday named Ron Klain to oversee the government’s response to the crisis, making Klain the first Ebola czar. The White House gave Defense Secretary Chuck Hagel the authority last week to mobilize Reserve forces to combat the crisis. The Pentagon is looking at eight reservists with engineering skills needed to construct buildings.

Ebola has become a political as well as a health care problem, with some Republicans calling on the White House to ban flights from West Africa.

Fauci’s feat of appearing on the five major talk shows is referred to as the Full Ginsburg, named after William Ginsburg. He was Monica Lewinsky’s lawyer and the first to blanket the shows in 1998.
