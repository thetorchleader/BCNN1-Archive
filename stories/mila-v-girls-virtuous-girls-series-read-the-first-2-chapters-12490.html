“Mila” (V-Girls: Girls of Virtue Series): Read the First 2 Chapters Free

May 18, 2013 Uncategorized

by Danita Whyte
with Danaé, Daniqua, and Danyelle Whyte

Hey!

I’m Mila – Mila Alexandria
Michelle Munroe – and there are two things I love more than
anything else in the entire world: the Bible and ballet! I know those
two things sound as though they have nothing in common but for me
they have always gone together. I guess I get it from my parents. My
momma, Megan Ann Munroe, loves music, and dancing comes naturally to
her. Before I was born, she was a popular Broadway dancer, and now she
dances all the time at our house. She dances in the kitchen when we
cook and dances with the mop when she cleans. I know it sounds crazy,
and looks even crazier, but she is really good at it and a lot of fun.
Now she divides her time between homeschooling
me, my four brothers,
our baby sister, and working as a part–time dance instructor on the weekends.

My daddy, Alex Tito Munroe, is almost
always away. He is a Marine and is currently on duty in Afghanistan.
Sometimes I miss him – A LOT – but when I start
feeling sad that he is gone I remember what he never fails to tell me
on the phone when we talk together:

“Biblegirl,
I am never really that far away from you because you are always in my
heart and always will be. Pray to God every day to keep us safe and
strong while we
are apart and bring us together
again real soon.”

Biblegirl is the nickname my daddy
gave me
after I won my first ribbon for quoting Scripture at our church. I love
to read the Bible and can quote tons of passages from it (not that I’m
bragging or anything), but I have a verse for everyone in every
situation. When I have a problem or when my friends have a
 problem, I always say a Bible verse for them, and now all of
them call me Biblegirl too. But I don’t mind and maybe by the end of
this story you will see why!

—Mila

Chapter
1

Today was the day!

Mila could hardly believe that it had
finally arrived. After years of practicing since she was six years old,
she was soon going to be on her way to compete in New York’s Junior
Ballet Talent Show Championship. The green numbers on her clock glowed
4 AM, but she had barely slept all night long and wished for the sun to
rise immediately.

“Just a few more
hours!” Mila whispered excitedly into the glass box that held
her sleeping pet hamster, Clip. She thought about the hundreds of girls
whom she was sure she would meet at the championship. Many of them
would probably be from out of state and most likely had been in ballet
training for years just like her. Pulling her flashlight out from
underneath her pillow, she glanced for what had to be the millionth
time at the glittery promotional piece she had cut from a fashion
magazine earlier that year. She was so glad her mom had agreed to let
her enter. The
junior ballet team that won the
championship would receive a huge gold trophy, a year‘s supply of chocolate, and
scholarships to
attend New York’s premiere dance and ballet school. The captain of the
winning team would be picked to play the part of the lead actress in
the world tour of Le Sacre du Printemps
(The Rite of
Spring).

Mila had her fingers crossed that she
would be picked as captain of the winning team. She really wanted to go
on a ballet tour of her own.

Mila read the time on her clock again
and
groaned. 4:03 AM. She was sure time had never moved so slowly, but she
was too excited to stay in bed any longer. She glanced over at her baby
sister who was sound asleep in the twin bed.
Mila quietly got up, put on a pair of sneakers, and
tip-toed out
their bedroom door and down the hall. She had to be careful not to trip
over her brothers‘ skateboards
and soccer balls
which they had carelessly left scattered about – again.

Fourteen–year–old Mila Munroe lived with her
mother,
two older brothers, two younger brothers, and baby sister in a Bronx
apartment that was entirely too small for them. Mila had to share a
room with her sister, Moisha Lee. Her mother had the second bedroom for
herself. Her youngest brothers, nine–year–old
Mark and seven–year–old Mickey shared the third bedroom while her oldest
brothers, Mike and Moore, slept in the living
room. Mike and Moore were seventeen–year–old twins and would soon be leaving for
college. Mila knew she would miss them.

Mila’s father, Alex Munroe, was a Marine
serving in Afghanistan. She was always glad when he came home on breaks
to spend time with them. Alex Munroe had promised that next time he
came home they would buy a large condo in Manhattan. Mila was looking
forward to that because then she wouldn’t have to share a bedroom with
Moisha Lee who somehow always ended up in her bed.

Closing the front door of their
apartment,
Mila crossed the passageway to Room 109. Her neighbor, Aly, lived in
Room 109 and was always glad to see Mila and serve her black apple tea
and tell her a story. Aly was thirty-two and lived alone. Some years
ago, she had lost her left arm and damaged the left side of her face in
an accident which she rarely talked about. She could hardly remember
anything about her past either. Mila knocked on her door, hoping she
wouldn’t be asleep.

She wasn’t.

“Hey, Aly!” Mila
greeted her as she opened the door. “I couldn’t sleep, so I
thought I would come over. I hope I’m not disturbing you.”

“No, not at all,” Aly
answered brightly. “I hardly ever sleep anyway except for
taking naps.”

“Really? How do you do
that?” Mila asked, shutting the door behind her and following
Aly into the clean white and yellow themed kitchen.

Aly winked. “Easy. You just sit
in a chair holding a spoon over the armrest. You place a saucer under
your hand on the floor so as soon as your eyes close the spoon falls,
hits the saucer, and you wake up at the sound.”

“No way. You can’t even sleep
for a minute doing that,” Mila laughed.

“Trust me, it works,”
Aly assured her, bringing out two red cups. She moved quite easily
doing normal things despite the loss of her arm. Soon they were both
eating coffee cake squares and sipping black apple tea. “What
brings you over here so early in the morning?” Aly asked Mila.

“I couldn’t sleep because I am SO excited!” Mila
gave a
huge smile. “Today, I leave for my ballet talent show
championship. I just can’t wait to be in the competition!”
She jumped up and spun around a few times to show Aly some moves.
“I’ve waited for this day my entire life almost,”
Mila continued excitedly. “Have you ever been to a ballet
show, Aly?”

Aly smiled back at her. “I
have,” she answered. “I used to perform in them
too.”

Mila stopped spinning. “You were
a real ballet dancer?”

“Once upon a time,”
Aly nodded. She appeared to be thinking hard and Mila knew she was
trying to remember something from her past. “I think I even
have a picture of it.” Aly disappeared into another room but
soon returned with a photo album and a pair of blue pointe shoes. Mila
took the photo album and quickly flipped through some pages. There were
many photos of a very different Aly. She had both her arms and her face
was happy and effortlessly radiant. There was a cast picture from The Nutcracker performance with
a crowd of people smiling
together for the camera. Aly was in the center. “This was my
ballet instructor,” Aly informed Mila, pointing to a tall,
slim, stern-faced woman posing beside her. “Miss Larochelle,
she was the absolute best.”

“Woah!” Mila breathed.
“Why didn’t you tell me? Who are the rest?“

Aly thought hard for awhile then
frowned.
“I can’t remember,” she said finally.
“The only face I recognize is Miss Larochelle’s. I don’t know
the others.”

Mila finished looking at the photo album
and closed it. “Why did you stop? Was it the accident that
made you quit?”

Aly was frowning again. “I don’t
know, Mila. I told you before that I can’t even remember being in an
accident. I never drive anywhere now. It is as if a lampshade has been
cast on the entire light of my childhood and all I can recall is Miss
Larochelle and The Nutcracker. I can’t recall losing my arm. I can’t even remember my last name, yet
everyday I know there has to be more to me and I want to find it out
but I just don’t know where to begin…” Her voice trailed off.

Mila felt very bad for her older friend.
Aly had often told her that she wanted to find out who she really was.
Aly went to church every week with Mila’s
mother and siblings and worked as an
assistant at Mila’s school library but neither she, nor anyone else,
knew that much about her. Sometimes when Aly got really upset because
she couldn’t find out who her family was Mila would come over to cheer
her up.

“Don’t worry,” Mila
said, giving Aly another huge smile. “Even if you never find
your family, I will always be your friend and Jesus will be too. He
said ‘I will never leave you nor forsake you.’ Tell you what
– when I come back from the ballet championship I’ll help you
find your family and I’ll pray about it while I’m away.”

Aly hugged her. “You’re the best
and I’ll be praying for you to do well.” Aly handed Mila her
old blue pointe shoes. “Since I don’t need these anymore you
can have them.”

“I’ll be sure to wear
them,” Mila assured her as she headed for the door.
“Thanks for everything. Bye!”

“Bye, Mila. Good luck!”

Chapter
2

“Milaaaaaaaaaaaaaa!”
Her seven-year-old brother Mickey came running up to her yelling
hysterically as she opened the door to their apartment. “Mark
kidnapped your hamster again and won’t tell where it is!” It
was 6:00 and everyone in her family was up and getting ready when Mila
returned from Aly’s place.

Mila followed Mickey into the kitchen,
spotted Mark hiding under the table, and gave him a mean look.
“Tell me where you put Clip now!” she demanded.

“To tell or not to tell
– that is the question,” he said in reply, smiling
sweetly. Mila relaxed her face, “I’m getting momma.”

“Yo, Biblegirl!” her
older brother Mike greeted her. “You were up early. Sneaking
around in your pajamas again, I see.”

“No, I just went to see Aly. We
ate a ton of coffee cake squares and, look, she even gave me her old
ballet shoes!”

“Nice, but I guess that means
you aren’t hungry for my world-famous blueberry pancakes with extra
whipped cream, huh?”

Mila grinned and grabbed a plate,
“I’ve already ruined my ballet diet. I’ll take
three.”

“What about my world-famous
bacon?” Moore asked. He was also at the stove frying.

“No thanks, I think you’re
burning them anyway,” Mila informed him.

“It’s called crispy,”
Moore replied laughing.

Mila’s mom entered the kitchen balancing
baby Moisha Lee on her hip. “Morning, Mila,” she
greeted her daughter. “I was about to say you had gone off to
the competition on your own. Why aren’t you dressed? We have to leave
in a few minutes.”

“I know. I went to Aly’s and she
gave me her old ballet shoes.” Mila started on her pancakes.
“Would you please make Mark give Clip back? I have to say
goodbye.”

“I don’t have him, momma.
Honest, I don’t,” Mark called out from under the table where
he was dumping way too much syrup on his plate. “Her silly
hamster is always getting lost.”

“Clip is not silly,”
Mila replied, shooting him another mean look.

“No time for that,
honey,” her mom interrupted. “Clip will show up.
He’s probably sad you’re leaving him anyway. Now go and get dressed in
a hurry.”

Stuffing her last pancake into her
mouth,
Mila threw her mom a half salute and ran out of the kitchen to her room
to get ready. She was wearing a pink, glittery dress that she had saved
just for the occasion with matching boots heavily-covered in sequins.
She pulled her thick, curly hair into a ponytail, looked around for
Clip one more time, then dashed back to the kitchen pulling two travel
bags behind her.

“Look at our little sister going
off all by herself,” Moore pretended to wipe away tears.
“I think I’m going to cry even though I promised not
too.”

“Cut it out,” Mila
grinned. “I’m not really that little anymore, and I’m not
even leaving the state and, don’t worry, I’ll be sure to call you all
and tell about all the fun I’m sure to have.”

“We’re proud of you,
Mila,” Mike said pushing Moore away. “And dad is
too. Just keep God first. Remember to do all the things you have been
taught in training and stay calm. You’re a born natural when it comes
to ballet and will certainly succeed because —“

“‘I can do all things through
Christ who strengthens me,'” Mila finished. “I
couldn’t forget it even if I wanted to.” Mila gave her
brothers a group hug before turning to Moisha Lee who was sitting in
her highchair making a mess with applesauce. “You can have
our room all to yourself only while I’m gone,” she told her
giving her a kiss and a pat on the head. Moisha Lee gave a huge smile
in return.

Mila and her mom took her bags down to
the
car and soon they were on their way. Mila was so excited she could
hardly sit still. They drove out of their apartment complex to the main
highway and started towards downtown New York City. Mila was sure she
had never seen a day more bright and wished both her parents could be
there to see her off.

Opening one of her bags slightly Mila
took
a peek at Aly’s blue pointe shoes that she had placed in there before
leaving. They seemed to be moving on their own in a most unusual
fashion then the movement stopped for a while. Mila unzipped the bag to
create a wider opening. The shoes started moving again –
shaking and bouncing slightly. Then the pair split apart to reveal a
tiny furry head with large, round, bright eyes staring up at her.

“Clip!” Mila exclaimed.

“What’s that honey?”
her mom asked from the driver’s seat.

“Clip was hiding in my bag the
whole time. Looks like he wants to win a ballet championship
too!” A note was on a red string tied around Clip’s neck.
Mila tore it off and read it. It was written by Mark:

Hi Mila!

I think
you will be lonely being all by
yourself and away from us darling brothers so Clip is coming to keep
you company. I am a million percent sure you will win the trophy.

Love, Mark

Mila took out her phone to send him a
text:

i have
Clip. u da best. luv u lots 🙂

She didn’t have to wait long for a reply:

yeah, I
know.
