Use of Voiceprinting at Some Banks Sparks Privacy Concerns

October 20, 2014 Finances

JPMorgan Chase & Co and Wells Fargo & Co are using callers’ voiceprints to fight fraud, according to an investigation conducted by the Associated Press.

A recent AP survey found that more than 65 million people have had their voiceprints taken.

Privacy advocates and some legal experts are questioning the practice because callers don’t give their consent for biometric analysis.

“There should be regulation on both the state and federal level that govern practices with respect to the collection, use and sharing of that data so that we don’t go deeper into George Orwell’s world,” Tech and privacy lawyer David Klein said.

Some in the industry argue this is a more secure alternative for authentication.

“It’s in the background. It doesn’t affect the call in any way,” Shirley Inscoe, an analyst with the Aite Group, told Yahoo Finance. “Nobody even knows it’s happening.”
