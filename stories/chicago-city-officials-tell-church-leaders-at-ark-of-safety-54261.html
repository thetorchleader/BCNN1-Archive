Chicago City Officials Tell Church Leaders at Ark of Safety Apostolic Faith Temple to Shut Down Church Immediately due to Safety Concerns

January 30, 2015 Black

Ark of Safety Apostolic Faith Temple, 5247 W. 23rd St. in Cicero, run by Bishop Herman Jackson, has been ordered to close immediately because of multiple safety hazards. (Town of Cicero)

Cicero officials on Thursday ordered the closing of a church in the western suburb, calling the house of worship a “safety nightmare,” and have barred any future services at the 24,000-square-foot building.

Town officials said in a news release Thursday evening that action was necessary at the Ark of Safety Apostolic Faith Temple, 5247 W. 23rd St., because the building does not have water, electricity or heat.

According to the temple’s website, the building formerly housed Redeemer Lutheran Church and Elementary School.

The church’s founder and senior pastor, Bishop Herman Jackson, could not be reached for comment.

“We have bent over backwards to help the pastor keep his church open because we care about the congregation,” town spokesman Ray Hanania said. “Last Sunday, we allowed services in the church portion of the building, which also includes a shuttered day care center, on his promise that he would have contractors make repairs this week. Nothing has been done.

“Yet Bishop Jackson has threatened to convene his congregation services in the church this Sunday, despite the hazards which in many cases are life-threatening. And we find that disregard of the health and well-being of his congregation to be unacceptable.”

Hanania called the conditions at the church “a safety nightmare.”

Officials said other hazards include damaged and collapsed ceilings, water and mold in the walls bulging outward, filth and garbage strewn throughout, and broken exit and emergency signs and damaged doorways.

“We are worried about the safety of his congregation,” Hanania said.
