Some Reasons Why You Feel Old

April 23, 2015 From Around the Block

© Stressed man (Michael H/Getty Images)

YOU’RE STRESSING OUT

Failure to adapt to the stresses of modern life can accelerate the aging process, and contribute to blood sugar issues, weight gain, aches, and a plunge in energy or disinterest in sex. “Whenever I ask a man if he’s stressed, he generally tells me something like, ‘Not any more than normal,’” says Corey B. Schuler, a certified and registered nutrition practitioner. “Well, here’s the secret: His situation is probably not normal physiologically.” Stress also causes aging at the cellular level, he says, because the “machinery” of cell function and replication slows down and malfunctions.

No matter what your age, there are things that can make you look and feel older.

SHOTS OF SUGAR ARE SAPPING THE LIFE OUT OF YOU

Not only is eating too much sugar bad for your waistline, but too much of the sweet stuff can lead to heart disease and diabetes, and is up there with smoke and excess sun exposure as major causes of wrinkles. Collagen and elastin, the protein fibers that keep skin firm and elastic, can also become damaged by a high-sugar diet. To get back on a low-sugar track, Schuler recommends eating minimal processed foods that contain refined sugars.

YOU’RE FORGETTING HEALTHY FATS

If you’re working on getting leaner, don’t cut out good, healthy fats like omega-3 fatty acids. Lack of these nutrients can negatively impact the speed (and how) you age. To keep feeling and looking younger, incorporate into your diet more oily fish, walnuts and flax seeds, which help the skin stay supple and wrinkle-free. Omega-3s also help boost brain health and keep the heart in good shape.

YOU DON’T STRETCH

You’re too young to wake up with sore muscles and other aches and pains. As we age, we lose flexibility, and stiffness sets in as muscles lose their full range of motion. This stiffening of the muscles can also lead to injury when working out. To make sure you’re still able to reach your full range at any age, try incorporating some yoga moves into your warm-up regimen. A little limber goes a long way.

YOU’RE GETTING TOO MUCH SUN

Lather on the sunscreen all the time. Failing to protect skin from ultraviolet radiation and burning over time can alter your DNA and eventually cause skin cancer. Adults over the age of 40, particularly men, have the highest annual exposure to UV, according to the Skin Cancer Foundation.

In addition to being a cause of skin cancer over time, excess exposure to rays will loosen the elastin in your skin, leaving more fine lines visible. If you can avoid being outdoors when the sun’s rays are most powerful (10 a.m. to 4 p.m.), do it. If not, cover up with SPF (nothing lower than 30) and reapply every hour if you’re spending time outside.

SOURCE: Men’s Fitness
