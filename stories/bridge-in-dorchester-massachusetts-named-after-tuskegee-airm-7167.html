Bridge in Dorchester, Massachusetts Named After Tuskegee Airmen

July 18, 2012 Uncategorized

Given the challenge of naming a bridge on American Legion Highway in Dorchester, Julian McNeil and Gerry Ellis-Williams wanted their choice to inspire young men like themselves and to honor African-Americans who have made major contributions to American history.
Pictured: Mayor Thomas Menino (right) and area veterans dedicated the bridge on American Legion Highway over Morton Street in Dorchester.
They decided to call the structure the Tuskegee Airmen Bridge. The Airmen were the all-black unit that fought through racist American attitudes to become the first African-American pilots battling Nazi Germany and imperial Japan during World War II. About 14 Massachusetts residents were pilots in the unit, which became known as the Red Tails for the color painted on their aircraft.
“I have a lot of gratitude because they paved the way for a lot of African-Americans in the United States today,” Ellis-Williams said after the naming ceremony attended by some of the surviving Tuskegee Airmen and Mayor Thomas M. Menino. “They make me realize that there’s really not too much you can’t do when you put your mind to it and when you dedicate yourself to it.”
“They showed that no matter what is going on — even if the times were segregated then and they aren’t now – that once we come together and realize what we are capable of, anything is possible,” said Ellis-Williams, 19, a student at Mt. Ida College in Newton.
The naming assignment came to McNeil and Ellis-Williams through the Menino administration and the Boys and Girls Club in Dorchester.
During a ceremony Tuesday, Menino praised the Airmen as they sat underneath a yellow- and white-striped tent erected on the street to provide some shelter from the sun. The ceremony marked the end of the rebuilding of American Legion Highway, a major Dorchester artery.
