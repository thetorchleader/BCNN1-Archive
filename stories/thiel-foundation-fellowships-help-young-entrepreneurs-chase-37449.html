Thiel Foundation Fellowships Help Young Entrepreneurs Chase Their Dreams

September 6, 2014 You Can Work...

Peter Thiel, co-founder of PayPal, attends the Allen & Company Sun Valley Conference on July 10, 2014, in Sun Valley, Idaho.
(Photo: Scott Olson, Getty Images)

For 83 young entrepreneurs, a traditional classroom setting didn’t cut it. Longing for an innovative environment, these students dropped out of elite universities to pursue alternative paths to success through the Thiel Fellowship. 

Created by PayPal co-founder Peter Thiel, the Thiel Foundation awards fellows a grant of $100,000 to chase their entrepreneurial endeavors in Silicon Valley or abroad over the course of two years. Since 2011, about 400 youths from more than 50 countries have applied to the fellowship program in the hopes of becoming the next global change maker.

For many of the participants, abandoning college for the fellowship was easy, but after experiencing life in the Valley, some have begun to realize the start-up life isn’t all that glamorous.

“Being a 19-year-old start-up founder is not as cool or as fun as it sounds,” said Mark Daniel, a 2013 fellow and co-founder of Strut, an e-commerce company that announced its seed-funding round of $1.5 million last month. “You have to do a lot of soul searching, a lot of trial and error to find your groove.”

Daniel says the media romanticizes entrepreneurship by only presenting the highlights of the lives of successful entrepreneurs. “People shouldn’t view the fellowship as, ‘Hey, I’m dropping out of college to be a Thiel fellow,'” Daniel said. “People should approach it with, ‘I’m dropping out of college to be a full-time entrepreneur,’ and there is no glamour in that.”

Although many fellows enjoy the freedom associated with the program, others have noted the great level of self-direction needed to succeed. Noor Siddiqui, a 2012 fellow, agrees with Daniel, remembering periods of negativity while running her health care IT company called Remedy.

“There are times when I feel none of this is amounting to anything,” Siddiqui said. “But life, just like entrepreneurship, is getting up more times than you fall.”
