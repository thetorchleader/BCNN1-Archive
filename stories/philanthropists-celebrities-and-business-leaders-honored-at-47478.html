Philanthropists, Celebrities and Business Leaders Honored at Ebony Magazine’s Power 100 Gala

November 30, 2014 Black

“Selma” director Ava DuVernay and actor David Oyelowo share a moment before the gala. (Photo Courtesy Chelsea Lauren)

Celebrities and some of the most influ­ential African Americans gathered at The Avalon in Hollywood on Nov. 19 to honor Quincy Jones and 99 others. Ebony Magazine’s Power 100 Gala usually oc­curs in New York, but this year the company decided to move it to Los Angeles to attract more talent.The list salutes the achievements of powerful African Americans in the country such as TV produc­er Shonda Rhimes, Little League star Mo’Ne Davis, gospel singer and actress Tamela Mann, producer Pharrell Williams, physi­cist Neil deGrasse and fit­ness guru Shaun T.

“As one of the hon­orees, I feel great to get picked and I’m just really inspired to work harder to help as many people as I can,” said gospel singer Tamela Mann. Her hit song, “Take Me to the King” landed her a BET award in June and in the top 100 on the Billboard charts. “It re­ally took me by surprise by being honored, but I really thank God for this. I hope my influence will continue to bring families together. My husband and I are all about family, so we want people to continue to invest in their personal families and bring hope.”

Many of the honorees and celebrities who walked the red carpet took the time out to reflect on how they want to continue to be a positive role model in the African American commu­nity. “My goal is to work hard and make people that support me proud because they help me everyday. That’s the greatest thing I can do for the community,” honoree and co-host of En­tertainment Tonight Kevin Frazier said.

Comedian and actor Wayne Brady hosted the star-studded event. Perfor­mances in honor of Quincy Jones from Doug E. Fresh, Slick Rick, Patti Aus­tin, James Ingram, KEM, Savion Glover and Chaka Khan set the mood for the evening.
