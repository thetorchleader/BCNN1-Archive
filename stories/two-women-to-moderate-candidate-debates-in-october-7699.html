Two Women to Moderate Candidate Debates in October

August 14, 2012 Uncategorized

The moderating duties for the four presidential and vice-presidential debates this year will be evenly split between male and female journalists for the first time.
Pictured: Candy Crowley, CNN
Jim Lehrer of PBS, Bob Schieffer of CBS, Candy Crowley of CNN and Martha Raddatz of ABC will be the moderators, the Commission on Presidential Debates said Monday. The first debate will take place Oct. 3 in Denver.
The announcement followed a period of public scrutiny over the fact that only one woman has moderated a presidential debate in the quarter-century that the commission has been holding them.
Three 16-year-olds from New Jersey posted an online petition, “It’s Time for a Female Moderator,” this year and gained more than 100,000 supporters. When they tried to deliver the petition to the commission’s office in Washington this month, no one was available to meet with them.
On Monday, the commission indicated that Crowley would become the first woman to moderate a presidential debate since Carole Simpson in 1992. But the announcement still fell short of some expectations.
The Oct. 16 debate to be led by Crowley, the host of the Sunday morning public affairs program “State of the Union” on CNN, will have a town-hall-style format with citizens asking the questions, not a traditional debate format with a moderator asking the questions.
Raddatz, the senior foreign affairs correspondent for ABC, will moderate the vice-presidential debate, with a traditional format, on Oct. 11, a few days before the event moderated by Crowley.
Lehrer, the former anchor of the “PBS NewsHour,” will be back for his 12th turn as debate moderator, leading off on Oct. 3. Schieffer, the host of “Face the Nation” on CBS, will moderate the last presidential debate on Oct. 22.
The selections were first identified Monday by the Drudge Report.
SOURCE: New York Times
