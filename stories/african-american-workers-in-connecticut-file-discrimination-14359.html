African-American Workers in Connecticut File Discrimination Lawsuits

March 18, 2014 Uncategorized

Several African American employees at the state Department of Correction allege in three federal lawsuits that the agency has discriminated and retaliated against them because of their race.

The lawsuits were filed between September and November of last year. One involves 12 plaintiffs who are employed in a variety of roles at different prisons.

“The issue is that this appears to be a system-wide practice of discrimination by the agency,” said Josephine Miller, an East Hartford attorney representing the group of 12.

According to the lawsuit filed by 12 employees, the DOC subjects black employees to investigations of baseless complaints by white employees, harsher discipline than white employees and retaliation when they file complaints of discrimination or harassment.

The agency has also refused to investigate complaints made by black employees and has not given them the same training opportunities as white employees, the lawsuit alleges.

The Department of Correction declined comment on the lawsuits, but it addressed its discrimination policy.

“Although we are unable to comment on an ongoing lawsuit, the Department of Correction takes any allegation of discrimination in the work environment very seriously, and is committed by policy and ethical consideration to a diverse workforce free from harassment,” said Andrius Banevicius, a department spokesman.

The other lawsuits were filed by two members of the DOC’s Affirmative Action Unit, the department that investigates complaints of discrimination and harassment.

Robert Jackson was hired as director of Equal Employment Opportunity and supervisor of the Affirmative Action Unit in 2002. He alleges that he tried hiring and promoting qualified minorities, but that his recommendations were challenged by Daniel Callahan, the human resources director at the time. He also alleges in the lawsuit that Callahan and Legal Affairs Director Sandra Sharr attempted to reverse the findings of investigations by the Affirmative Action Unit.

Jackson was terminated in 2011 after an investigation found that he had retaliated against two white female employees. His lawsuit alleges that the investigation was deficient.
