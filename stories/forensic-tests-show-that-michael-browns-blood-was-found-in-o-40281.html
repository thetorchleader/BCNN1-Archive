Forensic Tests Show that Michael Brown’s Blood Was Found in Officer Darren Wilson’s Car

October 18, 2014 Black

Clergy members and demonstrators march to the Ferguson, Mo., police station on Oct. 13, 2014.
(Photo: Joshua Lott/AFP/Getty Images)

Forensic tests just released in the Ferguson, Mo., police shooting of an unarmed black teenager more than two months ago are shedding new light on the incident.

Tests found now-deceased Michael Brown’s blood on Officer Darren Wilson’s gun, police car door and uniform, The New York Times reported in an article in Saturday’s paper.

At the time of the shooting, Wilson told investigators that he was trying to leave his SUV when Brown pushed him back in. He says he was pinned in his vehicle and in fear for his life as Brown reached for his gun, The New York Times reported.

Brown’s family says the teen was killed as he raised his hands to surrender to the officer.

Wilson told authorities that Brown punched and scratched him repeatedly, leaving swelling on his face and cuts on his neck, the Times reported.

But Sharpton, who eulogized Michael Brown’s funeral, dismissed Wilson’s reasoning.

“First of all, if you stopped him — Michael Brown and his friend — walking down the street, what led to the scuffle? … Secondly, how does he and you get in your car? You trying to do what by yourself?” Sharpton asked, according to Business Insider. “Now, if I go with you with your story all the way to that — that Michael Brown was shot, gets up off you in the car — why are you trying to tell me that a man … ran back at you when he knew you had the gun and you already shot him?”
