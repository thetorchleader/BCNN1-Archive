Soldier Indicted for Producing Child Porn During Deployment In Afghanistan

March 25, 2015 National

The office of Andrew Luger, U.S. Attorney for the District of Minnesota, announced on Wednesday, March 25, 2015, that a Minnesota National Guard soldier has been indicted for producing child pornography while deployed in Afghanistan.
(Photo: Stephen Maturen, for USA TODAY)

A Minnesota National Guardsman was indicted on Wednesday for producing child pornography while he was deployed in Afghanistan.

Andrew Schiller, of Lakeville, Minn., convinced a 14-year-old girl from Minnesota to send him nude photos over the Internet while he was in the midst of a deployment, according to the office of U.S. Attorney Andy Luger, the top federal prosecutor in Minnesota.

Schiller, 28, who holds the rank of 1st lieutenant, was arrested on March 19 and appeared before U.S. Magistrate Judge Becky Thorson on Wednesday for detainment hearing. He is being held pending trial.

Schiller contacted the girl via Skype from September 2013 to January 2014. Prosecutors say he asked her to send explicit photos to him, and she complied.

Army Criminal Investigation Division (CID) initially began investigating Schiller in March 2014 after receiving a complaint from a Missouri woman who discovered inappropriate Facebook communications between her minor daughter and Schiller, who at the time was stationed in Afghanistan, according to court documents.

Schiller offered an Army investigator a sworn statement in which he admitted to searching for underage girls on the Internet and asking them for pictures of a sexual nature.

He also told the Army investigator that some of the minor females with whom he communicated provided him with nude photographs and that he had asked for and received nude photographs from three minor females during the past five years.

Prosecutors also say Schiller asked dozens of underage girls to send him photos through online platforms including Facebook, Skype, Meet Me, MyLOL and KIK. He would send sexually-explicit images of himself to encourage them, and he sometimes promised money or alcohol in exchange for photos and videos.

In a single week in March 2014, Schiller contacted 86 girls through MyLOL, which advertises itself as the number one teen dating site in the USA. Terms of service of MyLOL prohibit users overs age of 20 from using the site.

“On MyLOL, Schiller targeted, in particular, minor females who lived on or near military bases,” according to a detention filed by federal prosecutors. “In one communication between Schiller and a fourteen-year-old girl, Schiller stated that he wants to get to know her better, become her boyfriend, and spend a weekend with her in his dorm.”

Schiller enlisted in the Minnesota National Guard in February 2007 and deployed to Afghanistan in August 2013 with the 849th Mobility Augmentation Company.

Schiller is still a member of the National Guard, but is “pending separation from the military,” according to Col. Kevin Olson, a Minnesota National Guard spokesman.
