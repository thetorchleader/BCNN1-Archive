Money Being Raised to Save Historic Mt. Reah Missionary Baptist Church in Houston

March 21, 2015 Black

Mt. Reah Missionary Baptist Church in Clark, Texas, is in need of some TLC. Milton and Doris Davis are planning to hold a fundraiser Saturday, April 18, in the hope of saving the 1937 building that served a congregation founded in 1877.

Mt Reah Missionary Baptist Church has been a part of Liberty County since 1877. Now, the historically black church, which has been inactive since 1996, is looking to reopen and to inspire local citizens to help them on that journey.

“We are planning to raise funds to make repairs and get it open again,” said Doris Davis, a trustee for the church. “We want to be able to hold services and host community events.”

After Davis and her husband, Milton, retired in 2000, they moved to 25 acres in Romayor, where they were eventually led to the historical church.

“We realized when we got here that we didn’t know where his uncle was buried,” said Davis. “We found out after some research that he was buried at the church and we wanted to put up a memorial for him.”

That decision eventually led them to the realization that the land was so overgrown, it would be nearly impossible to get to the grave site, where Davis’s uncle is buried alongside several slaves who attended the church in the 1800’s, as the church was originally founded by former slaves who worked in the area.

Doris and Milton have now teamed up with members of the Liberty County Historical Commission.

“We are teaming up with them to do an archaeological dig,” said Davis, adding that many of the gravestones have sunken into the ground due to the immense overgrowth. “We are hoping to get it cleared away and get the church open again. It is still standing there and it is in good condition. It just needs some repairs.”

Davis has also conducted research leading to the discovery that some former members of the church are still alive and residing in the area — a fact that gives her hope that the church may once again conduct services one day in the future.
