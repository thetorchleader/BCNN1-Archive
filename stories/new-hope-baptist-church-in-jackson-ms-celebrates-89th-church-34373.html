New Hope Baptist Church in Jackson, MS Celebrates 89th Church Anniversary

August 19, 2014 Black

Members of New Hope Baptist Church play and sing Sunday at a service celebrating the church’s 89th anniversary.
(Photo: KATHERINE BURGESS/The Jackson Sun)

Through the Great Depression, World War II and the civil rights movement, members of New Hope Baptist Church have worshipped in the wood-paneled sanctuary on Davis Street.

On Sunday, the church held two services to celebrate its 89th anniversary.

“We live in a society now where the church is on a decline,” said the Rev. Reginald Boyce, senior pastor. “You have a lot of churches that are dying out, a lot of churches that are closing down. For our church to reach 89 years is indeed a milestone.”

The church has about 175 members with about 110 people attending each Sunday, Boyce said. Many members are deeply involved in the community, he said.

The Rev. William Smith, pastor of Polk Chapel Christian Methodist Episcopal Church of Memphis, spoke at the 11 a.m. service. The Rev. Marvin Mercer, of Mt. Olive Baptist Church of South Fulton, spoke at the 3 p.m. service.

In the morning service, Smith urged members of the congregation to turn to their neighbors and say, “After 89 years, we still need to give God the glory.”

He spoke from the Book of Acts, telling of how the apostles prayed and praised God in prison.

He also spoke of how Paul and Silas were both called to spread the gospel.

“I believe this is what God is saying in these last days,” Smith said. “We’ve got to understand that all of us here are in this together. … For 89 years, we got to learn how to struggle together. We got to realize we’re not just on an island all by ourselves, but if my brother is hurt, then I’m hurt. If my sister is blessed, then I’m blessed. We are connected.”
