Christian Laettner Still Remembered for Duke Heroics

April 7, 2015 Sports

Christian Laettner’s most memorable college moment came when he hit a buzzer-beater to send Duke to the Final Four in 1992. (Photo: AP)

They lined up with anything and everything for Christian Laettner to sign, men and women, young and old, most of them wearing blue Duke garb, even one brave young man in a Kentucky No. 5 jersey, and he sat at Buick Arena inside the Convention Center signing, posing and smiling for photos. And if you showed up in an “I Hate Christian Laettner” T-shirt, he would have signed that too, and you might have gone looking for an “I Love Christian Laettner” T-shirt.

He was the star of Mike Krzyzewski’s first national championship team in 1991 as Duke shocked the world and undefeated UNLV and then beat Kansas at the Hoosier Dome. A year later he stomped on a Kentucky player named Aminu Timberlake before stomping on all of Big Blue Nation with what is remembered in college basketball lore as The Shot, a miracle one-dribble, 17-foot turnaround with 2.1 seconds left in overtime at the Spectrum to get Duke to the Final Four.

His old Duke team is playing for Coach K’s fifth NCAA championship Monday night against Wisconsin, and if every Blue Devils player shows up with Laettner’s on-court disposition, big-game presence and raging, maniacal will to win, the Badgers will be in trouble.

There was only one Laettner, of course, and except for some minor graying on his 45-year-old head, he still looks like he might be able to give Coach K quality minutes off the bench for One More Shining Moment.

When his 90-minute autograph session ended, I asked Laettner how he would have liked Duke to have a crack at an undefeated Kentucky team Monday night.

“That would have been great,” Laettner said, “but I never even thought of it, you know, because I was thinking about, ‘They gotta beat Michigan State, and if they beat Michigan State we’ll worry about the next team.’ ”

Do you think they would have had a good shot against Kentucky?

“Yes, but I don’t have to worry about that anymore, do I?” Laettner said, and smiled.

Do you think they could have beaten them?

“Oh yeah,” Laettner said. “Oh yeah.”

Why do you think they would have beaten them?

“ ’Cause they’re a good team, and as you saw [Saturday] night, any undefeated team could be beaten if someone zones out on ’em, and Wisconsin played a great game [Saturday] night so that’s why they won,” Laettner said.

So you would have been confident that your old school would have denied history.

“Of course,” Laettner said, and smiled again. “Of course.”

He visited with the Blue Devils on Sunday morning.
