In Search of America’s Young Black Leaders of Tomorrow

September 9, 2012 Uncategorized

The question seems simple enough: who is considered a leader in the black community? Immediately, President Barack Obama and his amazing wife Michelle came to mind, followed by a slew of other notable African American figures (Cornel West, Oprah Winfrey, the regulars). 

However, when the question became who is an upcoming black leader in the millennial generation — specifically someone outside of the entertainment world — I was stumped. As hard as I tried to think of someone, anyone, I couldn’t.
As someone who has always considered herself well versed in issues that affect young people and the black community, the fact that I couldn’t come up with a single name was disturbing. I knew there had to be somebody out there that should be called the next Cory Booker; it just had to be me who didn’t know who … right? Wrong. Apparently, I wasn’t the only with this problem.
“I think one of the defining aspects of our age is a lack of Black leadership (that is known on a national stage) that we look up to. Even for those of us who believe deeply in the power of government, we can’t help but be a bit disappointed by how large-scale political figures seem to be behaving,” said Daniel Stringer, a PhD graduate working in tech education in North Carolina.
And that sentiment was echoed again and again by a plethora of young black people that couldn’t name a well-known person who reflected their definition of leadership.
“I can think of individuals who I know personally who are doing great things,” Stringer added, “but it’s harder to think of big names.”
I have to agree. Some of the people that I admire most in this world are ones that I am lucky to call a friend or cherished family member. Yet there needs to be other people that African Americans can rally around because they are working to better our lives.
So I went on the hunt, pouring through the 40 under 40 of this, the top 10 rising stars of that, and so on. And in the midst of my research, I had a bittersweet realization.
As a millennial, I am proud to see many young people making a huge impact on the world, whether it’s business, politics, education, arts, and so on. Even better, a large number of these young people are female, like the 36-year-old president of Yahoo Marissa Mayer, and from diverse backgrounds like Muslim-American Huma Abedin (a top aide to Hillary Clinton).
For African Americans, however, my findings were not as promising. Just seeing the statistics regarding the high rate of black male incarceration, unemployment, and lack of presence in arenas outside of the sports and entertainment world, I was a little disheartened.
Despite these initial results, I dug a little deeper, refusing to believe that was it. And with a little help from my friends (and Google), I began finding exactly what I was looking for.
