In Honor of Black History Month, OnFaith Lists 15 Black Christian Leaders Who Are Shaping the Face of the Church

February 6, 2015 Christian

From debuting at #1 on the Billboard 200 Album Chart to starting a ministry that engages Reformed African Americans to beginning a movement to diversify half of all churches, these black leaders are shaping the face of Christianity in America. They are authors, pastors, speakers, rappers, and leaders who engage in discussions of multi-ethnic churches, race, urban culture, family — always in light of the gospel.

So, in honor of Black History Month, here is a (non-exhaustive) list of black Christian leaders you should know.

Eric Mason
Epiphany Fellowship | Philadelphia, Pennsylvania

Why you should know him: Eric Mason is the founding and lead pastor of Epiphany Fellowship. He also serves as the president of Thriving, a ministry that aims to train and provide resources to minorities pursuing urban ministry. He serves on the executive board of Acts 29, a church planting network, and has authored multiple books, among them Manhood Restored and Beat God to the Punch.

Thabiti Anyabwile
Capitol Hill Baptist Church | Washington, D.C.

Why you should know him: Born Ron Burns, Thabiti Anyabwile changed his name in college following his conversion to Islam — in Swahili, Thabiti means “stern, upright” and in Arabic, Anyabwile means “God has set me free.” After visiting a Temple Hills, Maryland church led by a TV evangelist he and his wife had listened to, the two began following Jesus. Thabiti now serves as church planting pastor at Capitol Hill Baptist Church, led by Mark Dever, and is a councilman and blogger (see: Pure Church) for The Gospel Coalition.

Lecrae
Reach Records | Atlanta, Georgia

Why you should know him: Lecrae is a Grammy-award-winning rapper whose latest album, Anomaly,debuted at #1 on the Billboard 200 Album Chart. (It was the first time an artist occupied the #1 spot on both Gospel Albums and the Billboard Top 200.) He’s also co-owner of the record label Reach Records, which has signed other artists like Trip Lee and Andy Mineo. He’s performed on The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon and the BET Hip Hop Awards.

Jemar Tisby
RAANetwork | Jackson, Mississippi

Why you should know him: Jemar Tisby is co-founder of the Reformed African American Network (RAANetwork), a site that hopes to fuel the reformation in the African American community by providing biblical resources and connecting Reformed African Americans to one another. He also started the African American Leadership Initiative at Reformed Theological Seminary to recruit minorities and train Christians for cross-cultural ministry.

Tony Carter
The Front Porch | East Point, Georgia

Why you should know him: Tony Carter started The Front Porch, which publishes content aimed to start “conversations about biblical faithfulness in African American churches and beyond.” He also serves as the lead pastor of East Point Church and has written multiple books, including On Being Black and Reformed and Blood Work.
