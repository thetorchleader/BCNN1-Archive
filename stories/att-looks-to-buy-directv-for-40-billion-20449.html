AT&T Looks to Buy DirecTV for $40 Billion

May 1, 2014 Media

In this Wednesday, Oct. 17, 2012, file photo, an AT&T logo is displayed on an AT&T Wireless retail store front, in Philadelphia. A&T has reportedly been in talks with DirecTV about an acquisition of the satellite TV provider.
IMAGE: MATT ROURKE/ASSOCIATED PRESS

Telecommunications giant AT&T has made an inquiry to DirecTV about an acquisition of the satellite TV provider, according to a report from the Wall Street Journal.

A combination of the two would create a subscriber television base to rival another recent mega-merger — Comcast’s acquisition of Time Warner Cable. The Journal estimated an acquisition would be worth at least $40 billion, roughly DirecTV’s market capitalization as of Thursday.

SEE ALSO: Comcast to Acquire Time Warner Cable in Industry-Shaking Deal

DirecTV declined to comment. AT&T did not immediately return a request for comment.

Adding DirecTV would instantly make AT&T one of the largest subscriber television providers in the US. The two companies have an existing partnership that bundles television and cellphone service. AT&T’s existing television business, U-Verse, is only available in limited areas.

The overall decline in growth — and recent contraction — of pay TV subscribers has helped lead to numerous mergers, as companies look to acquisitions to fuel growth. Satellite TV has proven to be more resilient than landline cable providers.

Growth has slowed but, DirecTV was able to still add customers in the fourth quarter of 2013. DirecTV closed the year with about 20.3 million subscribers.

With a mature U.S. cable market yielding almost no new customers, acquisition and merger talks have flown around the industry. AT&T is far from DirecTV’s only suitor; Dish Network’s CEO reportedly approached DirecTV in March.

AT&T is in a somewhat similar position as it grapples with competitors in the telecom industry, particularly T-Mobile. Not content to battle over smartphone subscribers, the company has also begun to explore the expansion of its fiber optic Internet network as well as a streaming video service similar to Netflix.
