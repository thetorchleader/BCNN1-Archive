Study: Children Born to Older Mothers Less Likely to Suffer Burns at Home

May 9, 2013 Uncategorized

Researchers found young children under five with mothers aged 40 and over were less likely to suffer a scald in the home, often caused by children reaching to grab boiling saucepans, than those born to teenage parents.
The study also found children living in socially deprived areas had the greatest risk of suffering scalds.
Safety measures at home, such as using saucepans on rear cooking rings out of the reach of children and always supervising baths being filled, are believed to contribute to the risk.
Dr Elizabeth Orton, co-author of the study at the University of Nottingham’s Division of Primary Care, said: “The results from our research offer significant insight into those groups who are at most risk, which would enable GPs to deliver targeted interventions to patients during clinical consultations and hopefully reduce the pain and misery of scalds for many children.”
The study, published in the journal Burns, found the risk to children decreases as the age of their mothers increases. It found children born to parents aged between 30 and 40 were 30 per cent less likely to suffer scalds than if their mother was aged under 20, while those with a mum over 40 had a far lower risk, and were 70 per cent less likely.
