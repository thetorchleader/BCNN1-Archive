College Senior Keltin Jordan Organizes a Celebration of 50 Years of Cultural Change at Texas A&M University

March 23, 2014 Uncategorized

Keltin Jordan

The culture of America over the past 50 years has experienced almost unparalleled change. Moreover, change in this country has been one of its only constants since its inception.  

On Thursday, Texas A&M University will celebrate 50 years of cultural change during a program called “50 Years of Inclusion: A Cultural Transfusion.”

The idea, which was created by Cadet Keltin Jordan of Huntsville, is to highlight the achievements of minority and female Aggie graduates for both Texas A&M and the Corps of Cadets, while acknowledging the progress and impact that inclusion has had on the A&M campus and Aggie culture.

In 1963, the thought of a black man or Asian woman attending the all-white university was unthinkable. Today, Aggieland includes students of all races, genders and ethnic backgrounds.

Change will always be measured by what is constant. Jordan, a senior at Texas A&M, is that constant upon which change can accurately be measured.

In May, Jordan will graduate with a degree in supply change management. It would also appear that he has learned well the logistics of his craft. It was Jordan who connected the dots and organized Thursday’s program.

He set up the panel, which features Texas A&M Regent Elaine Mendoza, Col. Pauletta Blulett of the U.S. Air Force, 2013 Muster speaker Bill Jones, former A&M student body president Malcolm Hall and Texas Rep. Gene Wu. The program will take place in the MSC 2300 Ballroom D&E starting at 6:30 p.m.

Jordan’s starting point began with the two-parent home of Quentin and Brenita Jordan.

“You get a balance with both parents at home, because if you don’t you only have one aspect,” Jordan said. “From my mother I learn the value of scholarship and nurturing, but from my father I learned how to carry myself and the value of hard work and dedication to my craft.

“Being with my father I would see him work all day and come home and say let’s do yard work. He would have me out there, especially when I didn’t want to. He gave me the discipline to handle responsibilities,” Jordan added.
