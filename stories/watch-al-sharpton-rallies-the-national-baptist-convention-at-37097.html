WATCH: Al Sharpton Rallies National Baptist Convention Attendees

September 4, 2014 Black

Speaking in New Orleans at the annual conference of the largest group of black Christians in the United States, civil rights activist Al Sharpton urged religious Americans on Thursday to join the fight against the shooting by police officers of unarmed black men. “This is not a secular problem,” Sharpton told hundreds of delegates of the National Baptist Convention USA Inc. 

“It is a problem of all of us. And the black church has always been the independent strong voice that could not be compromised or silenced. And we cannot start being silenced and compromised now.”

Sharpton’s speech was met with resounding applause, bringing the audience in the Morial Convention Center clattering out of folding chairs for a standing ovation. He tied the killing of Michael Brown, shot six times by police in Ferguson, Mo., to the deaths of other unarmed black men in police custody, from Los Angeles to Staten Island, N.Y., to New Iberia.

To Sharpton, the series of deaths marks only the latest chapter in a long and unfinished march toward racial justice. To tell the story of that march, and to illustrate how these deaths are part of a 50-year fight for civil rights, Sharpton placed Attorney General Eric Holder’s announcement that the Justice Department would investigate Ferguson police in Holder’s lineage as a descendant of those who fought in the 1960s for civil rights in the South.

Sharpton said Holder was directly related to a young black couple who was turned away from the University of Alabama by then-Gov. George Wallace.“That young man, Eric Holder, is going into Ferguson as the first black attorney general of the United States, sent by the first black president of the United States,” Sharpton said. “We marched. We suffered. We went to jail. We elected an Obama.”

He said President Barack Obama’s election was not the end of the fight, as he said the voting rights that black Americans have fought to attain are being gradually stripped away by new and stricter voter identification laws. His voice trembling and growing stronger as he invoked God, Sharpton called on the audience to continue to fight for the right to vote.
