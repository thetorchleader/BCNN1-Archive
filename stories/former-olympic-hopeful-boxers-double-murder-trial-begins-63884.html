Former Olympic-Hopeful Boxer’s Double Murder Trial Begins

March 26, 2015 Black

Kenneth K. Lam, Baltimore Sun
James Berry is pictured outside the gym where he trains.

Christopher Webster was with best friend Howard Grant Jr. in 2008 when there was an attempt on his life, and he was also with Grant when he was killed days later. He told police the attacker was James Berry III, a friend to both who had been Webster’s boxing trainer.

Now, as Berry stands trial for murder this week, Webster had to be arrested to get him to come to court as a witness. With Grant’s father looking on from the audience, Webster disavowed any knowledge of the shooter’s identity and claimed police had coerced the statement.

Berry, 27, is on trial for the killings of Grant and his cousin Justin Berry in an ambush on Pennsylvania Avenue on Oct. 12, 2008.

Berry, a once-promising young boxer with Olympic dreams who is not related to Justin Berry, has been charged in a total of three attacks resulting in five deaths, which are being tried separately.

Webster’s testimony on the second day of trial Thursday underscored law enforcement’s difficulty in obtaining information and bringing indictments against Berry until late 2013.

Prosecutors say the suspects and victims were part of a group of close friends who dealt drugs and hung out in an area they called “D-block,” near the corner of Division and Bloom streets in Druid Heights.

The feud began when one of the members of the group, Brian Goodwyn was killed in June 2008. Prosecutors say James Berry spread a rumor that fellow member Romie Zeigler was responsible. Ziegler was killed days later at Goodwyn’s candlelight vigil.

Assistant State’s Attorney LaRai Everett told jurors in opening statements that the friends became divided, with Howard Grant believing James Berry had set up Zeigler.

Over the ensuing months, Grant was ambushed and shot twice, but survived. His uncle was shot and paralyzed in one of the attacks, Everett said.

Everett said Berry remained vigilant: “If it at first you don’t succeed, try, try again, as the saying goes,” she said.

She said Berry sought the assistance of a Quinzell Covington to complete the task, and the murder was eventually carried out, with Justin Berry, a father and freshman at Morgan State University, losing his life along with Grant, who also had a young child.

Covington is expected to testify against Berry.

Andrew Northrup, one of Berry’s attorneys, told jurors that his client is innocent, and called the state’s case a “theory based on rumor, conjecture and innuendo, not evidence.” The witnesses have “shifting stories and reasons to lie,” he said.

He said Covington, in particular, has reason to tell police what they want to hear: Covington is already serving 25 years for a murder in Baltimore County and his plea agreement with prosecutors will not require him to serve additional time for the killings of Grant and Justin Berry.

Grant’s father, Howard Grant Sr., testified Wednesday that it was Webster who called him the night of the shooting.

“Dad” — that’s what some of his son’s friends called him — “Howard’s been shot. It’s bad. You need to get here,” Grant tearfully recalled.

After Grant’s testimony, James Berry could be seen wiping his eyes with his tie.
