Texas Rangers On Verge of Acquiring Josh Hamilton From Angels

April 25, 2015 Sports

Josh Hamilton watches in the dugout during the game against the Rays at Angel Stadium.
(Photo: Kirby Lee, USA TODAY Sports)

The Texas Rangers have reached a tentative agreement to acquire troubled outfielder Josh Hamilton from the Los Angeles Angels, an official with direct knowledge of the talks told USA TODAY Sports.

The official spoke only on the condition of anonymity since the deal still has not been completed.

A trade to Texas would be a stunning turn of events for the Angels, Rangers and Hamilton, who won the 2010 American League MVP award in Texas and departed after the 2012 season to sign a five-year, $125 million deal with the division rival Angels.

Yet, Hamilton struggled in his first two seasons in Anaheim, and after he informed Major League Baseball officials during spring training that he had a relapse, the Angels sought to sever their relationship with the former All-Star outfielder, who is owed $83 million through 2017.

The Angels will pay about $68 million of the money remaining on Hamilton’s contract, according to Yahoo Sports, while Hamilton is also expected to help offset the remaining $15 million, according to a person involved with the talks.

Now, this deal is close to being finalized, MLB.com reported. It would send him back to the club with which he enjoyed his greatest success, and acknowledged provided an excellent support network as he battled a crippling drug addiction that nearly sidelined his career a decade ago.

When Hamilton left the Rangers, he subsequently chided the Dallas-Fort Worth area as “not a true baseball town,” and that it’s “always been a football town.”
