Let the Church Say Thank You!

April 19, 2015 Black

Pastor Mark E. Whitlock, Jr.

Every Sunday morning, the church offers prayer, scriptures, songs, sermon, and the almighty offering, but does the church say, ‘Thank you?’ 

Churches’ giving has a consistent, yet somewhat colorful, past that includes traditional tithing, sacrificial offering, pledging, as well as raising funds through the sale of chicken and fried fish dinners.

A national study on Black church giving shows that, regardless of locale, at least 15 percent of Black churches have annual incomes of more than $50,000; offerings, special fund-raisers, and pledging are the most common methods to raise income. Some mega churches in Los Angeles receive over two million dollars in donations annually.

The Barna Group, a Christian Research company, says church giving has decreased by nearly 30 percent since 2000. People are giving to non-church groups like the United Way, schools, and international philanthropic organizations.

While many Black churches offer printed financial reports, there are far too many Black churches unwilling to publish financial reports. All churches must send a Statement of Contributions to all financial givers. The statement records the amount a donor has given in one year, but this is not enough.

Donors deserve acknowledgment for giving. Even when a donor requests no recognition, find a way to say ‘thank you’ anyway!  One way to retain a donor is to offer an appreciation call, letter, flowers, plaque, and/or email from the pastor/church leadership.
