‘Worship in Pink’ Urges Black Women to Get Mammograms

October 11, 2014 Black

Members of Sistahs 4 Life led several line dances Friday to promote breast health. (Casey Parks/The Oregonian)

There’s something about a line dance that draws a crowd.

Even when the steps are complicated — tap four times then shuffle before you cha cha — they look easy, a series of lefts and rights. Direction matters more than rhythm.

The dozen or so women at Legacy Emanuel Medical Center wanted to draw a crowd Friday afternoon. So they did the Wobble. They did the Cupid Shuffle and the old Electric slide.

The crowd did come, and the dancers used their platform to talk about mammograms.

The moves kicked of Worship in Pink, a month-long series of events aimed at urging women to have mammograms. The program, co-sponsored by Legacy Health and Susan G. Komen Oregon, is open to all but is especially geared toward African-American women.

African-American women are less likely than white women to develop breast cancer. But the mortality rate is higher for African-Americans.

Experts have suggested that one reason the rates are so high is African-American have historically been less likely to get a mammogram. That’s changing, though.

About 66 percent of African-American women older than 40 had a mammogram in 2010, according to Susan G. Komen. That’s on par with other races. Keeping that number high depends on promoting a culture of talking about it, Kendrix said.

“Early detection saves lives,” Kendrix said. “We know that many years ago, you really didn’t talk about health issues, especially in church. We really didn’t address them. Worship in Pink is opening up that dialogue.”

Kendrix lost her own mother, Inez Brewer, to breast cancer 26 years ago. Brewer was diagnosed in the fourth stage.

“She was one of those who didn’t talk about it,” Kendrix said. “S he knew something was going on. She was just getting sick. Finally she went to the doctor. When they found the lump, it was the size of a lemon. She knew it was there, but she was from that generation that didn’t talk about it.”
