Jimmy Johnson Offers Some Advice for RGIII and the Redskins’ Coach: ‘Swallow Your Pride and Get On With It; Good Things Can Still Happen’

November 23, 2014 Sports

In this Sept. 7, 2014, file photo, Washington Redskins head coach Jay Gruden talks to quarterback Robert Griffin III before an NFL football game against the Houston Texans in Houston. (Photo: David J. Phillip, AP)

Jimmy Johnson has a message for Jay Gruden and Robert Griffin III, and he won’t mince words.

You both need each other.

“I thought RG3 was wrong,” Johnson told USA TODAY Sports, referring to the young Washington quarterback who called out teammates after last Sunday’s disaster of a loss against Tampa Bay. “He was trying to be a leader, but in the wrong way. Right now, nobody’s a leader.

“Jay Gruden was wrong,” Johnson added of the first-year coach, who publicly lambasted his quarterback. “He was drawing a line in the sand. I think he went a little too far.”

As Washington heads into Sunday’s game against San Francisco, there is another dose of drama surrounding Griffin, which has become too typical the past two years.

And comical.

There were press conferences this week…that addressed previous press conferences.

“Good things can still happen,” insists Johnson, who coached the Dallas Cowboys to two Super Bowl titles during the 1990s and is currently an analyst. “The jury is still out on RG3, but clearly he’s regressed for one reason or another. They need to swallow their pride and get on with it.

“And Gruden has to realize that his success, if he has any, may rely on RG3.”

The situation resonates with Johnson as it illustrates the fine line and delicate balance of working relationships between key players and coaches.

Every NFL coach has to deal with the associated nuances on every level, and there is no one-size-fits-all scenario, given various personalities.

Johnson, for one, was not one to subscribe to the theme of treating everyone the same.

That never made sense to him, given that there are 53 players on an active roster with different backgrounds, cultures, experiences, worldviews, expectations, sensitivities, issues – and talent.

I reminded Johnson of the time he cut marginal linebacker John Roper for falling asleep in a meeting, then maintained that had Troy Aikman fell asleep he would have tapped him on the shoulder and whispered, “Wake up, Troy.”

“The successful coaches are the ones who are able to get the most of their people,” Johnson said, “and there are different ways to achieve that depending on the people involved.”

That’s why Gruden’s public remarks about Griffin – which included criticism about the quarterback’s fundamentals — hit home.

Although Griffin denies that there is a personal issue with Gruden, of whom he has spoken favorably since the coach was hired this year, clearly there’s a disconnect.

It’s also striking that after a rift with previous coach Mike Shanahan, the scrutiny is heightened about Griffin’s dealings with Gruden, formerly the Bengals’ offensive coordinator.

“Those relationships will never be expanded or get a bond through the media,” Johnson said. “The only way they’ll be able to do that is one-on-one.Some guys, you can rip in public. But you’ve got to remember that a lot of these guys, because of their talent, have been treated as special all their life. You could cause a scar that won’t heal.”

Of course, Johnson had a famously fractured relationship with Cowboys owner Jerry Jones that led to their split just weeks after repeating as Super Bowl champions.

Yet when it came to his players, Johnson had a strong track record.

But that took some work, Johnson reminded me.

“Troy was really standoffish that first year,” Johnson said, flashing back to 1989.

The Cowboys drafted Aikman with the No. 1 pick overall, but weeks later Johnson used a supplemental first-round pick to select Steve Walsh, his quarterback at the University of Miami.

“I drafted Steve for value,” Johnson said. “And look, we wound up getting a 1, 2 and 3 for him from New Orleans.”
