Navy Destroyer Commanders Found Guilty of Dereliction of Duty

October 10, 2014 Uncategorized

Cmdr. Curtis Calloway, Command Master Chief Travis Biswell and Cmdr. Ed Handley on May 31 aboard the James E. Williams via the U.S. Naval Institute.

Three officers of a Navy destroyer who were removed from command in mid-September while on deployment have been found guilty of dereliction of duty, Fleet Forces Command officials said Friday.

While the Navy did not release specifics of the incident that caused Cmdr. Curtis Calloway, former commanding officer of the destroyer James E. Williams; Cmdr. Ed Handley, former executive officer on the ship; and former Command Master Chief Travis Biswell to be dismissed from duty Sept. 16, the men were not accused of criminal wrongdoing. Earlier, Navy officials said the men had been reassigned as officials investigated the command climate aboard the ship.

Biswell, whose rank is the most senior enlisted sailor, also was disciplined for drunk and disorderly conduct. A separate Naval Criminal Investigative Service inquiry is under way involving multiple chief petty officers and a female petty officer during a 6th Fleet port call, an official familiar with the matter told the Navy Times.

Cmdr. Heidi Haskins is taking over as commanding officer. Capt. Tony Simmons, deputy commodore of Destroyer Squadron 2 of the Naval Surface Forces Atlantic, has been temporary commander since Calloway was removed.
