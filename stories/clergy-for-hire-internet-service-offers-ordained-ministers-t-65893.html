“Clergy for Hire” Internet Service Offers Ordained Ministers to Help People

April 7, 2015 Christian

DANIEL SANGJIB MIN/Times-Dispatch
Jay McNeal has formed Clergy for Hire, which offers ordained ministers to help people who need their services.

The seed of the Rev. Jay McNeal’s business came, he said, when he was trying to qualify for a Navy chaplaincy position.

His work at the time was in radio and television broadcast ministry, which left him short of Navy requirements for conducting weddings or funerals, administering communion, preaching sermons and other typical pastor functions.

So he worked up a website offering those services to anyone who asked — thinking there would be at least a few who didn’t have a church or a relationship with a pastor.

“I was overwhelmed by the number of requests I got,” McNeal said.

That was the beginning of Clergy for Hire, a fledgeling service McNeal is offering. So far he has recruited about a dozen ministers to be available for such services. He hopes to add more to keep up with the requests coming in.

As many churches shrink and hire fewer assistant pastors, he said, there is a supply of ordained ministers with time to do the work.

And demand is generated by people who aren’t connected to churches but want a pastor’s help.

No longer aiming for the Navy job, McNeal has a master of divinity degree and works at the Morton Library at Union Presbyterian Seminary.

He said he hopes Clergy for Hire will give more ministers an opportunity to interact with people where they are and “live into their calling.”
