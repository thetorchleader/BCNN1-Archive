Civil Rights Leader, Laken Cosby Jr., Dies at 83

June 15, 2014 Black

Laken Cosby Jr.

Laken Cosby Jr., a soft-spoken civil rights leader who in 1988 became the first African American chairman of the Jefferson County Board of Education, died Wednesday. He was 83 and had been sick for several years, said his friend Clarence Yancey, a longtime Democratic legislative district chairman.

Cosby, whose son Kevin is the senior pastor of St. Stephen Baptist Church, also served three terms on the Kentucky State Board of Education, to which he was appointed by Gov. Brereton Jones, and was director of housing and community services for the state Commission on Human Rights.

Upon his retirement from the county school board in 1993, on which he served for more than eight years, The Courier-Journal described him as a “forceful advocate for black students, teachers and administrators.”

Ben Richmond, president and CEO of the Louisville Urban League, said Saturday that while Cosby represented the 1st District, “he fought for all kids, especially African Americans,” and for diversity of teachers and administrators.

Raoul Cunningham, president of the Louisville chapter of the NAACP — a title Cosby held in 1982 and 1983, said Cosby was a strong advocate for civil rights in education and housing but a “very quiet individual who methodically would work to solve a problem.”

In a 1985 profile, The Louisville Times wrote that “if the local civil rights scene were depicted as a tumbling landscape, Laken Cosby would paint himself as a quiet stream.

“Not that he hasn’t fought in his share of battles,” the paper added, noting that he had marched and protested in the 1960 to promote open housing in Louisville.

Cosby himself was quoted saying: “We can’t use the solutions of the ‘60s for the problems we see now. Times have changed. We have to get to some serious negotiations.”

One of 13 children, Cosby was born near Huntsville, Ala. His family moved to Louisville where his father was a contractor and he graduated from Central High School

Kevin Cosby, in a tweet in January, said: “I honor my father, born in racism, poverty in 1930 Ala. Yet rose through education [to] become 1st black chair of JCPS school bd.”

Cosby served in the Air Force and ran a real estate company that bore his name. He was inducted in 2012 into the Commission on Human Rights’ Hall of Fame.

Working at the commission, he developed one of the nation’s first state-wide programs to test for racial discrimination in housing as well as a training program for real estate professionals. He later served as housing consultant for the state and national NAACP.
