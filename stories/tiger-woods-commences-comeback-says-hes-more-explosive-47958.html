Tiger Woods Commences Another Comeback; Says he’s “More Explosive”

December 3, 2014 Sports

Commencing on his second comeback of the year and the fifth in the last five years, Tiger Woods is starting anew this time around.

He has new Nike equipment in his golf bag and a new Nike golf ball at the ready.

He has a new swing to call upon and a new swing consultant on call.

And most importantly, he has a new back as he returns to competitive golf Thursday in the Hero World Challenge at Isleworth Golf and Country Club.

“The body is good. I’ve gotten stronger. I’ve gotten more explosive. I’ve gotten faster,” Woods said Tuesday.

“I just now need to hit more balls, but the body is good. I don’t have the sharp pain like I used to at the beginning of the year. I still have some aches and pains, just like anybody else who is my age and older. …

“But I’m past the rehab portion of it, and now I’m in the strength development of it, and I don’t have to do those tedious little rehab exercises. I can basically play with my kids and do whatever I want. We’ve been playing a ton of soccer in the backyard just about every day.”

He’s also been hitting a bunch of golf balls in the backyard of his Florida compound.

Woods, who had back surgery March 31 and missed the Masters and U.S. Open, has played in just eight events this season, finishing 72 holes on just three occasions.

He made his first return in 2014 at the Quicken Loans National in June, where he missed the cut. He then finished 69th in the British Open, withdrew in the final round from the World Golf Championships-Bridgestone Invitational and missed the cut in the PGA Championship.

He shut his golf game down after the PGA Championship.

After doing basically nothing for two months, his troublesome back slowly got more up to speed and Woods amped up his work on the range and in the gym.

During his down time, however, Woods took his thoughts to yesteryear and, after retrieving a VHS player from his mother, watched old tapes of his swing from his junior and amateur days and the early days of his pro career.

“It was quite interesting to see where my swing was then and how much force I could generate with a very skinny frame,” said Woods, who started 2014 as the world’s No. 1 but has slipped to No. 24. “How did I do that? How do I generate that much power? That’s kind of what we are getting back into it.”
