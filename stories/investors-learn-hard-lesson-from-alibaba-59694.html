Investors Learn Hard Lesson from Alibaba

March 3, 2015 You Can Work...

AFP/Getty

Alibaba (BABA), the Chinese Internet stock that you just had to buy last year, is reminding investors of the perils of too much hype.

Shares of the stock are down $3.65, or 4.4%, to $80.33 Tuesday – marking the shares’ lowest point since first selling stock to the public last September. That’s the stock’s lowest price since the IPO.

The takeaway is a brutal lesson to investors trying to be “players” in the world of IPOs. Privileged investors who got in at the offering price of $68 are still in the black. But the rest of us who aren’t friends of investment bankers are down more than 14%. That’s most regular investors bought the stock at or around the price on the first day of trading of $93.89.

And it’s not that the company is crumbling or anything. Analysts are calling for the company’s adjusted profit in 2015 to rise nearly 19% to $2.23 a share.
