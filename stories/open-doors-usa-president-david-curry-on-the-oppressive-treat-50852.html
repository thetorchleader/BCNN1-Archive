Open Doors USA President David Curry on the Oppressive Treatment of Christians In North Korea

January 3, 2015 Christian

The film “The Interview” has made headlines, not for its success or failure at the box office, but rather because it angered the wrong guy. The comedy’s plot is simple: Two bumbling tabloid reporters are asked by U.S. officials to kill North Korean dictator Kim Jong Un during a scheduled interview. As expected, when Kim learned of the film, he was not amused.

The response from North Korea was, as is typical of the regime, oppressive. The FBI confirmed that North Korea was behind the cyberhacks designed to intimidate the executives at Sony Pictures and the threats of a terror attack against potential theatergoers. In true dictatorial fashion, North Korea decided to suppress rather than persuade. It worked, at least for a while.

After theater chains decided they would not show the film in order to protect their customers’ safety, Sony announced that it would cancel the planned Christmas Day release of the film, only to change its mind again. It is an unfunny end to what was supposed to be a comedy.

Know what else isn’t funny? The Kim regime’s treatment of Christians.

Now, North Korea isn’t a great place to live for anyone who disagrees with the Kim regime. Human-rights groups estimate that more than 200,000 people are imprisoned in North Korea for having political or religious views contrary to those of Supreme Leader Kim. While every one of these prisoners’ lives matter, North Korea’s treatment of Christians is what earned it the top spot on the Open Doors World Watch List 12 years in a row.

It is ironic that a film about North Korea would have even been scheduled to appear on Christmas Day, given that North Koreans do not have the freedom to celebrate Christmas or any other religious holiday.

But regardless of whether North Korea is ranked No. 1 on the 2015 World Watch List, to be released Wednesday, or descends lower on the list, one thing is certain: The situation for Christians in the communist nation remains dire. According to our partners working in secret in North Korea, there are 50,000-70,000 Christians imprisoned in labor camps.
