Omaha Police Sergeant Marvin McClarty Sr. ‘Opened the Door’ for Other Black Officers

April 22, 2015 Black

Marvin McClarty Sr

A prominent Omaha police sergeant who was both an advocate for law enforcement and a critic of the Police Department’s community relations died of liver cancer on April 14.

Marvin McClarty Sr. was 76.

McClarty, who became an officer in 1962 and eventually rose to sergeant, served in the Omaha Police Department for 26 years.

With 13 other black police officers, McClarty started the Brotherhood of the Midwest Guardians in the late 1960s to unite black people serving in law enforcement.

“Had it not been for my father and the other guys … we would not see black police officers,” said McClarty’s son, Marlin McClarty, who is a retired police officer. “He opened the door for officers who came along.”

Marlin McClarty said that although his father had no regrets, he was disappointed that those from the younger generation didn’t respect what the older black police officers accomplished, because the younger people were “detached from what occurred back in the day.”

Along with former Police Officer Tariq Al-Amin, Marvin McClarty hosted a public television show for nearly 10 years called “Protecting the Village.”

Al-Amin, 58, called McClarty “a mentor and counselor.”

“He wasn’t going along with the script,” Al-Amin said. “He told it how he saw it and gave people a true expectation of the police.”

On the show, the two men discussed various topics concerning police and the City of Omaha. “It gave my father another vehicle in which to educate folks on how to interact with police and police accountability,” said Marlin McClarty, 51. “He was pro-police but critical, because he wanted to make sure that the citizens were getting the best that they possibly could from those that protect and serve.”

Marvin McClarty was diagnosed with liver cancer nine hours before he died. He had successfully battled lung cancer, his son said.
