LISTEN: Urban Christian News Network #595 with Daniella Whyte

March 13, 2015 Christian

1. According to PR Newswire, In response to a controversial choice by American Baptist College leadership to invite three active proponents of same-sex marriage to speak and lead worship at the College’s distinguished annual Garnett-Nabrit Lecture Series and preach at the worship services, a group of pastors within the National Baptist Convention have informally convened to express their concerns at a press conference to be held at the Renaissance Hotel on Tuesday, March 17 from 10 a.m. to 11:30 a.m. CDT. Known as the National Baptist Fellowship of Concerned Pastors, the pastors want to hold accountable the president and board of trustees of ABC for their misguided decision to invite the chosen speakers to helm the lecture series.

2. According to Press TV, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has acknowledged that his right-wing Likud party risks losing the upcoming parliamentary elections. Netanyahu told Israel’s Jerusalem Post newspaper that, “Our security is at great risk because there is a real danger that we could lose this election.” The Israeli premier further warned that a potential loss by his party in the polls, due on 17 March, would translate into a change in the Zionist regime’s policy with regard to Iran and Palestine.

3. According to Baptist Press, A south Florida church has received notice from local officials that it must obtain a business license or shut down, even though federal and local laws exempt churches from obtaining such licenses. Liberty Counsel chairman Mat Staver said, “That’s a violation of the First Amendment, but number two, it’s a violation of their own code because churches [in Lake Worth, Fla.] are not required to get business licenses.” Pastor Mike Olive of Common Ground Church called the city’s treatment of the congregation “a war on Jesus.”

4. According to ABC News, Protest leaders outside the Ferguson Police Department have called for the group to disband for the night. With light rain falling, leaders said around 11:10 p.m. Thursday that the demonstrators should head home. By 11:30 p.m., nearly everyone but members of the media had left the scene. It was a marked contrast to the previous night, when fights broke out during a protest before two officers were shot. Both officers have been released from the hospital.

5. According to New York Daily News, Bill Badger, hailed as a hero after he tackled Jared Loughner, the gunman who shot Rep. Gabrielle Giffords in a Tucson, Ariz., grocery store parking lot, died Wednesday of pneumonia. He was 78. Badger, a retired Army colonel, was wounded before he helped tackle and hold down Loughner on Jan. 8, 2011. Loughner opened fire during an event featuring Giffords, killing six people and injuring 13 others — including Giffords and Badger. Giffords said Wednesday that she was saddened to learn of Badger’s passing. She was shot in the head and nearly killed — and still struggles to speak and walk.

6. According to Reuters, A Johnson & Johnson subsidiary pleaded guilty on Tuesday to selling liquid medicine contaminated with metal and agreed to pay $25 million to resolve the case. The subsidiary, McNeil Consumer Healthcare, pleaded guilty to one federal criminal charge. In 2010, the company launched mass recalls of certain children’s over-the-counter-medicines made at its Fort Washington, Pennsylvania plant. The recalls kept widely used products such as Children’s Tylenol off pharmacy shelves and seriously tarnished J&J’s once-sterling reputation. Carol Goodrich, a spokeswoman for McNeil said the plea agreement “closes a chapter” and that the company has “been implementing enhanced quality and oversight standards across its entire business.”

7. According to the AP, Islamic State militants have accepted a pledge of allegiance by Nigerian-grown Boko Haram extremist group. Boko Haram has been weakened by a multinational force that has dislodged it from a score of northeastern Nigerian towns. But its new Twitter account, with increasingly slick and more frequent video messages and a new media arm all were considered signs that the group is now being helped by IS propagandists.

8. According to Reuters, Around 25 people may have been killed and are still under the rubble of a shopping center that caught fire in the Russian city of Kazan, Interfax news agency cited the head of the regional emergencies ministry as saying on Thursday. The blaze broke out on Wednesday in a first-storey cafe at the center, killing seven people, and sending flames and black smoke high into the air. Emergency workers were continuing to clear away rubble late on Thursday in Kazan, about 500 miles east of Moscow.

9. According to CBN News, If you whine about waking up early for church, a congregation in South Korea might make you rethink those complaints. The Myungsueng Church in Seoul has five morning church services beginning at 4:50 a.m. This year the congregation marked 35 years of rising early to pray. CBN News joined with Korean partners, C Channel, for the celebration. One attendee told CBN News Producer Stephen Little he’s been coming to the service for four years to be “spiritually enriched.”

10. According to Reuters, At least five people were killed and another about 100 feared trapped after a cement factory collapsed in Bangladesh on Thursday. Soldiers and sailors in the port town of Mongla helped emergency services search through the rubble and pull out more than 40 survivors. The factory, run by a subsidiary of the Bangladesh army, was still being built in the town 210 miles southwest of the capital, Dhaka.
