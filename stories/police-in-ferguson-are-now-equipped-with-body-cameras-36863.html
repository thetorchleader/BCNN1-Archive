Police in Ferguson Are Now Equipped with Body Cameras

September 3, 2014 Black

Members of the Ferguson, Mo., police department wear body cameras during a rally Aug. 30, 2014, in Ferguson.
AARON P. BERNSTEIN/GETTY IMAGES

Following the donation of 50 body cameras by companies Safety Vision and Digital Ally, Ferguson, Mo., officers have started to wear the equipment attached to their uniforms, which records video and audio.

There was something different about officers in the Ferguson, Mo., police department over the weekend as protesters continued their calls for justice in the fatal shooting of Michael Brown.

Officers have started wearing the body cameras provided by two companies, Safety Visions and Digital Ally, the St. Louis Post-Dispatch reports. The cameras were donated following the fatal Aug. 9 shooting of the unarmed teenager by one of Ferguson’s officers.

The equipment attaches to the officer’s uniform and records video and audio. Representatives from the companies were on site on Saturday training the officers to use the cameras.

“We are still playing with them,” Ferguson Police Chief Thomas Jackson said. “The quality is good.”

According to the Post-Dispatch, each officer will be equipped with a camera. Batteries for the devices were drained on Saturday due to the constant use, but are expected to last a normal full shift.
