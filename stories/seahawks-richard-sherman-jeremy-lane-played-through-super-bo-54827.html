Seahawks Richard Sherman, Jeremy Lane Played Through Super Bowl With Major Injuries

February 2, 2015 Sports

Seahawks CB Richard Sherman hurt his left elbow in the NFC Championship Game.
(Photo: Ted S. Warren, AP)

Monday dawned as a gloomy day for the Legion of Boom.

Just hours after a crushing loss in Super Bowl XLIX, Seattle Seahawks coach Pete Carroll revealed that 40% of his vaunted secondary will face serious physical obstacles in the offseason.

All-pro cornerback Richard Sherman, who injured his left elbow in the NFC Championship Game, will require Tommy John surgery.

Carroll also said nickelback Jeremy Lane suffered a “significant” broken arm Sunday in the Super Bowl loss to the New England Patriots. Lane was hurt after running back his first-quarter interception of Patriots quarterback Tom Brady.

Two weeks earlier, Sherman was a victim of friendly fire when Seahawks strong safety Kam Chancellor helped him tackle Green Bay Packers running back James Starks. Despite agonizing pain, Sherman played through the injury in the defeat of the Packers and did not miss a practice during the lead-up to the Super Bowl.

Baseball pitchers typically need a year or more to come back from Tommy John surgery, which requires a tendon transplant from elsewhere in the body to replace the elbow’s ulnar collateral ligament. But since Sherman doesn’t need to throw the football, his recovery should be significantly shorter.
