Will Terrorists Target Vulnerable NYC Bridges Next?

September 7, 2014 National

Pedestrians make their way from Brooklyn to Manhattan along a walkway on the Brooklyn Bridge on Aug. 27.
(Photo: Melanie Eversley, USA TODAY)

The bridges of New York City — landmarks of the Big Apple’s skyline and symbols of the nation’s multibillion-dollar transportation system — are a major focus for security officials as the anniversary of 9/11 looms.

The target of terrorist plots in the past decade that range from severing suspension cables on the Brooklyn Bridge to blind sheik Omar Abdel-Rahman’s plot to obliterate the George Washington Bridge during rush hour, the bridges are difficult to protect because of their highly accessible spans across prominent waterways.

Two recent breaches on the Brooklyn Bridge and one on the nearby Manhattan Bridge have brought heightened security alerts. And, 13 years after the World Trade Center attacks, the incidents have shown that it’s not impossible for people to find ways to go where they are forbidden.

On July 22, two German artists hoisted white flags atop two towers on the bridge. “People climbed the bridge and it took hours to accomplish that task,” said Manny Gomez, a retired FBI special agent and New York Police Department sergeant. “What could terrorist groups have done in that multi-hour time period with a dirty bomb, a nuclear bomb or a chemical device?”

The latest breaches underscore why there is such sensitivity around the bridges, which security documents show have been identified time and time again as the Holy Grails of terrorist targets.

Gomez and other experts are warning that officials need to focus more on protecting New York’s bridges. The more iconic crossings such as the Brooklyn Bridge and the George Washington Bridge could be targets because they represent Americana, much like Yankee Stadium or the Super Bowl.

Mayor Bill de Blasio said on Aug. 25 that his office is investigating how to better secure the Brooklyn Bridge. Former NYC security official Nicholas Casale warns that heightened security around the structure has to be done well.

Transportation is a top-priority target of terrorists, says Casale, former chief of counterterrorism for the Metropolitan Transit Authority, the parent agency to New York’s subways and public buses. He points to bombings in the London tube in 2005, the Madrid rail lines in 2004 and the Moscow Metro bombing the same year.

“To me, it’s incomprehensible that we’re discussing this 13 years after 9/11,” Casale said, adding, “If we don’t believe we are in the crosshairs of terrorists, then let the buildings reopen up, take away the street barriers.”

Terrorist groups such as Al-Qaeda have long eyed the Brooklyn Bridge and another famous connector, the George Washington Bridge linking New York and New Jersey, as targets. Among the plots:

  • Al-Qaeda operative Lyman Faris planned to cut the cables of the Brooklyn Bridge under the direction of al-Qaeda leader Khalid Shaikh Mohammed, admitted mastermind of the 2001 terror attacks. Faris was arrested in 2003, pleaded guilty and went to federal prison.
  • Al-Qaeda operative Abu Zubaydah mentioned bridges as possible terror targets in a classified Guantanamo Bay document obtained by the New York Times in 2011.
  • In 1993, officials foiled a plot by Omar Abdel-Rahman to target the George Washington Bridge, Holland Tunnel, Lincoln Tunnel and other sites.
  • In 2011, former New York City Mayor Rudolph Giuliani told Forbes that officials had “plenty of intelligence” that Islamic extremist terrorists were targeting the George Washington Bridge and other local sites.

The Brooklyn Bridge is one of the oldest suspension bridges in the country, and for a time, it was the longest suspension bridge in the world. It stretches just over a mile across the East River, connecting Lower Manhattan to downtown Brooklyn.
