House in Northampton, Mass., Part of African-American Heritage, to be Restored Instead of Demolished

January 6, 2015 Black

A house in the Florence neighborhood, thought to be part of local 19th-century African-American history, will be restored and renovated instead of torn down.

Contractor Walter Marek of Westhampton, who in May said he wanted to demolish the building, is now pursuing a structural repair and historically-aware renovation with the help of Northampton architect Tris Metcalfe and restoration carpenter Kris Thomson.

The single-family home at 205 Nonotuck Street was long known in the neighborhood for its orange paint, picket fence, and overgrown plantings, including mature magnolia trees and a giant Rose of Sharon.

In May, the city’s Historical Commission imposed a one-year demolition delay on the property after Marek filed for a permit to tear the house down. Marek, appearing before the commission, told them he had an option on the property and wanted to demolish the vacant building and build a new house in its place.

Local historians who attended the meeting countered that the little orange house was part of the city’s unique 19th-century African-American history, and asked the commission to use its powers to impose the one-year delay so an alternative solution could be sought.

Steve Strimer, director of the Ruggles Center for Early Florence History and Underground Railroad Studies, said the house was inhabited by a woman named Hannah Randall in 1850. Randall was one of many free blacks and fugitive slaves living along Nonotuck Street at the time, he said, arguing that the fabric of the neighborhood should be preserved.

Marek and his lawyer Donald Abel maintained that the building was beyond repair, was burdened by a tax liability, and could not be feasibly restored. After the commission imposed the delay, the two quickly left the meeting and declined to comment on their next move.

“We’ll see,” said Marek, when asked if he would back out of purchasing the property.

Now, eight months later, the magnolias have been carefully pruned, other vegetation has been cut back, a new foundation has been poured, and the house is undergoing structural repairs.
