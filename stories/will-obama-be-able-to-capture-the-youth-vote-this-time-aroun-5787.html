Will Obama be Able to Capture the Youth Vote This Time Around?

May 8, 2012 Uncategorized

While Barack Obama already broke the fundraiser record — holding more events than presidents Jimmy Carter, Ronald Reagan, George Bush Sr., Bill Clinton and George W. Bush combined — he kicked off his “official” re-election campaign on Saturday at college rallies in Virginia and Ohio. Unfortunately, as Obama tries to reignite the “Yes We Can” youth fire that carried him to victory in 2008, the fizzled enthusiasm and dwindling crowds show that no, he can’t.

According to ABC News, aides in the Obama campaign promised an “overflow” crowd at the Schottenstein Center. They wanted to prove the president is still highly popular among young people and still able to energize large crowds. Unfortunately about 4,300 of the 18,300 seats remained empty.
The Columbus Dispatch reported that, when the large crowd didn’t materialize, Obama organizers scrambled to move people from the seats to the floor of the gym in order to make the crowd appear larger on television.
If they want to give the appearance of an “overflow” crowd, perhaps Obama’s campaign organizers would be wise to choose smaller venues.
At a Feb. 27 rally at Ohio State’s St. John’s Arena in 2008, with a seating capacity just over 13,000, the space appeared to be full. But that was during the height of Obama’s popularity. Photos from the 2008 event, compared to the March 5 Twitter photo taken by Ryan Williams, show how a smaller space would not draw so much attention to what The Hill described in April as “Obama’s enthusiasm gap.”
Another example of how a smaller venue would help maintain the illusion that Obama is still popular with the youth was his rally at Virginia Commonwealth University. Even though Obama couldn’t pull enough students to fill the 18,000 seats at the Schottenstein Center on Saturday he did manage to fill every one of the 8,000 seats of the Siegel Center.
But maybe it’s Ohio. This wasn’t the first time Obama struggled to draw an audience in the crucial battleground Buckeye State.
In September 2010, with less than an hour to go before Obama was scheduled to speak at the Cuyahoga Community College’s Western Campus recreation center, 75 seats remained empty. So event organizers scrambled across the campus recruiting students to fill the void.
For a rally at Cleveland State University’s 13,000-seat Wolstein Center in October 2010 only 8,000 showed up.
Comparatively, Obama drew a crowd of 35,000 at Ohio State University when he campaigned for former Governor Ted Strickland that same month. Then again, musical guest John Legend gave a free concert for that event.
