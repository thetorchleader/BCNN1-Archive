Nasdaq Closes Above 3000 for First Time Since 2000

March 14, 2012 Uncategorized

The Nasdaq, powered this time by iPad and iPhone maker Apple and not money-losing Internet companies, closed above 3000 on Tuesday for the first time since 2000, extending an early-year rally that has been powered by an improving U.S. economy and a lessening of the fear factor that haunted markets last year.
One week after stocks suffered their worst drop of the year — a 1.5% dive dubbed the 24-hour correction — stocks regained their mojo. The Nasdaq, up nearly 17% this year, closed Tuesday at 3040, its highest level since Nov. 15, 2000.
The last time the tech-dominated index was trading at these levels, Wall Street was eight months into a bear market, the Yankees had just beat the Mets in the “Subway Series,” Julia Roberts was winning awards for her role in the film Erin Brockovich, and the nation was embroiled in a vote-counting controversy involving presidential candidates George W. Bush and Al Gore.
The Nasdaq, Dow Jones industrials and Standard & Poor’s 500 are all at bull market highs. The Dow, which last month finished above 13,000 for the first time since May 2008, extended its gains, surging 218 points to 13,178, its best close since Dec. 31, 2007.
