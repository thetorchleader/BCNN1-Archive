Ways to Thrive in Small Business

August 20, 2014 You Can Work...

Apt2B reaches out to Millennial buyers through tools such as Facebook and Instagram.
(Photo: G/O Digital)

Are you among the many small business owners who believe their biggest challenge is finding new customers? In today’s changing environment, driven by increased smartphone adoption, finding new customers isn’t the problem. The challenge is helping new customers find you.

To make this happen, local business owners must prioritize social media, so that when customers search for solutions – whether it is a plumber to fix a broken toilet or the perfect restaurant to celebrate an anniversary – your business rises to the top of the rankings.

Here are six tips to take your small business to the next level online:

1. Build a narrative for your brand.

“Everyone’s selling sofas online at this point, so we have to be really unique,” says Mat Herman, a second-generation furniture salesman and co-founder of Apt 2B in Los Angeles.

When you visit Apt2B.com, you’ll see that Herman and his staff are building a conversation that goes beyond product details. It’s a virtual “store next door.” For instance, instead of staid employee bios, you “Meet the Landlords,” and even get a glimpse of the neighbors in apartments 2A and 2C.

MORE: Leaders in Local by G/O Digital

2. Interact with your customers and make it a priority.

“There are so many challenges in this economy being a private school,” says Kristin Dickson, superintendent of Page Private School based in Southern California. “We teach children. Education is our priority, so marketing really gets put on the back burner. We found that we just simply didn’t have the time to take care of all those critical elements that marketing requires these days.”

Continuing the legacy of her great-grandfather, Dickson has refocused the image of the Page Private School, as well as its web presence. She updated the website, initiated a pay-per-click campaign, sought help with Search Engine Optimization (SEO) and even built a page on Facebook. Since then, the school has seen a jump in open house attendance and enrollment.
