Christian Faith Powering NL Champs In World Series

October 29, 2014 Sports

Travis Ishikawa #45 of the San Francisco Giants watches batting practice before Game Three of the 2014 World Series at AT&T Park on October 24, 2014 in San Francisco, California.
Elsa/Getty Images North America

After winning the World Series in 2010 and 2012, the San Francisco Giants are at it again trying to bring another Major League Baseball championship pennant to the fans at AT&T Park. But many within the powerhouse ball club attribute their success to more than a stellar pitching staff or hard-hitting batting lineup — they point to God and their Christian faith.

Serving more than two decades as the vice president and assistant general manager of the San Francisco Giants’ Baseball Operations Department, Bobby Evans maintains that above all else in the game of baseball, the most important aspect of his job is to honor Jesus Christ.

“You want your life to point people to Christ,” Evans proclaimed, according to the Christian News Journal. “It starts for me with my own relationship with Christ. That’s going to direct and dictate what influence I have for Christ in my family, in my marriage and in the workplace.”

As a testimony to his faith and adherence to his Christian values, Evans made a tough decision that could have hurt the Giants’ chances of winning their World Series Championship in 2012 when he approved putting outfielder Melky Cabrera on the bench to serve a 50-game suspension. Cabrera had tested positive for high levels of a testosterone, indicating his use of a banned performance-enhancing substance.

Even though Evans knew that the punishment wouldn’t be popular with many fans and those within the organization, he stuck to the Christian principles guiding his life. A number of baseball commentators had anticipated that Cabrera would be reinstated to the Giants’ roster so that he could help the team win the 2012 World Series, but Evans decided to do what he knew was biblically right and kept him on the bench. Despite their loss of a great player, the Giants went on to sweep the favored Detroit Tigers four games to zero to win their sixth World Series. Just two years later, Evans and the Giants are going for their seventh.

Evans indicated that he could let other factors and logistics guide his decisions, but he stresses that everything he does needs to fall into alignment with his faith in Christ, including putting his family first and the decisions he makes to run the most successful organization in Major League Baseball.

“Even with home games, you’re at the ballpark til 11 o’clock at night after a night game,” Evans pointed out. “And so whenever possible, I try to have my family at the ballpark.”

While serving in the Giants’ front office, he makes sure that nothing compromises his duty as the head of his first ministry — his family.

“I make sure that if we have a night game, I’m up to take them to school the next day,” Evans continued. “I coach Little League teams and just make sure there’s no priority that gets ahead of them.”
