Laura Gomez on What It Will Take to Get More Latinos Into Technology

December 1, 2014 LCNN1, Technology

Laura Gomez, a tech entrepreneur who didn’t learn English until she was 10, has some ideas about how Silicon Valley can diversify its workforce.
(Photo: Christopher Wiggins, USA TODAY)

Laura Gomez has a quarter-million Twitter followers and has worked for some of the most powerful companies in high tech.

She belongs to the 3% — the 3% of workers in the industry who are Hispanic.

The 35-year-old entrepreneur came to the USA at age 10 from Mexico and didn’t gain legal status until her college years. Encouraged by her teachers, she looked headed for a career in computer science. She took a detour into economics and sociology, with an emphasis on race.

This background has given her a singular voice on how to transform a Silicon Valley workforce that’s largely white, male and Asian.

Most major Silicon Valley companies, including her former employers at Google and Twitter, have released sobering statistics about the demographics of the high-tech work force. To Gomez, that means these companies – innovative as they are – could be drawing from an even richer pool of ideas.

“Diversity comes from experience,” she said in an interview at USA TODAY’s offices.

Years of working in product development means she can speak geek.
