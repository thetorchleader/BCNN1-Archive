In Detroit, Michelle Obama Urges Democrats to Vote

October 10, 2014 National

First lady Michelle Obama rallies Democrats to get out the vote Oct. 10, 2014, in Detroit.
(Photo: Kimberly P. Mitchell, Detroit Free Press)

First lady Michelle Obama had one overriding message Friday in her visit to Detroit Music Hall: Vote on Nov. 4.

That’s the primary strategy for Democrats this year, getting a good portion of the 900,000 Democrats who didn’t go to the polls on Election Day 2010 to cast their ballots.

“We won, because we showed up and voted,” in 2008 and 2012, Obama said to a crowd of about 1,800 people.

Her main task was to herald the candidacies of Mark Schauer and Gary Peters, the Michigan Democrats who are their party’s nominees for governor and U.S. Senate, respectively.

“Gary and Mark, they get it. They know the American dream because they have lived it,” Obama said. “So, Michigan, if you want a senator and governor who share your values, then you’ve got to vote for Gary Peters and Mark Schauer. You’ve got to do it.”

The two top-of-the-ticket candidates are hoping to capitalize on the popularity of the first lady, whose favor-ability numbers are in the mid-60s.

“We’ll give Carl Levin the type of honor he deserves,” said Peters of Bloomfield Township, Mich., who is hoping to replace retiring Sen. Carl Levin, also a Democrat. “And a very important way to honor his incredible legacy is to send a Democrat to Washington, D.C.”

Schauer noted that the policies of GOP Gov. Rick Snyder have hurt Michigan families, and he reiterated his promises to repeal a tax on retiree pensions and the right-to-work law that Snyder helped usher in during his first term in office.

“His choices may work if you’re wealthy or big corporations,” Schauer said of Snyder. “But if you’re a student, teacher, parent or senior, his choices aren’t working for you, and they’re not going to.”

Nearly the entire Democratic ticket came to the rally — along with an enthusiastic crowd.
