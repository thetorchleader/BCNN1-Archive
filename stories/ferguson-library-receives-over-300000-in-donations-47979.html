Ferguson Library Receives Over $300,000 in Donations

December 3, 2014 Black

Amid daily protests, there was this call for peace on an abandoned building in Ferguson, Mo., where a public library remains a community hub. — Associated Press

The tiny library usually gets $3,000 a year from donors but it’s in a spotlight.

From inside the safe haven of the Ferguson Public Library, director Scott Bonner twice watched rogue groups of protesters try to kick in the front doors last Monday night.

With a fire extinguisher in his hand to stop potential fires, he turned both groups away on a day when fear and anger were prevalent.

He asked them to stop, and they did, kicking some more, yelling “insulting things,” and the second time throwing a glass bottle at the door of the library.

It’s one detail of life in Ferguson, Mo., that Bonner shared in a wide-ranging interview on Reddit about what it’s been like for him and for Ferguson’s families since a grand jury decided last Monday not to indict white police officer Darren Wilson in the killing of black teenager Michael Brown last August.

In the interview, Bonner, a married father of four who has only been on the job since July, also shared this: that donors all over the country had in just two days given the library $200,000, or about half its annual budget. Since then, that total has eclipsed $300,000. By contrast, in typical years, the library gets about $3,000 in donations. Why the outpouring? The library stayed open Thanksgiving week although the possibility of riots prompted the school district to close. The library became a hub by doing what it does: Inviting people in to be educated.
