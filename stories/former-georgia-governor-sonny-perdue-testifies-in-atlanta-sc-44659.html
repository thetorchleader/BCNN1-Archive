Former Georgia Governor Sonny Perdue Testifies in Atlanta School Cheating Scandal, says Cheating Was ‘Conspiratorial’

November 11, 2014 Education

Sonny Perdue at APS Trial

The former Georgia governor who ordered a state inquiry amid suspicions of widespread cheating in Atlanta’s public schools testified Monday that he felt the wrongdoing was pervasive and that academic officials had tried to impede a public accounting of it.

“Even if a teacher were in the classroom, the amount of erasures that we saw from wrong to right could not have even been done within that testing period,” the former governor, Sonny Perdue, said. “It became fairly evident to me that something had happened outside that classroom testing environment to those test documents, and that had to be conspiratorial.”

Mr. Perdue offered his assessment during a brief turn on the witness stand during the trial of 12 Atlanta educators whom prosecutors have accused of participating in cheating they have portrayed as endemic in this city’s schools. Defense lawyers objected to portions of the former governor’s testimony, including his use of “conspiratorial.”

Mr. Perdue, a Republican who left office in 2011, was governor when the first allegations of misconduct became public in 2008. They shook a district where academic achievement had long lagged but had become abundant under Dr. Beverly L. Hall, the superintendent of schools.

The state ultimately commissioned a so-called erasure analysis, as well as an inquiry that included Georgia Bureau of Investigation agents. Those reviews preceded criminal charges against 35 educators; more than 20 of them have since pleaded guilty.

Mr. Perdue told jurors on Monday that he had asked local officials to investigate after questions emerged about the validity of test scores in Atlanta and a school district in southwest Georgia. But Mr. Perdue said the subsequent reviews had been inadequate and that Dr. Hall’s commitment to a thorough inquiry had been fleeting.
