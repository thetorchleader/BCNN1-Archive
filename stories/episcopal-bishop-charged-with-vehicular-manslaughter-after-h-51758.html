Episcopal Bishop Charged With Vehicular Manslaughter After Hit-and-Run Death

January 9, 2015 Christian

The Right Rev. Heather Cook, the No. 2 bishop in the Episcopal Diocese of Maryland, was charged with vehicular homicide Jan. 9, 2015, in connection with the death of a bicyclist in December. (Photo: Episcopal Diocese of Maryland)

An Episcopal bishop was charged Friday with vehicular manslaughter in connection with a hit-and-run crash that killed a cyclist last month.

The Right Rev. Heather Cook, 58, of the Episcopal Diocese of Maryland also was charged with drunken driving, failure to remain at the scene of an accident resulting in serious injury and death, and using a mobile device while driving. She turned herself in to authorities Friday afternoon, said David Irwin, a lawyer representing Cook.

Cook’s blood-alcohol level was 0.22% after the Dec. 27 crash, nearly triple Maryland’s legal limit, Baltimore State’s Attorney Marilyn Mosby said. Cook initially left the accident scene after her Subaru Forester ran into bicyclist Thomas Palermo, 41, of Baltimore, but she returned about 20 minutes later.

Palermo, a cyclist for more than 25 years, died the same day of head injuries, according to a medical examiner. He was the father of a 6-year-old daughter and a 4-year-old son.

Cook, elected to the No. 2 spot in the diocese May 2, was put on leave after the wreck and is facing a church investigation concerning her conduct and her fitness as a church leader.

“We are deeply heartbroken over this, and we cry for the Palermo family, our sister Heather and all in the community who are hurting,” the Right Rev. Eugene Taylor Sutton, head of the Episcopal diocese that includes Baltimore and 10 of Maryland’s 23 counties, said in a statement.
