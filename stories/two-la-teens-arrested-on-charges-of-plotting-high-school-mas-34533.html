Two LA Teens Arrested on Charges of Plotting High-School Massacre

August 19, 2014 National

South Pasadena High School was the target of a mass shooting plot by two teenagers. (PHOTO CREDIT: AFP)

Two US teenagers arrested on charges of plotting a high-school mass shooting had a “very methodical” plan and wanted to die in the gunbattle.

The 16- and 17-year-olds, who police did not identify, wanted to kill as many people as possible and “planned to die through a shootout with the police,” Arthur Miller, the police chief in the Los Angeles suburb of South Pasadena, said.

The plot to kill three staff members and as many students as possible at South Pasadena High School, which has 4500 students, was “very methodical and it seemed very viable,” said Mr Miller.

He gave few details of the plot but said the pair had a “chilling” demeanour and displayed no emotion when interrogated.

Two high-school members alerted authorities, who monitored the suspects’ social media behaviour. After they accumulated compelling evidence, police obtained a search warrant and went to their homes.

Police did not find weapons, but did learn by looking at their computers that the teenagers were researching how to make explosives, and there was a possibility of obtaining an automatic weapon, Mr Miller said.

The parents cooperated with police, and both teens — neither of whom had a criminal record — were arrested on charges of conspiracy and making credible threats.

Police said in a statement that one of the students was arrested without incident, while the resisted and tried to flee.

There was no date set for the shooting spree, and Miller said the plot was still in the planning stage.

FBI agents will help in the probe, Miller said.

The new school year starts tomorrow in Los Angeles and students outside the school expressed concern over the apparent plot.

“I feel a lot more suspicious that someone could still be working on that plan,” 16-year-old Malika Palacio said.

Samuel Garcia, 17, said he trusted school security and was glad that they were arrested, but his mother Cindy was not as sanguine. “That’s terrifying,” she said.
