For Comedian Joan Rivers, No Occasion Was Off-Limits, Not Even Her Own Funeral

September 7, 2014 Entertainment

Friends, family and fans gathered for the funeral of Joan Rivers in Manhattan on Sunday. Here, her daughter, Melissa Rivers, and her grandson, Cooper, left Temple Emanu-el in Manhattan after the funeral.
Damon Winter/The New York Times

For the comedian Joan Rivers, no occasion was off-limits, not even her own funeral.

She once wrote that she wanted her funeral to be “a huge showbiz affair with lights, cameras, action,” with craft services, paparazzi and publicists on hand. “I don’t want some rabbi rambling on; I want Meryl Streep crying, in five different accents,” she wrote in her 2012 book, “I Hate Everyone …Starting With Me.”

On Sunday, that day arrived as hundreds of people gathered at Temple Emanu-El in Manhattan to say goodbye to the comedian, who died on Thursday at 81. A private funeral service was held in the synagogue, but fans and spectators amassed outside, on Fifth Avenue opposite Central Park, some wearing their Sunday best to honor the fashion-savvy Ms. Rivers.

With paparazzi snapping away, a parade of television personalities, journalists and entertainment stars entered the building: Diane Sawyer; Barbara Walters; David Letterman; Howard Stern; Rosie O’Donnell; Whoopi Goldberg; Sarah Jessica Parker and her husband, Matthew Broderick; and Donald Trump, among others.

Inside, white gardenias lined the front of the synagogue as an organist played classical music. Shortly before 11 a.m., the New York City Gay Men’s Chorus began its performance, which included “What a Wonderful World,” “There Is Nothing Like a Dame” and “Big Spender.” The Broadway luminaries Audra McDonald and Hugh Jackman performed as well, and there were several tributes, including one by her daughter, Melissa Rivers, who often appeared by her mother’s side on camera.

As the service ended around 12:30 p.m., a group of bagpipers and drummers from the New York Police Department marched down the center aisle playing “New York, New York,” before pouring out onto Fifth Avenue, where they played “Give My Regards to Broadway.”

As the television host Charlie Rose exited, he described the funeral as “moving, funny, loving,” and said Ms. Rivers “would have liked it.”
