Rick Warren to Give Final Sermon at Mars Hill Church Before It Officially Dissolves

December 25, 2014 Christian

Rick Warren, pastor of Saddleback Church in Lake Forest, California, speaks at the Pastors’ Conference 2014, ahead of the Southern Baptist Convention’s Annual Meeting, on Monday, June 9, 2014, in Baltimore, Md.
(Photo: The Christian Post/Sonny Hong)

Rick Warren will give the last sermon at Mars Hill Church as the Seattle-based former mega church officially dissolves itself on January 1.

The Mars Hill Church is going out with a bang after imploding in a series of scandals and misconduct allegations through much of 2014.

Mega church pastor Rick Warren, bestselling author of The Purpose Driven Life, will speak to Mars Hill congregations on the church’s last Sunday before dissolving. Warren delivered the Invocation Prayer at President Obama’s 2009 inauguration.

“Our dear friend Pastor Rick Warren from Saddleback Church has graciously offered to preach the final sermon for us,” Mars Hill said in its e-mail newsletter. “Each of our churches will be showing a special message from Pastor Rick, based on John 12:24.

“Our hope is that this timely message will be an encouragement for the people that have called Mars Hill Church their home, as we reflect on the great impact our church has had on the kingdom of God, as well as an ecouragement (sic) for the new churches emerging out of Mars Hill, and the potential they have to continue bearing much fruit.”

Mars Hill officially ceases to exist at midnight on New Year’s Eve.

Its former lead pastor, Mark Driscoll, has launched a web site with donations going to Learning for Living, self-described as “an application pending registered 501(c)3 nonprofit organization.

Warren and Driscoll have longstanding ties. The Seattle-based Driscoll treated the Southern California pastor as a kind of mentor, and reveled in association with Warren.

In a 2006 book, Driscoll effused that Warren was “one of the most important Christians in our generation,” adding: “Sadly, there was a time when, as an arrogant young punk, I was critical of his work.”

Later, Driscoll would recall: ”I first heard from Pastor Rick Warren perhaps a few years ago. He said he wanted me to fly down to a conference he was hosting, hang out, and get some time together.”
