Colleges Responding Faster to Subject of Rape

December 16, 2011 Uncategorized

A question about rape on a fraternity survey at the University of Vermont has prompted a protest rally, a chapter suspension, an investigation and some praise for the university’s response.

The university’s chapter of Sigma Phi Epsilon
was suspended by the university and by the fraternity’s national
headquarters after university interim President John Bramley learned
Monday about a pledge survey that included the question, “If I could
rape someone, who would it be?”

The suspension
means the chapter cannot conduct fraternity activities without someone
from the university administration or the fraternity’s national
headquarters present, said Annie Stevens, associate vice president for
student and campus life. How else the university responds is still
unclear, she said.

A campus rally drew 200
students Thursday. An online petition demanding the fraternity’s ouster
from the 12,000-student campus had 3,300 signatures by Thursday evening.

“We’re
still trying to understand exactly how this form came to be, who
created it, if it got distributed, in what way,” Stevens said.
