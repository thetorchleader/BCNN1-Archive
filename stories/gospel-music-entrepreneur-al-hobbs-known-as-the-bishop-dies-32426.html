Gospel Music Entrepreneur Al Hobbs, Known as ‘The Bishop’, Dies of Cancer at 71

August 4, 2014 Christian

Al Hobbs (Photo: Star file photo)

Al Hobbs — known for his work at radio station WTLC, for his gospel recordings and for founding the StarQuest talent competition — died on Thursday at age 71.

Hobbs’ high-profile tenure at WTLC began in 1970 and ended in 1993. His gospel recording label, Aleho, issued dozens of albums between 1992 and 2003, and he led the Indianapolis Mass Choir in the 1980s.

At the time of his death, Hobbs served as vice-chairman of the Gospel Music Workshop of America. The Alabama native graduated from Central High School in Louisville, Ky., as a classmate of Muhammad Ali. According to reports, Hobbs battled cancer for the last three years.

During a 2008 interview with The Star, Hobbs talked about simultaneously tackling his work with Aleho, Indiana Black Expo’s StarQuest talent search, Circle City Classic’s Fall Fest bazaar and the national gospel workshop.

“If a task is in front of me for five minutes, I can give it 100 percent,” he said. “When it goes off my desk, it’s gone. But my recall captures everything I did. The big problem for me is that I wanted to accomplish so much. Quite honestly, the prayer of my life became, ‘OK, Lord, I’ve done this. Got something else for me?’ I’ve always been about increasing not only my zeal, but those things the Lord could use me to accomplish.”

Hobbs originally came to Indianapolis in 1960 to attend Butler University, but couldn’t enroll because he learned his mother “spent the tuition money fixing the furnace in her duplex.” He found a job at an A&P grocery store, and Hobbs worked in the grocery industry when he jumped to radio in the early days of WTLC.
