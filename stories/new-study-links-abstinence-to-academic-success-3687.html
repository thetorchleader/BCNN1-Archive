New Study Links Abstinence to Academic Success

February 9, 2012 Uncategorized

Students at Indiana schools that encourage them to avoid sex until adulthood tend to demonstrate better academic achievement than peers at other schools in the state without such programs, a Purdue University professor asserts in a new study. 

Scecina High School students (from left) Lauren Skirvin, Jason Breeden, Shawn Kelly, and Greg Hansberry spoke to eighth graders at Little Flower Catholic School about abstinence in 2001. / Kelly Wilkinson / The Star 2001 file photo
Sociology Prof. Kenneth Ferraro researched 42 Indiana high schools — half of which offered a specific “abstinence education” program called the PEERS Project.
The program recruits high school students to talk to younger pupils in grades 6 though 8 about avoiding risks, including those associated with teenage sexual activity. The talks by the high school students are usually given in science or health-and-wellness classes.
The 42 schools studied, Ferraro said, are similar in enrollment, community size and racial demographics.
“We were interested in whether abstinence education programs were good, bad, or benign for academic performance,” Ferraro said, according to a release from the PEERS Project touting the results. “We found that school corporations with a specific abstinence education program had a higher percentage of their high school sophomores pass the math portion of the ISTEP+ Graduation Qualifying Exam (GQE) in 2008-2009 than was the case for matched controls.”
The study, published in a recent issue of the American Journal of Health Studies, was co-authored by Karis Pressler, a Purdue sociology doctoral candidate.
Not everyone, however, believes in the PEERS Project’s emphasis on abstinence. A Planned Parenthood of Indiana official said abstinence programs often are too narrow in their focus.
“(For) reducing unintended pregnancy and the spread of sexually transmitted diseases in Indiana,” said Chrystal Struben, an education specialist at the organization, “one of the most proven and effective ways to do this is through comprehensive sex education.”
