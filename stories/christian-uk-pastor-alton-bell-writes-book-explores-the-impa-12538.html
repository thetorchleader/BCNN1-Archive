Christian U.K. Pastor Alton Bell Writes Book Explores the Impact of Slavery on Black People

May 19, 2013 Uncategorized

CHURCH LEADER Alton Bell’s new book Breaking The Chains of Mental Slavery explores the impact of slavery on black people. Here, he tells Soul Stirrings why he wrote the book

SOUL STIRRINGS (SS): What inspired you to write a book about breaking the chains of mental slavery?
Pastor Alton Bell (PAB): The recurring patterns presented by the men in my family. They were either victims of the criminal justice system or just not progressing. I started to research these issues in 2003, to determine what was holding them back and circumventing their progress. It became apparent that issues from the enslavement of Africans were at the root of many of the issues, particularly the rage and anger and the mental destruction caused by enslavement.
   
SS: You address this book to black Christians with a Caribbean background. In what ways do you feel we are enslaved to our past?
PAB: Christians are located in a particular culture with a specific world view. During slavery, slaves used a variety of methods to survive including signifying, which involved ‘playing fool to catch wise’ and ‘obeah.’ After the abolition of slavery, the Christian ex-slaves still signified and also visited the ‘obeah man’ when things were really bad. Since these and other generational links have never been broken, many Caribbean Christians are still tied to their dysfunctional past.  
 
SS: How, in your view, do the effects of slavery manifest themselves in the way black people behave?
PAB: Many young black people still disrespect or fear authority figures, particularly in the church. Many are still operating in rebellion and still suffer from esteem issues. There is still the ‘crab in a barrel mentality’ and there is the black on black violence evidenced on our streets. Illegitimacy and sexual promiscuity are still prevalent. The emasculation of the black male (failure to take responsibility) and the ‘strong black women’ syndrome are rife.
