Rev. James Christian Has A Big New Vision for Big Zion AME Zion Church In Mobile, Alabama

August 8, 2014 Black

Rev. James Christian is pastor of Big Zion AME Zion Church in Mobile, Ala. (Carol McPhail/cmcphail@al.com)

Located near Church Street Cemetery in downtown Mobile, Big Zion AME Zion Church has played many roles over the years – as a church for slaves, a community gathering place, a beacon for Methodism and a training ground for up-and-coming ministers in the AME Zion denomination. Its building is on the African-American Heritage Trail.

But Rev. James Christian, appointed pastor last November, says it’s time for the historic church to become even more visible in Mobile. A self-described “revitalizer,” Christian wants to reach out to the community, represent the love of Christ and, along the way, add to the 200 members now listed on the roll.

“We must be visible within the community and not be isolated,” said Christian, who took a break from a meeting to outline his vision. “We’re willing to work with anyone who’s willing to make a difference.”

Christian, 67, is a Booneville, Miss., native who grew up in Ohio and Pennsylvania. His last assignment was an eight-year stint as pastor of Solomon Temple AME Zion Church in Moss Point, Miss. During his tenure there, membership grew from 60 to 300, he said.

He has served as a pastor twice in Alabama, including in McIntosh, but Big Zion is his first appointment in Mobile. Like all Methodist denominations, the AME Zion Church employs the Wesleyan tradition of itinerancy, an appointment system that moves pastors from congregation to congregation on a regular basis.

Dressed in a pinstripe suit with an iPhone headset around his neck, Christian described how he “followed in the footsteps” of his great-grandfather, grandfather and mother into the ministry. God called him at age 15, but he took the circuitous route.

“It was just one night during church; it was something so prevalent in my life,” he recalled. “I could hear the voice saying, ‘I’ve called you for greater works.’ As a young boy, I thought I had other things I needed and wanted to do.”

Those things included studying broadcasting at International Broadcasting College in Dayton, Ohio, receiving bachelor and master’s degrees, and working a reporter for a TV station near Oklahoma City.

But God had other plans. “After many years of fighting that call, I had to surrender,” he said. Christian went back to school, earning a master of Ministerial Leadership from Amridge University, formerly Southern Christian University, in Montgomery.

His wife, Sondia, is also an ordained elder, with a doctorate in education.
