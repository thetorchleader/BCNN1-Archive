How to Avoid the 3.8% Medicare Surtax on Investment Income

April 28, 2015 Finances

The net investment income tax, or NIIT, is actually a 3.8% Medicare surtax on investment income. It first took effect in 2013. So after filing your 2014 return, you’ve experienced the joys of this extra tax for two years. Now you may be ready to get proactive by taking some steps to stop, or at least slow, the bleeding for this year and beyond. Good! Let’s get started.

3.8% net investment income tax basics

The NIIT can affect higher-income individuals who have investment income. (It can also affect estates and trusts, but we will only focus on individuals here.)

While the NIIT mainly hits folks who consistently have high income, it can also strike anyone who has a big one-time shot of income or gain this year or any other year. For example, if you sell some company stock for a big gain, get a big bonus, or even sell a home for a big profit, you could have to pay. What to do? Here are strategies to employ between now and year-end to avoid or minimize your exposure to the 3.8% tax.

The following types of income and gain (net of related deductions) are included in the definition of net investment income and therefore potentially exposed to the NIIT.

•Gains from selling investment assets — such as gains from stocks and securities held taxable brokerage firm accounts

Real estate gains, including the taxable portion of a big gain from selling your principal residence or a taxable gain from selling a vacation home or rental property.
