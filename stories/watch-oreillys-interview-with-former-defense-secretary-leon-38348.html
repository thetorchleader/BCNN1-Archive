WATCH: O’Reilly’s Interview with Former Defense Secretary Leon Panetta was Shocking to Many

October 9, 2014 National

Tuesday night Leon Panetta’s book tour brought him to the O’Reilly Factor. O’Reilly conducted what turned out to be a masterful interview, the results of which were nothing short of devastating for President Obama.

Panetta has been appearing on every network and saying more or less the same thing: President Obama has been lost for the last two years, seemingly unable to make good decisions or unwilling to fight hard for them. Not surprisingly, the response to Panetta’s message has broken along partisan lines in the media.

On MSNBC, Al Sharpton did an entire segment titled “Loyalties vs. Royalties” in which the content of Panetta’s statements were ignored in favor of the host and two guests carping about his decision to speak up at all. The situation wasn’t much better on CBS where CBS This Morning ran part of Charlie Rose’s interview with Panetta including this question, “There are those who say, you know, he appointed you to two of the highest positions that this country has to offer. Just wait until he’s out of office before criticizing.”

CNN’s Gloria Borger played a clip of VP Biden suggesting former administration officials shouldn’t be writing critical books and then asked, “Does he have a point there?” CNN also brought on former Deputy White House Press Secretary Bill Burton to call Panetta “dishonorable” as well as “sad and petty.”

Consider for a moment how odd this situation is. Members of the national media are, sometimes eagerly, suggesting Panetta is saying too much. They’re not questioning his truthfulness. No one has claimed Panetta is lying or even fudging facts. And still the media wonders if he should be talking at all. On MSNBC Dana Milbank made clear the result, “the effect here is to embolden Republicans right on the eve of the election.” And that’s bad of course. It doesn’t even require explanation on MSNBC and goes more or less unchallenged on CBS.

Things were different on Fox News where Bill O’Reilly came at Panetta from a very different perspective. Instead of accusing Panetta of saying too much, O’Reilly challenged him to say even more and say it more plainly. And that led to a series of exchanges in which Panetta felt forced to defend the President even as he continued to say that he’d made some significant mistakes. As a result the audience got to see the real edge of Panetta’s discontent.
