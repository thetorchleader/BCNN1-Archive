WATCH: In Denver, President Obama Highlights Economic Gains, ‘Fast Recovery’

Date not recorded in the original archive National

President Obama, speaking to a crowd of hand-picked guests in Denver Wednesday at Cheesman Park, touted gains made in the economy following the recession that challenged the start of his presidency.

“We have come farther and recovered faster than almost any other advanced country on Earth,” the president said.

The president also spoke of the Coloradans he met with Tuesday night at the Wazee Supper Club in Lodo, and reminisced about his first job selling ice cream for Baskin Robbins.

Alex Dooley, who had dinner with Obama Tuesday night, introduced the president to a couple hundred guests under a section of the park shaded by evergreens.

The president spoke of efforts to make lives better for the middle class.

Obama continued his populist message, telling guests: “I’m here for every American who works their tail off and does everything right and who believes in the American dream and asks for nothing but a decent chance in life for themselves and their families.”

He said: “I’m here to say I hear you. And sometimes it’s as simple as that — that I’m listening and paying attention. … I see myself in folks, because I remember my first minimum wage job — at Baskin Robbins by the way, wearing a cap and an apron — and how a little raise would have helped.

“I think about what it was like for me to finance college and child care costs. … Your stories are ours. You’re why I ran. What I want to make sure of is, as screwed up as Washington gets, that everyone here understands that there is progress to be made and that there’s someone fighting for them.”

In his roughly 25 minute speech Obama hit on equal pay, attracting more high tech jobs to the United States and making college more affordable. He attacked Republicans for not backing his proposals to help enhance the economy.
