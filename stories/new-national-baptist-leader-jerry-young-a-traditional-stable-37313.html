New National Baptist Leader Jerry Young — a ‘Traditional, Stable Choice’ — Admits Convention Needs Vast Reform, Must “Act Like a Denomination”

September 5, 2014 Christian

The National Baptist Convention’s president-elect, Jerry Young, sits with former president, William Shaw, before taking the podium on the final day of the annual meeting. (NOLA.com)

Jerry Young, a Jackson, Miss., pastor, has been elected the new president of the National Baptist Convention, the largest group of black Christians in the United States. The convention’s former vice president, Young emerged from a pack of five candidates as the most established, traditional choice, a stable rock through what might be a tumultuous five-year term as issues of race and faith have become the focus of national conversation.

Still, Young seeks to push the convention in new directions. He said he wants the convention to expand its profile, with its leaders becoming regular voices in the public. “The community needs to be able to hear from the faith community,” Young said in an interview Friday. “The church has got to be socially responsible. … The Bible says that Jesus went about doing good – preaching, teaching and doing good – to all the people, whether they were saints or sinners.”

The convention has emerged from its scandal of 15 years ago, when president Henry Lyons was found guilty in federal court of misappropriating $4 million in convention funds, including money donated by a Jewish organization to rebuild black churches that had been burned by arsonists in the South. Lyons’ successors worked to build back the convention’s reputation, but the membership – currently estimated at 7 million – has been stable rather than rising, convention spokesperson Morris Tipton said.

The next five years might define the convention’s ability to remain an active and relevant voice in American life, Tipton said. “The next president will go, I think, a long way in determining how galvanized the churches have become, as we seek to leverage the power and influence that the convention has,” he said. “We must have a greater voice in the nation and in international politics.”

Introducing Young to an audience at the Ernest N. Morial Convention Center, pastor John Payne Sr. of Mobile, Ala., alluded to the Lyons scandal as he delivered a sermon on the selflessness required of a leader. “A lesser man will use privileges to promote himself,” Payne said. “Jesus used his heavenly privileges for the sake of others, or for our sake.”

Tall and thin, Young stooped slightly over the convention’s microphone, looking like the image of a quiet scholar burdened by a public duty. In his first address since his election Thursday night, he spoke in slow and careful phrases, letting seconds of silence hang in the air, as though seeking to transform the echoing convention center hall into a somber, sacred space.

Young described how the convention’s existing administration might need vast reform. He said the convention should not first be focused on rabidly hungering for new members but rather needs to purge itself of those who have sought to join for reasons other than their faith in God. “I do not want anybody on the bus who just wants to get on the bus for what they can get out of riding on the bus,” Young said. “I’m trying to pull together the folk who we believe ought to be on the bus.”

In an interview, Young said he envisions the convention expanding its role and, in turn, how it is organized. For 134 years, the convention has stood as an affiliation of Baptist churches that do not work together full time but rather meet only several times per year. “It needs infrastructure. It doesn’t have the capacity to respond to various issues. … Got to become systematic, full time. … We’ve got to build capacity.” That capacity would transform the convention into its own religious denomination, Young explained.

“A lot of good things have happened because of our tradition,” Young said. “But the problem is, over our nearly 140-year period, no one has actually challenged us to act like a denomination.”
