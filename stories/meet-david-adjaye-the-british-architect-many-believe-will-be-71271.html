Meet David Adjaye: the British Architect Many Believe Will be Picked to Design President Obama’s Library and Museum

May 1, 2015 National

David Adjaye (2010 Rex Features)

When Barack Obama sits down to choose an architect to design his presidential library in Chicago, he will be picking from the planet’s greatest talents.

But denizens of the architecture world believe he may already have a favourite: the British architect David Adjaye.

The 48-year-old Mr Adjaye would be the first non-American to design a presidential library, the official museum-archives built to commemorate US leaders’ term in office.

Born in Tanzania, he rose to prominence in Britain in the 1990s by designing homes for celebrities like Ewan McGregor and Alexander McQueen.

He has since become one of the world’s major architects and broke through in the US by winning the contract to design the National Museum of African-American History and Culture, a museum of black history being built less than a mile from the White House.

Mr Obama spoke at the museum’s groundbreaking ceremony in 2012 and later that year invited Mr Adjaye to a dinner held in honour of David Cameron. The architect sat at the head table with the president and prime minister, joined by George Clooney and Warren Buffett, the billionaire American investor.

Although Mr Obama has nearly two years left in office, preparations are already underway to raise funds for the presidential library, which will reportedly be built in his home town of Chicago.

An announcement on the architect is expected to be the next step in the process and will happen before Mr Obama leaves the White House in January 2017, according to the library’s website.

“Everybody says it’s going to be David Adjaye,” Robert Stern, the architect behind George W Bush’s library in Texas, told Architect Magazine.

Witold Rybczynski, an architect who sat on the advisory committee for the Bush library, said that Mr Adjaye’s African roots made him a natural consideration for the first black president’s library.

“He’s a very good young architect with a high profile and African connections and is doing a high profile project in Washington,” Mr Rybczynski told The Telegraph.

Choosing a non-American might prove controversial, as could past comments Mr Adjaye has made about the US.
