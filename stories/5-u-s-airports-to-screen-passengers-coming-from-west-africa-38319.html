5 U.S. Airports to Screen Passengers Coming from West Africa for Ebola

October 9, 2014 National

A doctor of the national public health institute controls the temperature of a man at the airport, in the Ivory Coast on August 13, 2014, as part of protective measures against the Ebola virus
SIA KAMBOU/GETTY IMAGES

Travelers coming in from Liberia, Sierra Leone and Guinea will have their temperatures taken by federal officials before entering the country.Passengers landing in five U.S. airports who came from any one of the three West African nations most affected by the Ebola outbreak—Liberia, Sierra Leone and Guinea—will have their temperatures taken as a way to screen for the virus, the New York Times reports.

The screenings will begin as early as this weekend, and will take place in Kennedy International in New York City, Washington Dulles International in Dulles, Va., O’Hare International in Chicago, Hartsfield-Jackson International in Atlanta and Newark Liberty International in Newark, N.J. since approximately “90 percent of the people arriving from the three countries come through [those] five airports,” federal officials explained on Wednesday.

Affected travelers can look forward to detection procedures that “include taking the passengers’ temperatures with a gun-like, noncontact thermometer and requiring them to fill out a questionnaire after deplaning,” the Times reports.

Aside from sending healthcare and military personnel to West Africa, in addition to millions of dollars in aid, the airport screenings are widely seen as the US government’s first large-scale domestic attempt to keep the outbreak from wreaking havoc on US soil. Especially since the nation has been on edge when a Liberian man was diagnosed with Ebola in Texas last week. Thomas E. Duncan died on Wednesday.

“We work to continuously increase the safety of Americans,” said Dr. Thomas R. Frieden, the director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention in Atlanta.

Dr. Frieden went on to connect the dots between how America’s safety is tied to West Africa’s capacity to overcome the outbreak.
