NY Jets Quarterback Mark Sanchez Not Progressing, Tim Tebow Being Underused Says Former NFL Great Sterling Sharpe

November 16, 2012 Uncategorized

Green Bay Packers great says that he can’t see any development in Sanchez while attending Dr. Theodore A. Atlas Dinner in Staten Island, where Daily News columnist is honored.
A few former NFL heavyweights weighed in on the state of the New York football teams Thursday, with Sterling Sharpe questioning what the Jets “are good at,” and Giants icon Phil Simms not backing down from his remarks that Eli Manning is not an elite NFL quarterback.
“I said it last year at the Super Bowl. I go, ‘Where did this word elite come from? Why is that now the defining word for quarterbacks?’ ” Simms told the Daily News Thursday. “But it is what it is.”
Sharpe, the former Packers wide receiver, was even more pointed with his assessment of the free-falling Jets, saying he’s seen no progression from the starting quarterback, Mark Sanchez, since he led the team to consecutive AFC Championship Games his first two years in the league.
“I’m disappointed in (the Jets). I’m speaking as a fan and an analyst,” Sharpe said. “I don’t see growth from that team. I can’t say that the two AFC championship teams have gotten better, from that point to this point. Right now, I don’t see growth in Mark Sanchez. I don’t see a running game that’s better. I don’t know what they’re good at. I think they’re a team that’s searching for an identity.”
Sharpe, Simms, former Jets coach Eric Mangini and other sports luminaries were present for the 16th annual “Teddy Dinner” at the Hilton Garden Inn on Staten Island. The event raises money for the Dr. Theodore Atlas Foundation, a community-service organization founded by the boxing trainer and named for his father. A large part of this year’s fund-raiser will help victims of Hurricane Sandy. Daily News columnist Tim Smith was also honored at the dinner, receiving the 2012 Jack Newfield Award for a column he wrote this year profiling gyms in the five boroughs that Atlas helped keep open to steer inner-city kids away from violence.
“It was a complete surprise,” Smith said.
