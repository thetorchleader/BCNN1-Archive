Twitter Makes Changes to Attract New Users, but Seems to be Irritating the Old

August 19, 2014 Technology

Twitter headquarters

When starting a company, appealing to a narrow niche of passionate customers is a good strategy. As Y Combinator co-founder Paul Graham put it: “[Y]ou can either build something a large number of people want a small amount, or something a small number of people want a large amount. Choose the latter. Not all ideas of that type are good startup ideas, but nearly all good startup ideas are of that type.”

But eventually, unless what you’re selling is luxury or exclusivity, you’re going to want to broaden your market. And when that happens, you might find yourself with a problem like the one Twitter has now: The more devoted the core of fans you started out with, the harder they’ll buck when you have to change the thing they love so much.

They’re bucking now, as Twitter rolls out changes to the rules that govern what a user sees in his or her stream of updates, or timeline. In the last few days, the service has started showing some users tweets that users they follow might have received from accounts they follow — what you might call second-degree follows. It has also started notifying users in some instances when someone they follow marks a tweet as a “favorite.”

Whether you consider these changes major or minor probably comes down to whether you self-identify as a Twitter power user. To those who spend hours a day with the service and have done so for years, it’s nothing less than a fundamental rewiring of Twitter’s architecture. The Verge calls it “downright blasphemous“; the Wall Street Journal warns that it might be a desecration of “a sacred place.”

But if you’re not the sort of person who spends all day with Tweetdeck open, tending and pruning your follow list like a precious bonsai tree and keeping a weather eye out for every trending hashtag, the principle of only seeing tweets from people you follow seems less holy. To neophytes and part-timers, Twitter’s strict organization isn’t a spam filter; it’s a hurdle, and everything interesting is happening on the other side.
