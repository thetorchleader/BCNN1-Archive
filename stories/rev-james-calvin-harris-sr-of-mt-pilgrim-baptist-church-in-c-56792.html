Rev. James Calvin Harris Sr., of Mt. Pilgrim Baptist Church in Columbus, GA Nears End of his Term After Serving 64 Years as Pastor

February 14, 2015 Black

Mike Haskey mhaskey@ledger-enquirer.com James Harris has been the pastor of Mt. Pilgrim Baptist Church in Columbus for 64 years. 02/10/15 MIKE HASKEY — mhaskey@ledger-enquirer.com

When he began preaching, his first congregation consisted of farm animals.

“I would preach to the mules, really anything that had an ear,” said the Rev. James Calvin Harris Sr., recalling those days as a young boy.

Harris, who will turn 90 in April, is nearing the end of his term as senior pastor at Mount Pilgrim Baptist Church in Columbus, a position he took in November 1951.

“God is telling me it is time to move on,” he said.

A celebration will be held at the church in April.

“I never thought I would be any place this long, but I feel blessed to have done so,” he said.

Harris recently wrote an autobiography called “What God Can Do With Nothing,” which is available at the church on Old Cusseta Road and online at Amazon.com. The sixth of seven children, Harris was born in Leesburg, Ga., on the farm his father owned. His mother was a schoolteacher. His parents were very active in their church.

On the farm were cows, hogs, chickens and ducks. Cotton was the main crop. Wood was used in the house for heat and to cook. Water was drawn from a well. The family made its own soap from the fat of hogs.

“We walked to school,” he said.

He recalled that as a young boy, he got lonely, and that’s when he began to preach.

And it wasn’t only for animals. He started preaching for his grandparents and an aunt and was served teacakes as a reward.

Harris said when he was 14, he got his calling. It was then he heard a voice say to him three times, “Go preach my Gospel, saith the Lord.”
