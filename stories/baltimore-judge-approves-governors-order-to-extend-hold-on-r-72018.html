Baltimore Judge Approves Governor’s Order to Extend Hold on Riot Suspects

May 5, 2015 Black

© David Goldman/AP Photo Baltimore police arrest a man for violating a curfew, May 2, 2015, in Baltimore, Maryland.

A Baltimore Circuit Court judge ruled that Gov. Larry Hogan did not violate the Maryland constitution when he signed an executive order allowing people arrested during the riots to be held longer than 24 hours before seeing a court commissioner. 

Judge Charles Peters said the jail system was “simply overwhelmed by the large number of arrests that occurred,” about 200 that night alone.

“No one tried to hold them longer than they should’ve been held,” Peters said. “Very soon after this incident, the state resumed prompt presentments and released 100 individuals.”

Hogan took steps to keep people jailed for two days without charges — nearly twice as long as usually allowed — by request of the city, which said police officers were having difficulty preparing paperwork while maintaining a presence on the street.

Even after the delay, more than 100 people were released without charges after police were unable to sort out their cases. Police have said they will continue to investigate and could still bring charges.

Monday’s court ruling was prompted by petitions filed by three men arrested during the rioting. One of them, Keefee Washington, was Tasered after police said he was part of a group of men throwing rocks at officers.

His attorney, Deborah Levi, said that in her client’s case, police were not too overwhelmed to file paperwork — his arresting officers promptly filed charging paperwork at the jail when Washington was dropped off. But he sat for another 40 hours awaiting processing.

Levi said that at Central Booking, those arrested during the riots were held separate from other detainees, “crammed into cells” with overflowing toilets and without proper food or medical care.
