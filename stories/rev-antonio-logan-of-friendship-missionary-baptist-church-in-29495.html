Rev. Antonio Logan of Friendship Missionary Baptist Church in Hickory, NC Watch his Church and Community Become What It Was Meant to Be

Date not recorded in the original archive Black

Friendship Missionary Baptist Church’s the Rev. Antonio Logan shows off his birthstone ring that his mother gave him when he was 15 years old. July 1 was his 56th birthday. (ROBERT C. REED)

The Rev. Antonio Logan’s hands traced loops through the air as he talked. His right hand mimicked his left hand, speeding through the air, drawing invisible shapes on either side of his body.

His hands quickened with the increasing pace of his words.

Logan’s beard spread out in even paths along his jaw. A pair of thick-rimmed black glasses sat firmly on his nose even when he threw his head back to laugh.

Only a red flash from his right hand broke the symmetry of the preacher as he waved his arms. When his hands slowed and he interlocked his fingers on the desk in front of him, the red light settled into a worn, gold ring. The oval-shaped ruby is Logan’s birth stone, though he is quick to note the ruby is not real. His mother gave the ring to him when he turned 15.

Logan, now 56, is the pastor of Friendship Missionary Baptist Church. August will mark 11 years at the Hickory establishment.

The church, he said, has changed since he started preaching 30 years ago in Asheville. When he got to Hickory, he said people were just starting to lose their jobs. Others were picking up second jobs to try to make ends meet.

Trials are a season of life, he added, just like the good times are. That’s what Logan tells his congregation.
