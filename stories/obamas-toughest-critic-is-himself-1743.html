Obama’s Toughest Critic Is Himself

November 27, 2011 Uncategorized

President Barack Obama has plenty of critics — Republicans, liberals, the media — but the person hardest on the president, his administration and the Democratic Party is, at times, Barack Obama.
Obama has admitted that he’s “screwed up,” is “frustrated” and might only deserve one term in the White House. He’s acknowledged that he hasn’t fulfilled campaign promises and has admitted that the change he vowed to bring to Washington hasn’t arrived.
Whether the self-critiques are born of political savvy, humility or candor, they help establish the president as an honest, sympathetic figure heading into the 2012 election, a leader willing to acknowledge that the sky-high expectations for his 2008 hope-and-change campaign have fallen short in reality.
But his public soul-searching also highlights just how large those discrepancies are — and hands his eventual Republican opponent ready-made attack ads, in the president’s own words.
The Republican National Committee this month marked the start of the one-year countdown to the presidential election with a minute-long web video using Obama’s remarks. “We haven’t made enough progress on the economy,” he said in one clip. “We’ve lost our ambition, our imagination,” he said in another, adding, “We’ve gotten a little soft.”
RNC research director Joe Pounder said there is “no better critique out there” of Obama’s record than the one he himself provides, particularly on the economy. “In this election, Republicans are looking forward to using Barack Obama’s own words against him time and time again on issue after issue,” Pounder said.
Obama’s self-critiques began early in his term — a notable departure from his predecessor, George W. Bush, who wasn’t known for his introspection.
“I’m frustrated with myself, with our team. … I’m here on television saying I screwed up,” Obama said two weeks after taking office. “I’ve got to own up to my mistake,” he said of his decision to nominate Sen. Tom Daschle to his Cabinet despite his failure to pay taxes.
The president suggested in the same interview with NBC News that he might not be able to earn a second term in the White House if the economy did not improve. “A year from now, I think people are going to see that we’re starting to make some progress, but there is still going to be some pain out there. If I don’t have this done in three years, then there’s going to be a one-term proposition,” he said.
And, in the nearly three years since, the president hasn’t stopped acknowledging his mistakes and limitations.
