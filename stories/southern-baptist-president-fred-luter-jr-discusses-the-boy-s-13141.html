Southern Baptist President Fred Luter Jr. Discusses the Boy Scouts, Racial Diversity and His Goals for His New Term

June 23, 2013 Uncategorized

Earlier this month, the Rev. Fred J. Luter Jr. was given a standing ovation by the messengers of the Southern Baptist Convention as he was elected to a second term as president of the nation’s largest Protestant denomination.
Luter, 56, is a New Orleans native who started his ministry preaching on the streets to anyone who would listen. Today, he is senior pastor of Franklin Avenue Baptist Church in New Orleans, which has an estimated 7,000 members. He is also a husband and father, as well as an avid Saints and LSU fan.
Luter took a few minutes while in Theodore recently to answer a reporter’s questions on a few topics, including the Boy Scouts, racial diversity and his goals for his new term.

AL.com: Do you think that the issue of Boy Scouts and their membership policy to allow gay scouts will be a divisive issue for the denomination? Some churches, including yours, decided to separate from the organization, while others will stay connected.

Luter: I have no doubt that it will not be divisive, simply because of the fact that we have options here. There are some churches that will continue to be with Boy Scouts because they will separate themselves from, maybe, the leadership of the Boy Scout organization.
I don’t see it being divisive at all. When the resolution was voted upon, we were 99.9 percent in agreement. So I know we do a lot of infighting in our convention, but this is one issue I promise there will be no divisiveness about.

AL.com: What reaction have you received since stating that Franklin Avenue Baptist would pull out of Scouting?

Luter: From the convention side, the reaction was very positive. We knew before we went to the convention that the Boy Scout issue was going to be something that we would want to talk about. We knew that there would be some type of resolution or motion because so many Southern Baptist churches are involved in the Boy Scout troops.
A lot of us are extremely disappointed in the position that the leadership of the Boy Scouts took. Many of us, like myself, grew up as Boy Scouts, so it was a passion for us. To see now the position that they’re taking, it really surprised and really hurt many of us.
