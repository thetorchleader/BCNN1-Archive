Man Charged With First-Degree Attempted Murder in Shooting of NYC Police Officer

May 4, 2015 National

Demetrius Blackwell, who is charged with attempted murder in the shooting on Saturday of a New York City police officer, appears on Sunday for his arraignment in Queens County Criminal Court. His lawyer said he denied the claims. (PHOTO CREDIT: Theodore Parisienne/PRESS POOL)

A repeat felon accused of shooting at two undercover police officers Saturday was arraigned Sunday and formally charged with two counts of first-degree attempted murder on a police officer.

The arraignment came as new details emerged about a brash shooting that left a New York City police officer in a medically induced coma Sunday, fighting for his life.

The suspect, Demetrius Blackwell, 35 years old, was also charged with one count of aggravated assault on a police officer, two counts of second-degree criminal possession of a weapon and one count of first-degree assault, according to Queens District Attorney Richard A. Brown.

In a Queens courtroom Sunday, Mr. Blackwell, who is being held without bail, appeared wearing a torn paper jumpsuit and entered no plea. If convicted, he faces up to 25 years to life in prison. He is due back in court Friday.

“He’s only accused. He’s presumed innocent. He denied the claims,” said Mr. Blackwell’s lawyer, David Bart. “Everyone should give me a little time to develop the case.”

Meanwhile, plainclothes officer Brian Moore, 25, who was shot through the window of his unmarked police car, remained in critical condition at Jamaica Hospital on Sunday night. Officials say Officer Moore was shot in the face; the other officer, Erik Jansen, 30, was uninjured.

Officer Moore is the fifth New York City Police officer to be shot since December.

The incident unfolded just before sunset Saturday when Officers Moore and Jansen noticed that the alleged gunman, Mr. Blackwell, was grabbing the waistband of his pants, according to the criminal complaint.

According to a law-enforcement official, Officer Moore slowed his unmarked police car alongside Mr. Blackwell, identified himself as a police officer and then asked, “Do you have something in your waistband?”

Mr. Blackwell responded, “Yeah, I got something,” and then removed a gun from his waistband and fired several shots at the officers, the official said.

Mr. Blackwell was arrested less than two hours after the shooting and just steps from the crime scene at 104th Road and 212th Street in the Queens Village section of south Queens. Neighbors said Mr. Blackwell was relaxed and smoking cigarettes as he watched the manhunt unfold Saturday night with them on the street in the quiet, residential neighborhood.
