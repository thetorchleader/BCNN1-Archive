Jesse Jackson Tells Alabama State University Students to Go ‘Beyond the Bridge’

February 5, 2015 Black

As the 50th anniversary of the Selma-to-Montgomery march approaches, civil rights icon Rev. Jesse Jackson was in Montgomery on Friday — speaking to a gathering of Alabama State University students and meeting with the Montgomery Advertiser’s editorial board — to emphasize that much of the work accomplished by that famed march has been undone.

“The one thing that must be noted is that the Voting Rights Act of Aug. 6, 1965, and the Voting Rights Act June 25, 2013, are not the same,” Jackson said, referring to the Supreme Court’s decision to strip some of the protections from the act.

“We’ve seen hostile acts against (the right to vote),” Jackson continued. “Voter ID laws have hurt. They’ve removed voting machines from college campuses. Shortened the time to vote. Those who sought to deny the right to vote never quit trying.”

For that reason, Jackson and his Rainbow/PUSH Coalition are pushing a constitutional amendment that ensures the right to vote. Last week, Rep. Mark Pocan, D-Wis., and Rep. Keith Ellison, D-Minn., introduced the Right to Vote Amendment, which would ensure the right to vote and protect it.
