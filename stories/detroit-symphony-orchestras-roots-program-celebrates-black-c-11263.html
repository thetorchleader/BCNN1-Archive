Detroit Symphony Orchestra’s ‘Roots’ Program Celebrates Black Contributions to Music

March 7, 2013 Uncategorized

African-American musicianship will be on rich display Friday and Saturday as the Detroit Symphony Orchestra kicks off its 35th annual Classical Roots program with a world premiere and performances by one of the opera world’s most-celebrated bass-baritones.
Pictured: Classical Roots benefits DSO programs such as the African-American Orchestral Fellowship. (Detroit Symphony Orchestra photos)
Classical Roots is a black-tie concert and fundraiser that honors the contributions African-Americans have made to classical music. Last year’s gala raised $175,000. Proceeds help underwrite DSO programs such as the African-American Orchestral Fellowship, which pairs young professional musicians with DSO mentors. The gala kicks off Saturday with a reception and strolling dinner. Dessert and dancing will take place in the black-tie Afterglow following the concert.
Music director Leonard Slatkin will conduct the world premiere of “Sukkot through Orion’s Nebula” by celebrated composer James Lee III. A University of Michigan graduate, Lee is director of musical studies at Baltimore’s Morgan State University.
This will not be the first Lee premiere Slatkin introduces to the world. In 2006, the National Symphony Orchestra under Slatkin’s direction premiered Lee’s “Beyond Rivers of Vision.” The last Lee work the DSO performed was “A Different Soldier’s Tale” in 2010.
Also this weekend, a pair of classic Wagnerian solos will showcase the talents of operatic bass-baritone Eric Owens, who will sing Wotan’s “Farewell” from “Die Walkure” and “Die Frist Ist Um” from “The Flying Dutchman.”
Originally, James DePriest, the former musical director of the Oregon Symphony who died Feb. 8, was to conduct this year’s Classical Roots concerts. DePriest was one of the first black men to achieve international renown as a conductor and led the DSO on 12 occasions, the last in 1997.
His loss not only was a blow to the Classical Roots program, but to individuals at the DSO who knew him well.
“The last time we saw each other was on the streets of New York,” says DSO president and CEO Anne Parsons, describing a chance meeting with DePriest several years ago. “He asked, ‘Why don’t you ever have me back to Detroit?’ So we had a lovely chat about getting him back. It’s very sad he wasn’t able to return.”
