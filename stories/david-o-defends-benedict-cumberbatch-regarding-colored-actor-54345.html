David ‘O’ Defends Benedict Cumberbatch Regarding “Colored” Actors Comment

January 30, 2015 Entertainment

Actors David Oyelowo, Benedict Cumberbatch, and Jessica Oyelowo attend 20th Annual Screen Actors Guild Awards at The Shrine Auditorium on January 18, 2014 in Los Angeles, California. (Photo by Dimitrios Kambouris/WireImage)

David Oyelowo, who played Martin Luther King Jr. in the Oscar-nominated film “Selma,” is defending British actor Benedict Cumberbatch after he used the word “colored” to describe black actors.

“We use the term ‘people of color’ in America. Is that immensely different to what he said? I don’t think so, just focus on what he was trying to say,” Mr. Oyelowo said at the “Selma” premiere in London on Tuesday, The Associated Press reported.

Mr. Cumberbatch came under fire last week after arguing on “The Tavis Smiley Show” that “as far as colored actors go,” there were more opportunities in the U.S. than Britain, “and that’s something that needs to change.” He later apologized for his “thoughtless use of inappropriate language,” AP reported.

Mr. Oyelowo lamented that things have “definitely gone too far in terms of political correctness.”

“(Mr. Cumberbatch) was actually being very supportive of the likes of me, and other black actors and actresses,” he said. “We do have greater opportunities in the U.S. in relation to the U.K.”

“Selma,” was nominated for best picture and best song at the Academy Awards, but questions of racism have been raised about the snub for best director and best actor.
