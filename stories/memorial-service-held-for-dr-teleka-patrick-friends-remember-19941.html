Memorial Service Held for Dr. Teleka Patrick; Friends Remember ‘Her Commitment to a Love for Jesus and Prayer’

April 28, 2014 Christian

Even though Dr. Teleka Patrick called Kalamazoo home for only a short time, the impact that she and her story had on community members was evident Thursday evening during a memorial service to celebrate her life.

While there will likely always be questions surrounding Patrick’s death, the service held at the Trinity Temple Seventh-day Adventist Church in Kalamazoo Thursday aimed to grant closure to the more than 50 people in attendance.

“It was a good opportunity to draw closure,” said family friend David Ruff following the nearly 90 minute service. “We’ll miss her terribly, but I guess we can draw peace from being together as a family and keeping her memory because it’s what we have left.”

The service at Trinity Temple comes nearly three weeks after Patrick’s body was found April 6 by a fisherman in Lake Charles in Porter, Ind. The lake is just north of where police found Patrick’s gold 1997 Lexus ES300 abandoned on Dec. 5 in a ditch off westbound I-94.

Two separate autopsies concluded Patrick died as a result of asphyxiation from drowning. Kalamazoo County Sheriff Richard Fuller has previously said there are no indications of foul play and the death is believed to have been accidental, although an official cause of death won’t be determined until a toxicology report is released.

Patrick, 30, a first-year medical resident with the Western Michigan University Homer Stryker M.D. School of Medicine, was last seen on the night of Dec. 5 after working a shift at Borgess Medical Center.

Police have said they believe Patrick was heading to St. Louis to meet a friend that night when her car got a flat tire on I-94 in Porter, Ind. and went into a ditch.

Dr. Robert Strung, chair and director of the Western Michigan University Medical Psychiatric Department, said during the service that although he didn’t know Patrick well, he knew her to be a bright, motivated hard worker who excelled at a level far above what would be expected of a typical first year medical resident.

“We saw her as an excellent doctor and a very good person,” Strung said, adding that he hopes the discovery of Patrick’s body has brought closure to her family. “We will all move on with our lives and careers, but we will never forget Dr. Teleka Patrick.”

Others in attendance spoke about Patrick’s unwavering Christian faith.

Despite being a member at Trinity Temple, Charles LeBlanc said he was amazed when he met Patrick and discovered she would often attend all three of Kalamazoo’s Seventh-day Adventist churches. He said he was particularly impressed that she would even attend prayer meetings, especially since young people don’t often come.

“I’m inspired by her academic achievement,” LeBlanc said. “I’m inspired by how she impacted the society, but most of all, I was inspired by her commitment to a love for Jesus and prayer.
