Many Christian Mothers Angry Over Ruling on Morning-After Pill

April 10, 2013 Uncategorized

Terri Westwood, a 37-year-old mother of three, is raising two teen daughters in Savanna, GA. She stresses sexual purity and the hardship of teen pregnancy, but is disturbed by the federal court ruling making the morning-after pill available to girls of all ages–no questions asked.
Thursday, U.S. District Court Judge Edward Korman made a shocking move, overturning the 2011 decision by Health and Human Services Secretary Kathleen Sebelius, requiring girls under 17 to obtain a prescription for the Plan B One-Step contraceptive or any of its equivalents.
Korman’s counter-ruling requires the FDA to make levonorgestrel-based contraceptives available over the counter to women of all ages, and without a prescription.
“It teaches my girls that they can have sex, unprotected, and then just ‘get rid’ of a baby with no questions asked,” Terri told EEW Magazine editors in a written statement.
She’s not alone in her negative sentiments, as many Christian mothers are still reeling from the idea that the morning-after pill could be sold to girls as young as age 11.
 “It encourages a callous attitude toward abortion,” argues Terri, “and it also leads to reckless sexual behavior. This is just a bad idea.”
