Alex Rodriguez Hits 659th Homer, Moves Within 1 of Willie Mays

April 27, 2015 Sports

Yankees designated hitter Alex Rodriguez hits his 659th home run.
(Photo: Brad Penner, USA TODAY Sports)

Alex Rodriguez doesn’t know how the Yankees plan to celebrate his 660th career home run, or if they plan to at all.

“I don’t have a marketing degree,” Rodriguez said jokingly. “I’m just focused on playing baseball.”

The Yankees’ star-crossed slugger now stands just one home run away from finding out. Rodriguez belted No. 659 of his career and finished with three RBI as the Yankees won the rubber game of their Subway Series with the mistake-prone Mets, 6-4, Sunday night at Yankee Stadium.

Rodriguez hit a solo shot with two outs in the first that bounced off the top of the wall in right-center and into the home bullpen. He added an RBI double during a four-run second and brought home Brett Gardner on a fielder’s choice in the fifth.

“We all know he can hit. He’s always been a good hitter. We’re just happy he’s healthy and swinging the bat the way he is,” said Andrew Miller, who pitched the ninth for his seventh save in as many chances. “You’d have to go ask each person how surprised they are. I’ve been on the other side of it long enough to know that he can hit a baseball. Fortunately, he’s on our side now.”

Rodriguez’s home run answered a two-run Mets first off starter Nathan Eovaldi, who was hit hard early and allowed four runs on seven hits before manager Joe Giardi lifted him with one out in the fifth. Chasen Shreve recorded the final two outs to earn his first career win and start a parade of relievers. All were brilliant, holding the Mets hitless over the final 4 2/3 innings.

The Yankees chased Jon Niese after five innings. The Yankees had five doubles, including four during their second-inning rally, and took advantage of four Mets errors.

“The bullpen was awesome. Everybody was awesome,” Eovaldi said. “They kept us in the game. We were scoring runs early. It was frustrating that we had to go to the bullpen early and I wasn’t able to pitch deeper into the game.”

But Eovaldi’s struggles were quickly overshadowed by the work of his five relievers — highlighted by a three-strikeout eighth for Dellin Betances — and the impending Rodriguez milestone. He moved within one of Willie Mays for fourth on baseball’s all-time list and within one of triggering a $6 million milestone clause in his contract.

“It’s pretty amazing when you think about it,” Girardi said. “Six hundred sixty home runs — that’s a lot of home runs, man.”

Sunday’s homer ended a seven-game drought for Rodriguez, who last went deep (twice) on April 17 in Tampa. He also recorded the 523rd double of his career, with two outs in the second, a hit that matched Mays for 43rd on baseball’s all-time list.

“I’ve said it a lot. Willie was my father’s favorite player. I just remember hearing about Willie Mays and Mickey Mantle all the time,” Rodriguez said. “He’s one of my heroes. I’m excited about the win today. I’m excited about the home run. And I’m having fun.”

On the heels of serving a one-year suspension for using performance-enhancing drugs, he remains uncertain how fans or his team will view homer No. 660, which could come as soon as Monday night, when the Yankees open a three-game series with the Rays in the Bronx.

“No idea,” Rodriguez said. “I am just so grateful to be back playing baseball and so happy to be back in Yankee Stadium and in New York.”
