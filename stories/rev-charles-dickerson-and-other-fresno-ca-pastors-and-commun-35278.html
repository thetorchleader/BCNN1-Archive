Rev. Charles Dickerson and Other Fresno, CA Pastors and Communnity Leaders Discuss Ferguson and Improving Cooperation between the Public and Police

August 24, 2014 Black

Rev. Charles Dickerson

United by a desire to see community policing improved, community members and pastors gathered Saturday evening in southwest Fresno to talk about recent events in Ferguson, Mo. 

Organized by the Baptist Pastors Conference at the Corinth Baptist Church, the open forum discussed ways that the relationship between police and the public can be improved, especially when it comes to racial profiling.

Calling Ferguson a “template for a nationwide crisis,” the Rev. Charles Dickerson, the conference president and bishop of ANEW Pearly Grove Baptist Church, said outrage from the recent events should be harnessed for positive discussion. Riots erupted in Ferguson after a white officer fatally shot an unarmed black teenager.

Discussion between community leaders and the Fresno Police Department already are underway, such as the periodic meetings between the coalition Faith in Community and Police Chief Jerry Dyer and his deputies.

But Booker T. Lewis II, a board member of Faith in Community and pastor of Rising Star Missionary Baptist, said there is a distance between those in high-level positions and the average patrolman.

“In the conversation that we have with Chief Dyer and his deputy chiefs, it can have all the good intentions of the world, but our police force here is so big that there’s a huge gap between them and the patrolman on the street,” Lewis said.

Lewis, one of several Fresno clergyman to visit Ferguson on Wednesday to participate in protest and pray, said there should be an improvement in police training and cultural sensitivity.

“They need training not only about how to handle their weapons and how to handle confrontation; they also need training on cultures,” Lewis said.
