Poll: Most Rich Americans Say U.S. Still In Recession

March 11, 2012 Uncategorized

Wealthier Americans aren’t very optimistic about the economic recovery, with a surprising 63% saying the United States is still in a recession, according to a new poll.
Some 55% think the economy won’t fully recover until 2013 or later, and 14% say the recession will never end.
The results come from the January 2012 survey by Ipsos Mendelsohn Affluent Barometer, which studies the lifestyles, spending patterns and media habits of today’s affluent Americans. Ipsos defines affluent adults as those who have an annual household income of $100,000 or more.
The overall outlook about the economy is split almost evenly. Forty-one% of affluent Americans are pessimistic about the U.S. economy and 38% are optimistic, a statistical tie. However,things could be worse. This level of optimism is up from the lows of 2011’s fourth quarter.
Survey respondents were also asked exclusive CNBC questions about private equity firms, which have been put into the spotlight during the U.S. presidential campaign because of Mitt Romney, who once ran Bain Capital.
Only 34% of the affluent are “very” or “somewhat” familiar with private equity firms; of those, 46% believe private equity firms affect the U.S. economy positively vs. 19% negatively.
The breakdown of those who look at private equity firms favorably was significantly split on political party lines; 62% self-identified as Republicans and 27% as Democrats.
