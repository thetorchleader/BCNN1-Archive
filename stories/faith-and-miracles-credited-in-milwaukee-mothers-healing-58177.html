Faith and Miracles Credited in Milwaukee Mother’s Healing

February 23, 2015 Black

Rhonda Hill (left) of Milwaukee, a patient in the ICU of Aurora St. Luke’s Medical Center, is visited last week by her grandmother, Shirley Stewart of Milwaukee.

For 14 weeks last fall, Rhonda Hill and a group of women dug deep into the Book of Acts, Luke’s biblical account of the planting of the early church and of the great suffering — beatings, imprisonment, death — endured by many who spread the Gospel of Jesus Christ.

For a woman like Hill — deeply religious, a believer in the power of the Holy Spirit to transform lives — the Bible holds great meaning. And looking back, she sees those discussions as a preparation for this time.

“I didn’t know that my suffering was going to take this shape,” Hill said as she lay in a dimly lighted room in the intensive care unit at Milwaukee’s Aurora St. Luke’s Medical Center.

“I think about the promises of those words, of the message of Paul to his people,” she said, her voice quiet, her round eyes closing from time to time.

“Those disciples were glad to suffer,” Hill said. “But I’m not there yet.”

Hill, 35, has been at St. Luke’s since Feb. 5 when, for reasons still not clear, she began bleeding in her brain. The mother of a young son, Hill, who serves as director of discipleship for the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America’s Greater Milwaukee Synod, had gone to work that day with a raging headache.

When she began to vomit and collapsed into a chair, her colleagues insisted on taking her to the emergency room.

It was the first of many miracles, Hill, her friends and her family say. They see the hand of God — alongside those of her physicians — in every positive development, every piece of good news. Had they taken her home, as Hill had insisted, she could have lapsed into a coma, doctors told her. She could have had a stroke, or bled to death.

“One of the doctors came in here and told her she had a miracle,” said Shirley Stewart, Hill’s 73-year-old grandmother, who had been holding vigil in her room around the clock for days.

“God did that,” said Stewart, who rushed home from preaching The Word on a mission trip in Mississippi to be with her granddaughter.

“I told him, I’m down here doing your work, you gotta heal my grandchild,” said Stewart, who attendsTabernacle of Joy, a pentecostal church on the north side.

“It makes you feel good to see God deliver his people,” said Stewart. She exudes an unwavering and joyful faith.

“I just love the Lord.”
