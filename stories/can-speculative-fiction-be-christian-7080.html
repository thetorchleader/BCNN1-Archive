Can “Speculative Fiction” be ‘Christian’?

July 16, 2012 Uncategorized

BOOK REVIEW: “Mortal” by Ted Dekker and Tosca Lee, FaithWords, 418 pages, $24.99
Lincoln (Nebraska) author, consultant and former Mrs. Nebraska Tosca Lee has co-written “Mortal,” the second book in a trilogy entitled “The Books of Mortals,” with best-selling author Ted Dekker.
The first book in the trilogy, “Forbidden,” was a best-seller in October 2011. And “Mortal” was on the The New York Times list of best-selling hardcover fiction after it came out last month.
So well have these works been received that the publication date of the third work, “Sovereign,” has been moved up to June of 2013.
When Lee and Dekker began their multicity personal kickoff tour to promote “Mortal” in Lincoln at the SouthPointe Barnes & Noble in early June, at least 90 people queued up with copies of the book for autographs and a chance for a word with the authors.
These works are considered “Christian speculative fiction,” a niche category that both authors seem to think fits them too tightly. Interviewed by Jeff Gerke, an independent publisher of such works, and asked what made her want to write Christian speculative fiction, Lee answered, “I didn’t. I really didn’t.”
She demurs that, inspired early in her literary development by Marion Zimmer Bradley’s “The Mists of Avalon,” she was just writing fantasy when, during a drive along a Nebraska road, her mind wandered. She conceived the idea of a fantasy mixed with realism in which a serious demon — not one who just tempts people to smoke and steals small change — speaks. And her first published work “Demon: A Memoir” was born.
For his part, Dekker, who has cited Stephen King’s “The Stand” as an inspiring novel in his life, told the same interviewer, “Well, I’m not sure what I write is Christian speculative fiction.”
As reviewer, I have detoured to this discussion of genre because after making my way through “Mortals” — with its foreheads split open by axes, bodies cleft in twain and armies of robotlike clones sent to eradicate noncompliant nomadic tribes, with blood on every page — I had to put the book down and ask, “What would a Methodist do? Where is the Christian in this?”
