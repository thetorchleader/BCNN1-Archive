Growth of Christianity in Africa Can Be Found In Texas

January 4, 2012 Uncategorized

Nowhere is the explosive growth of Christianity in Africa more evident than right here in West Texas.
A new report from the Pew Forum on Religion & Public Life titled, “Global Christianity,” shows that the largest growth in Christianity during the past 100 years has occurred in sub-Saharan African countries such as the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Ethiopia, and Nigeria.
The report showed that in 1910, 1.4 percent of the world’s Christians lived in sub-Saharan Africa. By 2010, the year the survey data was gathered, that number had grown to 24 percent.
The result is that now, rather than Christianity being taken into African nations by missionaries, African converts are spreading the message to other parts of the world.
“It’s now being spread out of Africa,” said Larry Henderson, who teaches in the Halbert Institute for Missions at Abilene Christian University.
Henderson said that in Europe, many of the larger churches are filled with African Christians who have fled their homeland because of persecution.
In Abilene, a number of churches have embraced refugees relocated from African countries through the International Rescue Committee. South Side Baptist Church, for example, houses a thriving African refugee church called The International Evangelical Church.
And, Trinity Baptist Church hosts New Song Ministry, comprised primarily of Christian African refugees.
In the Catholic Diocese of San Angelo, which includes the Abilene area, nine priests are from African nations, said Bishop Michael Pfeifer. An abundance of African Catholic priests has caused them to look for positions elsewhere, and Pfeifer is happy to have them in his sprawling diocese with numerous pastorates to fill.
Pfeifer said that years ago before becoming bishop of the San Angelo Diocese, he went to Africa as a member of his religious community, the Missionary Oblates of Mary Immaculate (OMI). The priests opened a mission in Zambia which has grown “by leaps and bounds,” he said, as has Christianity over much of Africa.
