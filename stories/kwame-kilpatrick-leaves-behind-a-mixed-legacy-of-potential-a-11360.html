Kwame Kilpatrick Leaves Behind a Mixed Legacy of Potential and Purpose Sidelined by Pride and Power

March 12, 2013 Uncategorized

At his first inaugural — a gala of tears, laughter and babies crawling on the stage — the new mayor referred repeatedly to a fresh new entity, the Kilpatrick administration.

Ten years later, a jury has convicted the former mayor of running “The Kilpatrick Enterprise” — a criminal organization that grazed on taxpayer and contractor dollars as if the mayor were a king, and the city’s contracts, his rightful tribute.
His first term began with a surge of hope and energy and quickly devolved into disappointment and public outrage that was still unwinding, a decade later in this five-month trial.
Dramatic, charismatic and seemingly immune to guilt, Kilpatrick counted some of Detroit’s most powerful business leaders among his allies, at least in part because of his apparent gifts as a dealmaker and leader. But he would lose them all.
In his closing statement, Kilpatrick’s court-appointed lawyer, James C. Thomas, told the jury that Kilpatrick had been “demonized” by the media — an argument that the former mayor’s apparatchiks have repeatedly advanced over the years.
But he’s never been a demon so much as a charming man determined to be less than his best self: the prized son who embarrasses his mother, the husband who cheats on his wife and children, the mayor who betrays his city. “I thought he could be a good leader,” said one of the jurors, a Detroit resident who said she voted for him twice, only to be sickened by some of what she heard at trial.
The disappointment she described was shared by many of those who hoped, at the outset, he would deliver greatness rather than great disappointment.
Over a decade of economic collapse in Detroit and Michigan, he became symbol of hope undermined, of public promises betrayed by private greed, impulse and desire.
