Graduates Who Have Beat the Odds

May 30, 2015 Black, Education

Every year, millions of students don their caps and gowns and cross the stage, putting them on a path to college, a career and young adulthood. For some, it’s the completion of a remarkable journey. They have beaten the odds and overcome incredible barriers including poverty, homelessness, gang violence, unstable home lives, the loss of loved ones or life-threatening medical conditions. The academic accomplishment of these students is the main reason the United States is now celebrating an unprecedented 81 percent high school graduation rate.

In celebration of their achievements, The Root is partnering with Communities in Schools, the nation’s largest dropout-prevention organization, which supported these striving scholars, athletes, artists and citizens to change the picture of education in our country. (We’re using first names only.)

Financial hardships and the loss of her beloved grandmother threatened to keep Maci, of Leavenworth, Kan., from finishing high school. A school program that provided meals to her family and emotional counseling kept Maci focused on school and gave her the confidence to enter local beauty pageants. Maci was recently named Miss Meadowlark and will compete in the Kansas State Pageant for a chance to advance to the Miss America contest.

Communities in Schools of Mid-America

Raised by her elderly grandmother in Charlotte, N.C., Georgina (center) was used to helping out at home. But she still found time to give back to her school and community through service projects, volunteering with local charities and leading student organizations. She graduated with honors and has been accepted to Hampton University where she will study communications this fall.

Communities in Schools of Charlotte-Mecklenburg

Nick is a young man who was referred to Communities in Schools for basic-needs support, but was very reluctant to accept help due to his lack of trust in outsiders. With time and consistency, the site coordinator at his school in Washington, D.C., was able to build a close relationship with Nick and his family. Nick is interested in playing football in college, and will attend Cheyney University of Pennsylvania.

Communities in Schools of the Nation’s Capital

When David’s mother suffered a heart attack last year and began to have to use a wheelchair, the Washington, D.C., native quickly fell behind in his course work. With intervention from his Communities in School counselor, David caught up and made grades worthy of the honor roll. He made his mother proud when he accepted his diploma this spring. He is headed to the University of the District of Columbia for two years and then plans to move on to additional college or a career.

Communities in Schools of the Nation’s Capital

Malik’s story may have turned out very differently if he had stayed in a traditional school. A special education student with attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, the Atlanta resident found it difficult to learn in a traditional classroom setting. His placement in a special performance learning center—which meets the needs of at-risk students—allowed him to flourish and become a model student. Malik will enroll at Tuskegee University this fall and hopes to play basketball while studying for his degree.
