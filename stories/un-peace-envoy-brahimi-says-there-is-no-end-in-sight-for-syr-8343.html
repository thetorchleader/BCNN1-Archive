UN Peace Envoy Brahimi says There Is No End in Sight for Syria Conflict

September 25, 2012 Uncategorized

There is no end in sight for the war in Syria, the peace envoy Lakhdar Brahimi has warned, as Save the Children said children are being “badly traumatised” after witnessing killings and torture.

The UN-Arab League envoy said “there is no prospect for today or tomorrow to move forward,” in remarks to reporters after briefing the UN Security Council on his talks in mid-September with President Bashar al-Assad.
Reporting on his first visit to Syria since assuming his post, Brahimi said Assad “knows something must change” but he only wants a return to “the old Syria” which he and his father have ruled for more than 40 years.
Mr Brahimi painted a grim picture of the 18-month conflict, reporting on food shortages, the “medieval” torture of detainees, and damage to all but 200 of Syria’s 2,200 schools.
The veteran troubleshooter, who took over as envoy from former UN secretary general Kofi Annan on September 1, also appealed to the divided 15-nation Security Council for united backing for his efforts.
The Syrian war has divided the Security Council, where Russia and China have already wielded their veto powers three times to resist international action demanded by Western and many Arab states.
President Barack Obama was to lead Western demands for action on Syria at the start of the UN General Assembly on Tuesday.
Mr Obama will be one of the opening speakers at the annual meeting of world leaders where the Syria conflict, mounting fears of a military strike on Iran and anti-West protests in Muslim nations are set to dominate.
