Smokestacks at Reds Stadium Catches Fire During Game Against Giants

May 16, 2015 National

A smokestack catches fire at Great American Ball Park. (PHOTO CREDIT: David Kohl, USA TODAY Sports)

Smokestacks are supposed to smoke, but at Great American Ball Park they’re only supposed to smoke on command.

The smoke that came from the right hand smokestack in the Reds’ game against the San Francisco Giants was the real deal — a fire began and Cincinnati fire officials responded to the ball park to extinguish it. No one was hurt.

The fire was extinguished at about 9:10 p.m. CDT, approximately 20 minutes after officials received the call to respond to the fire. Cincinnati fire crews raised ladders to the top of the structure and extinguished the blaze after several minutes. The fire began around the sixth inning with the Reds trailing the Giants 6-2.

The twin smokestacks usually shoot flames when a Reds pitcher strikes out an opposing batter, and they also pop fireworks when a Red hits a home run.

Friday, the smokestacks started blazing at the wrong time. Giants batter Casey McGehee temporarily stepped out of the box when the first flames appeared, then stepped back in.

Fireworks then popped out, again at the wrong time. Then, full-fledged flames again burst out of the right smokestack, the right being as viewed from behind home plate.

Smoke was emitting from the right hand smokestack persistently. Just before 9 p.m. fire officials climbed ladders to the top of the stack, and fought what appeared to be a small fire.

Spectators were temporarily cleared from the seats in the immediate area, then were permitted to return to their seats after the fire was put out.

Tim O’Connell, Reds vice president of ballpark operations, met with reporters in the Crosley Room behind the Great American Press Box in the eighth inning and told what he knew.

“In those tubes to get the flames up for the strikeouts, there are tubes fed by propane. One of the valves apparently failed on it. Quite frankly, the actions of the attendant really saved us from a disaster. He did a great job,” O’Connell said. “He worked very diligently to take the precautionary measures. All of his training and all the precautions we have in place, worked.”
