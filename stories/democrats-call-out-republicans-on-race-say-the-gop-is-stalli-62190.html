Democrats Call Out Republicans on Race; say the GOP Is Stalling on Loretta Lynch’s Confirmation because They Don’t Want Another Black Attorney General

March 17, 2015 Black

Democrats dropped the race card Tuesday in the fight over the delayed confirmation of attorney general nominee Loretta Lynch, who would be the first black woman to head the Justice Department.

“Race certainly can be considered a major factor in the delay,” Congressional Black Caucus Chairman Rep. G.K. Butterfield (D-N.C.), told reporters on a press call, organized as part of a push by Democrats to ramp up pressure on Republicans to allow a vote to confirm Lynch, a Brooklyn-based U.S. Attorney.

“She is as clean and pure as the driven snow,” Rep. Charles Rangel (D-Harlem) said in an interview. “But they [Republicans] just can’t stand another black as attorney general.”

“They look at her and they look at (President) Obama and they say, ‘Here we go again,'” Rangel said.

Senate Majority Leader Mitch McConnell (R-Ky.) said Sunday that he will not allow a vote on confirming Lynch until lawmakers the Senate finishes work on a bill to combat human trafficking. The measure is stalled due to Democratic objections to language restricting public funding for abortion that was included in the bill.

Obama nominated Lynch on Nov. 8. He agreed to McConnell’s request that the Senate consider her nomination after the GOP took over Senate control in January. Lynch has now waited longer for a confirmation vote than any nominee for the job in more than 30 years.

Republicans said Democrats are raising the issue of race in a disingenuous effort to rile up liberal voters.

One senior GOP aide called the effort “despicable.”

The only reason Lynch’s confirmation is held up “is because Democrats are filibustering a bill to prevent kids from being sold into slavery,” said Don Stewart, a McConnell spokesman.
