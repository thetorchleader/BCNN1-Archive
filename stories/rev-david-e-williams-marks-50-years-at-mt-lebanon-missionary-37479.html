Rev. David E. Williams Marks 50 Years at Mt. Lebanon Missionary Baptist Church in Columbia, SC

September 7, 2014 Black

Historic Mt. Lebanon Church, built in 1885, stands at the corner of East Eighth and Glade Street. (Courtesy photo)

We are known for what we do. We are identified not so much for what we say, but rather for the activities in which we engage.

How we spend our lives is how we will be remembered. The Rev. David E. Williams will forever be known as a man of God.

He has spent over 50 years as the pastor at Mt. Lebanon Missionary Baptist Church. It is the remarkable career of a remarkable man, as part of a remarkable congregation.

Fifty years! Imagine keeping an entire congregation in a condition of relative happiness for 50 years. There have been very few pastors who could ever do it. David Williams has, and it is a real achievement. But now it is time to rest. The Rev. Williams is retiring next Sunday, Sept. 14, and his congregation will be celebrating his years of service.

David Williams attended Pearl High School in Nashville, then studied engineering at Tennessee A&I (now Tennessee State University) before getting the call to the ministry where he graduated from the American Baptist Theological Seminary in 1958. In 1963 he became the pastor of historic old Mt. Lebanon on East Eighth at Glade Street. He has also been active in the community during his years with us. In the past he has been PTA president at Carver-Smith, Chairman of the board at IBH Nursery School for 25 years, president of the local chapter of the NAACP, and also served as the first African-American member of the Maury County School Board. He has also served in many offices and roles on behalf of the polity of the Missionary Baptist Church in Tennessee.

The associate pastor of Mt. Lebanon, the Rev. Felix Payne, brought us a copy of the invitation to the festivities at Mt. Lebanon to be held next weekend for the years of service of the Rev. Williams and his wife, Victoria. It will kick off with a reception on Saturday in the educational building from 2-4 p.m., and will be open to the community. The following afternoon, Sunday, Sept. 14, at 3:30 a special service will be held for the Rev. Williams presided over by the Rev. Enoch Fuzz of the Corinthian Missionary Baptist Church of Nashville. I love the man’s name. Reverend Enoch Fuzz. With a name like that you know that he is a tough minded man, plus he has the full backing of the Lord Jehovah behind him. My bet is that Enoch Fuzz is an American original, and I hope to get to hear him preach.
