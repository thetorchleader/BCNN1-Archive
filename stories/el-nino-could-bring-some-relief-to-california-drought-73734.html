El Niño Could Bring Some Relief to California Drought

May 16, 2015 National

A tow truck driver works to remove a stalled vehicle in flood waters, Wednesday, May 13, 2015, in Webster, Texas. The past few weeks have been very wet in the southern Plains, fueled by a weak to moderate El Nino. (PHOTO CREDIT: Cody Duty, AP)

El Niño is likely to persist well into 2015, a longer-than-expected stay that could bring some relief to the historic drought gripping the West, federal scientists announced this week.

As of this week’s U.S. Drought Monitor, a federal website that tracks drought, 98% of California, 99% of Utah and 99% of Nevada remained in drought, which extends from northern Washington to the Southwest.

Washington’s drought has worsened to the point that Gov. Jay Inslee declared a drought emergency for the entire state on Friday. California has been in a drought emergency since January, sparking mandatory water restrictions for the first time in the state’s history.

The El Niño climate pattern, defined as warmer-than-average sea-surface temperatures in the central Pacific Ocean, affects weather around the world and often helps spur precipitation in the western and southern U.S.

“There is an approximately 90% chance that El Niño will continue through the summer 2015, and a greater than 80% chance it will last through 2015,” the Climate Prediction Center said in a statement.

This El Niño officially began in March and has now reached a “weak to moderate” state.

If a strong El Niño does develop and persists into the winter, the likely U.S. impacts would include wetter-than-average conditions across the southern half of the country, from California through Texas to Florida, according to Weather Underground meteorologist Jeff Masters.

“The pattern of El Niño strengthens the southern storm track across the U.S., especially during the winter, spring and autumn months of the year,” said AccuWeather meteorologist Ben Noll.
