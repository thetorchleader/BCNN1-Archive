Ebony Magazine Has Been Sued for $5 Million Over Articles About Georgia Teen Kendrick Johnson’s Death

September 3, 2014 Black

Kendrick Johnson

The parents of two boys who were accused of playing a role in the death of Kendrick Johnson are suing Ebony Magazine for a series of articles on Johnson’s death and whodunit conspiracy, according to the Jasmine Brand.

The parents of Brian and Branden Bell filed suit against Ebony and writer Frederic Rosen after several stories were published about Johnson’s body being found at a Georgia high school rolled up in a gymnastic mat on January 11, 2013.

Rosen is known for his “true crime” articles. He named the brothers Chris and Clark Martin in his articles. But the parents claim this led to their sons being threatened in school — being that everyone put two in two together.

A white sophomore football player fictitiously named Chris Martin feuded with Johnson. His brother was Clark Martin, a high school senior. The sons of a Valdosta FBI agent used the feud as a motive to murder Johnson.

Nevertheless, the boys were threatened as folks knew who Rosen was referring to as their father, Rick Bell is a FBI agent. They still receive threats on social media.

In April 2014, Rosen published an article revealing that the brothers broke out in rage after Johnson had sex with one of their girlfriends.

The parents of the accused pointed out Johnson’s death was ruled accidental positional asphyxia. He was trapped in a standing gym mat while trying to retrieve a pair of tennis shoes in the gym.
