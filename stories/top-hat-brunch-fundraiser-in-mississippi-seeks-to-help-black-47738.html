Top Hat Brunch Fundraiser in Mississippi Seeks to Help Black Women

December 2, 2014 Black

Favian Kellingsworth of Ridgeland enjoys the 23rd annual Holiday Top Hat Brunch at the Jackson Hilton on Saturday.
(Photo: Kathy Matheny/Special to The Clarion-Ledger)

The ballroom at the Jackson Hilton had a distinctively regal feel Saturday during the 23rd annual Top Hat Brunch presented by the Central Mississippi chapter of the National Coalition of 100 Black Women.

Participants were treated to brunch, live music and a parade of elegant headwear of all shapes, sizes and colors.

The event, traditionally held the Saturday after Thanksgiving, is designed to raise funds for NCBW initiatives, including academic scholarships, mentoring programs as well as advocacy on health and policy issues. Since its inception, the Top Hat Brunch has helped the NCBW raise over $220,000 for scholarships benefiting African-American females on their way to college.

Sharolyn Smith, president of the Central Mississippi NCBW chapter, attributed the event’s success and longevity to the unique fundraising approach the Top Hat Brunch presents.

“These women look forward to this occasion because they get to display themselves with lady-like graciousness,” Smith said. In her address to attendees, Smith called the Top Hat Brunch an example of the “cheerful giving” that is so important — especially during the holiday season.
