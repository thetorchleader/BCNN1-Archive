Some Angry After Indicted Tennessee Pastor Is Allowed to Leave Country Before Trial

January 30, 2015 Christian

Pastor Mike Whitsey

In September of 2012, the Channel 4 I-Team found Pastor Mike Whitsey was raising money from his congregation without telling them the church was in bankruptcy.

One Sunday morning, the congregation at Abundant Life Christian Church in Murfreesboro arrived to find the doors locked. Some have no idea what happened to their money.

Whitsey was indicted on money laundering charges, accused of buying land to hide the proceeds from drug sales. His passport was seized and he was ordered not to travel outside of middle Tennessee.

Whitsey’s trial was slated to begin on Feb. 17.

A month before that trial, Whitsey wanted to take a trip out of the country. His federal probation office and the U.S. Attorney said it was OK for him to go.

“Clearly, when a person is taken into custody and their passport is surrendered, you think the government has issue with this person leaving the country,” said Jim Todd, Channel 4 legal analyst. “Now they’re reversing stream, and it’s dangerous.”

In filings in federal court, Whitsey’s attorney said he wanted to go on a mission trip to Haiti. But some have asked what’s keeping someone who may face prison time from traveling to Haiti and then just keep going?

“Obviously, there are a lot of countries, some closer than others, that don’t cooperate with U.S. extradition law,” Todd said. “And if you get there, you get to stay there.”

The I-Team wanted to ask Whitsey why he scheduled to go on a mission trip on Jan. 15. The day before he was allowed to leave, we went to the church’s new location.

Just before the I-Team went in, Whitsey pulled up. We went to talk to him, but he decided not to stop.

When Whitsey was indicted, he filed papers saying he could not afford to pay for his own attorney. So he got a federal public defender on the taxpayers’ dime.

Whitsey is now driving a silver Mercedes.

“I can only imagine taxpayers will see this and say we’re basically paying for this guy’s defense, and yet he’s driving around in a Mercedes,” Todd said. “You’re paying for this guy’s prosecution and you’re paying for this guy’s defense and you’ll pay to incarcerate him if he gets incarcerated.”

The I-Team went inside the church to see if anyone else could discuss the car, the mission trip to Haiti and the fact that the church is still asking people to give.

“Continue to commit to your vows, to your giving,” Whitsey said in a video. “Our goal is to raise $700,000.”
