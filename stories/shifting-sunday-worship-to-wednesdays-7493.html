Shifting Sunday Worship to Wednesdays

August 5, 2012 Uncategorized

Some churches boost attendance with midweek summer services

Marilyn Smith of Charlotte worships during the Wednesday night church service at Hickory Grove Baptist Church on Hickory Grove Rd. on Wednesday, July 25, 2012. The Wednesday night services are drawing a lot of people. DIEDRA LAIRD – dlaird@charlotteobserver.com
As New England sweltered, Sunday mornings came and went without a single soul showing up for worship in the hot, stuffy sanctuary of First Congregational Church of Salem, N.H. Even the pastor stayed home.
But God wasn’t forgotten.
Worship just waited until Wednesday evenings, when the cool comfort of the basement fellowship hall drew as many as 40 to sing and pray – 50 percent more than attended on summer Sundays.
“If people take a break from worshipping, they sometimes don’t pick that habit back up,” said Owen Williams, a deacon at First Congregational, a United Church of Christ congregation. Because Wednesdays keep people coming, “we have a depth of commitment throughout the year.”
Summer has a way of thinning out pews on Sunday mornings as people go on vacation. That poses challenges, especially for smaller congregations.
More churches, however, are rediscovering Wednesday – a traditional church night – as a prime time to gather for casual worship in summer. Early adopters report improved attendance, more donations and invigorated spirituality.
Charlotte-area churches don’t claim the increased numbers at Wednesday night services of other parts of the country. Hickory Grove Baptist pastor Matt Phipps said while their midweek services remain popular, it seems summer attendance numbers are down in general, no matter what the day.
“People take vacations, so we see the numbers go down a little. The numbers go down on Wednesday nights too, it’s the normal decrease,” Phipps said. This seasonal trend in attendance was echoed by other Charlotte churches and denominations.
“It is becoming more common for churches to experiment with different times, days and venues for worship gatherings,” said Elaine Heath, associate professor of evangelism at Perkins School of Theology at Southern Methodist University. “The prevailing attitude of church insiders, though, seems to be that the worship gathering that really ‘counts’ is the one on Sunday morning.”
Good Shepherd Church in Owatonna, Minn., added a Wednesday service and began immediately seeing new faces in the Lutheran Church-Missouri Synod congregation.
For others, the shift means overcoming resistance. The Rev. Vicki Keene, an interim pastor in the United Church of Christ, encourages a summer shift from Sunday to Wednesday services in every congregation she serves. Most parishioners have responded enthusiastically, she said, but some have pushed back.
