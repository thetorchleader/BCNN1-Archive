Former Virginia Governor and Wife to Begin Defense on Monday After Judge Denies Motions to Acquit

August 16, 2014 National

Bob and Maureen McDonnell begin their defense Monday after U.S. District Judge James R. Spencer on Friday denied motions to acquit them of all charges three weeks into their federal corruption trial.

Spencer said he would issue a written explanation of his ruling later. The jury was given Friday off.

The government rested its case Thursday in the trial that began July 28. The McDonnells were charged in a 14-count indictment alleging they accepted $165,000 worth of gifts and loans from Jonnie R. Williams Sr., the former CEO of Star Scientific, in exchange for helping him boost a new product, Anatabloc.

Among other things, Ryan Dean Newman, a lawyer for Bob McDonnell, argued Friday there was no evidence loans from Williams — a $50,000 check payable to Maureen McDonnell and a $50,000 check and $20,000 wire transfer to a family-owned real estate partnership — were personal liabilities Bob McDonnell had to list when applying for loans from TowneBank and the Pentagon Federal Credit Union.

Bob McDonnell added them in a handwritten amendment to a loan application that he faxed to the Pentagon Federal Credit Union three days after investigators first questioned Maureen McDonnell about Williams and the loans.

Newman also argued that there was no evidence Bob McDonnell committed any official acts in return for gifts and loans from Williams.

Newman said there has been ample evidence of “quid,” the gifts and loans, “But we’ve heard very little, virtually nothing, about what constitutes the official act — the Quo.”

“In this case the government has produced no evidence, none, that Mr. McDonnell attempted to influence any pending government decision,” said Newman.
