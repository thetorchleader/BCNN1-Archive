At Golden Globes, Common and John Legend Honor Unarmed Black Teens Killed by Police

January 12, 2015 Black

BEVERLY HILLS, CA – JANUARY 11: In this handout photo provided by NBCUniversal, Common and John Legend, Winners of Best Original Song – Motion Picture for ‘Glory’ in ‘Selma’, speak onstage during the 72nd Annual Golden Globe Awards at The Beverly Hilton Hotel on January 11, 2015 in Beverly Hills, California.
PHOTO BY PAUL DRINKWATER/NBCUNIVERSAL VIA GETTY IMAGES

Last night, Common took home the Golden Globe Award for Best Original Song, for “Glory”, which appeared on Selma. During his speech, the Chicago native took the time to pay tribute to the numerous unarmed black men who were killed by police over the last year.

Common, who was joined by John Legend, spoke about the feeling he received when he first stepped on the set of Selma and mentioned not only the recent killings of unarmed black men, but also the two Brooklyn police officers who were killed as well.

The first day I stepped on the set of Selma, I began to think this was bigger than a movie. As I got to know people of the civil rights movement, I realized: I am the hopeful black woman who was denied her right to vote; I am the caring white supporter, killed on the front lines of freedom; I am the unarmed black kid who maybe needed a hand but was instead given a bullet; I am the two fallen police officers murdered in the line of duty. Selma has awakened my humanity.
