Judge Hears from State Cop in Detroit Boy-in-Bbasement Case

April 29, 2015 Black

© AP Photo/Paul Sancya Charlie Bothuell IV, left, and Monique Dillard-Bothuell appear in court in Detroit, Tuesday, April 14, 2015.

A 12-year-old Detroit boy who was missing for 11 days last summer seemed relieved that his father wasn’t around when he was discovered in the basement, a police officer testified Tuesday. 

Detective Sgt. Ken Ducker of the state police said he used his flashlight to find Charlie Bothuell behind a barrel. He said there was a blanket nearby, a cereal box, drink bottles and a bowl with chicken bones.

“He asked me if his dad was home,” Ducker recalled. “I said, ‘no,’ and Charlie said ‘good.'”

Ducker testified at a hearing to determine if Charlie’s father, also named Charlie Bothuell, and stepmother Monique Dillard-Bothuell, will go to trial on charges of torture and child abuse.

Prosecutors say Charlie says he was struck with a plastic pipe, forced to perform rigorous workouts and given a limited diet. He went missing in June. Defense attorneys, however, claim the boy, now 13, is a liar who came and went on his own. The elder Bothuell insists he didn’t know his son was in the basement, which has no door at the top of the stairs.

Ducker said he searched the cluttered basement, roughly 600 square feet, with a federal agent to find evidence that could lead investigators to Charlie.

“I didn’t go into the basement looking for a dead child,” the sergeant said. “I didn’t think we’d find Charlie down there. I was not expecting to find him.”

Defense attorney Shawn Smith asked Ducker if he believed the boy was in the basement for all 11 days, but a prosecutor objected and the question wasn’t answered.

Charlie could testify Tuesday.
