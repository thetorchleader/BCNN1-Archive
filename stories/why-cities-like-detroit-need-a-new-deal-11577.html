Why Cities Like Detroit Need a New Deal

April 12, 2013 Uncategorized

Since President Barack Obama began his second term in January, I have been eagerly anticipating an announcement about how he will help the American people who are hurting the most.


When he was running for president, Barack Obama was a great friend to Detroit. Inside the White House, Obama has had little to say about the issues affecting big cities. (Carlos Osorio / Associated Press)
But I’m still waiting, and my disappointment and impatience have caused a burning in my soul.
So I must ask, Mr. President, what are you going to do to help black America? Those living in our cities full of crime, crumbling schools, extreme poverty and unemployment are suffering.
Yet I see nothing on the horizon, nor do I hear anything in the national dialogue, that indicates you are doing anything to address this crisis.
Every day, news reports highlight what you’re doing for every other group except urban blacks.
At the top of the list, you support the creation of a bill that would give same-sex couples the constitutional right to marry.
Along with that is your support of the U.S. Senate’s work on a bill that would lay a path to citizenship for the country’s 11 million undocumented immigrants.
All the while, strengthening the economy for everyone is your top priority. But our country cannot sustain a strong economy on top of urban abysses full of disenfranchised people who have no escape.
That’s why I’m proposing that you implement a plan to save urban, black America similar to President Franklin D. Roosevelt’s “New Deal” during the 1930s. This program created jobs that rescued millions of Americans from poverty after the Great Depression. The New Deal’s three planks were Relief, Recovery and Reform. That’s exactly what black Americans need right now.
