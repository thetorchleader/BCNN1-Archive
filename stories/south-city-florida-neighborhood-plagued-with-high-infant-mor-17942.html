South City, Florida Neighborhood Plagued with High Infant Mortality Rates; Pastors Such as Rev. E.L. Franklin Seek to Do Something About It

April 14, 2014 Uncategorized

E.L. Franklin is the pastor of Friendship Primitive Baptist Church on Putnam Drive in South City. / photos by Glenn Beil/Democrat

Babies in caskets are a sight the Rev. E.L. Franklin will never get used to.

He’s buried five infants, some no older than 6 months. Mothers, who miscarried, robbed of cradling their newborns and witnessing their first breath, don’t always want a funeral. They instead call Franklin to pray at their homes.

Franklin leads a small flock and presides over funerals at Friendship Primitive Baptist Church in Tallahassee’s South City: an area under siege with the highest concentration of infant deaths, low birth weights and repeat teen births of any area in Tallahassee.

“How do you explain to a mother that we serve a caring God, which I know we do, and still tell them that all things work together for good for those who love the lord,” said Franklin, folding his fingers while sitting last week behind a desk at his church on Putnam Drive.

South City is now a “hot spot.” Franklin, advocates and, most recently, city officials are alarmed. The community glows red on census maps due to the convergence of infant-related threats.

No one factor is to blame, but South City shoulders many. Basic survival dominates all. A pursuit for healthy living isn’t always a priority, which advocates preach is a direct link to a healthy baby.

Narrow streets have deep ditches instead of safe sidewalks. Gang activity is rampant. Rocks and bullets have taken out street lights. Violence keeps families and the elderly from venturing out after 10 p.m. Prostitution is yet another problem.

“Boy, did I get a rude awakening,” said Courtney Atkins, executive director of Whole Child Leon, a nonprofit providing children and families with resources to reach critical developmental benchmarks and wellness.

She and Franklin are the bedrock of a new South City Revitalization Council that’s mobilizing and fighting for improvements.
