JPMorgan Hacker Hits Citi, E*Trade Next

October 9, 2014 Technology

A man walks by a Citibank branch at the US bank Citigroup world headquarters on Park Avenue, in New York, in this November 17, 2008 file photo. (PHOTO CREDIT: Emmanuel Dunand AFP/Getty Images)

First they hit JPMorgan. Then the same shadowy cyber criminals tried to break through the virtual doors of other financial giants – only to find the locks too strong.

The group, which compromised secure information for 76 million households and seven million businesses in the JPMorgan attack, went on a hacking spree this summer and targeted Citigroup, E*Trade Financial Corp., Regions Financial Crop, HSBC Holdings and ADP. In the end, the most it got was a peek through a secure digital key hole.

JPMorgan reported in a filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission that the company’s security was strong enough that the attackers were not able to get to potentially more devastating data, such as account numbers, passwords, user IDs or Social Security numbers.

The would-be cyber thieves weren’t able to get in at all at payroll processing giant ADP.

ADP processes the paychecks of one in six Americans. The Roseland, N.J.-based company’s threat management team watched incoming Internet traffic that, given the addresses involved, was likely coming from the same group that launched the JPMorgan attack.

The hackers scanned ADP’s online defenses but were not able to breach them.

“We have not observed any issues associated with such scanning,” a company said in an email to USA TODAY.

“We will continue to utilize the information provided by members of the cyber intelligence community with regards to the recent JPMC event and will update our cyber defenses as necessary,” the company said.

Citigroup Inc., E*Trade Financial Corp., and Regions Financial Corp. also said they were not breached despite attempted infiltrations, the Wall Street Journal reported.

According to the New York Times, HSBC Holdings also saw similar attack profiles but was not breached.

Neither CitiBank nor E*Trade immediately responded to requests for comment.

The attacks, no matter that they were for the most part fended off, as still a major concern for U.S. law enforcement.
