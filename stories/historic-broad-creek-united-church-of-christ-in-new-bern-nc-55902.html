Historic Broad Creek United Church of Christ in New Bern, NC, Built by Freed African-Americans, Celebrates 150 Years

February 9, 2015 Black

Interim Pastor Antonio King Sr. delivers a message of hope Sunday during the 150th Anniversary Celebration of Broad Creek United Church of Christ in Oriental. Founded in 1865 by freed slaves, Broad Creek was the first African-American church in Pamlico County.
Crystal Garrett/Sun Journal

With just a dirt floor, an old wood stove and an abundance of faith, the first African-American church in Pamlico County was founded 150 years ago.

In the same year that the 13th Amendment abolished slavery, a group of freed slaves built what is now Broad Creek United Church of Christ in Oriental. The name of the church has changed a few times in the past 150 years and a new sanctuary has been built, but the members have remained.

On Sunday, the congregation celebrated its rich history.

Interim Pastor Antonio King Sr.’s message, focusing on the enslavement of the Israelites, who wandered through the wilderness for 40 years, seemed to serve as a parallel to the founders of the church, who strove for freedom after years of slavery.

King’s message was one of hope and inspiration.

“God delivered those who believed to the promised land,” he said. “They made it to the land of milk and honey … For years, our people were slaves. They were beaten and whipped, they struggled, but God finally set them free.”

King went on to describe some of the struggles faced by African-American slaves, along with the hardships people face today.
