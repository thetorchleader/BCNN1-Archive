Atlanta’s Ebenezer Baptist Church Lobbies Against Governor’s “Gun Carry” Bill

April 22, 2014 Christian

(Photo: 11Alive)

Governor Nathan Deal is just days away from signing a gun bill in to law that will allow worshipers to carry guns in to churches that opt in.

11Alive’s Kaitlyn Ross talked to religious leaders who have lobbied against it, and say the new policy won’t fly in their church.

The Easter celebration was special for the Rev. Raphael Warnock at Ebenezer Baptist Church.

“We must somehow resist the politics of fear, and embrace the politics of hope. I think that’s what Easter is about,” he said.

It’s a message he wants to deliver to Gov. Nathan Deal, who is set to sign the so-called “Gun Carry” bill Wednesday.

Some 65 religious leaders across the country lobbied against the bill, with Warnock leading the charge.

“If there is any place that people should be able to come and not worry about firearms, surely that place is the church,” he said.

Advocates for the 2nd amendment argue the bill allows people their right to bear arms. But for some worshipers at Ebenezer, that right should not extend to the church. Alberta King, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr, and Christine King Farris’ mother was murdered inside the church in 1974.

“My mother was killed in church with a gun, so I am very, very disappointed,” said Christine King Farris,
