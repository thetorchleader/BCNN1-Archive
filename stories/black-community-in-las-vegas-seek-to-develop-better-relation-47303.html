Black Community in Las Vegas Seek to Develop Better Relationship with Local Police

November 29, 2014 Black

While violence erupted in Ferguson, Missouri Monday night following the announcement of no indictment, in the Las Vegas valley, there were only peaceful demonstrations.

Community leaders and police gathered on Monday night to hear and discuss the grand jury’s decision.

Local African-American ministers and a former lawmaker say people are concerned about what the decision in Ferguson could mean in Las Vegas where police shootings are an issue.

The group is pushing for more communication with local law enforcement when it comes to the use of deadly force. They say they are working with Metro and North Las Vegas Police departments.

Inside Ebenezer Church of God, a small handful of pastors, ministers and former lawmakers gathered Tuesday to come to terms with the decision in Ferguson.

“Some of us knew the outcome before they even came down. We knew what was going to happen,” said Gene Collins, a former state assemblyman.

They group says the decision upset a lot of people.

“There are some angry individuals in this community and like I said, rightfully so,” Collins said.

“We want to better the relationship,” said Bill McDonald, Ebenezer Church of God.

The church leader says he wants even more conversations with local law enforcement regarding deadly force.
