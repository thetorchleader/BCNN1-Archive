LISTEN: Urban Christian News Network Weekend Report #117

March 21, 2015 Christian

1. According to Fox News, Friday’s suicide bombings during midday prayers at two mosques in Yemen that killed more than 140 people and injured over 350 more were likely the work of the Islamic State terror group and not Al Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula. ISIS militants claimed credit for the attacks in the Yemeni capital, as the nation continued its collapse into chaos, terror and death amid fighting between Islam’s two major sects. Al-Qaeda formally issued denial of responsibility and sources say that historically, Al-Qaeda has tried to limit civilian casualties, so the extremely high civilian death toll of Friday’s attacks — including scores of children — point to a different organization.

2. According to The Hill, Texas Sen. Ted Cruz is set to deliver a speech on Monday at Liberty University that is stirring speculation about a possible presidential announcement. Allies to the likely GOP 2016 hopeful are reaching out to political reporters and encouraging them to attend what they’re billing as a major address at the evangelical school about 150 miles from D.C. The conservative college founded by the late Jerry Falwell has often played a major role in the Republican fight for the White House, with hopefuls frequently making stops to speak to the evangelical base of the party.

3. According to the New York Daily News, seven Brooklyn children, the youngest five, the oldest 15, were killed as a devastating fire ripped through their home early on Saturday. Officials said the blaze tore through the Orthodox Jewish family’s home just after midnight. A mother and her eight children, were trapped in the inferno. One of the children died at the scene. The others died as they were rushed to area hospitals with severe burns. The mother and her lone surviving child were hospitalized with serious to critical injuries.

4. According to the Associated Press, a North Korean envoy says his country has developed nuclear missiles and is prepared to use them at any time. North Korean Ambassador to Britain Hyun Hak Bong said in a recent interview with British broadcaster Sky News that his government would use the missiles in response to a nuclear attack by the United States. Asked whether North Korea has the ability now to launch a nuclear missile, Hyun replied, “Any time. Any time. Yes. If the United States strikes us we should strike back.”

5. According to Fox News, with Russian aggression in eastern Ukraine continuing, the White House has announced that the U.S. will begin training 750 Ukraine troops. The news came after a phone call between Vice President Joe Biden and Ukrainian President Petro Poroshenko and coincided with Russians celebrating the first anniversary of their country’s annexation of Crimea. A Pentagon spokeswoman said, “Soldiers from the 173rd Airborne Brigade will conduct the training mission at the International Peacekeeping and Security Center in western Ukraine, near the border with Poland.” The U.S. training exercise had been put on hold for fear of antagonizing Russia.

6. According to Politico, Former CIA Director Gen. David Petraeus says the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant is not the biggest threat facing the United States in Iraq. ln written comments to The Washington Post’s Beirut bureau chief, Liz Sly, he said, “I would argue that the foremost threat to Iraq’s long-term stability and the broader regional equilibrium is not the Islamic State; rather, it is Shiite militias, many backed by — and some guided by — Iran.” He added that the Iranian regime “is ultimately part of the problem, not the solution” to the region’s issues.

7. According to The Jerusalem Post, the U.S. is reconsidering its staunch support of Israel at the United Nations and has not automatically accepted Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s statement to the American media affirming his support of two states for two peoples after pre-election comments that appeared to reject it. The White House press secretary said that, despite Netanyahu’s American media interviews, the US will “still evaluate” its policy on the Middle East peace process. Obama administration officials hinted broadly that the US may – for the first time – back a UN Security Council resolution laying down the principles for a two-state solution, or support a Palestinian bid to join the UN as a full member.

8. According to the Los Angeles Times, a federal law enforcement official said on Friday that preliminary results from an autopsy on the body of Otis Byrd, found hanging from a tree in rural Mississippi, strongly suggest the death was a suicide rather than a homicide or other foul play. The official declined to give further details, only adding that suicide is emerging as the apparent cause of death at this time. The death has shocked the area and the nation partly because there is no more powerful image in the South than the body of a black man hanging from a tree, the symbol of white racists’ refusal to accept the outcome of the Civil War and to continue their illegal fight for supremacy.

9. According to Reuters, a British teenager was sentenced to 22 years in jail on Friday for plotting to behead a soldier in London, a plan hatched to imitate the hacking to death of soldier Lee Rigby by two radical Islamists in the capital in 2013. Muslim convert Brusthom Ziamani was arrested last August, then aged 18, carrying a 12-inch knife and a hammer, wrapped in a black Islamic flag. Ziamani had told his ex-girlfriend in graphic detail hours before his arrest of his intention to behead a soldier on the streets of the capital.

10. According to the Los Angeles Times, Starbucks’ move to kick-start a national conversation on race relations — over coffee at its cash registers — has at least ignited a debate over whether the ubiquitous chain has any business leading such a conversation. Brand experts, meanwhile, wondered if the company’s “Race Together” campaign would attract or repel customers. The Seattle coffee chain this week began encouraging, but not requiring, its baristas to write the words “Race Together” on cups to get its customers talking about race. Its website features photos of the cups held by smiling workers and customers of many races. The campaign has drawn sharp criticism online, with many commenting on the company’s lack of diversity in hiring and whether employees are equipped to engage in these discussions, among other issues.
