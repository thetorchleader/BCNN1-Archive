Philadelphia’s First African-American Catholic Church Closes

November 15, 2014 Black

The St. Peter Claver Center for Evangelization, the site of the city’s first African American Catholic church, officially closed on Friday, Oct. 31. — AP PHOTO/FILE

The St. Peter Claver Center for Evangelization, the site of the city’s first African American Catholic church, officially closed on Friday, Oct. 31. The old St. Peter Church located at 12th and Lombard streets in South Philadelphia has garnered the nickname “Barney’s Church” because of William “Barney” Richardson of South Philadelphia. Richardson has been an outspoken and passionate advocate to save St. Peter’s.

Richardson’s family worshipped at the church for three generations, was among those who tried to keep the church open. So, the official closing of the center brought back memories for Richardson. Yet Richardson is not going to let the St. Peter Claver’s legacy die.

Since he learned of the official closing he’s been trying to gather letters to prove that the Archdiocese cannot legally sell the property located 12th and Lombard streets. He insisted that the property was donated to the community and not the Philadelphia Archdiocese. The senior is now spending his free time digging out those documents.

“My family went to this church for 80 to 90 years,” Richardson said. “When the church started we had many from South Philadelphia there. There were some Portuguese and West Indian Catholics who worshipped there. This was where Black Catholics worshipped.

“People also do not know that St. Peter Claver has the Our Lady of Victory shrine. Besides the church being historical for African Americans, that shrine is there. I think the Archdiocese could have had tours there with people coming to the first Black Catholic church and to the shrine. At one time some sisters (nuns) wanted to make Mass cards for the shrine to raise monies to save it,” Richardson said.

Adrienne Harris’ family, the Jones clan, also has a long history at St. Peter. Though she now lives in King of Prussia she hoped that the church would have remained open. She learned of the Oct. 31 official closing after reading it in the Tribune.
