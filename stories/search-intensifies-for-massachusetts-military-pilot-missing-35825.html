Search Intensifies for Massachusetts Military Pilot Missing After Plane Crash

August 28, 2014 National

A Massachusetts Air National Guard aircraft maintainer walks past an F-15C fighter aircraft at Barnes Air National Guard Base, in Westfield, Mass., Wednesday, Aug. 27, 2014. (Steven Senne, AP)

Additional personnel and aircraft from several states flowed into the remote George Washington National Forest early today as the search for a missing fighter pilot from the Massachusetts Air National Guard entered its second day with no no sign of the missing flyer.

As investigators began picking apart the crash site looking for clues to confirm whether the pilot ejected, investigators were marshaling police, fire, and military assets from surrounding states for an expanding search under the assumption that the pilot parachuted from the plane before it crashed and burned.

But the search for the pilot of an F-15C that crashed Wednesday morning was proving exceedingly difficult, due to the potential size of the search area and the extremely rugged terrain in the Blue Ridge Mountains about 130 miles southwest of Washington, D.C.

“They redid the search pattern numbers last night,” said Cotton Puryear, a spokesman for the Virginia National Guard, saying that there was still no solid information on what happened to the pilot.

Officials estimated that if the pilot ejected at high altitude, as high as 40,000 feet, he could drift at least a mile for every 1,000 feet he descended.

The effort here, being headed by the Virginia State Police with help from the National Guard, is being coordinated at an “incident response center” set up at the Deerfield Fire Department, which at daybreak was surrounded by satellite trucks and rescue vehicles.

Search techniques range from men on horseback to high-tech surveillance equipment.
