Penn State’s Worst Week Ever Comes to an End with a Loss; Now Reality Begins

November 13, 2011 Uncategorized

They left the field the losing team in their final home game, and still the student body stood as one and sent them off to the rest of their lives with one relentless chant.

Pictured: Penn State interim head coach Tom Bradley acknowledges the cheers after the game against Nebraska.

“We are …Penn State!”

Suddenly, maybe there was something good from this awful, awful week.

  • PHOTOS: Paterno’s career in pictures

“I felt loved,” linebacker Nate Stupar said later, “I felt everything was away, all the problems. I just felt cared about.”

“It sent chills through my body,” tackle Jordan Hill added. “It meant the world to us.”

And so game day came to Penn State, without Joe Paterno, after a week of broken faith, broken reputations and broken glass. But even the scoreboard has bad news in these terrible times.

Nebraska weekend brought a new reality to State College. Instead of a pep rally Friday night, there had been a candlelight vigil on campus for victims of child sex abuse, attended by thousands, trying to find something meaningful in the darkness.

On Saturday there was a football game, beneath a clear, blue sky. A lovely day in Happy Valley, and maybe that was a sign that there was light at the end of the tunnel.

The new reality meant a moment of silence for child abuse victims, before the Nittany Lions came onto the field walking, arm-in-arm, as slowly as a mournful drum beat. They went to midfield and prayed with the Nebraska Cornhuskers.

Then the game started. The will was there, to do something that would inspire cheers to sooth this stricken campus. It was the football that was lacking. Maybe the Nittany Lions really never had a chance; not after this draining, baffling, exhausting, excruciating ordeal.

They fell behind 17-0 in a strangely quiet stadium. “Eerie,” a security guard called it. But then they rallied and nearly pulled it off, losing 17-14. No storybook victory this day. Relief must come slowly to Penn State, in so many ways.

The new reality meant the first person to appear in the media room was not the new interim coach, but the new university president.
