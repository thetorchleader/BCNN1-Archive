2 Chefs Talk Empanadas on National Empanada Day

April 8, 2015 God & Food

A picture of celebrated Mexican chef Julian Medina’s empanadas. April 8th is National Empanada Day.
TACUBA RESTAURANT

What could be better than a day to celebrate empanadas? Wednesday, April 8th is National Empanada Day, and whether you call them empanadas, empanadillas, pastelitos or pastelillos- the names vary a bit in different Caribbean and Latin American countries – these pastries have crossed cultural boundaries and are increasingly popular in the U.S.

Why are empanadas so popular?

“They are very easy to eat with your hands, and you can fill them up with pretty much anything,” says Julian Medina, the chef of acclaimed New York City restaurants such as Toloache, Yerba Buena, Tacuba and Coppelia.

Empanadas are made out of dough and can be filled with all kinds of savory and sweet ingredients. In South America they tend to be baked, and in the Caribbean they are more likely to be fried.

Medina, who “challenged” chef Jose Garcés in “Iron Chef,” said different countries have their own fillings, and that is the reason the empanada is so versatile. At his restaurants, he has a variety of flavors, such as cheese and mushroom, chopped steak picadillo, shrimp and chicken tinga.

For those with a sweet tooth, empanadas can also be stuffed with guava paste.

This comfort food is so popular that a New Jersey man has made a living out of selling them.

Carlos Serrano, or Empanada Guy, was raised in Newark, NJ. He began his empanada business in the garage of his house and now he manages five food trucks and a restaurant. The menus all revolve around the delicious pastry.
