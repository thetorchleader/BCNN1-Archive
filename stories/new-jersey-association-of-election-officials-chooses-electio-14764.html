New Jersey Association of Election Officials Chooses Election Administrator Jerry L. Midgette As First Black to Lead the Organization

March 21, 2014 Uncategorized

At the 77th Annual Conference of the New Jersey Association of Election Officials (NJAEO) on March 18, Somerset County Election Administrator Jerry L. Midgette became the first African American to be elected as the association’s president. A former election lawyer, he also is the first election official from Somerset County to be elected president of the NJAEO.

“It is truly a great professional honor for me to be elected unanimously as president of the NJAEO by my friends and colleagues,” said Midgette, a resident of Franklin Park. “It is with a profound sense of gratitude, pride and humility that I accede to this leadership role of great responsibility.”

The NJAEO is a nonprofit, member-based association comprised exclusively of New Jersey county election officials. A key objective of the NJAEO is to develop and promote legislation that strengthens the efficiency and integrity of New Jersey elections and to cooperate with government officials in carrying out the provisions of all election laws.

Midgette has been a member of the NJAEO since 2006 and has served in various important leadership positions prior to being elected president, including chair of the Nominating Committee, By-Laws Committee and Legislative and Legal Committee. “My primary objective in participating in the association has been to make a notable contribution to its mission and relevancy to all our members and external stakeholders,” he said.

To that end, Midgette initiated and led a thorough and comprehensive review of the association’s by-laws, which culminated in the full membership voting and adopting all the proposed amendments recommended by his committee. This was the first major revision to the organization’s by-laws since their initial adoption in 1988. He also initiated and led the effort to implement a more formal and transparent process for identifying and recommending qualified candidates to the membership to be nominated, elected and ultimately to serve as officers-at-large.

He also participated in reviewing and commenting on countless bills and pieces of legislation for their potential impact on the election administration process in general and county election officials in particular. He has attended many legislative hearings and provided testimony on behalf of the association as well as the County of Somerset.

Midgette said the top three issues on his action agenda are developing and promoting legislation to strengthen existing election laws while also expanding early-voting opportunities for New Jersey voters; exploring and demonstrating the efficacy of new voting systems to facilitate the election administration process; and improving the efficiency and effectiveness of disseminating important election information to members and the general public.
