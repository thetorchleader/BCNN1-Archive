Ron Fournier Calls Obama’s Middle Class Speech ‘One of his Best, a Searing and Historically Poignant Account’

December 6, 2011 Uncategorized

President Obama’s “fair shot” address Tuesday may be remembered as one of his best, a searing and historically poignant account of the greatest challenge of the American experiment: How do we give every citizen, rich or poor, a path to the good life?
But his speech in Osawatomie, Kan., with its echoes of Theodore Roosevelt’s appearance in the same city a century ago, also exposed the limits of Obama’s presidency and personality. Obama is a man of his times, and this is a lousy time to command what TR called the “bully pulpit.”
Because of who he is and the hand dealt to him, Obama will find it much easier explaining America’s problems than solving them.
The address opened with a succinct summary of the American dream (“If you gave it your all, you’d take enough home to raise your family, send your kids to school, have your health care covered, and put a little away for retirement”) and how it’s been sacrificed to a global economy, suffocating debt and political polarization.
“But for most Americans, the basic bargain that made this country great has eroded,” Obama said. “Long before the recession hit, hard work stopped paying off for too many people. Fewer and fewer of the folks who contributed to the success of our economy actually benefitted from that success. Those at the very top grew wealthier from their incomes and investments than ever before. But everyone else struggled with the costs that were growing and paychecks that weren’t — and too many families found themselves racking up more and more debt just to keep up.”
“For many years, credit cards and home equity loans papered over the hard realities of this new economy,” Obama said. “But in 2008, the house of cards collapsed.”
The speech was laced with memorable moments.
