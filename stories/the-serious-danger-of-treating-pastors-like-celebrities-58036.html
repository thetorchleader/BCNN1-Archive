The Serious Danger of Treating Pastors Like Celebrities

February 22, 2015 Christian

by Jason Autry

I read a lot of articles and blog posts in 2014 addressing the current state of the local church pastor in America. Several were written in response to a couple of high-profile resignations and others were just offering commentary on some bizarre occurrences in pastoral life.

All of the pieces suggested a string of continuing problems within the pastorate that are resulting in moral failures and terminations of leadership. The issues most referenced were personal arrogance, the celebrity culture of American evangelicalism and the historical significance of hero worship.

This list is obviously not exhaustive nor is it new. Some nuance of all these factors have been plaguing ministers for ages. Today’s context has simply introduced new and creative ways to foster an insidious, over-sized ego, fall into sin and fuel an insatiable desire to be awesome.

When I say awesome, I mean being excessively honored; receiving global name recognition and having more followers on Twitter and Instagram than Justin Bieber. It’s being the talent envy of others while speaking at events attended by underprivileged, not so awesome pastors and writing transcendent books from the platform of a wildly successful ministry. That kind of awesome.

I admit it, the pursuit of awesomeness is seductive and presents quite the challenge in modern leadership. By human standards, it is much more stimulating to build a framework of magnificence than it is to be a servant. Settling for the role of a mere undershepherd is boring when the title of Captain Awesome is out there to attain. Yet, a servant and an undershepherd is exactly what God calls the pastor to be even in today’s progressive climate.

All of this has prompted a lot of personal and spiritual evaluation. The news of the last year has been sobering, convicting and led me to prayerfully reflect on why I don’t want to be awesome:

I’m Not Awesome
The definition of the word awesome is “to cause feelings of fear and wonder.” There is not one thing about me that personifies that meaning. Not my personality, skills, charisma, stature or bench press. Nor will there ever be.

I am simply a person whom God has gifted in a way that is tailored to my design. With his help in the application of my gifts, I can have an eternal impact in the world and be part of some pretty amazing things. Jesus said in John 14:12, “I tell you the truth, anyone who believes in me will do the same works I have done, and even greater works, because I am going to be with the Father.” 

The fact that Christ would choose to work in and through me to accomplish anything is what causes awe and wonder. I can’t generate that response on my own and I can’t produce those results apart from God’s Holy Spirit. He’s awesome and I just pray that I can be faithful to him.

I Want to Be Myself
If I can’t be awesome then I’ll just have to be myself, which is exactly what God desires for my life and leadership. I don’t want to be the awkward guy rooting around in the dark for an identity. A person with no identity is constantly in search of one, and the human desire to be somebody at all cost makes that search hazardous.

Sure, I deal with the significance and acceptance issues that plague a lot of leaders and because of that, I don’t want to sell out, wear clothes that don’t fit, espouse stuff I don’t believe, make decisions that defy the mission and talk in a contrived way just to get some people to think I’m important.

I want to stave off the temptation to do as Paul warned in Colossians 2:8 and be“captured by the empty philosophies and high-sounding nonsense that come from human thinking, rather than from Christ.”

I Need Accountability
Awesome people don’t need accountability. That’s why they’re awesome. No one in their realm has the intelligence, wisdom, verbal skills or benjamins to keep them honest. Underlings or even peers are simply wasting time trying to speak into any facet of their transcendent lives.

I’m a flawed man. I need accountability. I’ve seen the long- and short-term benefit of it in my marriage, parenting, leadership and ministry. Without the right people to hold me accountable, I will drift toward self-centeredness and satisfy temporal desires that will not benefit anyone in my sphere of influence or further God’s kingdom.

I’m an Adult
To be awesome, you apparently have to do, say and promote some pretty absurd things. Things that a middle school boy tries to impress his friends with, things a lucid, grown adult wouldn’t even think about saying or doing.

We live in desperate times. The issues confronting people of every age and demographic are intense and shockingly profound. They need an adult leading them, not a boorish, immature child who spouts unintelligent catchphrases, plays mind games and talks like Seth MacFarlane.

I Want to Value People
Being awesome in most arenas means you have to manipulate and treat people as essential, yet expendable cogs in the machine. Friendship, deep connection and transparency are as foreign as humility.

I don’t want to spend every inch of time margin leveraging human beings to tirelessly construct my own kingdom of megalomania. Where is the eternal value, joy and fulfillment of community in that? Where is that in the Bible?

If Philippians 2:3 (“Don’t be selfish; don’t try to impress others; be humble, thinking of others as better than yourselves”) is the standard of how we should interact with and respect people, then it stands in direct contrast to stepping on and over the very ones we are called to serve.

I’m Not Mad at Anyone
Being awesome over a period of time seems to cultivate a strange, belligerent egocentricity. One has to look no further than the biblical example of Saul or several modern illustrations for proof of this disturbing reality.

This explains why a leader talks about love, grace and forgiveness one minute, and the next he’s privately seething, publicly ranting over a peripheral issue, verbally abusing people or calling out some “haters” who aren’t listening anyway.

I’ve been that guy. I’ve been guilty of allowing pain, criticism and hurtful comments to make me an angry person. But that latent anger was nothing more than pride and the arrogant belief that I was awesome and above correction.

James 1:20 says, “For the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God.”The pastor who constantly pounds his chest and carries a large chip on his shoulder is negating his usefulness to God. He is preventing good and Christ-honoring things from coming through his ministry.

I Want to Finish Strong
There are exceptions to every rule, but more often than not, the individual pursuing the fulfillment of his or her own desires will eventually receive it to a bitter end. Captain Awesome has been known to have a tragic narrative and sometimes an agonizing swan song.

I’ve seen this play out in my own extended family. It is difficult to even talk about, and when I reflect on it, I am quite sure that is not the legacy I want for my life and leadership. Being awesome for a season is certainly not worth a lifetime of heartache, brokenness and collateral damage to my family and all who know me.

Jason is from Columbia, SC and now lives in Louisville, KY where he is the senior pastor at Springdale Community Church. He loves God and the life-changing power of the Gospel and finds fulfillment journeying with people, fostering creative conversations and speaking in diverse settings.
