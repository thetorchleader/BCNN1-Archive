Visit These Black Heritage Sites in Mississippi

February 11, 2015 Black

When my husband and I travel, we often follow a theme— visiting presidential libraries, famous writers’ birthplaces or some other. So in that regard our recent trek through Mississippi wasn’t unusual. But what prompted it was: a fried chicken dinner topped off with peach cobbler at the Country Platter, a soul food restaurant in Cleveland, Miss., a town of about 12,000 in the heart of the Mississippi Delta. Half a century ago some surprising things had happened there, we would learn.

Our meal was delicious and we wanted to compliment the chef, who turned out to also be the establishment’s owner, Jimmie Williams. When he emerged from the kitchen and sat down at our table to chat for a few minutes, soft-spoken Williams asked if we knew we were sitting in an “important historic building.” We didn’t.

Back in the early days of the civil rights movement, he explained, a number of important meetings had taken place at the Country Platter. Then-Sen. Robert F. Kennedy had come to Mississippi to assess the effectiveness of the government’s newly declared “war on poverty,” and in Cleveland, he saw that the battle was far from won.

Kennedy had met at the restaurant with several key black civil rights leaders, including the Rev. Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and his assistant, Ralph Abernathy, to talk about “how to address the poverty here and in other black communities and keep the (civil rights) movement going,” said Williams. “They made history here,” he added.
