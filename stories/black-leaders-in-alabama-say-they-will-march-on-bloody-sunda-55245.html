Black Leaders in Alabama say They Will March on ‘Bloody Sunday’, Not on Saturday When President Obama Plans to March

February 5, 2015 Black

The Rev. Jesse Jackson speaks during a press conference at the Alabama State House to discuss voting rights and the Bloody Sunday anniversary Friday, Jan. 30, 2015, in Montgomery, Ala. (Julie Bennett/ jbennett@al.com)

Black leaders in Alabama say they will march across Edmund Pettus Bridge on “Bloody Sunday,” not on Saturday when President Barack Obama plans to be in Selma.

“Bloody Sunday is sacred,” State Sen. Hank Sanders (D-Selma) said, during a Friday afternoon press conference at the Alabama State House. “It is sacred because blood was shed. It is sacred because it was a Sunday not Saturday.”

The march will continue as planned on Sunday, he said.

“We really appreciate the President coming, but this is about Bloody Sunday,” Sanders said. “It’s not about anybody else.”

Sanders, who has planned the march with others annually since the 1970s, spoke out last week about Obama’s decision to march on the 50th anniversary commemoration of Bloody Sunday where civil rights marchers were beaten on March 7. This year that date lands on a Saturday.

Alabama black leaders invited the Rev. Jesse Jackson to speak out regarding Bloody Sunday during today’s press conference, as he was in town discussing voting rights.

Jackson said the Voting Rights Act is eroding in the South since the Supreme Court struck down Section 4.
