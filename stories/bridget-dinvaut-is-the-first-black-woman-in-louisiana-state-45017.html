Bridget Dinvaut Is the First Black Woman in Louisiana State History to be Elected a Top Prosecutor

November 13, 2014 Black

DA-elect Bridget Dinvaut

Bridget Dinvaut will become one of only three women district attorneys in Louisiana — and the first African American woman in state history to be elected a top prosecutor — when she succeeds Tom Daley in St. John the Baptist Parish. Her election was automatic Monday when incumbent Tom Daley gave the secretary of state’s office his notarized letter of withdrawal from the runoff election.

Daley, 61, announced late Friday via Facebook that he was bowing out, citing poor health. He had a cancerous brain tumor removed in December, and he said his doctor informed him Friday morning that stress from the campaign had caused the cancer to re-activate and advised him to leave the race.

He said he and Dinvaut have met and are discussing transition plans. “I had a very constructive meeting with Ms. Dinvaut … , and we pledged to work cooperatively as a transition team to make sure that everything continues smoothly in the office,” Daley said Saturday. “I will complete my term. Ms. Dinvaut will be sworn in at the same time as (District Court) Judge (Mary Hotard) Becnel is sworn in for her successive term.”

Daley’s six-year term expires Jan. 11. He said Dinvaut, who resigned as a prosecutor to run for DA, probably would return to work in the office during the transition period. Dinvaut has not returned telephone calls seeking comment.

Daley is completing his first term as DA. He resigned his post on the 5th Circuit Court of Appeals to run for the office in 2008. Before that, he was a prosecutor in the St. John district attorney’s office for six years and a District Court judge.

Dinvaut, 52, spent the past four years as a prosecutor under Daley and headed up one of three criminal divisions. She is a former Sheriff’s Office patrol deputy and narcotics detective, and she worked as a pre-sentencing investigator in federal courts. She also had a private law practice.
