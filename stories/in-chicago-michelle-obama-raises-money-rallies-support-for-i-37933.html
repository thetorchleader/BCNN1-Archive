In Chicago, Michelle Obama Raises Money, Rallies Support for Illinois Governor Pat Quinn

October 7, 2014 National

First lady Michelle Obama, seen here in June 2012, will rally voters for Democratic Gov. Pat Quinn today at the UIC Pavilion. (Brian Cassella, Chicago Tribune)

First lady Michelle Obama is scheduled to return home today to raise money and headline a rally for Gov. Pat Quinn, marking the latest Democratic celebrity to hit the campaign trail for the governor as he seeks to fire up his base of support and drive voter turnout in a non-presidential election year.

The first lady’s visit comes less than a week after President Barack Obama hosted a $50,000-a-person Gold Coast fundraiser for Quinn that brought in an estimated $1.25 million. Quinn then spent the weekend campaigning with actor Martin Sheen, best known for his portrayal of liberal president Josiah Bartlet on the television show “The West Wing.”

Quinn also is expected to get some help this week from former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton, who is scheduled to be in Chicago on Wednesday to deliver a speech at a medical conference.

The Democratic governor faces Republican challenger Bruce Rauner, an equity investor who has poured in more than $14 million of his own money since entering the governor’s race in March 2013, according to campaign records.

The rally with Michelle Obama will take place at the University of Illinois at Chicago pavilion on the Near West Side. The event is open to the public, but attendees had to obtain tickets in advance through the Quinn campaign, which said there is now a waiting list. Doors open at 2 p.m., and the rally will begin at 3 p.m. The first lady is supposed to host a fundraiser for the governor earlier in the day, details of which the Quinn campaign has not released.

Quinn is betting on the Obama’s continued popularity in Chicago, even as Democrats across the country continue to shy away from the president and his low approval ratings. Support from the president and first lady could energize city voters, particularly African Americans.
