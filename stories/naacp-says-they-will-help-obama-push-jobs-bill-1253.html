NAACP says They Will Help Obama Push Jobs Bill

November 4, 2011 Uncategorized

With an estimated 1.4 million African-Americans unemployed, the NAACP is staunchly supporting President Barack Obama in his efforts to put people back to work during the worst economic crisis since The Great Depression.
President Barack Obama steps off Air Force One at Cote d’Azur Airport in Nice as he arrives for the G20 Summit Thursday. (AP)
Roslyn M. Brock, chairman of the national board of directors for the NAACP, met with Obama Tuesday to support passage of the President’s American Jobs Act and spoke with Obama about the staggering 16.7 percent black unemployment rate.
“I enjoyed speaking with President Obama about this crucial bill, which will help get the American people back to work,” Brock said in a statement.  “The unemployment rate among people of color is critically high. The NAACP will continue to prioritize job creation and anti-employment discrimination measures. We will do everything in our power to ensure the passage of this job-creation and community-stabilization legislation.”
While some black activists like Rep. Maxine Waters (D-Calif.) and Princeton University Professor Cornel West have criticized Obama publicly, Brock and the NAACP have signaled their support for the president as Obama prepares for a tough re-election battle in 2012.
Waters acknowledged that she does not have a relationship with Obama and has never met with the president, as Brock did this week.
West, for his part, has blasted Obama on many occasions for not doing enough for African-Americans, but Obama, who always takes the high road, says he’s trying to create jobs and bring a sense of hope for all Americans who are struggling.
The president’s plan – introduced in the U.S. Senate and in the House of Representatives – is designed to encourage employers to hire the long-term unemployed and specifically prohibit employers from discrimination against unemployed workers, which has been a specific priority for the NAACP.
