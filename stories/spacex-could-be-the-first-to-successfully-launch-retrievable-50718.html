SpaceX Could Be the First to Successfully Launch Retrievable Rocket

January 2, 2015 Science

SpaceX will test its retrievable rocket in a mission that will also deliver supplies to the International Space Station (ISS) early this month.

Scheduled for launch on January 6 or 7, the CRS 5 mission will carry food, spacesuits, science experiments, and other supplies that would have arrived at the space station in October if Orbital Science’s Antares rocket not exploded.

Once the supplies are delivered, the company will attempt to successfully land a rocket on a semi-submersible floating launch pad positioned on a barge. The pad is marked with an “X” to indicate the exact spot for its targeted return.

The Falcon lifter will travel higher vertically after delivering the supplies to the ISS. Several controlled burns will be executed to send it back to Earth. Current plans are for it to hover over the launch pad and slowly be lowered onto it.

Four retractable legs will enable the spacecraft to land safely on the targeted spot.

This is the first time the company is attempting to actually retrieve a spacecraft it returned to Earth. Previous attempts to do this were foiled by technical problems and bad weather.

SpaceX has successfully returned rockets to Earth, but these landed in the ocean and were not retrieved.
