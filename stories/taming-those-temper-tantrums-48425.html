Taming Those Temper-Tantrums

December 6, 2014 God & Family

© Provided by NewsCred

My son is not quite a year old, and he is already showing signs of potentially being able to pull off an Oscar-winning tantrum performance: Falling over sideways crying and lying motionless on the floor in despair (simultaneously pathetic, hilarious, and terrifying). My daughter is almost 4, and she’s become a drama queen extraordinaire, even going so far lately as to make herself cry – real tears! I am learning that tantrum taming is not one-size-fits-all, and how you approach a tantrum needs to change from age to age and child to child.

Whether your child is 2, 3, 4, or more, here are some methods that can work to circumnavigate a tantrum, or at least cut its duration considerably.

Distract, Distract, Distract

The littlest kids will be the easiest to distract in the throes of a tantrum. Anything sparkly, shining, moving, or unexpected can often jolt them out of their tunnel vision.

“If we’re out somewhere and the meltdown begins, I pull out my keys and – poof! – tantrum averted,” says Chloe, mom of one. “Even my wallet is a good distraction because it’s something she doesn’t see all the time. But if you dump out my bag you’ll also find a hodge-podge of novelty items, like colorful feathers, strings of cheap Mardi Gras beads, or tiny wind-up toys.”

And when all else fails, there’s always your smartphone.

Crouch Down

“Definitely get down to the child’s level – height-wise – to speak quietly to them,” says Jenilyn, mom of two. “I’ve found it works when you lower your voice and they have to quiet themselves to hear you.”

Be Empathetic

This may work better with preschoolers and slightly older kids who are in the process of giving names to their feelings. Don’t try to reason with your child. Simply say something like, “I understand you’re sad because you want to wear your pink shorts to bed but it’s too cold and you need long pants tonight.”

End of story. No further discussion. This works nine times out of 10 with my daughter – at least when I take the time to think and correctly identify the feeling she’s experiencing in the moment. A quick hug and a smile after laying down the law doesn’t hurt either.
