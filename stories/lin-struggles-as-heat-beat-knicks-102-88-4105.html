Lin Struggles as Heat Beat Knicks 102-88

February 24, 2012 Uncategorized

The tired New York Knicks and point guard Jeremy Lin ran into the buzzsaw Thursday that is the Miami Heat.
The Heat chewed up and spit out the Knicks and won 102-88.
Chris Bosh led the Heat with 25 points. Guard Dwyane Wade, who is playing at a high level after struggling with leg and feet injuries earlier in the season, had 22. And LeBron James continued his dominating year with 20 points, nine rebounds and eight assists.
“We just played maybe arguably the best team in the NBA – or playing the best right this moment,” Knicks coach Mike D’Antoni said. “They set a bar we have to get up to. We’ve got a lot of things to improve in a month, and we’ll see if we get there.”
Bit by bit, Miami stretched the lead in the second half and sailed into the All-Star break on an eight-game winning streak — all by double-digit margins — and with the league’s best record at 27-7. It’s the Heat’s best franchise start for 34 games.
The Heat also got a typically workmanlike effort from center Joel Anthony: no points, six rebounds, five blocks and outstanding defense.
“Joel Anthony is one of the cornerstones to what we do,” Heat coach Erik Spoelstra. “It’s why we liked him when he probably wouldn’t fit on a lot of teams, and I say that affectionately because we value what he brings. He covers ground. He does a lot of things that don’t show up in the box score.”
Lin struggled, shooting 1 of 11, and finished with eight points and eight turnovers, his most inefficient game as a starter. He and the Knicks looked spent on the second night of a back-to-back. It was also their fifth game in seven days.
“We have to be able to stop him, in a sense, before we worry about the other guys,” Wade said. “He’s a player, we understand that. It was just trying not to let him see the floor because he can pick you apart.”
D’Antoni wasn’t surprised Lin had a tough time against one of the league’s most versatile defenses. The Heat have size, length and speed at several positions.
“They’re one of the best pick-and-roll defenders,” D’Antoni said. “They come at you real quick. The first time you see it, speed is overwhelming. It took him a while to adjust and he didn’t have the legs it looked like. He played last night, and it happens.”
It was the first time the Heat faced Lin. In Miami’s 99-89 win against the Knicks on Jan. 27, he did not play.
Miami impressed Lin.
“They did a great job of making me uncomfortable,” Lin said. “We’re a no-excuse team, so fatigue really doesn’t matter. … It was the speed and the athleticism. Credit to them. … I can’t remember another game where it was hard just to take dribbles.”
