Paterno’s Death Leaves Heartbreak and Questions about Legacy‎

January 23, 2012 Sports

They gathered around the bronze statue of their former football coach shortly after the news spread Sunday morning. Less than a mile away from Beaver Stadium, Joe Paterno died at Mount Nittany Medical Center at the age of 85.
A blue and white scarf draped around the statue’s neck and hundreds of candles, letters and flowers rested at his feet. The collection of rosaries, mass cards, footballs, students’ identification cards, license plates, t-shirts and posters grew as the chilly Sunday continued. Hundreds of fans crowded downtown retail stores to purchase Paterno books, DVDs, t-shirts and cardboard cutouts.
Throughout the day, much of the coaching world, a legion of former players and even a former U.S. president weighed in. Then came the statement from his former longtime assistant, Jerry Sandusky.
“This is a sad day! Our family, Dottie and I would like to convey our deepest sympathy to Sue and her family,” read the statement, which was released to the news media by Sandusky’s lawyer. “Nobody did more for the academic reputation of Penn State than Joe Paterno. He maintained a high standard in a very difficult profession. Joe preached toughness, hard work and clean competition. Most importantly, he had the courage to practice what he preached. Nobody will be able to take away the memories we all shared of a great man, his family, and all the wonderful people who were a part of his life.”
Odd punctuation aside, Sandusky commenting on Paterno’s legacy seemed uncomfortably ironic. A life of great achievement would be tinged by sadness and regret as a result of the Sandusky child sex-abuse scandal.
Paterno was criticized for not going to law enforcement in 2002 once he was told by then-graduate assistant Mike McQueary that McQueary had seen Sandusky allegedly sexually abusing a young boy in a shower on campus. “I wish I had done more,” Paterno said in the aftermath. The university’s Board of Trustees fired Paterno with three games left in the regular season. Only 74 days later, Paterno was dead.
