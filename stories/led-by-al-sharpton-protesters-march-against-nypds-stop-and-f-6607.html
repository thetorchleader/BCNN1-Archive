Led by Al Sharpton, Protesters March Against NYPD’s ‘Stop and Frisk’ Policy

June 17, 2012 Uncategorized
Demonstrators have taken to the streets of New York City to march against the New York Police Department’s “stop and frisk” policy, which allows officers to stop people deemed suspicious.
Critics say the program unfairly targets blacks and Hispanics, while Mayor Bloomberg has defended the program as effective in curbing crime.
Updated at 5:06 p.m. ET:
Several thousand demonstrators, led by activist Al Sharpton, are taking part in a silent march down New York City’s Fifth Avenue in opposition to the NYPD’s “stop and frisk” policy, according to The New York Times.
The Times describes the march as a “slow, somber procession.”
Two of those taking part, Wade Cummings, a 46-year-old teacher, and his son, Tarik, 19, said they had been stopped by the police though they had done nothing wrong, the Times reports. The son said he had been stopped three times, the father once, according to the Times.
Said the father, “I’m concerned about him being stopped and it escalating,” the Times reports.
The group of marchers appears diverse, according to the Times. There are union members and NAACP members, members of Occupy Wall Street and representatives for student groups.
Benjamin Jealous, NAACP president and CEO, told the Times the idea for the march came about in Selma, Ala., three months ago, during the annual commemoration of the 1965 “Bloody Sunday” march.
“Stop and frisk is a political tool, victimizing one group of people so another group feels protected,” the news organization quotes Jealous as saying.
