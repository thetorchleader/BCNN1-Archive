Persecuted Shouwang Church in China to Celebrate Last Outdoor Service on Christmas Day

December 24, 2011 Uncategorized

Key issues in Chinese
religious rights are taking a pivotal turn at year’s end as the
beleaguered Shouwang house church in Beijing will celebrate its last
outdoor service on Christmas Day.

Compass Direct News
reports that the house church, which had been denied access to a
building they had purchased as a worship venue, has been meeting
outdoors — and facing weekly arrests — since April 10.

A
source in Beijing told Compass that next year church leaders will renew
their fight to retain the venue the government had denied the
congregation.

“They
said the church would actively pursue realizing the goal of returning
to meet indoors after Christmas,” the source said, adding: “And they
still believe the best way to resolve the issue is that the government
would permit the church to enter into the space it bought.”

Also, “disappeared” human rights lawyer Gao Zhisheng has ostensibly resurfaced — in an undisclosed prison.

After
20 months of secret detention, the Beijing First Intermediate People’s
Court ordered Christian human rights lawyer Gao to serve what had been a
suspended three-year prison sentence — supposedly for violating terms
of his probation.

The order came just as the five-year probation period for Gao expired yesterday (Dec. 22).
