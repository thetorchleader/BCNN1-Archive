Johnny Manziel Hit by Fake Lawsuit Accusing Him of Sexual Harassement

May 24, 2014 Sports

(Photo: Brian Spurlock, USA TODAY Sports)

Johnny Manziel might have the singular talent, a rock star status beyond almost any other Heisman Trophy winner, a college lifestyle most undergrads would dream of and a legion of followers in Cleveland and numerous other cities.

But in facing what seems to be a hoax legal complaint, Manziel is not alone.

Jacksonville Jaguars quarterback Blake Bortles faced a similar injunction for a restraining order in federal court last week, a claim that was handwritten and submitted falsely under the name of Minnesota Vikings quarterback Teddy Bridgewater.

Philadelphia Eagles coach Chip Kelly faced one from the same Pennsylvania inmate.

Both were promptly dismissed.

Although rare, these types of frivolous lawsuits find high-profile athletes as targets because of their stature. With citizens guaranteed the right to file these lawsuits, which often make claims based no way in fact, celebrities face this as a fact of life.

“We want to have an open society. We want to have access to the courts to all people,” said Keith Watters, past president of the National Bar Association. “(But) there are tools at their disposal for the quick dismissal of these cases.

“It’s more of an annoyance than a problem.”

Claiming Manziel sexually harassed a woman for more than a year, a person filed for a restraining order against him and is seeking $25 million in damages. Filed last week in federal court in Florida, the complaint makes salacious claims of Manziel sending nude photos to a woman.

His agent has called it a fake, and the woman whose name is on the suit has said she is in no way involved. Instead, it seems to be the hoax of an unknown person that — thanks to the immediacy of social media — served a brief purpose Friday of smearing the QB’s name.

“It’s insanity,” Erik Burkhardt, Manziel’s agent told USA TODAY Sports. “You can read the thing for yourself. What some people will do for publicity is just embarrassing. That’s all I’ve got to say.”

Later, Burkhardt told USA TODAY Sports it is “absolutely possible” Manziel could seek legal action in response to the court filing, though Manziel’s team of representatives is still early in its evaluation process.

“We absolutely will protect our client to the fullest extent of the law,” Burkhardt said.
