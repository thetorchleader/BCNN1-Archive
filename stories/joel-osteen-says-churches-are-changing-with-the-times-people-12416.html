Joel Osteen Says Churches Are Changing With the Times, People Less Interested In Denominations

May 17, 2013 Uncategorized

Megapastor Joel Osteen has his supporters and his detractors.
The former point to his passion for helping people and unblemished record of good works since becoming senior pastor of Lakewood Church in Houston, Texas, 14 years ago. Opponents note that Osteen doesn’t even have a theology degree and is no more than an inspirational speaker — albeit a very successful one.
Various sources put Osteen’s net worth at $40 million.
Southcentral Pennsylvania residents can make up their own minds May 31, when Osteen — with his wife and co-pastor, Victoria — makes his first appearance in the area at the Giant Center in Hershey.
“I’m very excited to be coming to the Hershey area,” Osteen said, adding that his father-in-law once lived in Harrisburg.
Osteen agreed to answer some questions about faith, nondenominational trends, current events and his own critics.
What can people expect from an evening with Joel Osteen?
It’s a two-and-a-half hour event. There’s a lot of music. My wife, Victoria, is involved. There’s a lot of inspirational stories. My mother overcame cancer and she’ll talk about that. My kids are involved. I’ll be sharing my story … It’s a lot of fun.
What do you tell people who wonder where God fits in our busy lives in 2013?
God can be as involved in your life as you want him to be. I’ll really encourage people to get God out of your Sunday morning box. He really wants to be involved in your everyday life.
Your church joins many other nondenominational churches that are wildly successful. It is a trend here in York County as well, with our three or four best-attended churches being nondenominational. What does this mean for the future of organized religion and can the mainline denominations survive?
It seems like the churches that are changing with the times … and making the Scripture relevant, that’s what’s really important. People are not as interested, at least in what I see, in the denominational name. I don’t see that.
The times have changed where people are not so set on going to a particular denomination. … I have friends who were staunch Baptists and now they don’t even put Baptist in their name.
How do you connect with 40,000 worshippers? How can people feel comfortable in such a large church?
We never tried to have a big church. My parents started it with 90 people and it continued to grow. When I talk to people from the pulpit, I talk like I’m talking to one person. So I think a lot of it comes from the pulpit. …
After every service, I spend time meeting about 500 people. I’ll take about an hour at an area backstage to meet people. So I tell people, ‘We’re a big church, but I’d love to meet you.’
