About this Serial Novel

When a Christian minister, John Coverly, is burdened by the moral decay in America, he resolves to do something about it. In a series of dreams, God gives him messages for the President of the United States. As tragedies and upheaval rock the nation, John Coverly takes his messages to the president, giving him God’s view on the status of the nation. Will the President listen? Will the nation be restored?
EPISODE #1
CHAPTER ONE
Prophet John Coverly and his wife, Gracie-Lynn, were relaxing in their home after returning from a rather exhausting two weeks of visiting their three children and grandchildren, ending with a one week revival in California at their oldest son’s church.
“This is what makes the Christian life worth living,” Prophet Coverly said to his wife. “Seventeen people trusted Jesus Christ as their Saviour. It has been a tremendous week indeed. Praise the Lord!”
“You can say that again,” his wife agreed. “No telling how many Christians were revived and got on fire for the Lord. You certainly know how to bring down the house.”
“Those Christians needed to be shaken up and I am glad God gave me the right message for them.”
Gracie-Lynn smiled. Her husband had no fear when it came to preaching God’s Word. The harder the look on the people’s faces, the harder he preached. His one aim in life was to see souls saved because as he often said, “When this life is over that is all that will matter–how many souls you brought to Jesus Christ.” He always liked to quote, “One life to live and it will soon be past; only what’s done for Christ will last.” Gracie-Lynn often thought back to the day they met.
She had stopped by Burger King on her way to work to purchase her favorite–sausage, egg, and cheese biscuit with two hash browns and a small orange juice. While waiting for her order, John Coverly approached her. He was not what she would call handsome, so he did not appeal to her physically; but he did have a contagious smile.
“Excuse me, ma’am, may I please ask you one question?” Not waiting for an answer, he continued, “If you were to die today, where would you go–Heaven or Hell?”
That question stuck with her all week long. No one had ever asked her such a direct and thought-provoking question before. Death certainly was not on her mind. At twenty-two years of age, she was enjoying her single life, doing what she wanted to do and pursuing her life’s dream, which was to work with mentally retarded children. Her weekends were filled with visiting museums and attending other social events in the community. Life after death was not on her mind, and she rarely attended church.
The more she thought about the question put forth to her that early morning, the more uneasy she felt about the direction of her life. She read more than once the Gospel tract he gave to her with the same question printed on the front: If You Were to Die Today, Where Would You Go–Heaven or Hell? She mulled over it throughout the week. As providence would have it, their paths crossed again at the same Burger King the following week, where, as they conversed, he explained to her the path to eternal life.
Two verses stuck in her mind: For God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world; but that the world through him might be saved. He that believeth on him is not condemned: but he that believeth not is condemned already, because he hath not believed in the name of the only begotten Son of God (John 3:17-18). Gracie-Lynn asked Jesus to save her after expressing her belief in His death, burial, and resurrection. As they prepared to go their separate ways, John said to her with a laugh, “You came to Burger King to get nourishment to continue living your physical life, now you have eternal life as well.” Soon, she started attending his church. One thing led to another, and now they were celebrating thirty-eight years of marriage. “Yes,” Gracie-Lynn would often joke with her husband, “that’s what I got for hanging around Burger King–salvation and a godly husband.”
Prophet John Coverly was recognized by many as a modern-day prophet. He was very much in tune with God. He saw things before they happened, and he had insight into the lives of people and events as they affected the world. He was called into the Gospel ministry at an early age, and was also blessed with the gift of evangelism. His motto was, “If it talks, breathes, moves, or eats, it is a candidate for evangelism.”
Turning the television channels, they both turned their attention to the six o’clock news. The president had been inaugurated a year earlier, and the American public was still anticipating great results from his economic, political, and social policies.
“That’s good news from our president,” Gracie-Lynn said, referring to the news report. “It’s good that he’s plugging all that money into our education system. That’s how anyone is going to make it in America or anywhere in the world for that matter.”
“Plugging in is a good word,” her husband agreed, “but investing would be a better word. He’s investing, through education, in the lives of college students, who, once they get that degree and secure a job, should turn around and pay back into the economy.”
“Makes sense,” Gracie-Lynn agreed. “I guess that’s how he plans on paying back China for the huge debt we owe them.”
“I’m sure that’s one of the ways.”
A few more minutes into the news, her husband said, “You know he keeps mentioning equality for all people, including homosexuals. Little by little he’s letting them into mainstream America. I wonder why he’s so adamant about them having equal rights.”
“I guess we’ll find out sooner or later,” his wife said. “I know folks are expressing more and more dissatisfaction with some of his economic decisions. It seems like he’s not for a certain type of people.”
“That’s because their trust is in man and not in God.”
Gracie-Lynn left the room during the commercial break to make them both turkey sandwiches. After about two minutes, her husband shouted at her.
“Gracie, hurry. Breaking news!”
Gracie-Lynn hurried into the living room. There had been a random shooting at a mall in Memphis, Tennessee. “Senseless. Senseless,” she kept muttering to herself.
“One tragedy after another. I tell you what, the demons of hell have certainly been let loose in America. We are reaping what we have sown, and will continue to reap if we don’t repent,” her husband said.
Prophet Coverly fell asleep before the news was over. During his sleep he had a very vivid dream–one that began a new chapter in his life and set him on a new mission.
 
 
CHAPTER TWO
He was led up a high mountain where God told him, “Look to the north, to the south, to the east, and to the west, and tell me what you see.”
Prophet Coverly peered around as far as his eyes could see. “I see people: some on their knees with heads bowed; others with outstretched hands towards heaven; still others with tears streaming down their faces. They are crying out, but I can’t hear what they are saying.”
“Yes, Coverly. They are crying out to Me, because the leadership in this country has turned their backs on Me. My pastors are failing Me; they are fleecing the flock instead of tending to their needs. Christians are not living holy and obedient lives. There is much sin all around.” Extending His hands to the crowd, God continued, “These dear people are looking for someone to show them what to do. These are the humble saints of God who remember that this country was founded on My principles and who see its people throwing these principles behind their backs. They are cr

ying out for healing in this land.”

“Yes, Lord, that is true. The godly principles on which we were built and which have carried us over two hundred years have been eroded and we are standing on slippery ground. I can see our founding fathers turning over in their graves,” Prophet Coverly responded.
“I place most of the blame on the churches,” God said.
“How so, Lord?”
“The churches have compromised My Word. Pastors no longer preach what I tell them. They preach messages to tickle the people’s ears and to get the people to applaud them. They see sin and corruption, yet they sit back with arms folded squinting their eyes at the sin going on around them, yet they do nothing about it. My pastors have turned their backs on the cries of the innocent,” God said. “If I can just find one who will stand in the gap.”
Prophet Coverly looked out yonder across the multitude as God continued. “The sins of My people, the church, and the sins of this nation, America, which I have blessed so much, are disgusting before My holy eyes; their sins stink in My holy nostrils.”
Prophet Coverly’s mind drifted to the words of Isaiah: “Ah, sinful nation, a people laden with iniquity, a seed of evil doers, children that are corrupters; they have forsaken the Lord, they have provoked the Holy One of Israel with anger, they are gone backward.”
A holy fear gripped Prophet Coverly as he heard God pronounce judgement on his beloved country.
“Your country will soon become desolate; your cities I will burn with fire; strangers are going to take over your land; if you refuse my words and continue to rebel, you shall be devoured by the sword.”
“Lord, what can we do? What shall we do?” Prophet Coverly cried out.
“I need someone full time for Me. There are too many part-time, half-baked preachers. I need someone who loves Me, fears Me, obeys Me, and will be faithful to Me to stand in the gap. Where are the seven thousand who have not bowed down their heads to Baal? Where is that one who, like Isaiah, will say, ‘Here am I, Lord, send me,’?”
As Prophet Coverly looked over the crowd, he noticed all heads were turned towards him. A multitude of people were pointing in his direction. Turning to the north, fingers were pointing towards him; turning to the east, fingers were pointing towards him; turning to the south, fingers were pointing towards him; turning to the west, fingers were pointing towards him.
“Who will stand in the gap for these confused searching souls? Who will cry out to Me for mercy even as Abraham did for Sodom and Gomorrah and for his nephew, Lot? They are looking for someone to lead them.”
Prophet Coverly’s eyes filled with tears as he saw the desolation of the nation. It was on a downhill spiral and moving at a fast pace.
“I have heard their groanings, but I don’t see true repentance yet,” God said. “As I said earlier, it’s not just the President, but pastors, preachers, Christian husbands, Christian wives, fathers, mothers and children who are of the age of accountability, need to fall on their knees before Me and fast and pray and repent. I ask again: who will stand in the gap? Who will cry out for My mercy upon this country and its people? Who will intercede? Who? Who?”
An uneasy silence filled the air. Tears streamed down Prophet Coverly’s face. He looked over the crowd with compassion. He looked over his beloved America. He looked into the faces of the multitude–many had an empty look, a searching look, a confused look. They needed someone to lead them. They needed someone to tell them what to do, and to show them what to do. Even the President needs someone to stand in the gap for him before God; he needs someone to intercede for him.
“Who will go? Who? Who?”

“John, John, wake up! You’re having a bad dream,” Gracie-Lynn said shaking her husband.
He looked up at her in a daze muttering the words, “Who? Who? I will go. Here, send me, God.”
Gracie-Lynn shook him until he was wide awake.
“Where…Where am I?”
“You’re home. What are you talking about? You were having a bad dream,” his wife told him. “You were squirming and moaning–having a nightmare, I guess.”
“No, no,” he said with a wave of his hand. “I am a Christian. I don’t have nightmares because the Spirit of God is in me.”
He shared with his wife the vision he just had and ended with: “Whether out of the body or in the body, I do not know, but someone has to be a spokesperson for God at this time; someone has to warn this president of the impending destruction if he continues on the road he is on; someone has to warn our pastors and preachers of their lackadaisical attitude towards the things of God; someone has to warn Christians about taking God lightly; someone has to stand in the gap.”
EPISODE #2 

CHAPTER THREE

Friendship Heights (Washington D.C. metropolitan area)
10:30 P.M.

Fox was the one who had ordered the hit.

Ivan Gunner was the one who was carrying it out.

Under the cover of darkness, he emerged from the manhole, dressed as a city sanitation worker. He turned the corner onto a residential street and stopped at the two-story beige stucco house with the Spanish tiled roof. A low stone wall surrounded the property, and stone steps and a walkway led up to the front door. On both sides, the house was surrounded by thick, lush foliage. Ivan stepped over the foliage and into the shadow of the trees.

He never questioned why Fox had contacted him, and given him the order. He never even questioned Fox’s real name. As long as Fox paid up, Ivan would keep his nose out of his business. As far as he was concerned it was one corrupt Washington politician trying to take out another corrupt Washington politician. And Ivan was the one they hired to do the dirty work. In a few hours, Fox — whoever he was — would be on TV expressing his sadness over the murder and demanding “justice.” Ivan was sure of it.

Ivan shook off the contemplation and gazed up at the row of awning windows on the lower level of the house. Three were shuttered. One — the kitchen window — was open, just as Fox had said it would be. Ivan had a clear view of the refrigerator and a row of stainless steel kitchen appliances lined up on the counter. Now it would just be a matter of time before Senator Phil Lehman walked past the window and to the refrigerator to get a beer, or the microwave to warm up a meal, completely unaware that he would never get the chance to enjoy either. Making sure he was well-hid by the plant growth and couldn’t be seen from the street, Ivan settled down on the cold grass to wait.

While watching the window, he thought about what he would do with the one hundred and fifty grand he had been promised by Fox. Perhaps he would buy a house in Mexico or Brazil. Or maybe he would buy a Harley and go riding across country just for the heck of it. But, now he had to concentrate. Shaking the random thoughts out of his head, he fixed the silencer onto the barrel of the semi-automatic pistol. He had done it so many times before. He flexed the muscles in his arm and leaned his head back against the trunk of the large tree, with heavy, low branches. Keeping one eye on the window, he recalled everything about Phil Lehman that he had read on the internet: he was a four-term senator, the former chairman of the American Progressive party, and the owner of an automobile manufacturing company. He had been married once, but his wife had died. He was a father and grandfather. Something seemed to needle Ivan’s mind — that last part about Lehman being a father and a grandfather. Ivan, himself, had no kids. But he hadn’t had a father either. Drifting into his childhood, those old questions that he had thought were long-buried were beginning to surface when he noticed a blur of movement in front of the window.

Instinct kicked in. Ivan moved forward into a crouching position. Raising the gun to eye level, he aimed it at the space between the edge of the window and the sill, waiting for Lehman to reappear. Placing his finger on the trigger, he steadied his breathing and counted his heartbeats as he waited. One… two… three…

Lehman stepped back into the window’s view, busy doing something at the counter–tall, graying, and dignified. Ivan’s hand shook. He cursed and wondered if he was getting soft. Trembling hands were not one of his trademarks. Get a grip, Gunner, he told himself.

Lehman opened the refrigerator and bent down to peer inside. A few seconds later, he swung the door shut and turned back to the counter. Ivan squeezed the trigger. The bullet went through the open window. Lehman staggered backwards and gripped his chest with a cry of agony. Ivan waited to hear the heavy thud that meant Lehman was lying prostrate on the kitchen floor. Even if the bullet hadn’t killed him, the bleeding certainly would, and there would be hours, perhaps even a whole day, before anyone noticed that something was wrong.

Ivan got up off the grass where he had been kneeling and drifted into the darkness.


CHAPTER FOUR

The White House (Washington D.C.)
11:00 A.M.

President Robert Rushmore leaned back in the executive chair in the Oval Office and steepled his fingers over his chest. The two years since his election had flown by. Even though he had entered office with the goodwill and approval of an overwhelming majority, it only took a few months for his cozy relationship with the press and with the people to wear off. He had made more than a few political enemies — even some members of his own party had publicly criticized his efforts to push through controversial immigration reform. But there were benefits that came with that accomplishment: his Latino allies were happy, he thought he had done the right thing, and he had delivered on a key campaign promise. Making it less complicated for immigrants to enter the country legally would only improve the diversity and strength of American society, he thought. Now if only he could get to the other items on his agenda…

First, however, he had to survive the midterm elections. That meant coming out with a majority of lawmakers who supported him either elected or re-elected in both the House and Senate. That was the task at hand. That was why he was holding a meeting with the top members of his political team in the Oval Office.

“Sir, the changes you wanted made to the speech are done.” The voice of Jamie Taylor, the head of his speech writing team interrupted his thoughts. She sat bent over her laptop on one of the two cream-colored couches in the middle of the huge sun’s rays rug that covered most of the floorspace in the Oval Office. Beside her sat Edward Clark, one of the president’s senior advisors, talking on his cell phone. Another advisor, Ronald Kushman leaned over her shoulder peering at the screen through large glasses. Across from them, on the other couch, sat Albert O’Malley, former U.S. attorney general and current chair of the American Progressive Party, who was plotting campaign strategy with Misty Spencer, the president of uPAC, a political action committee she had started herself while in college. Directly opposite the president’s desk, in one of the two blue-and-white striped chairs at the other end of the Oval Office, sat Vernon F. Laurence, the vice-president. His head nodded as he dozed intermittently. After his late night meeting and early morning duty of swearing in the new defense secretary, his lack of sleep was catching up with him. Behind him, an aide lingered at the door, his eyes glued to the screen of his BlackBerry.

Jamie tapped the keys on her keyboard. “I’m sending the full thing to you now,” she said.

President Rushmore reached across the desk for his iPad. He passed a hand through his close cropped dark hair and swiped the screen on his iPad to view the updated speech he was planning to give to the senators and congressmen of the American Progressive party in a few days at their annual retreat. He caught his reflection in the iPad’s glass screen and paused. Dark eyes and a chiseled face stared back at him.

After reviewing the changes to the speech, he looked up. “The speech looks good. The plans look good. Are there any other suggestions before we end this meeting?” he asked.

“No,” said Vice-President Laurence sharply, as he awoke suddenly. He tensed the muscles in his arms and sat up straight. As soon as I get out of here, I’m going to take a nap, he thought. “I have a meeting in fifteen minutes,” he said apologetically.

“Actually, I have a suggestion,” said Misty Spencer, taking the pen she had stuck in her curly, reddish-blonde hair and motioning toward a print-out on the table.

“Yes?” said the President.

“After looking at our party’s voter turnout levels for the last election and the midterms before that, I think we need to expand our message to include another demographic.”

“And which one would that be?” said the President.

“The faith communities.”

“Really?” said Edward Clark, his blue eyes unable to hide his thoughts that the suggestion was a joke. “The faith vote didn’t even matter in the election. And you think they’re going to show up for the midterms?”

“They just might,” said Misty. “Last week, the opposition launched a coordinated outreach effort to conservative faith communities in forty-two states. They’ve got some major pastors, the head of the biggest Jewish religious conference, and even a few leading imams behind them.”

The President leaned forward in his chair and rested his elbows on the desk. “And what’s their line?” he asked.

“They’re highlighting some of our agenda’s social policies that, well, frankly, a lot of people in those communities don’t agree with. After pushing immigration through, they know we’re going to push those next. If their party wins a majority in the Senate, they can defeat those efforts.”

“Did they ever hear of separation of church and state…or mosque and state?” Edward laughed.

Misty ignored him. “All I’m saying is, we can’t just let them push their message out there unopposed. We have to counter — at least get some leaders in these communities on our side. We can highlight our efforts to relieve poverty, reform education — stuff they’ll easily support. Maybe we can throw some issues that they want onto the platform. And we can spin the social issues. Let them know that we can work together, and we’ll make sure they’re not adversely affected.”

“You’ve got to be kidding,” Edward stood up and paced the floor as he spoke. “We don’t even have the money to try to reach a whole new demographic.” He looked over at Albert O’Malley, the party’s chair, for backup.

“Well, maybe we can do something about the fund raising…” Albert said slowly fidgeting with his bow-tie.

President Rushmore held up his hand for silence. He knew that Misty was a genius when it came to all-things political. He had relied on her political action committee and their “path to victory” to secure the number of electoral votes he needed to win the election. After working on a number of Senate campaigns and a gubernatorial race, she was already one of the country’s most celebrated political consultants. And she was barely thirty-five years old.

“I think Misty is right,” he said. “We neglected the faith community during the election, and they may be slow to respond to overtures from our party. But we have to try. The few votes we gain may just be what puts us over the top come November.” He turned to Misty. “I trust your judgment. Put a plan together, and bring it back here within two days — that’s Friday. Edward, we…”

“Sir,” the aide standing in the doorway cleared his throat, interrupting the President. He held up his phone. “I just got word that, uh, Senator Lehman’s been shot. He was found in his home.”

The President stood up. “Dead?” he asked. Lehman was a valuable political ally. He had pushed for Robert Rushmore to be at the top of the ticket at the party convention two years ago, and he also was good at twisting the arms of stubborn senators to get them to support the President’s policies.

The aide nodded. “I’m afraid so, sir. He was already gone when they found him.”

The president sank down into the executive chair. He pressed his fingers to his forehead.

“My meeting,” the vice-president said in a hushed, urgent tone. He got up and slipped from the room.

CHAPTER FIVE

Emmanuel Temple Church (Charlotte, N.C.)
1:30 P.M.

Rev. John Coverly shook hands with the last of his parishioners. “God bless you, brother. Have a great week,” he said as a middle-aged church member with his wife and children made their way down the steps of the triumphal gothic revival style church building. Around three-hundred people attended the historic Emmanuel Temple Church in Charlotte each week. What the church didn’t have in size, it made up for in prominence. The voice behind the pulpit of Emmanuel Temple Church was recognized and respected by the city and the country. John Coverly had been that voice for thirty-two years, and even though he was almost sixty, he had no intentions of retiring. Entering the foyer and letting the large wooden doors slam shut behind him, he dabbed the sweat off his forehead and smoothed down the wispy strands that were struggling to hold on atop his balding head.

“Gracie-Lynn,” he called for his wife who was “resting her feet” in the back pew. They had been married for thirty-eight years, and their three children — two girls and one boy — were married and had started families of their own.

Inside the auditorium, where the custodian and other church officials were preparing to leave, Rev. Coverly stopped to take in the ornate, awe-inspiring architecture. The high, soaring ceiling was designed in such a way that it seemed as though you could look right up into the sky from the floor of the church. The twenty-four brilliant stained-glass windows, twelve on each side, let in soft, filtered, multi-colored light and depicted key scenes from the biblical narrative. The majestic fourteen thousand pipe organ regularly belted out music that made the whole building vibrate with worshipful sound. When combined with the voices of the faithful, the place felt holy. Rev. Coverly closed his eyes and let the feeling wash over him. He was glad that God was in this place.

He was interrupted from his reverie by his secretary, Mina. “Pastor, the real estate company called again about the land for the new school buildings. They want to know if we’re going to buy it.”

“God hasn’t told me yes or no yet,” said Rev. Coverly.

“Well, they said the church on Peterson Street is looking to buy it too. They want an answer from us.”

“When I get an answer from God, they’ll get their answer from me. Anything else I need to know today? I’m ready to go home and eat.”

“There’s this letter that came in yesterday,” Mina said pulling a white envelope from the binder she carried with her. “Some kind of invitation for you. Read it and let me know how you want me to respond.”

“Alright, I will.” Rev. Coverly slipped the envelope into the front of his Bible where he would be sure to see it in the morning.

“See you on Tuesday, Pastor,” said Mina.

“No, I’ll see you on Monday — it’s Missions Monday tomorrow,” said Rev. Coverly. “We’ve got my son from California and that missionary from South Korea coming in to get the young people fired up about witnessing for Jesus.”

“Right, I almost forgot,” said Mina. “See you then. Bye, Mrs. Coverly.”

“Bye now,” said Gracie-Lynn.

Rev. Coverly turned to his wife. “Where are we going for dinner this afternoon? Soul-food or Chinese food?” he asked.

“Chinese food today,” said Gracie-Lynn, slipping her feet back into her shoes, and adjusting her violet Donna Vinci church hat on her head. She got up from the pew. “The soul-food restaurant is packed this time on Sunday, and I don’t feel like standing on my feet waiting in line.”

“Alright, wait right there and let me get my keys from the office.”

Greater Praise House of the Lord (Newark, N.J.)
3:30 P.M.

Pastor Ellis Emerson sat in his church office. He had just finished taping the intro for his new sermon series, “The Blessed Believer,” which would air nationwide on the Daybreak Television Network. He pressed the button to power on his iMac so he could check his e-mail and catch-up on the activities going on at the other locations of the fourteen-member church network that he oversaw. From Nigeria and England to Australia and Mexico, and eleven other locations in the United States, Emerson was pleased with the growth of the Greater Church Outreach Network. In twenty seemingly short years, after being selected as pastor of the Greater Praise House of the Lord, he had built a 30,000-member mega church in Newark. The church’s sprawling campus was also home to a television studio, an elementary school, a high school, a bookstore, a restaurant, and a movie theater. Emerson believed that the church ought to embody not only the values of the Bible, but the values of the community, in order to reach the world. He mentally patted himself on the back for being so good at it.

The glass door to his office opened, and his wife, Valencia, walked in. She was tall, elegant — in a word, beautiful. Rev. Emerson gave himself another mental pat on the back. Marrying her was a good decision. As the first lady and the head of the ministry’s education program, she made the church — and him — look good.

Valencia sank down into the plush chair that members sat in when they came to be counseled by her husband. “I tell you, that new choir director is something,” she said. “He had the whole church on its feet today.”

“I know,” said Rev. Emerson. “Young kid just out of The Colburn School in California. He knows how to blend the contemporary style with a tinge of the classical. I like it.”

The glass door swung open again, and Rev. Emerson’s eighteen-year-old daughter, Desiree, and seventeen-year-old son, Antonio, marched in with their choir robes draped over their arms, headed for their father’s inner office where they had left their personal items before the worship service began.

“Hey, kids,” said Rev. Emerson.

“Hi, Dad,” they responded in unison.

Desiree was back in a moment with bangle earrings in her ears and her purse on her arm. “Bye, Dad. Bye, Mom,” she said as she headed for the door.

“Where are you going?” asked Rev. Emerson.

“Out,” she said. “I’ll be back before curfew.”

“Out where?”

“Rodney’s taking me to a baseball game.”

“Him again?” Rev. Emerson groaned. He was not too approving of his daughter’s taste in young men. “Didn’t you go out with him yesterday.”

“That was Thursday, Dad,” said Desiree.

Rev. Emerson shook his head. “Antonio,” he called.

“Sir?” Antonio hurried from the back office.

“Your sister’s going to a baseball game with Rodney. Why don’t you go with them.”

Desiree scowled. The last thing she wanted was her younger brother accompanying her on a date.

“Don’t wanna go to no baseball game,” mumbled Antonio.

“What language are you speaking?” said Valencia.

“I don’t want to go to a baseball game — with her,” Antonio repeated. Desiree smiled.

Rev. Emerson sighed, and waved his hand for Desiree to leave.

“Be back on time,” Valencia said as the door swung shut.

Antonio followed her out the door.

“Where are you going?” asked his mother.

“Nowhere,” he said. “I’ll be ’round till it’s time to go home.”

When the door swung shut again, Rev. Emerson sighed. “Ever since I got that girl a car, she hasn’t driven it home once.”

“She’s home every evening by her curfew,” Valencia reminded him.

“After dark,” Rev. Emerson pointed out. “We don’t get to spend time with either of them anymore.” He pulled up the Evernote app on his computer to review his schedule for the week.

“They’re growing up,” said Valencia. “We have to let them spread their wings.”

“Well, Desiree is spreading her wings a little too far. I’m trying to make sure she does not spread her legs and we end up with a baby out of wedlock with that crazy boy, Rodney. Isn’t it amazing that girls who are saved and brought up in the church still want a thug for a boyfriend? That boy graduated a year and a half ago from high school and he still hasn’t decided which college he’s going to or what he wants to do with his life. The other day I heard him bragging to his friends that he graduated from high school. Baby, when you and I grew up, we didn’t think that graduating from high school was a great accomplishment. Our parents expected us to do that. Nowadays, these kids think graduating from high school is a big deal. By the way, did you ever introduce her to Coverly’s grandson? That’s the kind of man she needs to be looking at — someone who’s been raised right.”

“Yes. Last month when we were in L.A. for the gospel choir competition, they met.”

“And?”

Valencia raised her eyebrows. “She said he was ‘mildly intriguing’ — her words, not mine.”

Rev. Emerson shook his head again as the door to his office swung open. It was his secretary, Renee.

“Pastor, this letter came in yesterday for you. We didn’t open it yet,” she said.

Rev. Emerson patted the empty space on his desk. Renee set the letter down. “See you on Tuesday,” she said.

When the door shut, Valencia picked up the stiff, white envelope. There were two lines in evenly spaced, formal black type in the upper left corner, and an American flag stamp in the upper right corner. She held the envelope out to her husband.

“You need to open this. It’s from the White House.”
