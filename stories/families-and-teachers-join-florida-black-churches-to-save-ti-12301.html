Families and Teachers Join Florida Black Churches to Save Titusville Schools

May 14, 2013 Uncategorized

South Lake Elementary students marched to St. James AME church in Titusville yelling “Save! Our! School!”


The rally at the St. James AME Church in Titusville. Tim Shortt, FLORIDA TODAY
Dozens of families, teachers and community members filed into the sanctuary for the rally.
“It’s a cause close to our hearts,” said April Von Eschen, a teacher at Gardendale Elementary, one of the schools closing later this month. “Our fight is still not over. We want a miracle.”
Organizers handed out flyers with emails and phone numbers of school board members, urging individuals to contact their representatives.
“Tell them to stop putting politics before our students,” it says. “Reconsider the closings.”
Rev. Glenn Dames, the church’s pastor and a schools advocate, said they want officials to use additional dollars from the state legislator to keep the schools open.
It’s never to late to do the right thing,” he said.
Much of the new $22.6 million comes with strings attached, school district leaders have said. They believe the rest may go to new charter schools opening or a new requirement to pay for dual enrollment tuition.
Retired Titusville High Custodian Ellnora Ingram said she hoped officials overturn their decision.
“They need to reconsider what the public wants instead of what they want,” she said.
The Save Our Schools rally tonight at St. James AME Church in Titusville is expected to mobilize supporters who have tried to block schools closures and have questioned how Brevard Public Schools is spending its money.
The rally will be from 5:30-7 p.m. at the church, which is lead by Rev. Glenn Dames. The schools’ advocate has been central to the fight to keep open Gardendale Elementary on Merritt Island, South Lake Elementary in Titusville and Clearlake Middle in Cocoa.
