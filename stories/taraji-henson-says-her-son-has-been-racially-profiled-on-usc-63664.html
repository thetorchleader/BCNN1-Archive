Taraji Henson Says her Son Has Been Racially Profiled On USC Campus; USC Responds

March 25, 2015 Entertainment

CENTURY CITY, CA – FEBRUARY 07: Actress Taraji P. Henson attends the 67th Annual Directors Guild Of America Awards at the Hyatt Regency Century Plaza on February 7, 2015 in Century City, California. (Photo by Alberto E. Rodriguez/Getty Images for DGA)

Escapism is the name of the game on actress Taraji P. Henson’s hit series Empire, which sees her onscreen alter ego Cookie Lyon decked out in furs, spewing shocking one-liners and living the lavish life of hip-hop royalty.

But, in real life, Henson and her son Marcel, 20, have faced the harsh realities of racial profiling, she says.

In an interview with Uptown magazine, Henson shares that she decided to transfer her son to D.C.’s Howard University rather than the University of Southern California after he was the alleged victim of a racially-charged encounter with campus police.

“My child has been racially profiled. He was in Glendale, California and did exactly everything the cops told him to do, including letting them illegally search his car. It was bogus because they didn’t give him the ticket for what he was pulled over for. Then he’s at University of Southern California, the school that I was going to transfer him to, when police stopped him for having his hands in his pockets. So guess where he’s going? Howard University. I’m not paying $50K so I can’t sleep at night wondering is this the night my son is getting racially profiled on campus.”
