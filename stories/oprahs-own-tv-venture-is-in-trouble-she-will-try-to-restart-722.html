Oprah’s OWN TV Venture Is In Trouble; She Will Try to Restart

October 10, 2011 Uncategorized

Oprah Winfrey’s next chapter has been anything but easy. While she compared her 25-year-long talk show to “breathing everyday,” this new project has left her breathless. 

Discovery CEO Zaslav with OWN CEO Winfrey.
The joint partnership with Discovery Communications to launch a hit cable network under her moniker has so far flat lined. Not only has it been absent from the national conversation, when people are talking about OWN: Oprah Winfrey Network it is with an unflattering narrative: Weak ratings, muddled programming, a potential failure.
Now, with Winfrey at the helm as CEO, Discovery and OWN are gearing up for a relaunch of sorts. Beginning on Monday, the cable leaders will place all their efforts behind its new fall programming lineup, and its two brightest stars. Comedienne Rosie O’Donnell will host a 7PM daily talk show, The Rosie Show, which leads in to Winfrey’s first major contribution to the network, Oprah’s Lifeclass at 8PM. The latter will use select clips from the 4,561 Oprah episodes, along with new footage of Winfrey sharing personal revelations and life lessons.
According to today’s New York Post, Discovery is expected to spend $15 million on an ad push-50% more than it spent on the Jan. 1 launch of the network–on top of the over $200 million it’s already pumped in since conception. The two new prime-time shows will also be simulcast on four other Discovery channels, including TLC, Planet Green, Investigation Discovery, and Discovery Fit + Health. Ads will appear in print, across Discovery properties and on competitor networks.
Adding to a laundry list of problems the channel has faced–too high on the channel guide, unclear brand, not enough programming or Oprah–Winfrey herself admitted that she and her team had been too distracted by wrapping up her marquee show that not enough attention was paid to OWN’s launch. O’Donnell agreed that was a major issue on a conference call yesterday but resolved that ratings would turn around now that OWN leaders are focused.
When I spoke to Discovery CEO David Zaslav recently, he didn’t seem worried. Instead, he voiced excitement over the new programming, confirmed that the network’s 10 ad partners on multi-year deals were committed, and beamed that having Winfrey at the center of the channel was exactly what was needed. “This is the moment we’ve been waiting for,” he told me.
Others, however, point to potential issues in the master plan. Robert Thompson, the founding director of the Bleier Center for Television & Popular Culture at Syracuse University, said O’Donnell may be the network’s savior, “but I wouldn’t bet the farm.” O’Donnell’s career has featured several reinventions, but can she recreate the success of a six-year syndicated show that ended in 2002? More importantly, Thompson asks: Can Winfrey still do a hit show?
