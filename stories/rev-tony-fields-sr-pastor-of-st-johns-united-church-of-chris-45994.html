Rev. Tony Fields Sr., Pastor of St. John’s United Church of Christ in Fredericksburg, Pennsylvania, says It Is a Challenge Being a Black Minister Pastoring a Predominantly White Congregation

November 20, 2014 Black

The Rev. Tony Fields Sr. prays at the altar of St. John’s United Church of Christ in Fredericksburg recently. Fields was installed Oct. 26 as the pastor the church. (Bill Warner — Lebanon Daily News)

The Rev. Tony Fields Sr. could have taken the easy path, stayed in his comfort zone and found a black church in need of a pastor.

But God was pointing to a more challenging path, according to Fields, an African-American, who was installed Oct. 26 as the pastor of St. John’s United Church of Christ, where the congregation is predominantly white.

He is the first black minister in the 275-year history of the church located at 141 W. Main St.

“I think that’s why I’m here in the UCC, because it’s challenging to be a black minister in a white church,” he said recently while working in the church office. “I could have easily went back to a black church.”

Fields arrived in Fredericksburg in June, but not as a total stranger: He was the church’s seminarian in 2006-07. Fields says he has been well-received, yet there was no mistaking the surprise his presence initially caused for many church members.

“When people see me, they don’t say a word. But their body language speaks,” he said. “It’s like, whoa, he is African-American. There is a white politeness. They don’t say anything. But their body language speaks.

“But what I try to do is make that engagement. Let’s get beyond that. So I’ll walk up to them and shake their hand and say hello. I want them to see that I’m human,” Fields said.
