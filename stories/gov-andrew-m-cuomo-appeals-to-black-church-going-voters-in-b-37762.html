Gov. Andrew M. Cuomo Appeals to Black Church-going Voters in Brooklyn and Queens

October 6, 2014 Black

Gov. Andrew M. Cuomo, center, at the Christian Cultural Center in Brooklyn on Sunday. Credit Michael Nagle for The New York Times

Gov. Andrew M. Cuomo has been a reluctant campaigner so far this year, wrapping himself in the cocoon of incumbency and waiting patiently for what he clearly hopes will be a landslide re-election.

But even the most prohibitive favorites need to give attention to important constituencies, and on Sunday, Mr. Cuomo visited churches in Queens and Brooklyn to ask black congregants to support his bid for a second term. The visits came a day after he took a bus tour across upstate New York to court female voters.

On Sunday, offering a list of priorities tailored to his audience, Mr. Cuomo pledged to raise the minimum wage; expand jobs programs for young minorities; and work to see that schools in poor neighborhoods are put on an equal footing with those in affluent communities.

Mr. Cuomo seemed intent on communicating humility. Visiting the Greater Allen A.M.E. Cathedral in Jamaica, Queens, he spoke in raw terms about setbacks he had suffered, recalling his unsuccessful 2002 bid for governor and his divorce from Kerry Kennedy.

“I had a marriage that did not work, and it was a personally very difficult time for me,” Mr. Cuomo said. “And there are very few things as useless in life as a failed politician. You run and you lose, it’s amazing how many really great friends all of a sudden disappear. And I was at the bottom. I was lower than low.”

He then thanked the church’s pastor, the Rev. Floyd H. Flake, for giving him a “second chance” when Mr. Cuomo pursued a run for state attorney general in 2006. Later, visiting the Christian Cultural Center in East New York, Brooklyn, the governor expressed similar gratitude to the pastor there, the Rev. A. R. Bernard.
