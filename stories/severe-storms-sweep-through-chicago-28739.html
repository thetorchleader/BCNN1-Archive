Severe Storms Sweep Through Chicago

Date not recorded in the original archive National

Lightning can be seen over U. S. Cellular Field in Chicago. The game between the Chicago White Sox and the Los Angeles Angels was canceled due too heavy rains and thunderstorms. (Nuccio DiNuzzo, Chicago Tribune)

The storms came later than expected and without some of the vengeance forecasters feared, but heavy downpours and stiff winds came nonetheless Monday evening.

Lightning flashed in the prematurely dark downtown sky just after 7:20 p.m., and a steady rain began falling minutes later, the beginning of what could be hours of serious weather in the city.

The storm system, which raged across Nebraska, Iowa and Wisconsin earlier in the afternoon, promised heavy winds and held the potential for localized flooding, said officials at the National Weather Service.

Rain pelted the collar counties earlier in the evening, and radar showed thunderstorms continued there at 7:30 p.m. Even before the first raindrops fell in the city, the White Sox postponed their evening game because of the promise of ominous weather, eventually rescheduling it for Tuesday.

Reports of strong winds and heavy downpours streamed in from the suburbs as the brunt of the storm approached downtown Chicago just after 7 p.m., but in most cases it seemed to be a heavy storm and not an imminent danger.

“It wasn’t as widespread across northern Illinois as previously anticipated,” said weather service meteorologist Stephen Rodriguez, “but we still are monitoring a line of storms pushing into northwest Indiana and a second line of storms.”

Forecasters cautioned that the potential for severe winds and even localized flooding remained, and a severe thunderstorm warning remained in effect at 8 p.m. for parts of Will and Kankakee counties and northwest Indiana.

The storm darkened the downtown Chicago skies hours earlier than normal Monday, and was expected to bring heavy rains, wind gusts of up to 70 mph and possible hail to the region for hours to come.
