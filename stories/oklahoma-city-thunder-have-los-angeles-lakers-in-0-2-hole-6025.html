Oklahoma City Thunder Have Los Angeles Lakers In 0-2 Hole

May 17, 2012 Uncategorized

Kobe Bryant and coach Mike Brown said they weren’t concerned going into Game 2 vs. the Oklahoma City Thunder, even though they were humiliated by 29 points in the opener.
After the Los Angeles Lakers blew a seven-point lead late in the fourth quarter Wednesday, including two turnovers and an airball jumper by Bryant in the final two minutes of the 77-75 loss, they should be.
The Lakers were able to force the half-court, defensive battle they wanted but still are down 2-0 in this second-round series.
And even though this veteran team still has most of its core intact that won NBA championships in 2009 and 2010, with Bryant, Pau Gasol, Andrew Bynum and Metta World Peace, they seem deflated and generally don’t play well from behind.
The Lakers weren’t blown out, but questions about Bryant’s decision-making and Brown’s job security will be inevitable. Brown is in the first year of a four-year, $20 million deal.
Not only must Los Angeles hold serve at home, but to have a shot at coming from behind they must win in Oklahoma City, where they’re now 0-3 this season.
The Lakers were able to pound Oklahoma City inside with 7-footers Bynum and Gasol with great success as Brown chose to work his offense from the inside-out.
Brown, a disciple of San Antonio Spurs coach Gregg Popovich, is noted as a defensive coach and already has come under fire for his offense.
Though the Lakers won NBA championships from 2009 and 2010, owner Jerry Buss wanted the team to move away from then-coach Phil Jackson’s triangle offense and be more entertaining.
That hasn’t quite happened. Even though Brown led the Lakers to a third-place finish in the Western Conference, there was a Jan. 5-28 stretch when they failed to reach 100 points in 13 consecutive games. They were 7-6.
