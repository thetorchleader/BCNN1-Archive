Georgia Man Pleads Guilty to Attempting to Support and Join ISIS

May 28, 2015 National

Mug shot of 37-year-old Leon Nathan Davis, who is accused of trying to aid ISIS (Courtesy: Richmond County Sheriff’s Office)

Terrorism in Augusta seems like a far cry, but a local man walked away in handcuffs and chains after appearing in federal court for his attempt to connect with a violent group overseas.

The Federal Bureau of Investigations has been keeping a close eye on Leon Nathan Davis, III.  The 37-year-old Augusta man is in federal custody after he pled guilty to trying to help the terrorist organization Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant or ISIL, which is also known as Islamic State of Iraq and al-Sham or ISIS.

Assistant Special Agent to the Federal Bureau of Investigations, Angela Tobon, told media at a press conference outside of the court, “Us along with the Georgia Board of Pardons and Parole have been investigating him for over a year knowing that he had intentions to join ISIS.”

FBI agents explained how they caught Davis at Hartsfield-Jackson International Airport  in Atlanta, trying to fly to Turkey October 24 of last year.  Tobon said he purchased the ticket the day prior with hopes of being smuggled into Syria to join up with ISIS.

Patrick Holsey, with the State Board of Pardons and Parole, said, “He was initially arrested on a parole violation.  His attempt to leave the country at the time was a violation of his parole.”

Court documents show Davis was charged with Providing Material Support to a Designated Foreign Terrorist Organization. FBI agents said Davis confessed in court that he wanted to use himself to help the terrorist group.

“He stated he was going to join ISIS and either teach or fight, whatever they needed him for,” Tobon said.
