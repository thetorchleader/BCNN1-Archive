Charnice Milton, D.C. News Reporter, Shot and Killed While Waiting for Bus

May 30, 2015 Black

Charnice Milton
MY FOX DC

27-year-old Charnice Milton was born in Southeast Washington D.C., an area plagued with crime and poverty. She would go on to graduate from college at Ball State and earn a Masters from Syracuse. She wanted to make a difference in her community so she returned home to take a job with a local newspaper.

“She had her own condition of Asperger’s and it was presumed because of her special condition, she would never excel and never achieve any great feat,” Milton’s father Ken McClendon told My Fox DC.

“Yet watching our daughter walk across the stage at Bishop McNamara High School with honors, then going to Ball State and graduating with honors, then graduating at Syracuse with honors, and then staying here in Southeast Washington D.C., when she could have gone anywhere.”

On Wednesday night, a little after 9:30 p.m., Milton was shot and killed while she waited for the bus. She was across the street from Capital Community News where she worked since 2012.

“What we do know is that out of cowardice, a young man grabbed my daughter and put himself between the bullet and his life,” Ken McClendon, the victim’s father, told My Fox DC.

According to the Washington Post, a gunman drove up on a motorcycle and began firing shots. Milton was not the intended target. She would be transported to a local hospital where she was pronounced dead.

DC Mayor Muriel Bowser is calling for the public’s help in solving the case.
