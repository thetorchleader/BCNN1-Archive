6 Questions Chuck Todd Should Ask President Obama on “Meet the Press” This Sunday

September 6, 2014 Opinion

This Sept. 1, 2013 photo released by NBC shows NBC Political Director Chuck Todd on “Meet the Press” in Washington. (AP Photo by/NBC, William B. Plowman, File)

Newly minted “Meet The Press” moderator Chuck Todd will interview President Obama on Saturday. That interview will air on Sunday’s “Meet” — Todd’s inaugural show.

In advance of Todd’s sitdown, I brainstormed — along with some smart political heads in the WaPo newsroom — a handful of questions Todd could ask that might elicit interesting or illuminating responses from President Obama.  The six best are below. (And, if Chuck asks any of them, I demand an on-air shoutout. I kid. Or do I?)

1. What, specifically, is your timeline for making a decision on using your executive authority in regards the nation’s millions of undocumented immigrants?

Obama has been extremely cagey when it comes to the timing of his once-scheduled “summer” executive order regarding millions of undocumented Americans in the country. At a press conference Friday before leaving Wales, Obama dodged a question about whether concerns voiced by Senate Democrats about an announcement on immigration before the election had changed his timing plan at all. “My expectation is that very soon I will be considering what the next steps are,” Obama said, saying, well, something. Later, he added that he would make the announcement “soon” — although whether that means sometime before the Nov. 4  election is anyone’s guess. The one on one format should allow Todd to push Obama on specifics far more than he was willing to engage in on Friday.

2. You’ve taken a lot of heat for your summation of your foreign policy as “don’t do stupid [stuff].”  So, what sentence (or two) better describes your overall approach to foreign policy?

The last few months of Obama’s presidency have been dominated by foreign policy crises — from the Islamic State in Iraq to Russia’s invasion of Ukraine to the fighting in the Middle East. And, through it all, he has had to weather critiques — from Republicans and even some in his own party — that he lacks any cohesive foreign policy vision.  While boiling down such a vision to a few sentences is always tricky — and even trickier for a reporter to get a politician to do — this seems like an opportunity for Obama to re-set the debate in a meaningful way by introducing a phrase that isn’t “don’t do stupid [stuff]” into the national (and international) conversation.

3. Despite suffering some setbacks during his time in office, Bill Clinton reshaped the Democratic Party. To what extent have you done that, and which of these changes are most likely to outlast you once you’re gone? (Thanks to Juliet Eilperin for this one.)

This is a double-whammy: It’s a smart way to address the Obama legacy questions while also subtly raising the criticism — or at least the analysis — that the Obama phenomenon is entirely tied to the president and has not helped his party in any meaningful, long term way. It also forces Obama — assuming he would play ball with a question like this — to compare Clinton’s legacy to his own. [Rubs hands gleefully.]
