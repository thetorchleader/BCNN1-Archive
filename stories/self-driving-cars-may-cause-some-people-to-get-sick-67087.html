Self-driving Cars May Cause Some People to Get Sick

April 13, 2015 Technology

Ever tried to read a book in the car? Expect to have that same queasy feeling if you’re ever lucky enough to own or ride in a self-driving vehicle.

Researchers at the University of Michigan’s Transportation Research Institute say that while self-driving cars could make the roads safer and improve mobility, they might also make some people sick. Why? If you don’t have to focus on driving, you’re more likely to engage in activities that increase the likelihood and severity of motion sickness.

The researchers asked more than 3,200 adults in the U.S., India, China, Japan, Great Britain, and Australia what kinds of activities they would do instead of driving in a fully autonomous vehicle. More than a third of Americans said they would do things that could exacerbate motion sickness, like reading, texting, watching movies or TV, playing games, or working.

As a result, the researchers concluded that 6 to 12 percent of American adults riding in self-driving cars would experience “moderate or severe” motion sickness. It would be a similar story for those in India, China, Great Britain, and Australia.

“Motion sickness is expected to be more of an issue in self-driving vehicles than in conventional vehicles,” researcher Michael Sivak said in a statement. “The reason is that the three main factors contributing to motion sickness — conflict between vestibular (balance) and visual inputs, inability to anticipate the direction of motion and lack of control over the direction of motion—are elevated in self-driving vehicles.”
