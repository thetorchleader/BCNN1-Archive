San Jose City College Professor Writes Book Detailing the History of Blacks in San Francisco

April 14, 2014 Uncategorized

Author Jan Batiste Adkins, shown outside Third Baptist Church, tried to focus more on black contributions than struggles. Photo: Carlos Avila Gonzalez, The Chronicle

Jan Batiste Adkins, an adjunct faculty member at San Jose City College, had an epiphany one day while teaching history to a classroom of minority students.

“They would say, ‘I don’t see me in the book,’ ” Adkins says. “I would say, ‘But African Americans made quite a difference. Just look in the book.’ And I realized, ‘Oh, wait a minute, it isn’t in the book.’ ”

So she wrote a book, “African Americans of San Francisco” (Arcadia Publishing, $21.99), which captures the daily lives of prominent African Americans living in the city from the 19th century to the near-present. Adkins’ subjects, from Hall of Fame outfielder Willie Mays to influential pastor Dr. Howard Thurman, are examined as both important historic figures and human beings leading purposeful private lives.

“Why wait for someone else?” Adkins says. “Why not me?”

Q: Your book seems to focus more on the positive contributions of African Americans rather than their struggles.

A: You’re right. Many books tend to identify the struggle – all the things that didn’t happen, missed opportunities. I wanted to focus on the success stories – the positive changes and contributions African Americans made to the community. We can all discuss struggles, but I wanted to depict the opportunities – in everything I do.

Q: But you can’t completely gloss over the negativity, right?

A: I try very hard not to slant the story. But, yes, there was discrimination – in housing, in education. I brought those things out, but I think the contributions far outweigh everything else.

Q: The book prominently features people like the Rev. Frederick Douglas Haynes and places like the Third Baptist Church of San Francisco. It seems churches and pastors are a theme in your book. Why do you think that is?

A: Churches and pastors have always been important to the African American community (since) the days of slavery. Because of segregation, blacks had to create their own churches. The church is not just a spiritual institution – it’s one that promotes education, the family and civic duties.
