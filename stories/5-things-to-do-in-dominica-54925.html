5 Things To Do in Dominica

February 3, 2015 Lifestyle

The Commonwealth of Dominica (not to be confused with the Dominican Republic!) sits midway along the Eastern Caribbean archipelago, just a few miles from Martinique to the south and Guadeloupe to the north. Paul Crask

Little Dominica is a natural paradise that is often mistakenly lumped in with the Dominican Republic. While both Caribbean destinations have their appeal, they couldn’t be more different from each other. The D.R. is a tourism powerhouse of broad beaches and big resorts; Dominica is a green jewel, with its greatest appeal being its lush interior of waterfalls and verdant mountains, and its intimate guesthouses. Visitors won’t find a wild nightlife of casinos and nightclubs in Dominica, or over the top beach activities like banana boat rides and bungee jumping. The greatest majority of visitors to Dominica come for the island’s natural attractions and activities, its hiking, snorkeling and scuba diving.

Plunging Under Trafalgar Falls

Waterfall hikes can be full-day sweaty affairs with a beautiful waterfall as the hike’s reward, or they can require less exertion and result in the same reward. Trafalgar Falls, which is found on the west side of Dominica’s Morne Trois Pitons National Park, is one of the easily accessible waterfalls on the island, and also one of the most magnificent. The falls are about a 20-minute drive from Roseau, the main town on the island. Once at the visitors center it’s an about a 10-minute walk to the falls. Trafalgar is actually twin waterfalls. The locals refer to the upper one as “Father” and the lower waterfall, “Mother.” The upper waterfall plunges down to hot sulfur springs, where hikers can carefully ease themselves into the beneficial waters. The lower waterfall forms a natural swimming pool at its base. Travelers joining an organized tour should choose carefully, since some tours only bring the group far enough along the trail to an observation platform to view the falls from a distance. If a person has their heart set on a waterfall dip, they’ll be out of luck. Also, the greatest number of people visits the falls during midday, so hikers should arrive earlier or later if they want relative seclusion.
