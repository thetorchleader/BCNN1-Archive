Michigan’s Ruth Mott Foundation Hires First Black President

September 6, 2014 Black

Handy Lindsey Jr., the first black president of the Ruth Mott Foundation.
Courtesy Photo

The Ruth Mott Foundation has hired its first African American president.

After a nearly year-long search, Handy Lindsey Jr. officially began his tenure as president on Monday, Sept. 1.

Lindsay brings more than 30 years of philanthropic experience to the Ruth Mott Foundation.

“He was the principle architect and director for three foundations that he took from concept to operation, and he has worked at all levels of philanthropy,” said foundation chair Maryanne Mott in a statement.

“Handy has both the skills and the passion for building capacity in nonprofit organizations, and he has worked in a variety of settings from rural to urban and with communities that mirror the characteristics of Genesee County.”

Lindsey has three degrees from the University of Chicago, including a master’s in business administration. In 1980, he joined Chicago Area Foundation for Legal Service as a staff associate, and was promoted to executive director.

He also helped develop the East St. Louis Community Foundation, where he served as interim executive director in 1985-1986. He became executive director and treasurer of the Field Foundation of Illinois in 1988, and was later promoted to CEO. In 2004, he became president and CEO of the Cameron Foundation in Petersburg, Va.
