Long Island, NY, Crippled by Record Rain and Flooding; 1 Dead, Some Families Forced to Evacuate Homes

August 14, 2014 National

Vehicles including a town truck stranded as flood waters rise on Long Island Avenue in Holtsville, Long Island. (CHRISTIE M FARRIELLA FOR NEW YORK DAILY NEWS)

A record-setting rainstorm swept through Long Island early Wednesday, killing one person on a flooded highway and forcing several drivers to abandon their vehicles in the rising waters.

Suffolk County Police Chief James Burke said a person driving an SUV died after the vehicle was hit by a tractor-trailer on the Long Island Expressway near Dix Hills during the peak of the storm about 4:40 a.m.

Investigators believe the victim was driving slowly when it was hit by the rig, Burke said. The vehicle was engulfed in flames when emergency workers arrived and the victim was burned beyond recognition.

Firefighters later rescued motorists stranded on a handful of roads including the Southern State Parkway near Baldwin after the storm dumped up to five inches in just one hour before 6 a.m., officials said.

The rainfall caused trees to uproot and several power outages, but no other serious injuries were immediately reported.

In Islip, located on the south shore of Suffolk County, rainfall had totaled more than 13 inches by 8 a.m. – almost double the previous daily record of 6.7. inches set in August 1990, said Joe Pollina, a meteorologist at the National Weather Service.

The flash flooding also caused delays up to an hour for straphangers riding the Long Island Rail Road and customers were told to expect the impact to last throughout the morning.
