Great White Shark Spotted Off Coast of Washington

February 27, 2015 Science

Just when you thought it was safe to go back in the water: local residents of Ocean Shores, Washington have been warned that a great white shark has been spotted in the town’s coastal waters.

The state’s Department of Fish and Wildlife issued the warning after a necropsy of a dead seal found on a nearby beach confirmed the animal had fallen victim to a great white. The remains of the seal, which had been examined by both the DFW and the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, had been half-consumed, with only the rib cage and the upper half of its body intact; judging from the bite marks, the DFW and NOAA determined that a great white measuring around 18 feet long was the culprit.

So far, there hasn’t been any warning issued to locals to steer clear of the water, as attacks on humans are actually quite rare when it comes to great white sharks – despite what Hollywood movies might say. With local seal populations along the coast currently feeding on the smelt that inhabit the shoals of the region, the animals are often targeted by great whites and other predators like killer whales for food.
