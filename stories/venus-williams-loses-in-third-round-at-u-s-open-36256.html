Venus Williams Loses In Third Round at U.S. Open

August 30, 2014 Sports

Venus Williams lost to Sara Errani of Italy, 6-0, 0-6, 7-6 (5). Uli Seit for The New York Times

Almost concurrently, three women’s matches pounded away at the United States Open on Friday afternoon, and their combined effect was to all but detonate the expectations for one half of the draw.

Two matches were bona fide upsets: a 32-year-old former junior champion disposing of the No. 2 seed and a Swiss teenager bouncing the No. 6 seed. The third match went by the book according to the rankings but not according to anything else. That was Sara Errani’s 6-0, 0-6, 7-6 (5) victory over Venus Williams in a riveting and absurd match that kept the crowd at Arthur Ashe Stadium enthralled for two hours.

The score looked like a typographical error, but it accurately told the story of Williams’s pivoting between listless, error-prone swings and complete domination. In the end Errani, the No. 13 seed, played grittier points to win the tiebreaker. Errani had never beaten Williams, who was seeded 19th, and had never won more than three games in a set against her.

“Sometimes in tennis a match can change a lot,” Errani said. “These things happen. I was just trying to keep focus also in the second, but I stop a bit. I was a bit tired. I stop my legs a bit, not pushing too much, and she was coming in. So after the second, I just tried to relax a bit and tried to push again my body 100 percent.”

Errani might have expected to face the No. 2 seed, Simona Halep, in the fourth round. But Mirjana Lucic-Baroni wreaked more havoc on the draw with her 7-6 (6), 6-2 upset of Halep on the Grandstand Court. Lucic-Baroni, 32, had not reached the third round of the U.S. Open since 1998. She won the girls’ junior title at the Open in 1996.

“After so many years, to be here again, it’s incredible,” Lucic-Baroni said, describing a difficult journey of injuries and misfortune. “I wanted this so bad.”

The other upset was fashioned by the 17-year-old Belinda Bencic, who in her first Open dismantled the No. 6 seed, Angelique Kerber, 6-1, 7-5. Playing with all the spunk her age would suggest, Bencic jumped out to an early lead and did not flinch, even after going down an early break in the second set.

The noise level in Arthur Ashe was at a murmur as Williams and Errani set out on their bizarre alternating paths. The first set was a catastrophe for Williams, with 18 unforced errors, three double faults and a 30 percent success rate on her first serves. It was over in 26 minutes.

Williams seemed to rediscover her dominant self in the second set as she pounded 14 winners and kept Errani chasing powerful shots all over the court.

In the third, both players seemed to dig in, and the crowd responded with enthusiasm. Williams gained the upper hand and was serving for the set at 5-3 when she wavered again. She began committing errors once more, a skid that continued into the tiebreaker. When Errani won a pivotal point in a thrilling exchange at net between the two seasoned doubles players, she needed only one more winner to finish off the match.

“The last two points in the tiebreaker, she played really flawless tennis,” Williams said. “It was a little bit bad luck for me. But she played well.”

Williams played doubles with her sister Serena on Thursday night and she acknowledged that her busy schedule was not ideal for an early match Friday. After losing in singles, she won a second-round doubles match, her fourth contest in less than 48 hours. But she said playing doubles at the Open was important to her.

“I think the doubles always helps the singles, really,” she said. “I think in today’s match, when I got to the net, I usually volleyed really well.”
