Dozens Injured in 47-Vehicle Crash in Sarasota, FL

October 6, 2012 Uncategorized

Bob Sipkema and his wife were just approaching the Manatee and Sarasota county line on Interstate 75 when they saw the wall of vehicles.

Pictured: Massive car crash involving dozens of vehicles on the University Parkway overpass . In Sarasota October 5th,2012.PAT BRAMMELL/STAFF
“Everything was just stopped in front of me,” Sipkema said.
“There was just nothing I could do,” he said. “I jammed the brakes as hard as I could, but with the light rain on the road I just slid straight into it.”
The Sipkemas were one of 47 vehicles involved in a string of crashes at about 3 p.m. Friday that closed southbound I-75 for hours.
The numbers were staggering: 12 separate crashes, 52 injuries.
No fatalities were reported. Twenty-two people were hospitalized, three of which  were taken by medical helicopter; their injuries appeared to be serious but not life-threatening, said Florida Highway Patrol Trooper Kenneth Watson.
Southbound I-75 at the Manatee and Sarasota county line was closed from 3 p.m. to shortly after 9 p.m., FHP said.
The first crash happened in a heavy rain, and a chain reaction of collisions began, Watson said.
“When one person got into that crash, that vehicle began to rotate,” Watson said. “You had what you would call a domino effect.”
Vehicles involved in the crash included tractor-trailers, SUVs and passenger cars, Watson said.
David Williams, who was driving south when the accidents began, said the rain might have been a factor.
“Just as we drove into the rain, everything just stopped,” Williams said. “We got hit from behind by a car that was moving I think at the speed limit because he hit us so hard.”
Williams’ wife was taken to the hospital; he said her injuries did not seem to be major.
He couldn’t believe the size and magnitude of the crash.
“I’ve driven probably over a million miles but never been in anything like this,” Williams said. “I don’t even know which one of these cars hit us because everybody’s pointing in different directions.
