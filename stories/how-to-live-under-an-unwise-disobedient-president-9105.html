How to Live and Thrive Under an Unwise and Disobedient President

November 9, 2012 Uncategorized

by BCNN1 Editors

Many Christians in America do not consider our current president to be a good, moral leader of this nation. Some do not even believe that he is a Christian, despite his profession to the contrary. Of course, we at BCNN1 do not agree with the president’s positions on homosexuality, homosexual marriage, abortion and religious freedom. We know that God will continue to judge our nation if our leaders continue to promote and endorse such evil. It is our prayer and hope that President Obama will confess his sins and repent and have a change of heart and mind over the next four years and reverse course in these matters. This is not likely, but “with God all things are possible.”
However, for those of you who consider the president to be an “evil king”, please consider the fact that in the Bible, God’s people, the Israelites, had good kings and evil kings as well. Take a look at I Kings 15:9-11: “And in the twentieth year of Jeroboam king of Israel reigned Asa over Judah…And Asa did that which was right in the eyes of the Lord, as did David his father.” However, notice that just a few verses later, the Bible reads: “In the third year of Asa king of Judah began Baasha the son of Ahijah to reign over all Israel in Tirzah, twenty and four years. And he did evil in the sight of the Lord, and walked in the way of Jeroboam, and in his sin wherewith he made Israel to sin.” By the way, I don’t believe that homosexual marriage would have been successful at the ballot box if President Obama had not endorsed it. That is the power of presidential influence and that’s how a king or president can sin and lead other people to sin.
 
So, you see here that Israel had good kings who followed the Lord and evil kings who walked in sin and led the nation into sin. Not only that, but many Israelites had to live under heathen kings who worshipped false gods. For example, Joseph lived under Egyptian rulers. Daniel, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego lived under Babylonian rulers. Esther and Mordecai lived under Persian rulers. And first century Christians lived under Roman rulers.
 
Romans 15:4 says, “For whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning, that we through patience and comfort of the scriptures might have hope.” Considering that these examples of God’s people living under evil kings are included in the Bible, what can we learn from these situations that will guide us in living under an “evil” king in our own time.
 
1. First of all, remain faithful to God. Remember the experiences of Daniel and his friends once they were deported to Babylon? They were given the king’s meat and the king’s drink. However, they rejected it because that Babylonian food had been sacrificed to idols and some of it was cooked with blood which the Israelites were forbidden to eat. The Bible says that “Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king’s meat, nor with the wine which he drank: therefore he requested of the prince of the eunuchs that he might not defile himself.”
 
Daniel handled this issue with a respectful and tactful attitude. He did not create a scene or act as if what he was doing was a big deal. He humbly requested that he and his friends be given different food to eat. The Bible does not say that Daniel even told the Babylonian eunuch why he wanted to eat differently.
 
Daniel’s example should inform our own actions. The king’s evil and society’s evil is no excuse for us to indulge in wickedness. While living under an evil king, Christians should purpose in their hearts not to defile themselves, and remain loyal to God and obedient to his commands. Romans 12:21 says, “Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.”
 
2. Excel in whatever role God has placed you in. Whatever job God has given you to do — whether it is preaching, teaching, serving others, being a counselor, being a doctor, being a nurse, etc. — He expects you to do it well and do it with excellence. Daniel and his friends did this wonderfully. Daniel 1:19-20 tells us: “And the king communed with them; and among them all was found none like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah: therefore stood they before the king. And in all matters of wisdom and understanding, that the king enquired of them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and astrologers that were in all his realm.”
 
When they were taken to Babylon, the best of the Hebrew slaves were selected to be trained in Babylonian ‘wisdom, knowledge, and science’. Daniel and his friends excelled in this activity. They did their best learning and studying, not for the king, but for their God.
 
As Christians, we should do the same. Colossians 3:23 says, “whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men.” Just as the king noticed and was impressed by the excellence of Daniel and his friends, God can use you to make an impression on the “evil” king and cause him to act favorably to them.
 
3. Assist the king whenever you can. We see this example throughout Scripture, where God uses His people in a service role to aid the king to do something good. For example, God used Joseph to help save Egypt from famine. God used Daniel to serve as an adviser to at least two Babylonian kings and one Persian king. The three Hebrew boys rose up in Babylon to hold prominent government positions.
 
As Christians in America, we should not make the mistake of standing in opposition to an “evil” king so much so that we cannot congratulate and applaud him when he does something good. In fact, God wants us to be willing to serve the king if the king requests our service and if God opens the door for us to serve in a capacity that does not compromise our faith. Of course, we should not aid the king in doing anything evil for as the Bible says, “we ought to obey God rather than man.” John Wesley once said, “Do all the good you can, By all the means you can, In all the ways you can, In all the places you can, At all the times you can, To all the people you can, As long as ever you can.”
 
If the king is doing something good, assist him and support him in anyway that you can.
 
4. Speak out against evil when God calls you to do so. In the midst of the reign of an evil king, God will often raise up one (or several) of His people to be His mouthpiece to that king to pronounce warnings and judgment. When Saul disobeyed God, Samuel was God’s man to rebuke him. When David sinned, Nathan the prophet paid King David a visit to tell him, “thou art the man.” When King Belshazzar was partying and feasting, Daniel came in to proclaim God’s judgment which would come down on his kingdom that very night.
 
God may call you to be a voice of rebuke to the evil king when he is disobedient to God’s Word. If he does, you don’t have to be belligerent about it, you don’t have to call the man out of his name and not show due respect; instead, carry yourself with dignity, grace, class and humility. Respect the king, yet be unequivocal on God’s Word. Speak boldly, but with compassion. The king may not want to be your friend. He may not invite you to sit down for dinner. But being obedient to God is far more important than being in the good favor of an earthly ruler who will one day soon meet his Maker.
 
For those of you who may feel discouraged by the election results, remember that God has a role for you to play in this country for the next four years. Let us set out to play our role well.
 
from the Editors at BCNN1
