Man in Connecticut Charged with Homicide After 15-Month-Old Boy Dies in Hot Car

November 11, 2014 National

Benjamin Seitz, the 15-month-old son of Kyle and Lindsey Rogers-Seitz of Ridgefield, died July 7 after his father left him in the car for what police called “an extended period of time.” Photo: Contributed Photo

The father of a 15-month-old boy who died because he was trapped in a hot car all day has been charged with criminally negligent homicide.

The arrest Tuesday of Kyle Seitz on what is considered a minor homicide charge comes four months after a warm day in July, when the father forgot to drop off little Benjamin Seitz at day care and went to work in Danbury.

The child died from hyperthermia.

The story made headlines across the region. The longer the case went without police charging the 36-year-old father, who is due in state Superior Court in Danbury on Wednesday, the more observers wondered why it was not being handled with the same immediacy as other recent hot-car deaths in Connecticut, where parents were promptly charged.

There was no word Tuesday from Ridgefield police or from Danbury State’s Attorney Stephen Sedensky III about why it took so long to charge the father.

“Other than the basic facts of the case, there is nothing more I can discuss,” Sedensky said after the father turned himself into police on an arrest warrant.

The father’s lawyer, John Gulash, also declined to say anything other than his client had been released without bail.

Seitz and his wife have been proactive about sharing parts of their story with the media in the hope it will prevent future hot-car deaths. The couple agreed to a Time magazine feature published in September, for example, which showed a picture of Benjamin on his father’s shoulders just a few weeks before the toddler’s death.
