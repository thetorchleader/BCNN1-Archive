As Economy Gets Better, More Young Adults Are Leaving Home

January 31, 2015 Finances

The economy has been picking up solidly but it’s packing a secret weapon that could turbocharge growth this year: the millions of young adults still living with their parents.

Many are eager to move out on their own but have been held back by the vestiges of the Great Recession and housing crisis. Yet household formation — the addition of an owner- or renter-occupied unit to the U.S. housing stock— may finally be gaining some momentum. It’s a development that could pump life into the listless housing market and ripple across the economy.

The Census Bureau said this past week that the U.S. added an eye-popping 1.3 million occupied housing units in the fourth quarter and 1.7 million in 2014, mostly because of new renters. That’s up from an average of 570,000 units a year from 2007 to 2013, which was about half the normal rate based on population growth.

Many economists say these figures are often unreliable and volatile.Also, Census has eight different ways to measure household formation that produce varying estimates.
