What to Do With Veggie Scraps

April 23, 2015 From Around the Block

© Vasko Miokovic Photography

No one likes to waste food, but we do it more than we think. Actually, approximately 30 to 40% of the food supply in the U.S. is wasted, according to the USDA. And this happens at every stage of the food chain — the EPA says, on average, American households toss out more than a quarter of their food purchases!Awareness of the problem is slowly rising, thanks to various articles, studies, and organizations bringing it to the public’s attention. There are even plans for a Massachusetts store called Daily Table to sell expired yet perfectly edible foods and misshapen vegetables. And earlier this year, there was a food-waste pop-up orchestrated by renowned N.Y.C. chef Dan Barber, in partnership with chefs and food purveyors across the country.While it may take a chef to create a delicious burger out of leftover beet pulp, anyone can — with a little bit of effort — make a dent in the amount of useable food that gets trashed. Here are a few ideas to get you started using some of the stuff you might normally toss – you’ll eat well, and it’ll save you some money too!

1. Make vegetable stock.

Keep trimmings from onions, leeks, celery, carrots, mushrooms, green beans, corn cobs, potatoes, etc. in a bag in the freezer. When the bag gets big enough, throw them all into a pot with water to cover, bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer for about an hour (Simmer longer if the flavor seems weak.) Strain and discard the solids. Season with salt and pepper to taste.

2. Prepare beet greens.

Leafy beet greens are delicious prepared as you would collards or kale. Or use both the beets and their greens in this garlicky beet green pasta.

3. Cook broccoli stems.

They’re often the sweetest part of the broccoli! Cut into 1/4-inch thick slices (peel off the outer layer first if it seems tough) and cook along with the florets. Or, make broccoli pesto pasta!

4. Sauté chard stems.

They’re just as edible as the leaves, but they require longer cooking to soften. Thinly slice, then sauté in olive oil until tender.

5. Pickle watermelon rinds.

Pickling in general is a great way to use up an overabundant veggie. Why not use it for all that melon rind that would normally end up in the trash! These crunchy watermelon rind pickles are great in salads, or even sandwiches made with your broccoli pesto! Want to motivate yourself and encourage your family to waste less? Take the EPA’s End Food Waste challenge. And if you’ve got a creative way to use what others would normally throw into the compost pile, we’d love to hear it.
