“The Tatum Talks” Host, Tanya Tatum, Says ‘Preachers of L.A.’ Is Embarrassing Christianity

September 2, 2014 Black

Tanya Tatum

*A saint is just a sinner who fell down … but is anyone trying to get up?!

I was excited about last year’s premiere of “Preachers of L.A.” I wanted the cameras to shine a positive yet truthful spotlight on the lives of pastors, especially in a society where we expect Christians and their leaders to walk in perfection. Those expectations are stiff and unrealistic, setting most of those in the pulpit–and their congregations–up for failure.

What started off with profiles of six men bearing the responsibility of leading their flocks while balancing between work and home, has quickly developed into some unattractive displays of why Christians get a bad hypocritical rep. Although I knew drama would have to play a role in some of the story lines in order to draw viewers, I was hoping it would be in a way that didn’t overshadow the positive attributes of leading a Christian lifestyle. Yeah…no. T’ain’t what’s going down on Wednesday nights.

Bishop Noel Jones, a popular and profound pastor has been in an uncommitted, unconfirmed and unannounced relationship for more than 16 years with a woman who puts up a front that she doesn’t want more from the commitment phobic cleric. Week after week, she defends her right to remain unmarried when in the company of the pastors’ wives, and turns around with hope in her eyes that someday she’ll be “The only One” when she is in Jones’ presence. Jones strings her along, and she happily follows for the ride (and I presume the money). They continue to pretend that we don’t know they are having “relations,” but if you believe that, I have an igloo in Jamaica to sell you. Jones has a following of 30,000 members, and the example he is setting is disturbing as he carries on in longstanding sin.
