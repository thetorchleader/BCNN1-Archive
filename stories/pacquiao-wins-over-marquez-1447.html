Pacquiao Wins Over Marquez

November 13, 2011 Uncategorized

In a decision that sent the sellout crowd of 16,383 at the MGM Grand into an angry frenzy, Manny Pacquiao scored a controversial and unpopular majority decision victory against his sharp and determined rival Juan Manuel Marquez in the third fight of their trilogy Saturday night.
The decision caused the partisan Mexican crowd to explode into boos, jeering, chanting and derison at Pacquiao’s victory.
Pacquiao (54-3-2), who doubles as a congressman from the Philippines, retained his WBO welterweight title with the vctory, but it was an extremely unpopular one in the jam-packed and raucous Grand Garden Arena.
Marquez had jumped on the ropes in celebration and anticipation of what he thought was finally the one thing missing from his stellar career — a victory over Pacquiao.
The celebration turned to sheer disappointment when the decision was announced, and Marquez immediately left the ring.
The booing grew so loud that HBO’s Max Kellerman could not even interview Pacquiao, who with the victory retained his WBO welterweight title, over the noise.
“Of course they’re unhappy, they’re fans of Marquez,” said Pacquiao, who was guaranteed $22 million for the fight. “But I clearly won the fight.”
That’s the way judges Dave Moretti and Glenn Trowbridge saw it, anyway. Moretti scored it 115-113 and Trowbridge scored it 116-112 for Pacquiao. The third judge, Robert Hoyle, scored it a draw.
Nearly all of the writers on press row scored it in favor of Marquez, many by large margins.
“This is the second robbery of the two fights we’ve had,” said Marquez, who was guaranteed $5 million. “This one was even more clear than the first. I don’t know what else in the ring I can do to beat this guy.”
Marquez’s trainer, Nacho Beristain, said “It was robbery of the utmost.”
Indeed, Marquez, 38, said in his postfight press conference that he was so disappointed in the decision that he would have to take a long, hard look at the possibility of retirement.
“Honestly, it is a result of the fight that makes me think about retirement, because I prepared myself so hard, and 18 years of my career I’ve been working very hard,” Marquez said. “This fight was the biggest fight, we really wanted to tell the judges to score the fight the way it was happening, not the way they scored it.
“I am frustrated, right now, very frustrated.”
