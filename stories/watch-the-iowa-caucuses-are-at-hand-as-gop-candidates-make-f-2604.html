WATCH: The Iowa Caucuses Are At Hand as GOP Candidates Make Final Pitches to Voters

January 3, 2012 Uncategorized

Former House speaker Newt Gingrich, who led Iowa and national polls in November, said Monday he did not expect to win Tuesday’s Iowa caucuses, but will declare victory because he has survived an onslaught of negative advertising.
Despite his prediction, Gingrich said the race is too volatile to pick a winner, citing the final Iowa Poll over the weekend that showed 41% of likely caucusgoers still undecided.
Gingrich joined other Republican contenders in Iowa in making their final pitches for support Monday. Former Massachusetts governor Mitt Romney, who leads most Iowa polls, spent much of his time in eastern Iowa, while Texas Rep. Ron Paul rallied supporters in Des Moines and central Iowa. That’s also where former Pennsylvania senator Rick Santorum, who’s surging in recent polls, asked growing crowds to push him into the lead.
Leader keeps changing
The race to win this year’s Iowa caucuses has been particularly volatile, as most polls have shown Romney and a rotating cast of candidates at the top of the field. Two of those candidates, Rep. Michele Bachmann of Minnesota and Texas Gov. Rick Perry, toured Iowa Monday to rally their flagging support.
Bachmann, who won the Iowa straw poll in August, aired her first Iowa television advertisement Monday. The spot featured several photos of Bachmann while campaigning and a smaller photo of former British prime minister Margaret Thatcher, the “Iron Lady” of the 1980s who Bachmann now calls her political inspiration.
Meanwhile, Perry led a bus tour of western Iowa with a host of campaign surrogates, including Louisiana Gov. Bobby Jindal, his most prominent supporter from outside Texas. He and other candidates started to criticize Santorum, a sign of the latter’s rising support.
Focus on Obama
Romney told about 600 people at the Weber Paper warehouse in Dubuque, Iowa, that he could turn around businesses and help them grow, unlike President Obama. “This was the president, remember, who said, ‘If you let me borrow $787 billion, I’ll keep unemployment below 8%,” Romney said. “It hasn’t been below 8% since.”
After staying away from many of the traditional Iowa campaign events, such as the August straw poll in Ames, Romney has spent more time here in the days before the caucuses. He has stayed focused primarily on Obama and the November general election, despite not rising above 25% support in polls here.
