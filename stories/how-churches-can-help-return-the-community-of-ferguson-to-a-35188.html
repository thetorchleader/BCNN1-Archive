How Churches Can Help Return the Community of Ferguson to a Level of Trust

August 24, 2014 Christian

A makeshift memorial for Michael Brown on the spot on a Ferguson, Mo., street where his body lay for about four hours after he was shot by a police officer on Aug. 9. Credit Joe Raedle/Getty Images

A pastor in the St. Louis suburb ripped by riots, break-ins, and racial unrest in recent days believes churches can play a major role in returning the community to a level of trust among residents.

Churches in Ferguson, Missouri, are working to restore the community after days of riots and lootings. The crimes occurred after police officer Darren Wilson shot and killed black teenager Michael Brown. Stoney Shaw, senior pastor at First Baptist Church of Ferguson, says what the community is working towards is truth – but that demands facts.

“Secondly, we want justice,” he continues. “You know, if the police officer went over his bounds then he should be prosecuted. If he did what was right then let’s don’t crucify him.”

Thirdly, the pastor says the community must work together to restore the trust that existed before the incident. “It’s going to take a lot of talking and listening to each other and caring for each other – and our churches have to continue doing what they’re doing and that is helping people, loving people, listening to people.”
