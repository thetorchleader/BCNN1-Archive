Concord Church in Dallas Celebrates the Life of Pitcher and Advocate William Blair Jr.

April 27, 2014 African-American

Louis DeLuca/Staff Photographer
Mourners marked the life of Elite News founder and former Negro Leagues baseball player William Blair Jr. on Friday at Concord Church in Dallas. Blair died Sunday at 92.

Hundreds of friends and family members and dozens of elected officials gathered Friday at Concord Church to celebrate and remember William Blair Jr., a Negro Leagues pitcher and longtime advocate for blacks in North Texas.

Blair, who died Easter Sunday at 92, was remembered as a tough man who took joy in serving his community and honoring those who he felt were forgotten.

City, county and state officials, family members and pastors said he felt as comfortable speaking at a small southwest Dallas church as he did at City Hall. While strong and gruff, he was a pastor’s best friend and founded the Elite News, a weekly newspaper serving black churches in Dallas-Fort Worth.

“He was a mover and shaker in this community like no other,” Dallas Mayor Mike Rawlings said. “He broke so many barriers. He was something I admire. He never accepted things the way they were. He was an innovator.”

The three-hour service was a relay of people speaking and singing. The organ cooed and thundered with a gospel swing when pastors spoke words of comfort to Blair’s family.

More than 30 people shared stories about Blair, describing him as the godfather of getting things done, like helping politicians get elected. He spoke harsh words to accomplish things, frequently followed by “but baby, you know I love you,” and often with a cigar hanging out of his mouth.

“I saw Dad’s will,” said son Robert Blair, wearing a fedora just like his father frequently wore. “He was very generous with each and every one of you.

“He left you the legacy of a lifetime of service.”

Judges, former school board members and Dallas County District Attorney Craig Watkins recalled lunch meetings with Blair at Luby’s that began their friendships with him.

“I told him I wanted to run for DA, and his cigar fell out of his mouth,” Watkins said. “Later when we won the election, he said, ‘Well, looks like I got myself a district attorney elected.’”

Those new to town or new to the game of politics sought his blessing and advice. Not just because of his connections, they said, but because of the man he was.

Blair served in the Army during World War II, becoming the youngest black first sergeant in the war. In 1947, when mainstream newspapers neglected to cover black college sports, he founded Southwest Sports News to publish scores from games. The paper was later renamed Highlight News and then Elite News in 1960.
