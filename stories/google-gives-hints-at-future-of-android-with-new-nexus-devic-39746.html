Google Gives Hints at Future of Android With New Nexus Device Lineup

October 15, 2014 Technology

Google’s Android 5.0, “Lollipop”

Google’s Nexus lineup isn’t just an array of powerful, well-designed products. It’s also meant to be a show of where Android is going, and where Google wants manufacturers and carriers to focus.

So when Google says “be together, not the same,” it’s taking its philosophy of diversity to another level with the new Nexus lineup. Let’s take a quick look at how Google is setting up Android for 2015.

Big windows, not small phones. I hate this message, but it’s there. The phablet trend was all about mobile devices becoming primary Internet access devices, and about people wanting larger windows on the Web world. Google sees that as the future. For now, larger phones are also Apple’s weakness, so the Nexus 6 pushes the size envelope and plays to Android’s strengths, pushing Apple onto its back foot.

If you want quick, glanceable, one-handed computing, maybe you should get an Android-powered watch.

High-end chipset diversity. The Nexus 6 has a Qualcomm processor. The Nexus 9 runs Nvidia Tegra. The Nexus Player is a 64-bit Intel chip. Over the past few years, we’ve seen Qualcomm dominate the premium mobile processor market, fending off all comers. The other chipset makers to find significant success, most notably Mediatek, do so by undercutting Qualcomm’s prices rather than by offering differentiated performance.

Google really doesn’t like this, because nobody likes to be too dependent on one partner (see the next point.) Intel has had trouble getting smartphone clients because it’s still behind on modem integration, but it has an ongoing partnership with Asus and it’s a big deal in the set-top box world. Nvidia’s Tegra K1 plays out really well for big-screen gaming experiences, and Qualcomm is still the world’s leader in modems.

I’m also wondering if it’s telling that while successful low-cost chipset makers like Rockchip, Mediatek, and Allwinner are all coming out of Taiwan and China, Google picked three American chipset companies here.
