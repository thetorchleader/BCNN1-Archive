Rabbi, 3 Children Shot Dead Outside Jewish School in France

March 19, 2012 Uncategorized

France was plunged into mourning and national outrage Monday by the terrorist-style killings of three young children and a rabbi as they gathered for classes at a Jewish school in a quiet residential neighborhood of Toulouse.

The lone assassin rode up on a motor scooter and opened fire with two pistols, first shooting at a group of waiting children outside the school and then following some of them into a courtyard as they ran for safety toward the building, a local prosecutor, Michel Valet, told reporters. In addition to the four killed, six students were wounded, including a 17-year-old boy reported to be in serious condition at a Toulouse hospital.
The victims included a French-Israeli rabbi sent to France to help teach in the school, and his two sons, 3 and 6, along with a girl who was the daughter of the principal. Her age was given variously as 8 and 10.
The style of the killings and one of the weapons used led law enforcement authorities to suggest a link with the assassinations of three soldiers last week in the same area of southwestern France — and possibly a political motive arising from France’s military role in the Afghan war.
The first soldier was shot and killed in Toulouse on March 11 as he met a man who pretended to be interested in buying his motorcycle. Four days later, two other soldiers were killed and a third was badly injured as they took money from an automated teller machine in Montauban, about 30 miles to the north.
Police said the same kind of weapon, a .45 semiautomatic pistol of the kind once carried by U.S. armed forces, was used in all three incidents, including the one on Monday. Forensic tests later determined that it was the same pistol, according to the minister of the interior, Claude Gueant.
The evidence suggesting a single killer was involved in all three crimes gave rise to suggestions that the killer may be a Muslim extremist driven by a desire to avenge French participation in the Afghan war, which could also be consistent with the attack on a Jewish school.
The issue was considered extremely sensitive in France during the presidential election campaign. Public opinion already has been aroused by a controversy over halal meat, or meat prepared according to Islamic tradition, and President Nicolas Sarkozy’s repeated promises to limit the number of foreigners — chiefly Muslims — who enter France illegally or are granted legal residence.
