Pastor Robert Jeffress Slams Houston Mayor for Backtracking on Houston Pastors Subpoena

October 17, 2014 Christian

Pastor Robert Jeffress inside the sanctuary at First Baptist Church in Dallas, Texas.
(PHOTO: COURTESY OF FIRST BAPTIST DALLAS)

Robert Jeffress, the pastor of First Baptist Church in Dallas, said Thursday morning that the embattled mayor of Houston is backtracking after she first defended attorneys who are going after pastors in the Houston area.

Jeffress talked to AFR talk show host Sandy Rios after he appeared on “Fox and Friends” earlier Thursday morning, using both appearances to discuss the subpoenas issued to pastors for their sermon notes and private communications with church members.

Houston Mayor Annise Parker, a lesbian, is now claiming she didn’t intend for the subpoenas to be so “broad.”

But the mayor, on Wednesday wrote on Twitter that “if these five preachers used politics in the pulpit, then their sermons are fair game.”

A OneNewsNow story posted early Thursday morning reported that Mayor Parker told The Houston Chronicle that the subpoenas are “overly broad” in nature.

“I just want to say, just point out, what a hypocrite and a liar this mayor is,” Jeffress told Rios, pointing out that Parker is claiming ignorance about the demands in the subpoena after defending them hours earlier in her Twitter message.

“She better get her story straight,” said Jeffress, whose Pathway to Victory ministry airs on American Family Radio.

In The Houston Chronicle story, Parker played the role of victim – an ignorant victim.

“Let me just say that one word in a very long legal document, which I know nothing about and would never have read, and I’m vilified coast to coast,” Parker told the newspaper. “It’s a normal day at the office for me.”

The mayor was referring to the word “sermon” in the subpoenas. Both Parker and City Attorney David Feldman claimed that was an misunderstanding, although the newspaper noted that Feldman, a day earlier, was quoted as saying the sermons were relevant because the pastors mixed religion and politics.

“”If someone is speaking from the pulpit and it’s political speech, then it’s not going to be protected,” Feldman said, according to the Chronicle.
