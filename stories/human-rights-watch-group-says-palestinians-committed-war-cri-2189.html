Human Rights Watch Group says Palestinians Committed War Crimes during Gaza War

December 24, 2012 Uncategorized

Rights group says rockets aimed at civilians during November’s Operation Pillar of Defense is a violation of international humanitarian law, and that Hamas bears responsibility as the ruling authority in the Gaza Strip.


Members of Palestinian armed factions stand guard during a news conference held to mark the second anniversary of Israel’s three-week offensive in Gaza, in Gaza City December 27, 2010. Photo by Reuters
Armed groups in Gaza committed war crimes by firing rockets at Israeli population centers during Operation Pillar of Defense in November, Human Rights Watch said in a report released on Monday.
During last month’s hostilities between Israel and Gaza, approximately 1,500 rockets were fired at Israel, Human Rights Watch said, citing Israel Defense Forces figures. At least 800 hit the country, with 60 exploding in population centers. For the first time, rockets hit as far as the Tel Aviv and Jerusalem areas, and a large number of rockets were intercepted by the Iron Dome missile defense system.
“Palestinian armed groups made clear in their statements that harming civilians was their aim,” said Sarah Leah Whitson, Middle East director at the U.S.-based human rights organization. “There is simply no legal justification for launching rockets at populated areas.”
Three Israeli civilians were killed as a result of rocket attacks, and at least 38 were wounded, some seriously, the report said. Civilian property was also destroyed.
“Under international humanitarian law, or the laws of war, civilians and civilian structures may not be subject to deliberate attacks or attacks that do not discriminate between civilians and military targets,” Human Rights Watch said. “Anyone who commits serious laws-of-war violations intentionally or recklessly is responsible for war crimes.”
Armed groups in Gaza also bear responsibility for civilian deaths in the Strip as a result of the rocket attacks, the report said. Militant groups repeatedly fired rockets from populated areas inside Gaza, putting Palestinian civilians at risk both from IDF counter-fire and the rockets themselves.
The organization also said that during the November fighting, armed groups fired rockets through hatches in underground tunnels for the first time.
Some rockets that fell short of their targets hit civilians inside Gaza. The report cites one incident on November 16, when “a rocket that appears to have been launched from within Gaza hit a crowded street in the Gazan town of Jabalya, killing a man, 23, and a boy, 4, and wounding five people.”
“Under the laws of war, parties to an armed conflict are required to take all feasible precautions to protect civilians under their control from the effects of attacks and not to place military targets in or near densely populated areas,” Human Rights Watch said, adding that it had not found one instance in November “in which a Palestinian armed group warned civilians to evacuate an area before a rocket launch.”
