Dr. Frances Henderson Becomes First Black Woman to Earn Tenure at Maryville College

May 14, 2013 Uncategorized

After moving to Maryville six years ago from St. Louis to teach political science, Dr. Frances Henderson has landed herself in the record books as the first African-American woman to receive tenure at Maryville College.

Dr. Frances Henderson talks about her six years of teaching at Maryville College and what it means to be the first African-American woman to receive tenure there, at St. Paul AME Zion in Maryville, her church home.
Henderson was awarded tenure recently at the college and it will take effect in August. College officials believe Dr. John Perry was probably the first African-American professor who received tenure at MC. He was hired in 1985 to teach in the college’s physical education/health/recreation department. He was tenured in 1989 and was promoted to associate professor in 1996. He retired in 2003. Currently, Henderson is the only African-American professor on the faculty. There are some on staff.
“I am honored,” Henderson said as she sat recently at her church, Saint Paul A.M.E. Zion in Maryville. She came here as a brand new mother. “When I interviewed at Maryville College, my daughter was 4 weeks old,” she said. “When we moved here, she was only 7 months old.”
A reason to celebrate
Maryville College was founded in 1819, which was 194 years ago. Some in the community, like the Rev. Willa Estell, believe this achievement is definitely one to celebrate.
“We are so proud of her,” Estell said. “It is a huge celebration for her to have tenure at Maryville College. We just want to acknowledge and celebrate that.”
Henderson and her husband, Dr. Bertin M. Louis Jr., are now the parents of two daughters. Louis is assistant professor of anthropology at the University of Tennessee.
It has taken a long time to reach this historic event, Henderson said, but she sees the work Maryville College has done over the years to create diversity among students and staff.
“While it took 194 years, we can acknowledge it for what it is,” Henderson said. “I think it is a continuation of the college’s commitment to diversity. At the college, we have numerous strategic plans for diversity and offices that support our students of color and LGBTP (lesbian, gay, bisexual, transsexual, pansexual). We know the legacy of Kin Takahashi and the six students who re-integrated the college after it became legal.”
Takahashi was a Japanese student at MC back in the 1890s. He played baseball, organized a field day and founded the college’s first football team. He became one of MC’s most active alumni.
