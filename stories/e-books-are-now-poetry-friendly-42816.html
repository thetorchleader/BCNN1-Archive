E-Books Are Now Poetry-Friendly

October 31, 2014 Books

Billy Collins’s poem “Aimless Love” on Kindle as it looks in an enlarged font size, with lines spilling over and indented. Credit William P. O’Donnell/The New York Times

E-books [electronic books) turn poet-friendly, the New York Times reported on September 15, 2014.

There were no line breaks, and the stanzas had been jammed together into a block of text that looked like prose. But as technology evolved, publishers began to adapt. Some have hired programmers to hand-code poetry e-books so that line breaks and stanzas are maintained.

In 2013, publishers released about 2,050 poetry e-books, up from about 200 in 2007, the year the first Kindle came out, according to Bowker, which tracks releases.

As of late, best-selling Korean Christian author Rev. Dr. Jaerock Lee published his poetry book “Confession” in Korean and Simplified-Chinese by e-book format.

The Korean versions of “Confession” in hard cover, paperback and eBook format have been bestsellers in the religion section of Kyobo Bookstore, one of the largest bookshops in Korea. The Korean version of the e-book format is available through Ridibooks, Apple iBooks, and the Simplified-Chinese version is available through Amazon Kindle, iBooks, and Google Play Books.
