U.S. House Likely to Pass Sweeping Changes to Medicare in Rare Episode of Bipartisan Unity

March 27, 2015 National, zTransfer-UCN

Speaker John A. Boehner is being praised by members of both parties for a willingness to step up and strike a bargain with Democrats. (Doug Mills/The New York Times)

Leaders of both parties in the House expressed confidence on Wednesday that their chamber would soon approve sweeping changes in Medicare, even though the Congressional Budget Office released figures showing that the bill could add more than $140 billion to federal budget deficits in the coming decade.

President Obama endorsed the legislation. “I’ve got my pen ready to sign a good bipartisan bill, which would be really exciting,” he said.

The House plans to vote Thursday on the legislation, the product of a rare bipartisan effort by Speaker John A. Boehner and the House Democratic leader, Representative Nancy Pelosi. The measure has drawn support from many diverse organizations, including the American Medical Association, the American Hospital Association and liberal groups like Families USA, the Center for American Progress and the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities.

The bill would pay doctors based on their performance, eliminating a mechanistic formula that has threatened doctors with deep cuts in Medicare payments for more than a decade. Since 2003, Congress has repeatedly overridden those cuts, so the expected savings never materialized.

The nonpartisan Congressional Budget Office said that the bill would increase spending over current law by a total of $145 billion from 2015 to 2025. The bill would increase revenue by $4 billion, so budget deficits would increase by $141 billion, compared with existing law.

Fiscal conservatives have expressed concerns about higher deficits. But Mr. Boehner boasted that the bill “will save taxpayers money compared to the path we’ve been on — freezing physician payments every year.” The bill’s provisions would cost $900 million less than a 10-year freeze, which would cancel cuts scheduled to occur under current law, the budget office said.

Supporters of the bill said they hoped that the House would approve it with a big bipartisan majority, creating momentum for it in the Senate. It is unclear whether the Senate will vote on the bill before leaving town Friday for a two-week spring recess. Democrats may try to amend parts of the bill they find objectionable.
