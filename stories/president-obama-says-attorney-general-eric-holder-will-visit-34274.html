President Obama says Attorney General Eric Holder Will Visit Ferguson on Wednesday

August 18, 2014 Black

President Barack Obama speaks about the U.S. involvement in Iraq, as well as the situation in Ferguson, Mo., in the Brady Press Briefing Room of the White House in Washington, D.C., Aug. 18, 2014.
SAUL LOEB/AFP/GETTY IMAGES

Once again addressing the unrest in the town of Ferguson, Mo., after the slaying of unarmed teenager Michael Brown, the president spoke of tensions in the community, as well as Department of Justice plans to help ensure a smooth and transparent investigation.

President Barack Obama, back from vacation at Martha’s Vineyard, once again stood before the nation to address the racial tensions in Ferguson, Mo., as well as the tensions between law enforcement and the black community, saying that he’s spoken with the state officials and plans to dig deeper into the circumstances of unarmed teen Michael Brown’s death.

The president said that Attorney General Eric Holder will be traveling to Ferguson on Wednesday to meet with FBI agents and other Department of Justice personnel, who are currently conducting a federal criminal investigation. The DOJ Office of Community Oriented Policing Services Director Ronald Davis will also be making an appearance in the embattled town to work with police officials there.

Obama took the time to remind residents to remain calm in the face of the tragedy.

“While I understand the passions and the anger that arise over the death of Michael Brown, giving into that anger by looting or carrying guns, and even attacking the police only serves to raise tensions and stir chaos. It undermines rather than advances justice,” he said at the press conference Monday.
