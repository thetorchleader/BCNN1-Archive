White House Praises Baltimore Mom Who Publicly Disciplined Rioting Son

April 29, 2015 National

Toya Graham talks about pulling her teenage son, Michael, from the riots in Baltimore. (PHOTO CREDIT: CBS)

The Baltimore mom who was caught on camera slapping her son silly for rioting was applauded Wednesday by the White House.

What Toya Graham did was “a powerful expression about the role that parents can play,” spokesman Josh Earnest said.

“The thing that resonated with me is — was her expression that she was concerned about her son facing the same fate as Freddie Gray,” Earnest added. “And while I’m sure that it was not the immediate reaction of her son to feel like she was looking out for his best interest, there is no doubting that her reaction was one that was rooted in her concern for his safety and his well-being and her love for her child.”

Graham instantly became mother of the year when the clip of her reining-in her rebellious son when viral. A single mom, she told CBS News she did what she did to protect her boy.

“I could see the objects being thrown at the police, and I was like… ‘Oh my God, this is really happening right here with me,'” Graham recalled. “And lo and behold: I turn around and I look in this crowd, and my son is actually coming across the street with this hoodie on and a mask. At that point, I just lost it.”

Baltimore has been convulsed by anti-cop violence since the April 19 death of Gray while in police custody. A report detailing how the 25-year-old black man died is expected to be released Friday.

President Obama, who has condemned the mayhem, said in an interview Wednesday on “The Steve Harvey Morning Show” that the riots revealed that police departments need to build more trust in black communities. And he repeated again the need to address underlying problems afflicting African-Americans like poor education, drugs and limited job opportunities.

Newly-minted Attorney General Loretta Lynch blasted the “senseless acts of violence” that shook Baltimore but said the city is “struggling to balance great expectations and need with limited resources.”

Meanwhile, Baltimore struggled to get back to normal as 3,000 police and National Guardsmen patrolled the city’s streets — and the Baltimore Symphony Orchestra performed a free concert in a city park.

Police reported just 35 arrests overnight, mostly for violating the curfew. It goes back into effect at 10 p.m. Wednesday.

Some 100 demonstrators demanding answers about Gray’s death protested outside the Maryland state attorney’s office, but there was no lashing out at law enforcement.
