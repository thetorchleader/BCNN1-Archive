President Obama Becoming More Accessible to the Media

August 20, 2014 Media

President Obama and reporters at the White House.
(Photo: Saul Loeb, AFP/Getty Images)

We’re seeing more and more of President Obama these days.

The president is making more statements to reporters who have occasionally criticized him for being inaccessible, and he’s taking more questions from them — developments driven by major events and quite possibly the administration’s political problems.

Over the past 10 weeks, Obama has taken questions after making statements on at least nine occasions, including Monday’s discussion of Iraq and racial unrest in Ferguson, Mo., as well as brief news conference after the African leaders summit on Aug. 6.

Martha Joynt Kumar, a political science professor at Towson University who specializes in White House communications, said Obama has had 16 short “Q&As” with reporters this year through July; he had only 14 all of last year.

While there’s always a risk that the president “can get off topic” when taking questions, the benefits outweigh the costs, Kumar said, especially for late-term presidents who are struggling in the polls.

“You need to have the public know who you are,” Kumar said. “That’s an issue for him.”

White House officials attribute the rise less to strategy than to the press of major events, including Russian aggression in Ukraine, the missing plane from Malaysia, renewed violence between Israelis and Palestinians, and a surge in migrants (many of them children) from Central America.

“We have looked for moments for the president to answer questions directly from the media as a way to provide valuable insight into his thinking and for him to personally press his case,” said White House spokesman Eric Schultz.

Obama enjoys the interactions, Schultz said, and sees them as way to answer questions “on the minds of the American people” via the news media.

Other observers, including Republicans, see another reason for Obama’s availability: low approval ratings.
