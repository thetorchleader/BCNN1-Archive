Alliance of Pastors Come Together to Address Civil Rights Issues In the Nation

October 26, 2014 Christian

Pastor CeJay Johnson talks with reporter Troy Washington about the summit happening in Shreveport to address violence across the nation.

An alliance of pastors from across the country is coming together to address civil rights issues in the nation, and the fight has come to Shreveport.

They’re calling it a call to action. The ministers say the unrest going on in Ferguson, Mo. as well as other instances involving the African-American community and law enforcement has their attention, and they’re looking to encourage peace.

“Rioting is just a bunch of angry people that don’t know how to channel that energy,” Pastor CeJay Johnson said.

The recent violence across the country involving police and the public has prompted uproar, riots and unrest. Now a group of ministers is coming to together with one goal- to calm some of that tension while also seeking justice.

“Social injustices that are happening in America…This has to be a conversation that the church must involve itself in. How can we have better police and community relations?” Johnson said.

Starting Sunday, through Basar Preachers Rally, Incorporated, pastors from all over will work to address the matter of civil unrest in the nation.

“It’s going to teach people how to take that energy of frustration and anger. It’s going to tell you how to do that in a peaceful way,” Johnson said.

Johnson says that the summit’s influence extends far beyond the city limits of Shreveport and “connects in Flint, Michigan, in Jackson, Mississippi, in New Orleans, in East Texas- the Dallas area.”

The ministers are all on the same mission, a mission of peace and equality for all.

“From Eric Garner to Trayvon Martin, we’ve saw violence,” said Johnson, who added that it’s nothing new.
