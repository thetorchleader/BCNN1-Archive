After Meeting With National Security Team, Obama Says Other Nations Need to ‘Step Up’ Efforts to Contain Ebola

October 6, 2014 National

President Obama
(Photo: JEWEL SAMAD, AFP/Getty Images)

President Obama urged other countries Monday to do more to help contain the deadly Ebola virus in West Africa, calling it an issue of global security.

“We have not seen other countries step up as aggressively as we need them to,” Obama said after huddling with his national security team, a week after the first U.S. case of Ebola surfaced in Dallas.

He pledged “more pressure” in the days ahead.

The president and his aides, including public health officials, evaluated their anti-Ebola strategies and decided whether more measures are needed to help keep the virus out of the United States, aides said.

The chances of an Ebola epidemic in the United States are “extremely low,” Obama said, but he added that “we don’t have a lot of margin for error.”

Administration officials said they are pleased with screening procedures at airports but will study other suggestions — plenty of which are being made by lawmakers and members of the public.

“Our experts continue to be confident that the medical infrastructure that we have in place in this country is sufficient to prevent an Ebola outbreak from occurring,” White House spokesman Josh Earnest said.

He also referred to a comment Friday by Obama homeland security adviser Lisa Monaco, who said that “we are constantly going to evaluate what may be the most effective measures we can take.”

The administration is under increased pressure in light of the case in Dallas, where a man infected by Ebola during a trip in Africa is fighting for his life.

Obama and his national security aides also met as a nurse in Spain became the first person known to catch Ebola outside of West Africa.
