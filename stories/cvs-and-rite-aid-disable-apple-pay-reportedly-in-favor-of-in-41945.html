CVS and Rite Aid Disable Apple Pay, Reportedly in Favor of Incoming QR Code-Based System

October 27, 2014 Technology

The Apple Pay logo is displayed in a mobile kiosk sponsored by Visa and Wells Fargo to demonstrate the new Apple Pay mobile payment system on Oct. 20, 2014 in San Francisco City. (Justin Sullivan/Getty Images)

Apple Inc.’s mobile payment technology ran into a roadblock a week after its introduction as CVS Health Corp. and Rite Aid Corp., part of a consortium developing a competing system, disabled Apple Pay in their drugstores.

CVS and Rite Aid are among 220,000 U.S. merchants that already have technology in place to read the short-range wireless signals that enable customers of Apple Pay or similar services to make a purchase by waving their smartphones. The retailers weren’t among those specifically named as accepting Apple Pay when the iPhone maker revealed its system last month.

The drug retailers stopped Apple Pay last week, said a person familiar with the situation who asked not to be named. The website MacRumors.com earlier reported that the stores disabled so-called contactless payment systems, and Slashgear.com published a purported internal memo in which Rite Aid says it is instead focusing on the consortium’s system.

“This act by CVS and Rite Aid heralds the advent of the imminent battle in the mobile pay system,” Anindya Ghose, a marketing and information-technology professor at New York University, said yesterday in an e-mail.

Trudy Muller, a spokeswoman for Apple, declined to comment. The shares, up 31 percent this year, were little changed at $100.04 as of 7:19 a.m., before the market opened.

Spokesmen for Woonsocket, Rhode Island-based CVS and Camp Hill, Pennsylvania-based Rite Aid didn’t respond to voice messages and e-mails outside of regular business hours. CVS has about 7,700 retail pharmacies and Rite Aid has about 4,570.
