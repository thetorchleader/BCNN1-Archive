Great Expectations (Part 1)

November 28, 2012 Uncategorized
I Kings 8:22-24: “And Solomon stood before the altar of the Lord in the presence of all the congregation of Israel, and spread forth his hands toward heaven: And he said, Lord God of Israel, there is no God like thee, in heaven above, or on earth beneath, who keepest covenant and mercy with thy servants that walk before thee with all their heart: Who hast kept with thy servant David my father that thou promisedst him: thou spakest also with thy mouth, and hast fulfilled it with thine hand, as it is this day.”

In our last message in this series, we covered the topic of how David was still before God in the Tabernacle. As you might remember, the context of that message was David’s desire to build a Temple for God’s presence to reside in. However, God informed David through Nathan the prophet that while his intentions were good, he would not be allowed to build the Temple. But his son, who would be king after him, would build the Temple. 
Well, the context of our passage today is the fulfillment of God’s words to David. Here, in this passage, we have King Solomon who has completed building the Temple in Jerusalem. On this great occasion, the children of Israel are gathered in Jerusalem to celebrate the completion of the building of the Temple. 
Verse one of chapter eight tells us that “Solomon assembled the elders of Israel, and all the heads of the tribes, the chief of the fathers of the children of Israel, unto king Solomon in Jerusalem, that they might bring up the ark of the covenant of the Lord out of the city of David, which is Zion. And all the men of Israel assembled themselves unto king Solomon at the feast in the month Ethanim, which is the seventh month.” 
The Levite priests carried the Ark of the Covenant out of the tabernacle and into the newly built Temple. When they had set up the Ark in the Temple, the Bible tells us that a great cloud filled the Temple signifying God’s approval of what Solomon had done. 
Today, I want us to begin looking at Solomon’s prayer on this occasion and the lessons we can learn from it. Because this is such a long prayer, Lord willing, we will continue to study I Kings chapter eight in upcoming messages. Today, we are covering the first 22 verses so that we can get an understanding of the background of Solomon’s prayer. I have titled this series of messages “Great Expectations” because Solomon prays a great prayer to God, and he expects God to do great things for the nation of Israel. 
1. First of all, I want you to notice the greatness of the occasion. The completion of the first Temple was a great event in the history of the children of Israel. For hundreds of years, the Ark of the Covenant had resided in a huge tent called the Tabernacle which had been built while the Israelites were sojourning in the wilderness for forty years. Many decades after the children of Israel settled in the Promised Land, King David realized how strange it was for God who had safely brought the children of Israel into their homeland did not have a permanent home in that land. So, during the years of his reign, David gathered the materials and the money to build the Temple in Jerusalem. 
The finishing of the Temple was indeed a great event. And, in the midst of the great celebration that was going on, it would have been easy for the people to take pride in their accomplishment and forget the real reason for the celebration. However, Solomon did not allow them to do that. Before he prayed, he addressed the congregation and reminded them of God’s words to David and the fulfillment of His promise. 
In verse 20 of this passage, Solomon says, “And the Lord hath performed his word that he spake, and I am risen up in the room of David my father, and sit on the throne of Israel, as the Lord promised, and have built an house for the name of the Lord God of Israel.” Notice how Solomon reminds the people that it was the Lord who did all that had been accomplished.
Oftentimes, in our lives, when we accomplish something, we tend to forget that it was God who enabled us to gain success or to accomplish some task. Even in the church, there are people who go about doing God’s work without acknowledging God in the process. Instead, they trust in their own talents and abilities. 
E.M. Bounds said, “What the Church needs to-day is not more machinery or better, not new organizations or more and novel methods, but men whom the Holy Ghost can use — men of prayer, men mighty in prayer.”
No matter how great the church buildings we build are, no matter how innovative the ministries we put together are, no matter how many committees and coalitions we put together and join, nothing we do will be effective without prayer to God. And, we see that on this great occasion, Solomon does not forget that it was God who enabled him to accomplish so much.
2. Secondly, I want you to notice the greatness of Solomon’s humility. Solomon was the wisest man who ever lived. Solomon was perhaps the richest king on earth while he was alive. Solomon was indeed a great man, but he was also great in his humility.
Verse 22 reads, “And Solomon stood before the altar of the Lord in the presence of all the congregation of Israel…” Solomon did not appoint one of the priests to pray, but prayed himself in the presence of all the people. Despite all of his education, despite all of his learning, despite his pedigree and upbringing, Solomon was wise enough to learn how to humble himself and pray to a God he couldn’t see. Matthew Henry said that Solomon “knew how to express himself to God in a suitable manner, on the spur of the occasion, without a prescribed form.”
I believe that our nation would be much better off if we had more government leaders who knew how to humble themselves before God, pray themselves and lead the entire nation in prayer as Solomon does here. Solomon did not think he was too great to humble himself before God in the sight of the people. We saw in II Samuel how King David humbled himself before God, and here, we see how his son, Solomon, does the same. 
I Peter 5:6 says, “Humble yourselves therefore under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time.” When we approach God in prayer, it is crucial that we humble ourselves before Him.
3. Thirdly, I want you to notice the greatness of Solomon’s expectations. Verse 22 of our passage goes on to say that Solomon “spread forth his hands toward heaven.” By Solomon spreading forth his hands toward Heaven, he was outwardly expressing his inner desires towards God and his expectations from God. He offered up his prayer from an open heart — not only to show his expectations of God, but to readily receive what he requested from God. 
Solomon did not go into his prayer with timidity and trepidation. He knew that the God he was praying to was capable of doing great things. Ephesians 3:20 says that God is able “to do exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think.” The Bible says in Psalm 81:10: “I am the Lord thy God, which brought thee out of the land of Egypt: open thy mouth wide, and I will fill it.” At the very beginning of his prayer, Solomon spread forth his hands because he had great expectations from God. 
Andrew Murray said, “Beware in your prayers, above everything else, of limiting God, not only by unbelief, but by fancying that you know what He can do. Expect unexpected things above all that we ask or think.'”
In closing, my friend, let’s take Solomon’s example to heart. First of all, let’s acknowledge God in all of our doings. When we have great success, let’s not forget to thank and praise the One who enabled us to have that success. Secondly, let’s have great humility when we go before God in prayer. And, third, when we pray, let’s ask for great things and expect to receive great things from God.
– – – – – – – – – – – – – – –
Now, if you are with us today, and you do not know the Lord Jesus Christ as your Saviour, your first prayer needs to be what we call the Sinner’s Prayer. Please understand that you are a sinner, just as I am, and that you have broken God’s laws. The Bib

le says in Romans 3:23: “For all have sinned and come short of the glory of God.” Please understand that because of your sins, you deserve permanent punishment in hell. Romans 6:23 says “the wages of sin is death…” This is both physical death and spiritual death in hell. That is the bad news.

The good news is found in John 3:16 which says, “For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”  
If you believe that Jesus Christ died on the cross for your sins, was buried, and rose from the dead, and you want to trust Him for your salvation today, please pray with me this simple prayer: Holy Father God, I realize that I am a sinner and that I have done some bad things in my life. For Jesus Christ sake, please forgive me of my sins. I now believe with all of my heart that Jesus Christ died for me, was buried, and rose again. Lord Jesus, please come into my heart and save my soul and change my life today. Amen.  
If you just trusted Jesus Christ as your Saviour, and you prayed that prayer and meant it from your heart, I declare to you that based upon the Word of God, you are now saved from Hell and you are on your way to Heaven. Welcome to the family of God! I want to congratulate you on doing the most important thing in life and that is receiving Jesus Christ as your Lord and Saviour. For more information to help you grow in your newfound faith in Christ, go to Gospel Light Society.com and read “What To Do After You Enter Through the Door”. Jesus Christ said in John 10:9, “I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture.”
God bless you.
