“A.D.” Failing to Generate Same Ratings of “The Bible”

April 27, 2015 Entertainment

It’s quite a revelation.

Indeed, the truth is that since its Easter launch, NBC’s “A.D: The Bible Continues” has failed to attract nearly as many followers as its predecessor.

If the series were to go head-to head against the History channel’s epic miniseries, “The Bible,” then “A.D.” would be losing by a whopping 32%.

For the unenlightened, here’s the biblical back story. “The Bible” wound up being the most watched cable miniseries of 2013 and led to “A.D.”

Its creators, Roma Downey and her prolific television producing husband Mark Burnett (“Survivor” “The Voice,” “Celebrity Apprentice,” “The Sing-Off,” “Shark Tank” and more), sought an even bigger audience (and fatter payoff) by opting not to take the sequel to cable.

“‘A.D.’ is the story of the birth of a faith that has gone from 12 apostles to 2.5 billion people,” Burnett told the industry trade magazine Adweek. “This is a subject that should be on broadcast television.”

But for all of his talent and success, Burnett appears to have been unable to divine that this move was wrong.

You might even ask: what would Jesus do? The answer is: maybe just stay with History.

Miraculously, the cable channel crushed the big broadcaster. The three episodes of “A.D.” on NBC that have aired so far have averaged 7.9 million viewers. But the “The Bible” on cable snared an average of 11.4 million viewers during its 10-episode run.
