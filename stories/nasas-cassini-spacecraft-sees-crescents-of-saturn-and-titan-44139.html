NASA’s Cassini Spacecraft Sees Crescents of Saturn and Titan

November 7, 2014 Science

The crescents of Saturn and Titan as imaged by NASA’s Cassini spacecraft on Aug. 11, 2013 — raw data colorized (in Photoshop) by Kevin M. Gill. (PHOTO CREDIT: NASA/JPL-CALTECH/Space Science Institute/Kevin M. Gill)

At first glance, this image may appear to be a piece of modern art. But it is in fact a very beautiful observation of Saturn and moon Titan during a special cosmic alignment with the sun, an alignment that produced its very own piece of space art as well as a healthy does of science.

Known as a “high-phase observation,” NASA’s Cassini mission is looking almost directly toward the sun in this photo, but it is just out of shot of its camera’s frame. The result is a very thin crescent across Saturn’s upper atmosphere. Most strikingly, however, the sunlit crescent almost forms a ring around Titan. This effect is caused by the high altitude haze in the Titanian atmosphere, refracting the light around the moon, giving a very eclipse-like view.
