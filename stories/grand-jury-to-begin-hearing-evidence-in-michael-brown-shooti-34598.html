Grand Jury to Begin Hearing Evidence in Michael Brown Shooting Case

August 20, 2014 National

Police arrest protesters after a fight broke out in a parking lot during protest marches in Ferguson, Mo., Tuesday. (Photo: AP/Curtis Compton, Atlanta Journal-Constitution)

A local grand jury is expected to hear evidence Wednesday in the fatal shooting of unarmed black teen Michael Brown as an uneasy peace settles over this St. Louis suburb that has been wracked by more than a week of protests and violence.

The streets of the embattled town of 22,000 people saw pockets of violence during demonstrations Tuesday night and into Wednesday morning but on the whole was more peaceful than in recent days, police said.

“Tonight, the elders in the community, volunteers, activists and the clergy came out in large numbers,” Capt. Ron Johnson of the Missouri State Highway Patrol said Wednesday during a press conference. “They walked and talked with people. They urged order,” he said.

Days of protests in this largely African=American community have expressed anger over the shooting of the 18-year-old on Aug. 9 and the initial heavy show of force by police as demonstrators took to the streets.

The moves on the legal front come as U.S. Attorney General Eric Holder is set to arrive to be briefed on the federal civil rights inquiry.

In an op-ed for the St. Louis Post-Dispatch in advance of his trip, Holder promised a “fair and thorough” investigation into the shooting death of the 18-year-old.

He said “hundreds” of potential witnesses have been interviewed and cautioned that the inquiry will “take time to complete.”

“At a time when so much may seem uncertain, the people of Ferguson can have confidence that the Justice Department intends to learn — in a fair and thorough manner — exactly what happened,” Holder wrote.

Edward Magee, spokesman for St. Louis County Prosecuting Attorney Robert McCulloch, says local investigators have interviewed Ferguson police Officer Darren Wilson, 28, who is accused of shooting Brown, and he will be “offered the opportunity” to testify before the grand jury if he chooses.
