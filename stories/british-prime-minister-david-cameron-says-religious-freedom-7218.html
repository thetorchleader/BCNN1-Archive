British Prime Minister David Cameron Says Religious Freedom Must Be Protected

July 19, 2012 Uncategorized

British citizens may eventually win the right to wear religious symbols in the workplace, as the British Parliament considers changing the existing laws if necessary.
The government is opposing two cases filed with the European Court of Human Rights to gain the right to wear religious symbols such as a small cross. 
The Christian Institute was there recently when David Davis, a member of Parliament, discussed the situation with Prime Minister David Cameron.
“On the 4th of September the European Court of Human Rights is hearing the case of Miss Nadi Eweida, the lady who lost her job at British Airways for wearing a crucifix as a mark of her Christianity,” Davis says. “The behavior of British Airways in this was a disgraceful piece of political correctness, so I was surprised to see the government resisting Miss Eweida’s appeal. I cannot believe the government is supporting the suppression of religious freedom in the workplace — so what are we going to do about this sad case?”
