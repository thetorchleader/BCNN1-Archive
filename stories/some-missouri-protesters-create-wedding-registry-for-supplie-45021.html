Some Missouri Protesters Create Wedding Registry for Supplies to Use Upon Hearing Grand Jury’s Darren Wilson Indictment Decision

November 13, 2014 National

Demonstrators listen to speakers during a rally on the campus of Saint Louis University on Oct. 13.
(Photo: Scott Olson, Getty Images)

Protesters rallying against the shooting of an unarmed teen by a police officer in Ferguson, Mo., have created an unusual method to arm themselves with supplies: a wedding registry.

The group MORE (Missourians Organizing for Reform & Empowerment) is trying to use the power of crowdsourcing and the shopping website Amazon to collect supplies to use within the first hour after an anticipated grand jury announcement whether to indict Officer Darren Wilson in the fatal Aug. 9 shooting death of Michael Brown, 18.

The grand jury convened Aug. 20 to decide whether to indict Wilson. Court officials expect the grand jury to announce its decision this month.

While Ferguson residents await the decision, people from all parts of the community are preparing for a population to convulse. Police, city officials, schools, businesses and protesters meet daily in an effort to avert a return to the chaos that immediately followed the shooting, when protesters clashed, sometimes violently, with police.

There are about two dozen items on this “registry” ranging from plastic gloves, to serve food, to digital cameras that, “unlike phones … can’t be remotely hacked.”
