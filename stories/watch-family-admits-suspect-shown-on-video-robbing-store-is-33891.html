WATCH: Family Admits Suspect Shown on Video Robbing Store is Michael Brown; But Police Officer says he Didn’t Know Brown was a Suspect at Time of Shooting — Some Still Cry Foul

August 15, 2014 National

Police said Friday that the police officer who shot to death an unarmed 18-year-old did not know the man was a robbery suspect and stopped him because he was walking in the street, a revelation that seemed likely to add to public anger over the death of Michael Brown.

At a brief appearance Friday morning, the Ferguson, Mo., police chief, Thomas Jackson, identified the officer as Darren Wilson, as a six-year veteran of the Police Department with no prior disciplinary problems. Later, Jackson said Wilson was not aware that Brown was suspected in a robbery before their encounter. He said Wilson, 28, stopped Brown and another man because they were walking in the middle of the street.

Police also found “evidence of shoplifting” on Brown, Jackson said. He went on to describe Wilson as a “distinguished officer” who was devastated by what had occurred. “He never intended for any of this to happen,” Jackson said.

Police gave the media a copy of a police report on a call that was received regarding a strong-arm robbery that had occurred at a local convenience store. Portions of the incident were captured on surveillance cameras.  Jackson did not identify Brown as a suspect, but the police report did.

The release of the information did little to alleviate anger and skepticism among many residents of the St. Louis suburb and nearby areas, and it drew an angry rebuke from Benjamin Crump, the lawyer for Brown’s family.

“Michael Brown’s family is beyond outraged at the devious way the police chief has chosen to disseminate piece meal information in a manner intended to assassinate the character of their son,” Crump said in a statement. “There is nothing based on the facts that have been placed before us that can justify the execution style murder of their child by this police officer as he held his hands up, which is the universal sign of surrender.”

Missouri State Highway Patrol Capt. Ron Johnson, whom Gov. Jay Nixon has assigned to take over security in Ferguson, offered his own criticism of the Police Department’s release of the information, expressing concern they had given out details of the robbery without answering questions.

“These are two separate issues,” Johnson said of the reported robbery and Brown’s death. “I’m not going to say one justifies the other. I think if we’re going to give answers, we need not give hints. We need to say it.”

Many Ferguson residents expressed disbelief that Brown, who was black, had been involved in wrongdoing. Whatever the circumstances, they said Wilson had no reason to shoot him.
