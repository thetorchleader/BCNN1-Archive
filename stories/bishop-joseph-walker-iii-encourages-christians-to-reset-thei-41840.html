Bishop Joseph Walker III Encourages Christians to Reset Their Pursuit of Purpose

October 26, 2014 Christian

Bishop Joseph Walker III is pastor of Mount Zion Baptist Church in Nashville.
(Photo: File / The Tennessean )

Mark Twain said, “The two most important days in your life are the day you are born and the day you find out why.” One of the most common questions people ask me is “what is my purpose?”

Inspired by siblings who were lawyers and judges, I majored in English in hopes of entering law school. One semester before graduating, I received a call into the ministry. When I speak of a call, I am not referring to a loud, audible voice from heaven — though some have experienced that. For me, it was a nudging in my spirit that there was something else I was supposed to do.

Though I ignored it, it only intensified. I went through a process that ultimately led me to acquiescing to the call and enrolling in divinity school. Purpose was embraced, and I was immediately overwhelmed by a peaceful assurance that this is what I was put on the planet to do.

Resetting our purpose takes courage. It forces us to look critically at the direction our lives are headed, as well as the motivations that fuel it. Unfortunately, many of us subliminally allow others to place their expectations and dreams on us. Similarly, my siblings who were attorneys wanted me to follow in their footsteps. That career path was so ingrained in me that I never envisioned other possibilities.

It is essential that we recognize the pursuit of purpose is a process, not an event. Here are five steps that I learned during my pursuit:

1. Predestined. Your purpose is the answer to a problem. Unfortunately, many people miscarry their purpose. Being predestined does not omit you from consequences. For instance, a person who is unaware of their purpose often makes bad decisions. Many people lament, “I have nothing to live for.” It is disturbing that they are unaware that they have been preordained for something greater.
