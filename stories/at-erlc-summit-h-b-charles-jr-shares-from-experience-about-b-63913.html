At ERLC Summit, H.B. Charles Jr. Shares from Experience About Building Racially Diverse Church; Pastors Voice Caution On Pursuing Multi-Ethnic Ministry

March 27, 2015 Christian

David Prince leads a panel discussion with J. Josh Smith, H.B. Charles Jr., D.A. Horton, and Juan Sanchez.

During the evening session of the first day of the ERLC’s Leadership Summit on the Gospel and Racial Reconciliation, Jacksonville, FL, pastor, H.B. Charles Jr., shared from his experience building a church that is racially and culturally diverse. Recently, his predominately black Shiloh Metropolitan Baptist Church launched a church plant/merger with a predominately white church in Jacksonville. With that as his backdrop, and Ephesians 2 as his main text, he delivered a message titled, “Tearing Down the Dividing Wall: How the Gospel Shapes Racial Reconciliation.”

Pastor Charles stated that through the crucifixion and Jesus’ establishment of the church, ‘Jesus didn’t turn Gentiles into Jews, or Jews into Gentiles, but He created one new man. Christ has torn down the dividing walls in His body. Where He has torn down walls, who are we to rebuild them?’ He emphasized that Christ and the Gospel are what brings people together — not plans and initiatives created and driven by men, no matter how well-designed they are.

Also, during the evening session, several of the pastors cautioned church leaders on how they go about encouraging diversity in their churches. In a pre-recorded interview with the ERLC’s Daniel Darling, Pastor David Uth of the First Baptist Church of Orlando said pastors must be careful not to push multi-ethnic ministry. He said the best type of multi-ethnic church is brought about organically.

In a later panel on the Church and Multi-Ethnic Ministry, Pastor Juan Sanchez of HighPointe Church in Austin said he fears that multi-ethnic ministry will become just the next evangelical church “fad.” He also said that churches must work to bring together socio-economic groups as well, although he believes that this form of bringing people together is even harder than crossing racial and ethnic barriers.

Pastor J. Josh Smith, of the MacArthur Blvd. Baptist Church in Irving, TX, said, ‘Let’s talk less about building multi-ethnic churches, and more about building churches that reflect the community.’ He criticized how some churches define the type of members they want to attract to their church (the ‘Saddleback Sam’ method popularized by Pastor Rick Warren), and said churches must be willing to go after, receive, accept, and share the Gospel with whoever is in their community.

H.B. Charles Jr., who was also on the panel, said that in his experience, multi-ethnic ministry is not just about bringing people together during a worship service, but that true reconciliation happens when people live life together and truly get to know one another outside of the church walls. He was echoed by Pastor Smith who said, ‘The majority of racial reconciliation work in our church happens outside the church though relationships.’

Russell Moore interviewing John M. Perkins at ERLC Leadership Summit

Perhaps, the highlight of the night was Russell Moore’s sit-down with minister and Civil Rights activist, John M. Perkins. The 84-year-old Mississippian said, ‘I want to give my life—the rest of it—to young people and multiracial and cross-cultural church planters.’ He told his life story of growing up in the south, moving to California after his brother was killed, feeling like he didn’t belong to either the white church or the black church, and how he finally came to Christ.

Perkins said, ‘I want to preach a gospel stronger than my black interests and stronger than my economic interests… I want to preach a gospel that burns through racial barriers and brings all men into His kingdom.’ Declaring that the American Church was at a “pivotal place” on race issues, he said, ‘Jesus is good news for all people — ain’t no room in there for bigotry!’

–BCNN1
