J.C. Penney’s First-Quarter Results Show Unexpected Progress

May 16, 2014 Business

J.C. Penney’s first-quarter results were better than expected as lost customers start finding their way back.

The Plano-based department store’s 6.2 percent sales increase, a narrower loss and a new credit line all represented progress for the injured retailer. The sales increase was the second consecutive quarterly rise for Penney after two years of steep declines. Customer traffic was up in April for the first time in 30 months.

That kind of deep hole doesn’t fill back up quickly. Still, Penney CEO Mike Ullman was upbeat in a call with analysts.

After stabilizing the business last year and rebuilding its merchandise mix to reflect the styles and fit customers like, Ullman said, Penney is in a turnaround stage.

“We expect to carry this momentum into the second quarter as we continue to position the company for long-term profitable growth,” he said.

Penney said it expected to report similar sales increases and to improve margins during the second quarter and full year.

Penney reported its results Thursday after its stock closed at $8.37, down 24 cents. In after-hours trading, the stock price, which has declined more than 50 percent in the last year, was up more than 18 percent to around $10 a share.

Penney’s first-quarter net loss was $352 million, or $1.15 a share, compared with a loss of $348 million, or $1.58 a share, a year ago. The year-ago quarter included a tax benefit of $199 million.

Total sales were up 6.3 percent, from $2.64 billion last year to $2.8 billion.

Analysts surveyed by Thomson Reuters had forecast a loss of $1.25 a share and revenue of $2.71 billion.
