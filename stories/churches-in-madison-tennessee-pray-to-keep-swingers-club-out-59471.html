Churches In Madison, Tennessee, Pray to Keep Swingers Club Out of Their Community

March 2, 2015 Christian

Judy Marshall, left, prays with others during a prayer meeting at Goodpasture Christian School to oppose the possible move of a swingers club to a location near the school.
(Photo: Shelley Mays / The Tennessean)

Sunday afternoon, members of the Mt. Calvary Baptist Church could be seen walking beside the road in the rain, headed to a prayer meeting and asking others to join them.

Sandy McClain, pastor of the Mt. Calvary Baptist Church, was the first of several spiritual leaders who took to the stage in the auditorium of the Fine Arts Center at the Goodpasture Christian School, where just down the road a business catering to sexual swinging, known as The Social Club, is hoping to take root.

The club has operated at 700 Division St. but is looking to relocate to 520 Lentz Drive in Madison, adjacent to Goodpasture Christian School, where the back sides of the two properties touch.

A communitywide prayer meeting held at the school Sunday appealed to God and community leaders to keep that from happening.

“We have come together, all denominations, all races, for one cause — that Madison is a great community, and that we can keep it that way,” McClain said to the rousing sound of the music at his back and a couple hundred community members in the pews before him.

The prospect of the swingers club moving to Madison has proved controversial. The club has received most, but not all, of the necessary approvals to open. In an effort to block the club from Madison, the Metro Council is considering a ban on private clubs from properties zoned for office use.

That issue goes before the council Tuesday night.

And state Rep. Bill Beck, D-Nashville, has filed legislation that would prohibit private clubs from within 1,000 feet of schools, churches, parks and residences.

The club’s attorney, Larry Roberts, has vowed a legal fight over the fast-tracked zoning change working its way through the Metro Council.

“This particular business that my clients have poses no danger to the community,” Roberts previously told The Tennessean. He has criticized the city for trying the block the club while at the same time celebrating the Sports Illustrated swimsuit edition at a downtown event.

For those community members and church leaders such as McClain who oppose the addition of the Social Club to their community, its presence would signify a slipping away from the family, and primarily Christian, values in which they believe the majority of their community holds.

Such slippage, as McClain described it, not only puts Madison’s morals at risk, but that of the residential communities of Nashville in general.

“Madison is a great area, but I believe Madison is the tip of the iceberg,” he said. “If this element can come into our community, it can come into other communities. What is to stop if from next coming into Bellevue or Belle Meade?”
