WATCH: Routine Traffic Stop Turns Into Prayer Moment for White Police Officer, Black Man

March 22, 2015 Christian

Gadsden Police Officer Samuel Lowe, at left, sits next to Ronald Cowser. On March 18, the two of them had a chance encounter during a traffic stop that Lowe shared on Facebook. (William Thornton / wthornton@al.com)

Ronald Cowser was in a hurry on Padenreich Avenue when he looked in his rearview mirror Wednesday morning and saw police lights.

“Oh Lord,” he thought, pulling over in front of Paden Baptist Church. Gadsden Police Officer Samuel Lowe approached his Chevrolet Cobalt.

Cowser was driving slightly over the speed limit because he had just heard from his wife Wanda that she might need to go to the hospital.

But what began as a routine traffic stop became something else, the men say, due to a common bond.

It was near the end of Lowe’s shift. Lowe, 38, has been on the force for 12 years, and usually asks a question when he stops motorists for speeding, he says.

“Is there any reason you’re in a hurry?” he said.

Cowser explained that his wife was sick at home, and he was concerned about her. Lowe told him he could proceed on.

“God bless you,” Cowser said. Hearing this isn’t out of the ordinary for some drivers, Lowe said, and is always appreciated. But his next statement was a new experience.

“Can I pray for you?” Cowser asked.

Cowser, 58, is retired from the U.S. Army, and tells people he is a “prophet of God.” He had spent the previous day visiting hospitals to pray for patients and people he knows.

“The Lord has told me that I will make contact with people who will help me on my spiritual journey,” Cowser said. “And I try to be obedient.”

Lowe, taken aback, said he would never turn down an offer of prayer.

“I’d love it,” he said. Cowser stepped out of the car, the two men clasped hands, and Cowser began praying for Lowe, his safety, his family and his needs. “In a way, it was like he knew what was going on in my life,” Lowe said.

“I love you,” Cowser said, and the two men embraced by the roadside. Then they left, and Lowe realized he hadn’t even gotten the man’s name. When he finished his shift, he went home to his wife.

“Did you get cussed at today?” she asked.

“No,” he answered. “I got prayed for.”
