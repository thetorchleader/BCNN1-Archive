Rev. Myrue Spivey, Rev. Kerry Burkey, and Other Pastors and Faith Groups Are Making It Known that ‘Black Jesus’ Is Appalling, Blasphemous and Nobody Should Watch It

August 6, 2014 Black

Pastor Myrue Spivey of Palm Bay, Fla., conducts Sunday mornings worship service at Grace Bible Sanctuary in Palm Bay .
(Photo: Craig Rubadoux, Florida Today)

The Second Coming it is not.

But Adult Swim’s new comedy, Black Jesus, is raising the ire of pastors and faith groups across the nation with its portrayal of Jesus Christ as a profanity-using marijuana smoker who hangs with gang-bangers in Compton, Calif., and trades the wine of sanctification for cheap beer in a paper bag.

Adult Swim airs its programming at night on the Cartoon Network.

“I gathered our youth group and had them sit and watch the trailer of the show,” said Kerry Burkey, senior pastor at the 300-member Rockledge Church of Christ, who sees the show as a symbol of a decline in American pop culture.

“It was horrible, disgusting and completely offensive. Down to a person, everyone in the youth group was offended. It just shows where we are a nation. … We have no respect for God,” Burkey said.

The half-hour, live-action comedy series premieres on the Turner Broadcasting System-owned network Thursday and has little to do with Jesus’ race, faith or Hebraic heritage. Rather, critics say it offers a sullied look at a figure Christians call the Son of God.

Such satirical depictions are nothing new.

Rap superstar Kanye West — who turned up once on the cover of Rolling Stone magazine bloodied and wearing a crown of thorns — titled his last album “Yeezus,” a play on the name Jesus, and at his shows brought out an actor dressed as the Galilean for rambling dialogues. There were also movies like the musical Jesus Christ Superstar and comedy Monty Python’s Life of Brian, both panned by religious leaders during their heydays.

In Burkey’s case, the 20-year veteran of the pulpit is not calling for the series — which he sees as boldly sacrilegious — to be yanked off the air.

“I believe in the First Amendment, so I wouldn’t necessarily say take it off the air,” he said.

Instead, he sees it as a sign of the apathetic times and a call to action for followers of Jesus to study more about their faith.
