Ku Klux Klan Forces N. Carolina Town to Ban Blacks from Wrestling Show

August 14, 2012 Uncategorized

A local Ku Klux Klan group forced a North Carolina pro wrestling promotion to ban African-American wrestlers and fans from attending their most recent show.
In the small town of Denton, North Carolina, a small independent wrestling show was scheduled for June 23 at a local elementary school. A few days before the show was to go on, the promoter received a threatening text message from a local KKK affiliate, the White Knights.
“Do not bring no blacks into Denton. You have been warned. The White Knights.”
According to WCNC.com, the wrestlers were made aware of the text message and any African-American wrestlers scheduled for the show were told that their services were no longer needed. One of the African-American wrestlers who was scheduled for the show, James Brody, spoke to WCNC and let his opinions be heard.
“I was told I couldn’t go because they didn’t want any black athletes in Denton. I was pretty angry at first but, you know, I prayed about it and thought about it and I thought they don’t want me there that’s their loss not mine.”
Denton Police Chief, Mark Hicks, also spoke to WCNC and noted that they hadn’t received a complaint and didn’t know about the threat at the time.
“If we knew anything about the event we would have had an officer there. I have never heard of that group.”
According to the 2010 census, Denton, North Carolina has a population of 1,636, with only two African-Americans living in the town. The group known as the “White Knights” could be one of many white supremacist organizations that use the name, often found in the southern states. While the KKK no longer has the power and recognition it once had, there’s still a strong voice for white supremacists in America, mostly notably in the south. Earlier this year in North Carolina, the “White Knights of the Ku Klux Klan” held a cross burning ceremony for “white people only,” but it’s unknown whether the group is the same who sent the threatening text message.
