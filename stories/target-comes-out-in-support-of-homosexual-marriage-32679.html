Target Comes Out in Support of Homosexual Marriage

August 6, 2014 Business

STEVEN SENNE/AP
Target is adding its name to a ‘friend of the court’ brief that supports gay marriage, joining other large companies that are taking a stand.

The retail giant filed an amicus brief backing marriage equality in a pending court case. ‘It is our belief that everyone should be treated equally under the law,’ wrote Target’s V.P. of Human Resources Jodee Kozlak in her announcement on the company blog.

After years of standing on the sidelines, Target is aiming straight for the bull’s-eye by coming out in favor of gay marriage.

The Minnesota-based retail giant is throwing its weight behind a court brief that defends two federal judges who overturned gay marriage bans in Wisconsin and Indiana.

The company has been criticized for its unclear stance on the same-sex marriage debate in the past, but its announcement on Tuesday placed it squarely on the side of marriage equality — where it joins the likes of Starbucks, Intel and Apple.

“It is our belief that everyone should be treated equally under the law, and that includes rights we believe individuals should have related to marriage,” Target’s Executive Vice President of Human Resources Jodee Kozlak wrote on the company’s blog.

The company has been attempting to clean up its image with the LGBT community ever since the 2010 Minnesota governor’s race, The Star Tribune reports. At that time, Target, Best Buy and 3M donated hundreds of thousands of dollars toward an organization that supported Republican Tom Emmer, a vocal opponent of gay marriage. The three companies defended the move by claiming the money was meant to support a pro-business candidate. Still, gay rights activists blasted the businesses, and Target in particular, with threats of boycotts.

In 2012, Minnesota held a state referendum to ban gay marriage. Target, along with nearly all of the state’s biggest corporations, decided to stay silent about the issue. General Mills, however, came out staunchly opposed to the ban. The referendum failed and the state Legislature passed a bill recognizing same-sex marriage in 2013.
