Shonda Rhimes Officially Discusses ‘NYT’ “Angry Black Woman” Flap in Interview With ‘The Hollywood Reporter’

October 8, 2014 Entertainment

Everybody should just move on because Shonda Rhimes has.

That’s the impression from The Hollywood Reporter‘s interview with the woman it calls “TV’s savior” on the cover today.

Rhimes is not wringing her hands about that factually challenged, awkwardly-written New York Times article last month that seemed to imply she is “an angry black woman.”

It ticked off virtually everyone else, including 30 stars from her mega-hit TV dramas, and some people demanded the article be retracted. But not Rhimes, and not just because retraction was never going to happen. She tells THR that something good came of it.

“In this world in which we all feel we’re so full of gender equality and we’re a post-racial (society) and Obama is president, it’s a very good reminder to see the casual racial bias and odd misogyny from a woman written in a paper that we all think of as being so liberal.”

This is Rhimes’ first full response (as opposed to tweet-reponse) to the flap, THR says, and she is in a more reflective mood.

“Some really amazing articles were written that had the conversation that I’ve been trying to have for a very long time, which, coming from me, makes me sound like I’m just, ‘Rrrraw!,’ ” she says, mimicking a roar.

Not that issues of race and gender in Hollywood aren’t important to her. She says she once rejected a description as “the most powerful black female showrunner in Hollywood,” by crossing out “female” and “black.”

No modifiers necessary or relevant, she says.
