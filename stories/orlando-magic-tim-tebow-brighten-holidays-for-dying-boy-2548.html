Orlando Magic, Tim Tebow Brighten Holidays for Dying Boy

December 30, 2011 Uncategorized

It’s easy to become desensitized to the rush of holiday stories that document heartbreak and hopelessness.
Then you meet Blake Appleton. He is a precocious 8-year-old. Smiles with a wink in his eye. Hides behind his mom playing peek-a-boo with people he meets.
He is also dying.
About six months, the doctors say.
It’s hard to be specific, except for the realization that his three-year battle against brain cancer is pointless.
Blake has fought the good fight. He’s gone through 55 cycles of chemo, 30 radiation treatments, and 20 surgeries (4 of which were brain surgeries).
Finally, he said ‘enough mommy.’ It wasn’t the cry of a child whining about going to the doctor again. It was the call of a young boy wise enough to know that the consequences of stopping treatment means a death sentence.
He is OK with that, mostly troubled by the understanding that his mom Miranda Appleton and 10-year-old sister Morgan will live to mourn his death.
And so they carry on, day by day, despite other struggles. Miranda is a single mom unable to work because taking care of Blake is a full-time job. It looked like a bleak holiday season until the Fox affiliate in Orlando caught a glimpse of a story in a small paper in Polk County.
