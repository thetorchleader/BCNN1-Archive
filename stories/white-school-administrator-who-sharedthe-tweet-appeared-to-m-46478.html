White School Administrator Who Shared a Joke About Interracial Dating on Twitter is Falsely Accused of Racism

November 23, 2014 National

Amy Strickland, a high school assistant principal, apologized for retweeting a post about interracial dating, which many considered racially insensitive. She said her own daughters attended prom with African-Americans.

An assistant principal’s misstep serves as a reminder that much of what people share on social media is very public and subject to scrutiny.

Amy Strickland, an assistant principal at Booker T. Washington High School here, apologized after stirring up controversy by retweeting a post that many would consider racially insensitive. On Wednesday, she said in a statement that she forwarded the tweet to one of her daughters.

The tweet, sent in June, shows a picture of seven white girls and seven young black men at what looks like a dance.

The caption reads, “Every white girl’s father’s worse nightmare Or Nah.” It originated from an account called Or Nah.

“The tweet appeared to me at the time to have been an attempt at good-natured humor concerning mixed-race couples attending a high school prom,” Strickland said in the statement through her lawyer. “Because I have two daughters who in fact did attend proms with African American dates, I casually forwarded the tweet last June to one of those daughters.”

Students at her high school recently discovered the tweet. Her school has an enrollment of about 1,400 and a minority enrollment of 94%, according to U.S. News and World Report.

“Media reports suggesting that I am racially prejudiced are 1,000% false, as my record and my many students, colleagues, friends, and family members who are African American can and, if necessary, will attest,” she wrote. “I deeply apologize to anyone I have inadvertently offended.”

She said in the statement that she devoted years of her life to educating students and last year was named Teacher of the Year for a predominantly black school in Portsmouth, Va.

Still, many students at the school said it was hurtful to see the school leader’s retweet.
