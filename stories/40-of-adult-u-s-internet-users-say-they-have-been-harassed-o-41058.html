40% of Adult U.S. Internet Users Say They Have Been Harassed Online

October 23, 2014 Technology

A new Pew survey finds 40% of adult Internet users in the U.S. have been harassed online. (PHOTO CREDIT: Jacob Wackerhausen, Getty Images)

More than one-third of adult Internet users in the U.S. say they’ve personally experienced harassment online, according to a survey from the Pew Research Center.

The most common form experienced by users is being called an offensive name (27%) or having someone try to “purposefully embarrass them” (22%). As for the more serious forms of harassment, the survey found 8% of users have been physically threatened, while another 8% say they’ve been stalked.

“It was striking to see how different varieties of harassment impacted different groups on different platforms, and the range of reactions online harassment elicited,” said Maeve Duggan, main author of the Pew report, in a statement.

In most cases, users are targeted by someone they don’t know. The survey found half of respondents did not know the person harassing them.
