Most Businesses Shut Down During Staten Island Protest Over Death of Eric Garner

August 23, 2014 National

Against Da Grain’s storefront during the We Will Not Go Back rally in support of Eric Garner. Eric used to bring his kids in here to get haircuts, according to owner Sean White, who had faith in the community to have a respectful, calm rally. (Staten Island Advance/Vincent Barone)

Tompkinsville, usually a bustling nexus of local North Shore commerce, was boarded up, gated off, and virtually shut down during the “We Will Not Go Back” march and rally in support of Eric Garner on Saturday.

Thousands of marchers rallied past closed storefront after closed storefront along Victory Boulevard and Bay Street, though a few holdouts, from local business staples to chain food restaurants, remained open in good faith to support the community.

Carl Peckham took orders at the Everything Goes Book Store, the only shop open on the strip of Bay Street that served as the rallying point for the “We Will Not Go Back” march.

Everything Goes offered free water and bathroom use to any and all who came in as Peckham served up pastries and coffee to peckish protesters. “No justice, no peace!” chants blared through the shop every time a patron opened the front door.

“It’s going great. We decided to stay open because we’re a community center. It would be weird if we closed,” said Carl Packham, who will keep the book store open until 10 p.m., the business’ usual hours. “We also wanted to give people a place to get water and use a bathroom.”

Diana Cardova stopped in for a blueberry scone during her trip from Brooklyn.

“Walking from the ferry, everything was closed. I was shocked to see this place open,” she said as the Everything Goes bathroom line grew. “In the city, stores put up signs that say ‘For paying customers only.’ I’m glad these marchers get to see how nice these people are in here.”

The other eight shops that remained open in the Tompkinsville Park area were equally hospitable.

Rev. Mark Taylor was tremendously grateful that the Elegant Grill & Deli Inn was open and allowed for protesters to stop in and use the bathroom. Taylor came to march in support of Garner, representing the Church of the Open Door in Brooklyn.

“Besides these shops, there really aren’t any places for protesters to go to the bathroom. I’m so glad these guys are open, servicing their community,” he said.
