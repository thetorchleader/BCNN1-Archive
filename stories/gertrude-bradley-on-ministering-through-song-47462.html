Gertrude Bradley on Ministering through Song

November 30, 2014 Black

Gertrude Bradley

Ministering in song is more than a profession to Gertrude Bradley.  In fact, she considers it her ‘calling’ to sing inspirational music for the past 39 years at venues around the world.

A classically trained soprano, Bradley sang at the memorial services of Dodger greats, Roy Campanella and Don Drysdale; the Philharmonic Concert Hall in Berlin, Germany; the Lincoln Center Avery Fisher Hall in New York, and with conductor and violinist, Gustavo Dudamel, at the Hollywood Bowl.

She recently performed before 2,000+ people attending the ‘Wisdom From Above Luncheon with Dr. Betty Price’ and she is scheduled to participate in a concert at New York’s Carnegie Hall in 2016.

Two factors motivated Bradley to pursue this ministry, she recalled.  “The first factor that inspired me to be a minister in song was my innate love of music. I love to use my instrument.

“Second was my thirst for spiritual interaction, knowledge and involvement, which started in me as a young adult, and was prior to my salvation,” shared Bradley.

“For me, [my music ministry] is a divine appointment, a calling. Ministering in music involves allowing the Spirit of God and His anointing to flow through the vessel to reach the spirit of others,” she said.

When Bradley sings, her voice captivates and mesmerizes the audience, according to those who have witnessed her on stage.  “Once you hear her,” said one listener, “you realize she has an incredible gift from God.”  Bradley also has a strong relationship with the Lord.

“I am a Christian. I gave my life to Christ in 1980 and have been a child of the King for 34 years.  God is my all-and-all! I strive to live a life that reveals how good He is to me,” she declared.

Because of her love of God, Bradley can smoothly handle working as a marketing assistant for Crenshaw Christian Center and devoting time to her ministry.
