Bad Cooking Habits You Need to Break Now

April 26, 2015 From Around the Block

© Getty/Maurice van der Velden

We went to the pros — the experts in our Good Housekeeping test kitchens — to ask them what home cooking blunders are their biggest pet peeves. Here’s what they said.

1. Not having a plan — or a process

Think your whole meal through before you start and maximize for efficiencies, says Sherry Rujikarn associate food editor in the Good Housekeeping Test Kitchen. If you don’t turn on the oven or heat the water before you start everything else, you could waste lots of time. Or worse, poor timing could mean one dish finishes much later than another (hello, cold food) or you’re staring down a package of frozen chicken at 6:30 p.m.

2. Using dull knives

This is a cardinal sin. Cutting, chopping, dicing, whatever, with a dull knife is not only frustrating, but can easily lead to cutting yourself (ouch!). Give your knife a few passes on a steel sharpener before each use and sharpen them a few times a year, says Sharon Franke, director of the Good Housekeeping Institute Kitchen Appliances and Technology Lab.

3. Subbing ingredients

This is a biggie for baking: It’s a science, so don’t get too creative. When you swap out too much, your dish may end up turning out completely different — and not tasting good. If you find you want to change a lot, just pick another recipe that includes stuff you actually have.
