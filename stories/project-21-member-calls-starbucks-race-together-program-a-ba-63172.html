Project 21 Member Calls Starbucks’ “Race Together” Program a ‘Bad Idea’

March 23, 2015 Black

Wayne Dupree

A black conservative says Starbucks should stick to serving coffee and not become a forum for conversations about race.

The Washington Times reported last week on Starbucks’ new “Race Together” program, which gives baristas the option of giving customers cups with “#RaceTogether” written on them in hopes of starting a conversation. A spokesman for the company claims the program is “an opportunity to begin to re-examine how we can create a more empathetic and inclusive society — one conversation at a time.”

But the campaign has gotten off to an unpromising start, with widespread ridicule from both sides of the political and racial divide.

Wayne Dupree, a member of the Project 21 black leadership network, thinks it is a bad idea.

“Their business is to serve coffee — period. It’s a bad idea,” he insists. “Most people, when they walk in there they just want their coffee. They want to mind their own business, because they’re going through the day thinking about things that they have to do. They don’t want to be reminded about race.”
