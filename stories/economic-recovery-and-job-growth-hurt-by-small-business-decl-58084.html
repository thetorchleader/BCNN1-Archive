Economic Recovery and Job Growth Hurt by Small Business Decline

February 22, 2015 You Can Work...

© Daniel Acker/Bloomberg A job seeker waits to be interviewed during a Virgin Hotels job fair in Chicago on, Nov. 5, 2014.

Another week, another economic indicator that distracts us from the darkest of underlying economic trends: the decline of entrepreneurship in the U.S.

The Labor Department announced that applications for unemployment last week fell to 283,000. This data – which reveals ups and downs in layoffs – was, of course, characterized in the news as a sign of an improving job market. But there’s a big difference between fewer layoffs (good news, but not awesome news) and more new jobs (an increased employment rate). And we have a lot of people who need jobs, with what some refer to as the “real unemployment rate” (the Labor Department’s underemployment rate)at over 11 percent. Imagine the economy as a bucket of water – in this case, the water is jobs – with a hole in it. The hole just got a little smaller, according to the unemployment applications data, but the level of the water still isn’t increasing the way it should.

Historically, the U.S. has recovered from recessions much more quickly. The water in the bucket used to fill up much faster, fed by a job-pumping hose known as small business. But this recovery has been different – a fact noted by researchers and economists, but apparently not fretted over by our leaders or the media. A crashing economy is news; a pokey economy is boring. That doesn’t mean it isn’t important. In this case, it’s so important it’s worth shouting from the rooftops … but no one is.

Just in case any busy politicians are reading this, I’m going to slow down and make this next point very clear: The recovery is weak because small business is in decline. And please don’t be distracted by the first half of that statement, by the way. It’s so easy to think “well, weak is better than nonexistent.” The more important part is the second half. So I’ll repeat it: Small business is in decline.

And in case you haven’t heard why small business is important, here goes. Small business, historically, creates abouttwo-thirds of our nation’s net new jobs. Small firms employ almost half of the private sector workforce. They make up about half of our nonfarm gross domesticproduct. They are a major source of both innovation and economic stability. Not to mention opportunity for upward mobility.

Economists can, and do, disagree about a lot of things. But the decline in the rate of U.S. entrepreneurship is an important, iron-clad fact that no one is disagreeing with. An excellent piece by Washington Post reporter J.D. Harrison put all the scary data about this trend together in one place last week. I’m an avid reader of small business news, and I didn’t see Harrison’s piece until it showed up in the Kindle edition of the Washington Post yesterday – almost a week after it originally posted. This tells me something about the priorities of the Washington Post’seditors.

Like many Washingtonians, I also follow presidential candidate news pretty closely. And the only candidate I’ve seen comment on the small business part of the recovery is Hillary Clinton. That’s troublesome to me, as a Republican. This should be “our” territory. Republican candidates should be waving a big flag about this issue. But they aren’t.
