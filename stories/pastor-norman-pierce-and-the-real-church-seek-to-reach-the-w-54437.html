Pastor Norman Pierce and the REAL Church Seek to Reach the ‘Worst of the Worst’

January 31, 2015 Black

Parishioners get deeply involved in spiritual singing at Albany’s REAL Church, which holds services at the Flint RiverQuarium’s Imagination Theatre. (Staff Photo: Carlton Fletcher)

As Norman Pierce and some of the members of the newly-formed REAL Church gathered up the few belongings they were taking away from the Divine Connection Church, Pierce looked out over the pews, furniture and other belongings they were leaving behind. He turned to his wife, Kizzie, and said, “Baby, what did we just do?”

That was three years ago.

Today, the REAL Church, which has no permanent home and holds its services at the Flint River Quarium’s Imagination Theatre, is stretched at the seams, growing too big for the structure to hold it. Nondenominational services are drawing some of the community’s best-known businessmen and some of its biggest sinners.

“We’re definitely sinner-friendly,” Pierce, who founded the REAL Church in an effort to “build a true relationship with Christ,” said before a recent morning service. “We have professional people who come to hear the word, but we also have some of the worst of the worst of this community. We welcome them all, but there’s nothing like seeing an ex-criminal build a personal relationship with God.”

The REAL — Relevant, Empowering, Alive and Loving — Church had its genesis in, of all places, an Ohio hotel room. Pierce, who was in Cleveland for a pastor’s conference, was lying in bed, thinking over a conversation he’d had earlier in the day with a fellow pastor who had long been his mentor. He said his musing was interrupted by a voice.
