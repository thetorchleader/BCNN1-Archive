Pew Research Center Survey Finds About Half of Smartphone Owners Say They Couldn’t Live Without It

April 2, 2015 Lifestyle

© David Paul Morris/Bloomberg Pedestrians use Apple Inc. iPhones in front of an Apple Inc. store in San Francisco.

Nearly half of smartphone owners – 46 percent – describe their mobile devices as something “they couldn’t live without,” according to a new Pew Research Center survey.

Smartphone ownership has skyrocketed since Pew first started tracking such data back in 2011, when only 35 percent of the country owned one. That number has since climbed to 64 percent, up from 58 percent at the beginning of last year.

“Younger adults, as well as those who are more affluent and have higher levels of education, are among the most likely groups to own a smartphone,” Monica Anderson, a research analyst at Pew, wrote in a piece accompanying Wednesday’s report. “Three-quarters of smartphone owners reported using their phone for social media, while 60 percent took pictures or a video, and more than half (55 percent) got news on their smartphone at least once over the course of the one-week survey period.”

The Pew report, which surveyed smartphone habits among more than 1,600 respondents, found that smartphone owners were more likely to have sent a text message (97 percent) than make a phone or video call (92 percent) during the weeklong survey period. Another 88 percent sent an email during the window, while only 41 percent used their smartphone to either listen to music or access a map application.

“Smartphones are used for much more than calling, texting or basic Internet browsing,” Aaron Smith, a senior researcher at Pew, wrote in the report. “Users are turning to these mobile devices as they navigate a wide range of life events.”

The report highlights a growing digital commerce and banking trend among smartphone owners, especially those in the youngest age demographic. A reported 57 percent of smartphone owners have used their mobile devices for online banking in the last year, including 72 percent of individuals between 18 and 29 years old and 65 percent of 30- to 45-year-olds.

Another 44 percent of respondents said they had used their phones to research real estate listings, 43 percent looked up job information and 18 percent actually used their smartphones to apply for a job.

“Americans are not only using their smartphone to find information about jobs, but they’re also using their phones to apply,” Anderson wrote. “And among those whose household income is less than $30,000, that share is substantially higher, at 32 percent.”
