Gospel Bowl in Fresno, California, Brings Local Churches Together for Praise and Preaching

January 4, 2015 Black

While waving her purple tambourine from a southeast Fresno church pew, Sherry Yarbrough said, “I feel peace, I feel joy, I feel good.”

The 74-year-old Fresno woman was one of around 50 people praising God on Saturday afternoon during Gospel Bowl 2015. The all-day event at Saint Rest Baptist Church featured a lineup of around 15 black Fresno-area pastors passionately preaching from the pulpit, mixed in with about 10 choirs and performers.

While clapping and swaying to a gospel song, Yarbrough recalled that someone once told her she wasn’t a good tambourine player. It didn’t faze her.

“I play for the Lord,” she said. “Just me and him.”

Dee Davis Moore, who helped plan the Gospel Bowl, said of the worship, “We’re doing touchdowns for the Lord.”

This was Fresno’s second Gospel Bowl, spearheaded by L.H. McDaniel, who retired as Fresno’s deputy chief of police after 39 years with the department. The Gospel Bowl — its theme largely aimed at appealing to young people — was free, but donations were collected to help groups that support youth, including the Fresno Police Activities League, Give a Kid a Wig Project, Edison Babe Ruth Baseball Association and the Sweet Nectar Society. Last year, the event raised about $1,000.

Charles Francis, president of the area Babe Ruth league, said McDaniel was the “epitome of giving back.”

“He’s a retired deputy chief who has a good retirement — he doesn’t have to do this,” Francis said. “He can stay home and kick back in his easy chair and just enjoy the world, but he is taking it upon himself to go out there and put this thing together. I really admire L.H. His concept, his vision, it’s paying off.”

McDaniel hopes the Gospel Bowl inspires others to share their faith and help the people most in need — “the same thing that Jesus did.”

“We’re supposed to have Christ within us,” McDaniel said. “We want to be Christ-like and we want to take that out into the community. It’s not just what we say, we’ve got to live it.”

Pastor Jerome Jackson with Christian Community Baptist Church of Fresno said something similar in his Saturday sermon, using a football pregame huddle as an example.
