Historic Black Church in Louisiana, Augustana Lutheran, Celebrating Its 90th Anniversary This Month

September 6, 2014 Black

Phyllis Culbert with the church is the coordinator for the Augustana Lutheran Church’s 90th anniversary celebration set for Sept. 7.
(Photo: Tia Owens-Powers/ towens@thetowntalk.com)

An historic black Lutheran church in Alexandria celebrates a milestone Sunday.

Augustana Lutheran Church on Lower Third Street, founded in September 1924, turns 90 this month.

Its congregation welcomes former members and community members to a worship service at 3 p.m. that will feature a candle lighting ceremony for past members. It will be followed by a reception at Robert Perry Hall at 4 p.m.

Phyllis Culbert, chairperson of the anniversary planning committee, has been a member since birth. Her parents, Albert and Hazel Jacobs, joined the church upon moving to the area.

Culbert raised her own family in the church and now serves it as secretary/treasurer.

“It has had a tremendous effect on me,” she said. “I was married here. My kids were baptized here, christened here. My daughter’s getting married here in February.”

This is the first big anniversary event since the 50th in 1974. Culbert remembers coming home from college at Southern University to celebrate it with her church family.

She expects about 200 at this ceremony, which will fill the small church that hosts about 15 every Sunday morning.

“We’re celebrating in an effort to let people know we’re still here,” she said. “And we want them to come and worship God here.”

From 1915-1917, the Rev. Eugene R. Berger from Napoleonville led the first black Lutheran services in Alexandria that would lead to the church’s creation. Services were held monthly in people’s homes, according to Augustana’s history provided by Culbert.

Monthly services were discontinued, followed by five years of periodic services led by the Rev. W. J. Trevalon, pastor at St. Paul Lutheran Church in Mansura.
