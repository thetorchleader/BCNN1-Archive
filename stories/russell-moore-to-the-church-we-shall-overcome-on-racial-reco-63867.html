Russell Moore to the Church: “We Shall Overcome” on Racial Reconciliation

March 27, 2015 Christian

Russell Moore speaking at the 2015 ERLC Leadership Summit.

Dr. Russell Moore, president of the Southern Baptist Convention’s Ethics & Religious Liberty Commission, opened the organization’s leadership summit with an address titled “Black, and White, and Red All Over: Why Racial Reconciliation is a Gospel Issue.” The purpose of the summit is to address how the Gospel applies to racial reconciliation especially in light of recent racially charged events across America.

Dr. Moore, who is marking two years as head of the ERLC, noted the Southern Baptist Convention’s history with slavery and its lack of support for civil rights initiatives for African-Americans. He thanked his predecessor, Richard Land, for standing up to racism during his term and stated that ‘God is giving us another chance to get this right.’

Endeavoring to explain how many white Christians view the Christian community, he said, “White born-again Christians tend to assume the body of Christ is white, with room for everyone else… Sometimes we act as though racial reconciliation is an act of mercy to minorities… The kingdom of God is not about coexistence, the kingdom of God is about reconciliation. Racial reconciliation is not just about making sure that we all get along with one another. It is spiritual warfare.”

Branding racial division as an egregious affront to the Gospel and the blood of Christ, Moore said, ‘In order to get racial reconciliation right, we need to repent, not just rebrand… The cross and the Confederate battle flag cannot coexist without one setting the other on fire.’

He went on to explain that the church today ought to look like the Kingdom of God. He explained that the problem with Christianity today is that most people go to church with the same people that they would hang out with if Jesus Christ had never died on the cross.

As a first step toward reconciliation, Moore said, ‘Let’s start praying and asking God why our churches don’t look like the communities around them.’ He also suggested that pastors begin teaching their people about what true worship is in order to get beyond conflicts over differences of worship style between people of different races and cultures.

Concluding his address with a recitation of a popular children’s hymn — “red and yellow, black and white; they are all precious in His sight” — Moore said, ‘Jesus loves the little children of the world isn’t just a Sunday school song; it’s a declaration of war.’ Calling the audience to prayer, he assured them that ‘we shall overcome’ on bridging the racial divide.

The 2015 ERLC Leadership Summit is taking place on Thursday, March 26th – Friday, March 27th. It is being streamed live on BCNN3.TV and GLBN.TV.

Attendees gather in prayer at the front of the auditorium after Dr. Moore’s address.

–BCNN1
