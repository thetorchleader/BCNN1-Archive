How Two Baptist Churches Came Together as One: White Church Freely Gives Building, Treasury Money to Black Congregation

March 13, 2015 Black

WOODY MARSHALL/THE TELEGRAPHPastors Randall Mimbs and Roosevelt Glover in the sanctuary of Evergreen Bloomfield Gardens Church.

This is the story of how two Baptist churches came together as one.

With names like Evergreen and Bloomfield Gardens, you might think they were growing where they had always been planted.

But Sunday mornings were slim. Pews were empty. Hymns and sermons reached so few ears they echoed off the walls.

Evergreen is a black congregation that has been worshipping on the south side of Bibb County since the 1870s.

But when the Hartley Bridge Road exit at Interstate 75 was widened and commercial growth came to the west side of the interstate, the small church found itself surrounded by fast-food restaurants and got somewhat lost in the shuffle.

“We were down in the bottom and boxed in,” said pastor Roosevelt Glover. “We didn’t have a lot of visibility. People passed right by us. So we were looking to move. We started praying the Prayer of Jabez: ‘Oh, that you would bless me and enlarge my territory.’ ”

Bloomfield Gardens was founded in 1955 as a mission church of Pine Forest Baptist. But it wasn’t construction and development that led to its declining enrollment in recent years.

The all-white congregation was gradually surrounded by a neighborhood with changing demographics on Nisbet Drive off Chambers Road. By last October, only about a dozen worshippers remained.

Then something happened. Some might call it a miracle, others a testament to the power of prayer.

Bloomfield Gardens handed over the keys to its church building to Evergreen, along with more than $7,000 in the treasury.

It’s all yours, they said. The building, the property, the parking lot; this is not a gimmick. Just remember to pay the light bill, blessings.
