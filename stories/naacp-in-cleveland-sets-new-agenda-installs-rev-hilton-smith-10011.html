NAACP in Cleveland Sets New Agenda, Installs Rev. Hilton Smith as President

January 14, 2013 Uncategorized

The Cleveland chapter of the NAACP began 2013 with new leadership and the installation of its new officers and executive board members in a celebratory three-hour ceremony Sunday at Greater Abyssinia Baptist Church in Cleveland.


Rev. Hilton Smith speaks following his installation as president of the Cleveland branch of the NAACP Sunday, Jan. 13, 2013, at the Greater Abyssinia Baptist Church in Cleveland.
The Rev. Hilton Smith, elected to a two-year term as president in November, told the nearly 300 people gathered: “We are going to move onward and upward — that is our goal.”
Smith, 66, who works for Turner Construction Co., is also an associate pastor at Greater Abyssinia.
He laid out the group’s ambitious agenda at the tail end of a program that included prayers, hymns, 11 speakers and a youth step group. Smith said he wanted to focus the NAACP’s efforts on education and mentoring, economic development, fair housing, mental health issues, and increasing civic engagement through membership.
“Our youth know nothing about the NAACP,” he said. “We have to educate them, and re-educate them. We have some young professionals who are Uncle Toms, and we made them that way. They have to be taught the history.”
James Hardiman, legal director of the American Civil Liberties Union, told the crowd that while it was a day of celebration, “we have a lot of work to do.”
“We like to say that we are the oldest, largest civil rights organization,” he said. “We are also the poorest.”
Smith said he would like to see the group reach membership levels it saw in the 1960’s. Next month, the NAACP will ask every church in the city to host a membership drive toward that goal.
“Since the NAACP has had such a church base, that’s where our membership came from years ago, and we want to go back to that,” Smith said after the ceremony. “We’re going to do an all-out marketing plan. We’re going to market our young professionals of all ethnicities to come and be with us.”
