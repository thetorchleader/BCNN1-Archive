In Austin, Texas, President Obama Refocuses on Jobs

May 9, 2013 Uncategorized

President Barack Obama doesn’t have that much to show for the first four months of his second term. His outside game failed to prod Congress on gun control or the sequester. His inside game is a work in progress.
So the president traveled to Texas Thursday to talk about the one area where things do seem to be working for him right now: jobs.
“If you listen to all the doom and gloom in Washington and politics and you’re watching cable TV, you might get to kinda thinking nothing is going right,” Obama said at a suburban Austin high school. The unemployment rate is steadily falling, and the number of monthly jobless claims is the lowest in five years. “But the truth, is there are a lot of reasons for us to feel optimistic about where we’re headed as a country, especially after all the tough times that we’ve been through the last several years.”
But even the president’s jobs message comes with a qualifier. He describes an improving jobs outlook — but insists the recovery is being hamstrung by Republican intransigence on the sequester. That means he’s juggling competing approaches, as he publicly asks the American people to “lean on” on members of Congress — the same members of Congress he’s been privately schmoozing over dinner and golf.
Obama told crowds at a high school and a local technology company Thursday that things are getting better on his watch: the unemployment rate is lowest it’s been since 2008, corporate profits are in record territory, and the deficit is falling.
But, he said, congressional gridlock is holding back his efforts to turbocharge the economy. “I’ve sent Congress proposals on a whole range of ideas that will help in these three areas: creating jobs, helping families stay in their homes, lifting wages, helping more young people get a good education and afford college,” he said at the high school. “But some of them have been blocked in Congress for, frankly, political reasons.”
During his second speech Obama warned that “Washington” — his code for congressional Republicans — is harming the country.
“We’ve got to keep on moving forward and we’ve got to make sure that Washington isn’t administering self-inflicted wounds when we’re making progress,” he said.
A White House official said the trip doesn’t mark a change in strategy, like Obama’s March switch from campaign-style public events to private congressional dinners, but rather an addition to the president’s plate. It is, the official said, intended to “make sure part of the discussion includes a conversation about the economy, including a focus on some of the ideas that the president rolled out in his State of the Union address.” Just this Monday, Obama took to the golf links with Sens. Saxby Chambliss (R-Ga.), Bob Corker (R-Tenn.) and Mark Udall (D-Colo.)
But it’s far from clear the president’s return to the outside game won’t have an effect on his efforts to work the inside. Republicans in Congress protested Obama’s post-election barnstorming on the fiscal cliff. They did it again when he traveled the country before sequester was implemented. And Thursday, some Senate Republicans who have praised White House outreach efforts, including a series of dinners with conservative senators, were quick to attack the Austin swing as a return to the campaign politics that drew their ire before the launch of this spring’s charm offensive.
“I don’t know what the purpose of his trip is, but what he should be doing is working with us to find the problem,” said Sen. Ron Johnson (R-Wis.), who praised Obama after attending the first of his dinners at the Jefferson Hotel. “He should really be spending a lot more time with Republicans and their staff to find the problem properly and (then) go to the American people in a bipartisan fashion.”
