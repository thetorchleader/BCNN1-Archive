Pastor Charles Ellis With Ten Point Coalition Among Indianapolis Leaders Pushing for Earlier Curfew for Teens to Curb Crime

August 10, 2014 Christian

Indianapolis Ten Point Coalition (Facebook)

City leaders say they believe curbing crime starts with keeping teenagers from being out late at night.

Mayor Greg Ballard is pushing for an earlier curfew time for 15, 16 and 17 year olds in Indianapolis.

Right now, they can stay out until 1 a.m. on weekends unsupervised. Ballard wants the curfew to be 11:00 p.m. unless they are supervised or coming home from work, school or a church event.

It shouldn’t be a surprise hearing that most parents agree. 24-Hour News 8 caught up with a few parents who were enjoying the Indiana State Fair on Saturday night.

“In our household, it’s a tradition. Every year since they were three years old, we come to the fair,” said parent Nikke Shoultz.

She came to the fair with her whole family, even though Indy’s curfew law allows her 15-year-old daughter to be at there alone, until 1 in the morning, without mom to keep an eye on her.

“In my household, that would not happen,” she said.

But just glancing around the fairgrounds, it’s clear some parents didn’t mind.

“If they are out there, there needs to be responsible people with them,” said Pastor Charles Ellis.

Pastor Ellis is with the Ten Point Coalition, a group that walks the streets late at night encouraging teens to stay out of trouble. They even held training a few weeks ago on how to convince teens to go home when out past curfew.

“We cannot ask the police to do the job that we as parents and concerned citizens need to be doing ourselves,” he said.
