The Baptists Beat the Catholics: Florida State Defeats Notre Dame, 31-27

October 18, 2014 Sports

Florida State tight end Nick O’Leary (35) leans for extra yardage as he is tackled by Notre Dame cornerback Cole Luke (36) in the second quarter Saturday at Doak Campbell Stadium. (Photo: Matt Cashore, USA TODAY Sports)

Breaking down No. 2 Florida State’s 31-27 win against No. 5 Notre Dame on Saturday night:

THE BIG PICTURE: The more things change — the College Football Playoff, most notably — the more they remain the same: As was the case a year ago, Florida State stands perhaps as the best hope of unseating the Southeastern Conference, which has successfully put forth four Playoff contenders, all from the same division, after the first two months of the regular season. It’s a new year; it’s the same deal.

FSU remained unbeaten by fending off Notre Dame in a game that at once highlighted the Seminoles’ inherent flaws while illuminating how the defending national champion can still flip a switch when backed against a wall. The flaws are obvious: FSU houses an underachieving offensive line, a hit-or-miss running game and a too-young defensive front. If easy to locate the Achilles heels, it’s also simple to see how FSU, when pushed, has what it takes to push back against an elite opponent with Playoff and title dreams of its own.

WHAT WE’LL BE TALKING ABOUT: Florida State’s not a perfect team, particularly when compared to the version that stormed through the Football Bowl Subdivision — tested just once, by Auburn — to capture last season’s national championship. Yet one thing is impossible to ignore: Barring any missteps, injuries or blunders, the Seminoles seem destined to complete an unbeaten regular season.

Consider the schedule. Louisville’s defense ranks among the nation’s best, but the Cardinals’ sputtering offense might be unable to keep pace with the Seminoles’ attack. Virginia and Miami (Fla.) are enigmas. Boston College is barely a bowl team, if that. And Florida … well, Florida’s a disaster. Throw in the ACC Coastal Division winner — maybe Duke, maybe another — and FSU’s road to the College Football Playoff has been paved. With its issues, perhaps the Seminoles can adopt a simple mantra: survive and advance.
