New Jersey’s First AME Zion Church to Become a Historical Site

August 5, 2012 Uncategorized

One of the state’s earliest African-American churches is close to becoming the city’s latest historical site.

The City Council voted to designate the 19th-century First AME Zion Church as a municipal landmark this week, a move that would enshrine its cultural significance and provide the building with additional protections against unfitting alterations.
Mayor Jeffery Jones needs to sign an ordinance adopted by the council for it to become law.
By designating the church, the city will seize an “important opportunity” to preserve more of its history, said Gianfrano Archimede, executive director of the city’s Historic Preservation Commission.
A small group formed the church organization in 1834, about a decade after African-Americans in New York City, who were opposed to segregated worship and discrimination in white churches, founded the African Methodist Episcopal Zion movement, according to a commission memo about the history of First AME Zion.
“It is associated with the free African-Americans who worked and worshipped in Paterson prior to the Civil War and experienced an often difficult life,” the memo stated.
