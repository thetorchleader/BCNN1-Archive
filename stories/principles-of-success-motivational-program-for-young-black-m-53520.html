‘Principles of Success Motivational Program’ for Young Black Men Hailed as a Success

January 25, 2015 Black

Motivational Program participants included mentors (l-r, seated) Sean Madison, Dr. Ollie Hadley, Don Dyer, Darrell Brown, James Wheeler, Darrell Ellis, David Moss, and Joe Ellick (standing in left rear). The mentees were (l-r, second row) Ezekiel Joseph, Justin Hinton, Jelani Mora, Marcus Davis, Paris Gray, Donaven Williams, Jonathan Casey, Joshua Sullivan, Joseph Wilson, (l-r, third row) David Berry, Danyae Dunn, George Blackwell, Timothy Scott, Marcus Brown, Ted Citizen, Kenneth Savage, Shannon Alexander and Anthony Alcerro.

Last June, the Men’s Ministry of Brookins Community A.M.E. Church launched the ‘Principles of Success Motivational Program’ for young African American males.

The goal, according to Program Director Don Dyer, was to empower participants to direct and change their lives by developing self-confidence, goal setting, self-discipline and a work ethic adopted by successful people.  The youth were also required to read and discuss the book, ‘A Setback is A Setup for A Comeback,’ by Willie Jolley.

The initiative was an undeniable success with 22 young men initially signing up and 19 successfully completing the program last month.   During culmination ceremony at USC Town and Gown, the graduates shared remarks on their experience, heard inspiring words from Dr. Earl Perkins, LAUSD Assistant Superintendent, and received plaques heralding their accomplishment.

Outlining the program, Dyer explained, “We had strong support from Pastor Gregory McLeod and the members of the Men’s Ministry, led by James Wheeler.  They volunteered their time, talents and resources to ensure the program’s viability and relevance to today’s youth.

“We developed the motivational program to reverse the fact that African American youth graduate from high school and college at a far lower rate than white non-Latino and Latino males. We believed that this reduction was impacted by a number of factors including low self-esteem, the absence of fathers in the homes, insufficient successful African American role models, and a lack of understanding of the work ethic and attitude required to be successful.

“But, we also believed that by equipping African American youth with the skills and knowledge, they could change their lives and achieve their goals. To that end,  we exposed participants to a wide range of successful African American business owners, attorneys, corporate executives, doctors, educators, and community leaders who have faced and overcome many of the obstacles and challenges the young men have or will face,” said Dyer, a retired Price WaterhouseCoopers partner.
