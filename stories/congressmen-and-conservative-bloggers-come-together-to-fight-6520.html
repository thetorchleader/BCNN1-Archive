Congressmen and Conservative Bloggers Come Together to Fight Against SWAT-ting

June 10, 2012 Uncategorized

SWAT-ting involves spoofing someone’s phone, calling 911 about fake shooting, convincing cops to go to victim’s house.

A GOP senator is calling for a Justice Department probe into alleged “SWAT-ting attacks” on conservative bloggers, as dozens of conservative bloggers observed a “day of silence” to draw attention to the pranks.

SWAT teams, not shown, have been called to the homes of leading conservatives in pranks that have been called “disgusting and dangerous.”
SWAT-ting involves placing a fake 911 call reporting a shooting that prompts a full-throated emergency response to the victim’s home. U.S. Sen. Saxby Chambliss (R-Ga.) on June 5 sent a letter to Attorney General Eric Holder demanding an inquiry into instances of SWAT-ting.
“The use of SWAT-ting as a harassment tool is apparently not new,” Chambliss wrote in the letter, “but its use as a tool for targeting political speech appears to be more of a recent development. … The emerging pattern is both disgusting and dangerous.”
Chambliss demanded that Holder look into the matter and respond no later than June 29.
U.S Rep. Kenny Marchant (R-Tex.) also wrote a letter to the attorney general Thursday to demand action against the practice of SWAT-ting.
Marchant wrote that the trend is “alarming,” “embarrassing” and “potentially dangerous” for victims of the pranks, which he wants the Department of Justice to investigate immediately.
Dozens of well-known conservative bloggers joined together in a “Day of Silence” to show both solidarity for their fellow bloggers and support for the letters sent to Holder.
RedState founder and CNN contributor Erick Erickson is among the conservative bloggers to fall victim to the hoax.
“My first thought was, ‘What have the kids done?'” Erickson told ABC News on June 6 after seeing the police car outside his home May 26. “The police officer approached me in the driveway and said it was a call about an accidental shooting. According to the 911 call, the person claimed I had killed my wife.”
Erickson says he had warned police in Macon, Ga., he might be targeted after a fellow conservative blogger in California was SWAT-ted. Because police had received a heads-up from him, he says the matter was quickly resolved, ABC News reports.
