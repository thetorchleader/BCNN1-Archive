Michael Youssef to President Obama: Stop Confusing Islamic Fundamentalism With Islamic Extremism

March 4, 2015 Christian

Michael Youssef

Mr. President,

Your recent summit to “Counter Violent Extremism” was flawed from the start—beginning with its name. Extremism is indeed a threat (especially a threat to Christians), but that is not what we face when confronting ISIS. On that issue, I’m afraid you’re confused.

First let’s look at the definition of extremists: namely, people who advocate or resort to measures beyond norms to enforce their views.

To look at textbook examples of extremism, one doesn’t need to look abroad. Let’s enumerate some acts of extremism inflicted by the US government:

  1. Removing the Bible by force from schools after it had been studied in classrooms for hundreds of years.
  2. Likewise, driving prayer out of schools after it had been practiced for a similar time period.
  3. Dragging parents and students to court for either praying or studying the Bible in school.
  4. Destroying small businesses and persecuting their proprietors, whether a baker or photographer, for simply abiding by their religious beliefs.
  5. Losing government or corporate jobs for simply expressing one’s biblical belief.
  6. Threatening or removing military chaplains if they dare to exercise their faithful duty and call on the name of their Lord in public (again something that was practiced for hundreds of years).
  7. Demanding that people participate in training, corporate and otherwise, that they find ungodly, and punishing them if they refuse to do so.

That’s to say nothing about the humiliation inflicted on patriotic people of deep faith when a national leader describes them as bitterly clinging to their “guns or religion.”

Need I go further, Mr. President?
