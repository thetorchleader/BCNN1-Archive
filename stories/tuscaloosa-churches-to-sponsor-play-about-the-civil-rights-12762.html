Tuscaloosa Churches to Sponsor Play About the Civil Rights Movement

June 6, 2013 Uncategorized

A Tuscaloosa native’s play will be performed Saturday night as part of a commemoration of the 50th year since Birmingham became the focal point of the civil rights movement.

Unity Tuscaloosa, a local ministry, and 14 churches are sponsoring the performance of “Getting Out of Bombingham” at 7 p.m. Saturday at the Bama Theatre, 600 Greensboro Ave.
The play by George W. Stewart, a Tuscaloosa native and co-founder of Unity Tuscaloosa, follows the lives of five children through the pivotal events of 1963.
“It’s a great play, very thought-provoking. It really opens eyes as to what went on,” said Unity Tuscaloosa co-founder Brooks Mouchette. “Part of this has been forgotten. It’s to see what happened so we can see how far we’ve come. We’ve made great strides, but we’ve still got work to do.”
The play’s title refers to Birmingham’s nickname, earned through bombings aimed to intimidate blacks who challenged racial segregation. In September 1963, a bombing at the 16th Street Baptist Church killed four black girls.
Earlier, in May 1963, Birmingham authorities used police dogs and fire hoses against nonviolent black activists.
Public outrage over these events helped lead President John F. Kennedy to propose legislation that became the Civil Rights Act of 1964, which opened the U.S. social, economic and political system to blacks and other minorities.
In addition to the play, Unity Tuscaloosa will also honor six people for their contributions to improving race relations, and the group will kick off its “Unity Projects” program for 2014.
