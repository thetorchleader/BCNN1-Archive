CDC Report Finds that 1 in 5 Women are Raped, Often by Someone They Know

September 8, 2014 National

Nearly half of rape victims knew their perpetrators at least as an acquaintance, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

About 1 in 5 women have been raped in their lifetimes, a new report by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention finds. Nearly half – 44 percent – of women reported experiencing some sort of sexual violence in their lifetimes. While the numbers are much lower for men who are victims of rape (1.7 percent), about one-quarter of men (23.4 percent) were victims of sexual violence. The agency’s findings are the result of a phone survey completed in 2011.

The report found men made up the vast majority of perpetrators of rape and sexual violence upon women (99 percent and 95 percent, respectively), and a majority of victims personally knew their perpetrators. Many women – nearly half (46.7 percent) – who experienced rape in their lifetimes knew at least one of their perpetrators as an acquaintance; nearly just as many female rape victims (45.4 percent) had at least one perpetrator who was an intimate partner. Twelve percent of female rape victims were raped by at least one stranger in their lifetimes.
