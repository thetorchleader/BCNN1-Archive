Regent University and Area Virginia Churches Are Coming Together to Give Young Black and Latino Students Positive Educational Opportunities

April 26, 2013 LCNN1, Uncategorized

In a congregation of 3,500 at New Life Providence Church, senior associate pastor Kevin Turpin sees young black and Latino men with the potential to change their communities.

They are leaders already, engaged at school and involved with the church.
But the high cost of college education often keeps a diploma out of reach.
Through a new initiative at Regent University, the church might be able to change that.
In partnership with four churches, Regent has launched a new scholarship and mentorship program called Take 2 Through College designed to combat low college-going and graduation rates among black and Latino men. The university and sponsoring churches will provide full financial coverage for chosen students for up to four years, and the first eight to 10 students in the program are expected to start this fall.
“I think that this type of program reaches out to people who might not otherwise go to college, and it creates space for them to develop,” said program co-director Antipas Harris, who also serves as director of the school’s Youth and Urban Renewal Center. “We’re saying we want to get them in and mentor them to completion.”
For the coming year, Regent has joined with New Life Providence in Virginia Beach, Calvary Revival Church in Norfolk, Iglesia de Dios Ebenezer in Norfolk and The Potter’s House of Dallas, Texas. Two more partners are expected to join in 2014, and the school plans to eventually expand to partner with organizations and businesses.
Take 2 Through College is nearly three years in the making and addresses a need among what Regent President Carlos Campo calls an “invisible demographic.”
About 15 percent of black men and 23 percent of Hispanic men who began as first-time, full-time students at a four-year institution in fall 2004 earned a bachelor’s degree at that school within four years, according to figures from the National Center for Education Statistics. That’s compared with 36 percent of white men.
At Regent, the undergraduate population for the 2012 fall semester was about 19 percent black and 5 percent Hispanic.
The program is geared toward black and Latino undergraduate men but is not limited by race or gender, Campo said.
