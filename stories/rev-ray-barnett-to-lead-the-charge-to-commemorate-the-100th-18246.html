Rev. Ray Barnett to Lead the Charge to Commemorate the 100th Anniversary of William Sidney Pittman’s 1913 Arrival in Dallas as First Practicing Black Architect

April 16, 2014 Uncategorized

The late architect William Sidney Pittman continues to get what admirers say is long overdue recognition. This time the spotlight is on the 2013 centennial of his arrival in Dallas, an anniversary that went largely unnoticed.

Pittman was the brilliant but troubled craftsman who blazed trails across the nation as a black architect but died a pauper and was buried in an unmarked Dallas grave more than 50 years ago. He is noted for creating 14 buildings in Texas. Those are among about 50 nationwide; records for an unknown number of others have been lost.

The Rev. Ray Barnett is leading the charge to commemorate the 100th anniversary of Pittman’s 1913 arrival in Dallas as Texas’ first practicing black architect and one of the nation’s most accomplished blacks in his field.

Barnett is a preservationist who founded the African American Historic Preservation League and co-founded the Dallas County Historic Church and Cemetery Association. The league is sponsoring “Gone but Not Forgotten: William Sidney Pittman.”

“How many people really know about this man and his vast accomplishments?” Barnett asked. “You usually hear that he was hard to deal with and very bitter toward the end.”

The April 25-26 commemoration will get underway at the African American Museum at Fair Park. A tour of Pittman-designed buildings in Dallas, Fort Worth and Waxahachie will comprise activities from 11 a.m. to 5 p.m. April 25. Included will be Greater El Bethel Missionary Baptist Church, St. James AME Church and Knights of Pythias Temple, all in Dallas; Allen Chapel AME Church in Fort Worth; and Joshua Chapel AME Church in Waxahachie.
