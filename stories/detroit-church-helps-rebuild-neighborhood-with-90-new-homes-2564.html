Detroit Church Helps Rebuild Neighborhood with 90 New Homes

December 31, 2011 Uncategorized

Riding in a church van with “Jesus Saves” written on the back, the Rev. Oscar King III pointed to a row of new homes he helped build on Detroit’s west side.
“It’s completely changed the landscape,” King said while gazing down the block once marred by abandoned homes. “In the midst of decline and vacant homes, we decided we would stay here and reclaim this.”
During the past four years, King’s Northwest Unity Baptist Church has helped build 90 homes in the area around his congregation, near Wyoming and Fenkell streets.
Church officials set up a nonprofit that partnered with a developer. They worked with state and city officials to get tax credits and assistance through the Department of Housing and Urban Development’s Neighborhood Stabilization Program.
The last new tenants moved into their leased homes this month. The monthly cost depends on the occupants’ income levels. King hopes to expand the program in the future, saying stable housing is needed to rebuild Detroit.
The church had more than 800 applications for the 90 homes. One priority in selecting who received a home was making sure they would be able to properly take care of the property.
Those leasing the homes range from professionals to low-income to homeless Detroiters hit hard by the economic downturn. Rather than building mega churches or expensive facilities, churches should focus on projects like this to help people in need, some of the tenants said.
“We don’t need a million-dollar church,” said Jack Bostic Jr., 32. “We need more homes. I want to stay in Detroit. Detroit is going to rise again.”
