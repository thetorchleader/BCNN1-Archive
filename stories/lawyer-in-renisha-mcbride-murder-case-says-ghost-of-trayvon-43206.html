Lawyer in Renisha McBride Murder Case says ‘Ghost of Trayvon Martin’ Was in the Courtroom

November 3, 2014 Black

Theodore Wafer was convicted in August of second-degree murder and involuntary manslaughter in the death of Renisha McBride, 19.
YOUTUBE SCREENSHOT

A defense attorney for the white man convicted of second-degree murder in the shooting of an unarmed black woman on his porch said Martin’s death in Florida in 2012 influenced the outcome of her client’s trial.The shooting death of the unarmed Florida teen Trayvon Martin influenced the outcome of the trial of a white man who was convicted in August of killing a black woman on his suburban Detroit porch, the man’s lawyer told the Detroit News.

Cheryl Carpenter, a lawyer for Theodore Wafer, who was convicted in August of second-degree murder and involuntary manslaughter in the death of Renisha McBride, 19, also denied that racial profiling was a factor in the shooting. McBride was shot after she appeared on Wafer’s porch in the early hours of Nov. 2, 2013, after crashing her car.

“The pressure was different than any other case I’d ever had,” Carpenter told the newspaper. “We had the ghost of Trayvon Martin in the courtroom.”

McBride’s death is often equated with that of Martin, the unarmed black 17-year-old who was shot to death in Florida in 2012 by George Zimmerman.
