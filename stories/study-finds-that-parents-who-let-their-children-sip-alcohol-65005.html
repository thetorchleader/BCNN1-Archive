Study Finds that Parents Who Let Their Children Sip Alcohol May Lead to Later Problems in Life for the Children

April 2, 2015 God & Family

© Seb Oliver/Getty Images
Child reaching for wine

Moms and dads may think that initiating their kids with “just a sip” of alcohol may give them a healthier attitude toward drinking later in life, but new findings show just the opposite may be true. 

In fact, when parents offer children a sip at the dinner table or at social events, researchers say it may send mixed messages to kids about alcohol. A new study, published this week in the Journal of Studies on Alcohol and Drugs, found that kids who sipped alcohol by sixth grade were about five times more likely to have a full drink by the time they were in high school, and four times more likely to binge drink or get drunk.

“Our findings underscore the importance of advising parents to provide clear, consistent messages about the unacceptability of alcohol consumption for youth,” the study authors concluded. “Offering even a sip of alcohol may undermine such messages, particularly among younger children who tend to have more concrete thinking and may be unable to understand the difference between drinking a sip and drinking several drinks.”

The study showed children are most likely to have their first sip of alcohol offered by mom or dad. And some parents think that when they provide their children with alcohol it will remove its “forbidden fruit” appeal or the taste will serve as a deterrent, causing them to avoid it later, a previous study noted.

“We’re not trying to say whether it’s OK or not OK for parents to allow this,” Kristina Jackson, an associate professor of behavioral and social sciences at Brown University’s Center for Alcohol and Addiction Studies and lead researcher for the new study, said in a release.

Data for the analysis were taken from more than 500 Rhode Island students enrolled in a Web-based study on alcohol initiation and progression. Researchers used regular surveys to see whether the students sipped alcohol before sixth grade and examined associations between early sipping and alcohol consumption by the fall of ninth grade.
