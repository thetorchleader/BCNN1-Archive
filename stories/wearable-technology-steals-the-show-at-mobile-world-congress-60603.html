Wearable Technology Steals the Show at Mobile World Congress

March 8, 2015 Technology

A woman looks her mobile phone while attending the Mobile World Congress in Barcelona this week. (Picture: REUTERS)

Barcelona is widely known for its love of tapas, football and Gaudi, but it has also become a hotbed of technological innovation.

Every spring, the Spanish city groans under the weight of Mobile World Congress, which drew to a close on Thursday. An estimated 90 000 delegates descended this year, generating about €436-million (R4.6-billion) for the Spanish economy and creating about 13 000 jobs.

But aside from the financial benefits of delegates wining and dining, the biggest names in the technology world, bar one, use Barcelona to show off the latest phones, gadgets and ideas.

This year’s event was a whirlwind of flagship smartphones, virtual reality headsets and the next generation of wearable technology, namely smartwatches.

With Apple due to reveal details about the long-awaited Apple Watch in San Francisco tomorrow, the congress was where the US company’s rivals laid out their plans.

Predictably, the east Asian giants dominated proceedings. South Korean behemoth Samsung launched its latest smartphones, the Galaxy S6 and S6 edge -the latter sporting a three-sided curved display.

HTC, from Taiwan, also launched a high-end smartphone, the HTC One M9, and a fitness tracker, the HTC Grip.

But it was the surprise announcement of a virtual reality gaming headset, the HTC Vive, that attracted most attention.

The company hopes to launch the Vive before year’s end, and is poised to become the forerunner in an emerging field.

LG shied away from showcasing a new flagship phone to succeed its wildly successful LG G3, instead exhibiting a series of mid-range models alongside its new smartwatch, the LG Watch Urbane, aimed firmly at the luxury market.

Another rival to the Apple Watch has emerged in the shape of Pebble, which raised a record $15-million through crowd-funding site Kickstarter for its customisable smartwatch, the Pebble Time Steel.

Pebble sold more than a million of its first smartwatch, launched two years ago, with consumers attracted by a price below £100 and a battery that lasted a week. Its success has helped Pebble to emerge as the most powerful rival for Apple’s new product.

Connectivity was another key theme as Google senior vice-president Sundar Pichai announced plans to launch a mobile network in the US.

This will enable users to switch seamlessly between cellular and Wi-Fi connections, and between mobile networks.

It will be first rolled out on a small scale, but Google has the muscle to revolutionise the sector.

Facebook’s Mark Zuckerberg admitted he’d “love” to work more closely with Google in helping the developing world to connect to the internet.

Facebook launched its Internet.org project, which provides individuals and businesses in developing countries with affordable internet access in 2013.

Zuckerberg praised Google Search, one of the project’s first apps, and smirked when Mario Zanotti of mobile network Millicom referred to Facebook as Google.

Apple, the biggest of them all, bypasses the conference entirely, meaning the most keenly awaited gadget of 2015, the Apple Watch, wasn’t present.

But cynics are being disingenuous if they dismiss the Samsung S6 Edge’s curved screen, or the utrapixel front-facing camera on the HTC One M9 — to serve the selfie craze.

This year, smartwatches and virtual reality headsets also took centre stage. Both are at a crucial stage. We’re witnessing the launch of products that will either define their categories, or fail to make their mark entirely. For these reasons, the Barcelona congress has never been more essential.
