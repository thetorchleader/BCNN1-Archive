Texas Rangers Josh Hamilton Hits Four Home Runs In One Game

May 9, 2012 Uncategorized

Home Run Derby, except these count.

Texas Rangers outfielder Josh Hamilton, who put on a memorable show of power the day before the 2008 All-Star Game at Yankee Stadium, became the 16th player in history to hit four home runs in a game when he connected in the eighth inning Tuesday against Baltimore Orioles right-hander Darren O’Day.
All of Hamilton’s home runs were two-run shots, the others coming in the first, third and seventh innings as the Rangers beat the Orioles 10-3. He also doubled in the fifth and finished 5-for-5 with 18 total bases, which set an American League record and fell one shy of the major league mark.
“Obviously it’s, other than being in the World Series, the highlight of my big-league career,” Hamilton said. “I was saying after I hit two I’ve never hit three in a game before, and what a blessing that was. Then to hit four is just an awesome feeling, to see how excited my teammates got.
“It reminds you of when you’re in Little League and a little kid, and just the excitement and why we play the game. Things like that. You never know what can happen. It was just an absolute blessing.”
Hamilton connected twice off starter Jake Arrieta before facing lefty Zach Phillips in the seventh. Hamilton was hitting .378 off left-handers coming in and pounced on Phillips as well before delivering the record-tying drive off the sidearming O’Day in the next inning.
Hamilton, a free agent after the season, became the first major leaguer to go deep four times in a game since the Toronto Blue Jays’ Carlos Delgado on Sept. 25, 2003 against the then-Tampa Bay Devil Rays.
And it marked another improbable milestone in a career once imperiled by Hamilton’s well-chronicled battles with drug and alcohol addiction.
“It’s an absolute blessing,” Hamilton said on ESPN. “For everything I’ve gone through, to be in position to do that is awesome.”
