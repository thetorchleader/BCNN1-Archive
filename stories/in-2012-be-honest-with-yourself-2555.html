In 2012, Be Honest With Yourself

December 31, 2011 Uncategorized

With the New Year upon us, as we ponder the resolutions we might make, let’s go up into the attics of our minds, dust off a word many of us haven’t mouthed for a while, and make it part of our daily lives.
Confession. Resolved: I will go to confession. Daily.
Let me explain.
Confession, once reserved for musty confessional booths (Catholics), sometimes perfunctory religious rituals (Jews), and fire-and-brimstone sermons (Protestants) can be a powerful force in our lives. But we must look at it in a new and fresh way: an art to be practiced, not an exercise in guilt and self-loathing.
Because deep within us is the desire to do the right thing, to be a good and decent person. Of course, we often don’t live up to what is best in us, allowing that inner compass called a conscience to get so far out of calibration that we really do lose our bearings. And we feel rotten about it, not knowing how to help ourselves.
Strength in honesty
In these days of drip-dry morality, oversharing online and in front of TV cameras, it’s time to step back and look at what a real and honest daily look at ourselves can produce. We need to confess, and not to consider it a sign of our weakness. Just the opposite.
As my research on the subject has taught me, confession makes you strong — warrior strong — and if you need any reinforcement, even bypassing Dr. Freud and everything your mother told you, let’s take a look at the fierce Ku Waru warriors of Papua Guinea.
Before they went into battle, the warriors would gather around a roast pig or two and confess to each other things they had stolen, animals mistreated, other men’s wives that they might have done more than fancied. Their culture taught them that such secrets would sap their strength and cause them to be wounded or killed. 
Only through an honest confession could they go onto the battlefield at full strength.
We might cover more of our bodies with silk or oxford cloth instead of homespun loin cloths, but we are just as vulnerable in the skirmishes of our daily lives. Bearing the burden of the duplicities, false pride, greed, lusts, and self-importance that all of us have in various measures, we, too, are weaker. To carry this physic load is more than we can handle.
