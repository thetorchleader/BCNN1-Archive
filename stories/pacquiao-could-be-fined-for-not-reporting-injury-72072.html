Pacquiao Could be Fined for Not Reporting Injury

May 5, 2015 Sports

Manny Pacquiao is tended to by his team between rounds during his welterweight unification bout against Floyd Mayweather. (John Gurzinski/AFP/Getty Images)

In the wake of the highly anticipated Floyd Mayweather-Manny Pacquiao fight, which turned out to be an anticlimactic dud won by Mayweather in a unanimous decision, there have been many comments along the lines of, “What did anyone expect? Floyd’s fights are always like this.” Well, one thing a lot of people expected was to see Mayweather’s vaunted defensive prowess tested like never before by Pacquiao’s relentless pressure.

Mayweather certainly brought his defense, but the damn-the-torpedoes, attacking style that won Pacquiao so many fans all over the world was very much missing in action. He actually wound up throwing fewer punches than Mayweather, which would have been unthinkable before Saturday’s lucrative event.

After the fight, Pacquiao offered an explanation: He had been fighting with an injured shoulder. Many dismissed that as a lame excuse, but on Monday, an orthopedic surgeon at the prestigious Kerlan-Jobe Clinic told ESPN that the Filipino icon not only does have a shoulder injury, but it’s one that requires surgery and could shelve him for an entire year.

“We have an MRI scan that confirms he has a rotator cuff tear. He has a significant tear,” Dr. Neal ElAttrache told ESPN.

Even assuming Pacquiao’s surgery and rehabilitation go well, he is still looking at an absence of nine to 12 months, according to the surgeon. It was already unlikely that Mayweather would have agreed to a rematch, but this news makes it a certainty that his quest to match the 49-0 record of legendary heavyweight Rocky Marciano will come against a different opponent (despite the demands of a certain reclusive rock star).

Pacquiao’s next fight could come against the Nevada Athletic Commission, which is unhappy that the boxer failed to disclose the injury on an official pre-fight form. He, or someone in his camp, checked “No” next to a question asking, “Have you had any injury to your shoulders, elbows, or hands that needed evaluation or examination?”

“We will gather all the facts and follow the circumstances,” NAC chairman Francisco Aguilar told the Associated Press. “At some point we will have some discussion. As a licensee of the commission you want to make sure fighters are giving you up-to-date information.”

Pacquiao could be fined or suspended by the commission, and, technically, he could even face a felony charge by Nevada authorities for perjury. NAC officials denied a request by Pacquiao’s camp to inject him with Toradol, a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug, just an hour before the fight.
