Defense for Tyrone Washington Jr. Wants African-Americans on the Jury in his Murder Trial

October 11, 2014 Black

Tyrone Washington Jr.

40-year-old, Tyrone Washington Jr. and his attorney are making it clear, they’re concerned about the lack of racial diversity in the jury pool.

On October 20, jury selection is set to begin in the highly anticipated trial of Washington Jr.

He’s on trial for the 2013 death of Northwood native, 30-year-old Justina Smith.

Friday, Tyrone Jr. made his first appearance in pre-trial hearings and shared his concerns about the trial going forward.

Concerns which include the racial makeup of the jury set to hear the trial.

Washington and his attorneys are arguing that with a lack of African-American jurors, Washington may not receive a fair trial in the Webster County District Court.

“My client is terrified that he’s going to walk into court, two weeks from now and he’s going to be looking at 122 white faces looking back. Especially when you have an issue here where there’s a African-American defendant and a white victim, I think that is a concern, that on his part he should be concerned about,” said Defense Attorney, Charles Kenville.

The state replied by saying that they’re making sure they do all they can to provide a jury pool that fairly represents Webster County.

“It need not be an exact mirror of the community and reflect the various distinctive groups in the population of the community, It just needs to be from a source that does not systematically excluded distinctive groups,” said Iowa Assistant Attorney General, Laura Roan.

African-Americans represent 5 percent of the Webster County population, but jury surveys suggest that out of 122 potential jurors, there is only one African-American expected.

One thing that could throw that expectation off, is that jurors were given the option to leave the race column of the surveys blank.
