The Worst Cities for Businesses

May 29, 2015 Business

1 of 11 © nikonphotog/Getty Images; Moment Open/Getty Images; DenisTangneyJr/Getty Images

FEW COMPANIES IS THE COMMON THREAD FOR THESE METRO AREAS
Two years ago, when MarketWatch last ranked U.S. cities by their capacity to grow local business, six of those in the bottom 10 were in California.

This year, that number has fallen to three, thanks to budding prosperity in two of the cities and the fact that a third, Modesto, fell out of contention because it is no longer one of the nation’s 100 biggest cities. This time, the pain is spread a little more evenly across the country, with the bottom 10 cities located in such far-flung states as Massachusetts and Georgia, Texas and New Mexico.

Just because these cities are on this list doesn’t mean they are without redeeming qualities, at least according to our criteria. Some earned high marks for certain strengths but ended up at the bottom of the list because they fell woefully short elsewhere. See the ranking of the top 10 cities.

What consistently hurt these cities most — because this study was conducted by a financial-news website — was scarcity of publicly traded companies. Riverside’s failure, for example, to have any of its businesses listed on the New York Stock Exchange gave it the worst possible ranking in four of the study’s categories.
