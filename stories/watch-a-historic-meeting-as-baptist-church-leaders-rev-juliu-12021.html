WATCH: A Historic Meeting as Baptist Church Leaders Rev. Julius Scruggs and Pastor Fred Luter Meet at Alabama Church

April 26, 2013 Uncategorized

First, there was the joke – a surprising one involving a deacon who offered a series of “Good News” and then “Bad News” tidbits to her friend. Then, there was laughter – robust laughter that filled the sanctuary of West Mastin Lake Baptist Church in Huntsville, Ala., Thursday, April 25, 2013 – and was punctuated by Pastor Fred Luter Jr., president of the Southern Baptist Convention, stamping his foot on the platform while he joined the laughter.
With that bracing beginning, Luter launched into a half-hour sermon that ranged from quiet explication of John 10:10-12 to rhyming couplets, dazzling rhetoric and a passionate rhythm so musical that when the organist joined him for the last few minutes of his sermon, it seemed he was singing another verse with the gospel choir.
Luter, one of America’s greatest preachers spoke to a rapt crowd of about 150, some of whom had driven to Huntsville from Arab. His message was down-home Baptist and up-town powerful. Based on John 10:10-12, Luter preached, Jesus tells his followers that he is the shepherd, the doorway, to a life more abundant, a life protected against ravages of thieves who attempt to break in and kill and destroy.
“Jesus says, ‘Whatever you need me to be, I Am that I Am,'” Luter preached. “I am a mother to the motherless, a father to the fatherless, a friend to the friendless – but only for those who come through the door. Jesus says, ‘I am the way.'”
Luter paused and surveyed the congregation.
“Remember, the national anthem of Hell is ‘I Did It My Way,'” Luter said.
Luter warned the congregation to beware of the Devil, who comes to steal their families, their marriage, their kids, their dreams, their hopes, their joy, their smiles.
“By the look on some of your faces, he already got your smile,” Luter said, guaranteeing that every face in the room broke into a smile.
And what awaits those who come to Jesus, Luter said, is life more abundant than anything they can get anywhere else.
“Abundant life – that means a life full, complete, whole, life to the max,” Luter said. “That doesn’t mean you’re going to be rich – but richer than rich with a joy the world can’t give, a peace the world can’t take away.”
“You may not have everything you want, but, I promise, you will have everything that you need.”
