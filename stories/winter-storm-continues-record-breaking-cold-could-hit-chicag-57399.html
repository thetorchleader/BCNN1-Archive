Winter Storm Continues: Record-Breaking Cold Could hit Chicago

February 18, 2015 National

The worst of winter is yet to arrive.

Most of Wednesday will be spent with temperatures in the single digits, though with wind chill readings in the -10 to -20 range. But as if that weren’t bitter enough, Mother Nature will usher in even colder air overnight into Thursday morning, as the actual air temperatures drop to -2 in the city and about -10 degrees inland.

A Wind Chill Advisory kicks in at 6 p.m. Wednesday and lasts through 12 p.m. Thursday.

Thursday’s temperatures are predicted to reach the single digits for the high, which could break the record low maximum of 9 degrees set on February 19, 1936. NBC Chicago Storm Team Meteorologists predict wind chill readings during the day could drop to nearly -30 degrees, making it the coldest day so far this year.

Overnight low temperatures on Thursday could range from -2 to -12 degrees with wind chills between -20 to -30 degrees. The record overnight low was also set in 1936, dipping to a chilly -7 degrees.

The Illinois Tollway activated its Zero Weather Road Patrols Tuesday in anticipation of the sub-zero temperatures. The patrols operate around the clock to help stranded drivers when wind chill values fall below zero degrees.

The cold air and northwesterly wind flow will keep the lake effect snow machine going to our east in the Michiana snow belts where a Winter Weather Advisory is posted for La Porte, LaGrange, St. Joseph and Elkhart counties through Thursday evening at 6:00PM.

Between 4 and 8 inches of snow could fall in the affected counties while the area experiences extreme cold temperatures, with wind chill values of -15 to -20 degrees.

Blowing snow could also cause reduced visibility, impacting travel on snow-covered roads.

After a bitter start Friday the cold will ease as warm air advection starts, but this will likely bring increasing clouds and late day light snow. Saturday brings a shot at 30 degrees, but our attention will shift to a developing storm passing to our south which could bring accumulating snow to Chicago into Sunday.
