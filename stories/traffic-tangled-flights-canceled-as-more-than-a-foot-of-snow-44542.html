Traffic Tangled, Flights Canceled as More than a Foot of Snow Comes Down On Twin Cities

November 10, 2014 National

The season’s first significant snowfall in the Twin Cities and elsewhere in Minnesota — expected to top a foot in depth by Tuesday — arrived just before Monday’s morning rush hour, and it quickly had motorists reacquainting themselves with their winter driving skills.

Eventual snow depths will vary greatly, according to the National Weather Service (NWS), with the northern edge of the metro and into western Wisconsin topping out at 16 inches, and the southern Twin Cities area closer to a half-foot. Joining in are wind speeds ranging from 15 to 25 miles per hour, the NWS added.

By early afternoon, the weather service said late Monday morning, the heaviest snowfall will occur north of the Twin Cities, with a sleet and snow mix in the metro area.

As of late Monday morning, snow totals ranged from 7 inches in St. Cloud to 5.8 in Coon Rapids and 3.1 in Little Canada, according to the NWS.

Then late Tuesday, comes a sharp dip in temperatures for the metro that is expected to last for much of the week, with single-digit overnight lows in the forecast.

At the Minneapolis-St. Paul International Airport, the number of flights canceled in and out Monday has soared to roughly 175 so far, said MSP spokesman Patrick Hogan. Delta and Southwest kicked in their rebook-with-no-penalty policies. Check their websites for details.

A thin layer of ice blanketed by snow proved challenging as morning commute unfolded in the metro area, with more than 25 crashes on the board between 5 and 6 a.m.

For the morning rush in the Twin Cities, the State Patrol said it handled 128 crashes with nine injuries, along with 74 spinouts and vehicles off road.
