New Poll Shows the Reputation of Southern Baptists Is Waning

December 12, 2011 Uncategorized

Americans like Southern Baptists more than Mormons and Muslims but less than Methodists, according to a poll released this week seeking to gauge public perception of Southern Baptists.
A statue of Billy Graham stands outside the Southern Baptist Convention’s Nashville headquarters. / Mark Humphrey / Associated Press
Southern Baptist Convention President Bryant Wright, an Atlanta-area megachurch pastor, asked for the poll after launching a task force this year to consider changing the denomination’s name. That group asked denomination-affiliated LifeWay Research to poll public opinion about the denomination.
Researchers discovered 62 percent of Americans have a favorable view of Methodists, while only 53 percent have a favorable view of Southern Baptists. About 40 percent of Americans have a negative impression of Southern Baptists, the lowest rating among traditional denominations.
The denomination is particularly unpopular among people who don’t go to church, young adults and Hispanics.
That’s a concern for Southern Baptists, who are trying to grow their denomination by attracting those groups.
“Those who we are trying to reach may be among those who like us the least,” said the Rev. Frank Page, president of the convention’s Nashville-based executive committee.
Local Baptist pastors say they’re disappointed but not surprised by the poll results.
The Rev. Frank Lewis of First Baptist Church on Broadway said he knows Southern Baptists aren’t perfect. But unlike some churches, which have dropped Baptist from their name, his church has no plans to change its name.
