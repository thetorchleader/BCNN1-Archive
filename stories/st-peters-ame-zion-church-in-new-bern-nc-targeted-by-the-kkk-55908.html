St. Peter’s AME Zion Church in New Bern, NC, Targeted by the KKK

February 9, 2015 Black

St. Peter’s AME Zion Church on Queen Street in New Bern. On Jan. 24, 1965, three members of the local Ku Klux Klan (see inset) bombed two cars of NAACP members meeting in the church, along with bombing a local, black-owned mortuary. The defendants are, left to right: Laurie Fillingame, Raymond MIlls and Edward Fillingame.
Photo by Bill Hand/Sun Journal Staff; inset photo from the New Bern Sun Journal, June 1, 1965.

Fifty years ago today, Jan. 24, 1965, England was grieving the death of Winston Churchill, the man credited for bringing the nation through the Great War.

And in America, another Great War was taking place: the battle for equal rights for black Americans. And in New Bern, one of its violent battles was taking place. It happened like this:

St. Peter’s AME Zion Church had been a center of civil rights since its inception shortly after the Civil War by founder James Walker Hood. On this day in 1965, the church was the scene of a five-county rally of the NAACP. The main speaker of the night was Julius Levonne Chambers.

A Civil Rights activist, Chambers (1936-2013) is best known for his work with the NAACP Legal Defense Fund. In 1965 he was the director of a law firm representing the NAACP North Carolina chapter.

His name not only drew a crowd of NAACP members from across the region: it also pricked the ears of “Exalted Cyclops” Raymond Mills, leader of the Ku Klux Klan Klavern, the New Bern branch of the KKK.

Mills contacted fellow Klansmen Laurie Fillingame, 21, and Edward Fillingame, 23. Armed with sticks of dynamite, they men climbed into Mills’ red Plymouth and drove from their home in Vanceboro to New Bern.
