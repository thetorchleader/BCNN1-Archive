Civil War-era Sailors from Sunken Ironclad Monitor to be Buried at Arlington National Cemetery

March 7, 2013 Uncategorized

On Friday, the nation will honor two unknown warriors who “may very well be the last Navy personnel from the Civil War to be buried at Arlington.” But their identities remain a mystery.
Pictured: Although an attempt was made, using forensics analysis, to reconstruct the faces of two sailors whose remains were discovered inside the gun turret of the USS Monitor, their identities remain a mystery. (Photo: Spc. 2nd Class Gina K. Morrissette, U.S. Navy)
There were dreams of recapturing faded glory as the USS Monitor headed south under clear skies toward Confederate seaports 150 years ago last December.
The crew’s hope was “trying our metal against rebel works and making the ‘Little Monitor’ once again a household word,” William Keeler, the ship’s assistant paymaster, later wrote.
They had been a national sensation nine months earlier, after battling the terrifying Confederate ironclad CSS Virginia, formerly the USS Merrimack, to a draw off Hampton Roads and changing the course of naval warfare. Abraham Lincoln himself had come to meet the famous crew and see the iron turret dimpled from point-black rounds that bounced off during the battle. The sailors posed for pictures.
But there were no new, shattering headlines about victory at sea reminiscent of that day at Hampton Roads. That might come only with a journey to the Deep South.
In the wee hours of New Year’s Eve 1862, 16 miles off Cape Hatteras, gale-force winds and 20-foot waves swamped the warship as she was being towed and the Monitor foundered, 16 of 63 crew and dreams of restored celebrity slipping beneath the sea.
That is, until Friday, when a nation buries at Arlington National Cemetery two of the crew whose skeletons were recovered from the sunken wreck, their identities still uncertain.
“Their family kind of becomes the nation,” says David Alberg, National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration superintendent of the 1-square-mile sanctuary where the ship sank and most of it remains.
The sailors’ remains “may very well be the last Navy personnel from the Civil War to be buried at Arlington,” says Navy Secretary Ray Mabus.
Mabus and Civil War historian James McPherson will deliver remarks at a Joint Base Myer-Henderson Hall service Friday.
The Navy expects scores of relatives of those who served on the Monitor, including 21 descendants of some lost in the storm, to attend.
