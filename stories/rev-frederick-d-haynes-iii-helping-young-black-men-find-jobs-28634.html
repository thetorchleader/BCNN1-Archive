Rev. Frederick D. Haynes III Helping Young Black Men Find Jobs With Thr!ve Intern and Leadership Program

Date not recorded in the original archive Christian

The Rev. Frederick D. Haynes III (left) speaks to the Thr!ve interns while Georgetown University professor Michael Eric Dyson applauds. Nearly 100 young black men are participating in Haynes’ six-week intern and leadership program.
Donica Williams

On Monday 17-year-old Cory Smith of Mesquite will start his first job, earning $10 an hour.

Smith is one of nearly 100 young black men participating in the Thr!ve Intern and Leadership Program, a six-week program providing employment opportunities and skills.

The Rev. Frederick D. Haynes III, senior pastor of Dallas’ Friendship-West Baptist Church, founded the program in March.

Haynes’ program is in line with President Barack Obama’s My Brother’s Keeper Initiative. Through the five-year initiative, philanthropists and corporate leaders pledged to invest at least $200 million to ensure that young men of color reach their potential.

Last month, the unemployment rate for black Americans was 11.5 percent, while the rate for white Americans was 5.4 percent.

Approximately 137 Thr!ve applicants participated in an initial job readiness workshop and job fair. Those selected for internships will work at least 20 hours a week making $10 per hour, almost $3 above the federal minimum wage. The program also includes college tours, mentorships and workshops.

In 90 days, Haynes partnered with local business owners, corporations and volunteers, he said during a news conference last week.

The program raised $150,000, and interns will work with 35 companies, he said.
