Florida Neighborhood Named for Zion Hill Missionary Baptist Church Founder, Rev. Charles Pugh, Celebrates Its History and Emancipation

May 19, 2013 Uncategorized

Memorial Day weekend signals the annual May Day Festival of Central Florida in Historic Pughsville.


Attendees enjoy fish with sides of beans, cole slaw and salad during the Historic Pughsville Association’s free Community Fish Fry in 2012 in Winter Haven. The association will celebrate the May 20, 1865, emancipation of slaves in Florida with the May Day Festival of Central Florida during the Memorial Day weekend. (PAUL CRATE | NEWS CHIEF)
Although only four Pughsville families still live in the neighborhood, every year the May Day Festival of Central Florida continues commemorating the emancipation of Florida slaves May 20, 1865.
The free community fish fry from 5 to 9 p.m. Friday begins the celebration of the historically African-American neighborhood that once had a bustling business district with many residents working in the citrus industry.
The fish fry is at the corner of Second and Third streets Southeast and Post Avenue Southeast, where a weekend full of activities is planned by the Historic Pughsville Association.
Live entertainment will be provided all weekend by disc jockey D. J. Dave and by the The 37th Street Blues Band on Friday and Saturday.
A Gospel Fest and Praise Expression from 1 to 6 p.m. Sunday includes music from the historic churches in the neighborhood as well as several in Winter Haven.
“This is one way we reach out to the Winter Haven community,” said Patricia Smith-Fields, one of the organizers.
Bloomin’ onions, cole slaw, salad, breadsticks and oil to fry the fish are being donated by area restaurants. A team of men fries the fish and the association provides grits and baked beans.
It is one way the association honors many memories of the neighborhood named for the Rev. Charles Pugh, a founder of Zion Hill Missionary Baptist Church, one of the oldest black churches.
