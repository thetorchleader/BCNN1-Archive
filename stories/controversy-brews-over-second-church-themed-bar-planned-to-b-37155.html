Controversy Brews Over Second Church-Themed Bar Planned to Be Built In Athens, GA

September 5, 2014 National

The front of the Church Bar on Tuesday, August 26, 2014. (Photo/ John Roark/ johnroarkmedia.com)

Although the hundreds of pieces of art preach about crack whores, sex and Jesus Christ, Sister Louisa’s Church of the Living Room & Ping Pong Emporium is the closest thing to a sanctuary and is making a presence at the new location in Athens.

Established in 2010 in Atlanta, artist and bar owner, Grant Henry is bringing his controversial church bar to Athens. Waiting for the liquor license, Henry is hoping to open his second location in the middle of September.

Henry, alternatively known as artist Sister Louisa, stumbled upon the location at 254 W. Clayton St., after a visit to Athens less than a year ago.

After quickly finding a location, Henry is opening his bar in the building recently home to Jack’s bar.

“I love Athens. I love the downtown feel. I’m looking to buy a house so I can have a place close by,” Henry said.

The original bar in Atlanta showcases over 200 pieces of Sister Louisa artwork, portraying eclectic pictures of religious and historical figures. Henry has been the man behind Sister Louisa for 18 years and thinks his artwork walks a thin line.

“It’s not a joke to me. It’s not to push any buttons. My art stands on a fine line and it says really nothing about me as an artist,” Henry said.

Henry was a bartender for 10 years before changing an old coffee shop on Edgewood Drive in Atlanta into a church themed bar. Although wanting a dive style bar, Henry decided to decorate the space with his Sister Louisa artwork, creating a place to showcase his work.

“I did it more from an artistic point of view because I’m more of an artist than a bartender,” Henry said. “I turned it into a bar basically for my art. It’s more like an art gallery that sold alcohol.”
