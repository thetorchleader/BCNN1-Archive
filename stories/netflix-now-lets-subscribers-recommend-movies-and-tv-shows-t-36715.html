Netflix Now Lets Subscribers Recommend Movies and TV Shows to Facebook Friends

September 2, 2014 Technology, Uncategorized

Watching shows such as Breaking Bad on-demand can make it difficult to share the experience, often for fear of spoilers. (PHOTO CREDIT: AP)

Netflix is aiming to help subscribers to its on-demand film and television service rediscover the shared experiences offered by traditional scheduled broadcasting, with a major overhaul of the way it works with Facebook.

The company already allows subscribers to link their Netflix account with their Facebook account to automatically tell their friends what they have been watching, but the system has proved a limited success. In part it has been seen as contributing to a tide of unwanted ‘spam’ messages on the social network.

Rather than automatically update Facebook with their viewing habits, after they finish watching a film of television programme, Netflix subscribers who have connected their account to the social network will now be shown a gallery of their closest friends’ pictures and asked to directly recommend it to one of them.

Cameron Johnson, Netflix’s director of product innovation said: “In the past people were watching the same things at the same time. It was on at a certain time slot and the next morning people would come into work and chat about it.

“But now the user is in control and they can watch whatever they want to watch whenever they want to watch it. But there’s still this natural desire to connect.”

To make it more personal, they will be able to add a custom message that will pop up next time their friend logs into Netflix. If the friend does not subscribe to the online streaming service, which is believed to have broken the three-million-household barrier in Britain, it will appear as a direct message on Facebook.

The idea, according to Mr Johnson, is to recreate how people recommended their favourites in the pre-Netflix age, the so-called ‘watercooler moments’ driven by memorable film and television.

“Internet TV is really new and social networking is still fairly new trying to understand what the right model is takes a lot of testing,” he said.

The changes are not purely self-imposed, however. Facebook announced earlier this year that it would cut back on the amount of auto-generated material third parties were able to send to members’ friends, as part of its own efforts to increase engagement with the ‘newsfeed’ that is the main way people interact with its service, and also its advertisers.

“It worked for some people but what’s lacking is the real human connection,” said Mr Johnson. “The most important thing is that you’re able to connect to that handful of people that you talk to on a regular basis, those people that you would really have a conversation about movies or TV with in real life.
