Historic Civil Rights Figure, Rev. Jim Webb, Gives Rousing Sermon at Shiloh Baptist Church in Fredericksburg, VA

March 23, 2015 Black

PHOTOS BY SUZANNE CARR ROSSI/THE FREE LANCE–STAR
The Rev. Jim Webb spoke to the congregation at Shiloh Baptist Church (Old Site) on Sunday morning about overcoming struggles in life.

True to form, the Rev. Jim Webb delivered an energetic and fiery sermon on Sunday at Shiloh Baptist Church (Old Site) in downtown Fredericksburg.

The church invited the noted Baptist preacher and civil rights activist to give a sermon on the heels of the 50th anniversary of the Selma-to-Montgomery march, which served as a springboard toward African–Americans being granted voting rights.

On Sunday, he focused on the Book of Genesis and the story of Joseph’s travails—being betrayed by his brothers, enslaved, falsely accused and jailed in Egypt—before finally becoming the interpreter of the pharaoh’s dreams.

When famine later struck, Joseph reconciled with his brothers by feeding them.

The day was filled with singing and celebrating, but the congregation really sprang to life as Webb stood at the pulpit, deftly weaving humor with passionate outbursts of scripture, all the while relating his message to life’s constant challenges.

Throughout the sermon, the small man’s voice boomed through the packed church, often followed by shouts of “Amen” and “Yes” from the congregation.

The sermon’s focus on reconciliation also had an interesting twist because of Webb’s mercurial past.

On the one hand he has been painted as a hero for his courage in the nonviolent protests during 1960s civil rights movement. He has been lauded for his powerful preaching and his many good acts in churches and communities.

But Webb’s past also is one tainted with an arrest for theft and lawsuits by churches he once preached for, along with a litany of other allegations detailed in a 2012 Virginian–Pilot story while Webb was a pastor in the Virginia Beach area.

Regardless of questions about his past, Webb is seen as an undeniably energetic force wherever he goes. As former Shiloh pastor and Fredericksburg Mayor Lawrence A. Davies said after the sermon, Webb is adept at tying Scripture to what has happened and what is happening around the world.

And, Davies added, Webb has lived what he preaches, putting himself “in a vulnerable position” in Selma.

He said that Webb’s message of nonviolent, peaceful protest is crucial today, with unrest so prevalent.

In 1965, the then-16-year-old Webb became well-known for his peaceful approach during the contentious Selma protests.
