WATCH: Militants Say Attack Against Christians In Kenya Is Only the Beginning

April 3, 2015 Eye on Africa

A woman is rescued from a building on April 2 after government soldiers assaulted Islamic militants holding hostages at Garissa University in Garissa, Kenya. (Photo: Dai Kurokawa, European Pressphoto Agency)

The al-Shabab militants who stormed a Kenyan university, killing at least 147 people, warned the students before they died that it was only the beginning of attacks on Kenyan schools, according to local media.

The mortuaries in the Kenyan town of Garissa are so overwhelmed by victims of Thursday’s attack on Garissa University that they are shipping many of the bodies to Nairobi, the BBC reports.

Two security guards, one policeman and one soldiers were among the dead, along with four gunmen from the Islamist militant group.

At least 79 people were also injured when the militants stormed the campus in a 15-hour siege, apparently targeted at Christians and Islamic converts.

A curfew is in effect for two weeks for all of Garissa County as well as in three neighboring counties, said Police Inspector General Joseph Boinett.

The Somlai militant group al-Shabab, which claimed responsibility for the attack, has said that it is “at war” with Kenya for sending troops into Somalia in 2011 to pursue al-Shabab following a spate of kidnappings on Kenyan soil, according to the Daily Nation.

Kenyan officials have offered a $220,000 bounty for Mohammed Mohamud, who they believe planned the attack. On social media, the Interior ministry called him “the mastermind.”

Mohamud, who goes by other aliases, may have been assisted by two local youths who conducted surveillance on the university, Kenya’s The Star newspaper reported. He reportedly has claimed responsibility for a bus attack near Mandera late last year, which left dozens dead.

The Kenya police service also issued a wanted poster for six men it called “dangerous terrorists” who were suspected of being behind the attack.

A confidential report released by the government Friday described the six as “bloodthirsty, armed and dangerous,” the Daily Nation reported.
