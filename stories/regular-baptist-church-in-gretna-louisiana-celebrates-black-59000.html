Regular Baptist Church in Gretna, Louisiana Celebrates Black History Month with Weeklong Program

February 27, 2015 Black

Regular Baptist Church choir members are recognized during their 3rd Annual Black History program.

Regular Baptist Church in Gretna drew guests from both sides of the river recently in celebrating its third annual National African American History program, recognizing community leaders and legends.

History Ministry President Diane Coleman and Vice President Kenneth Coleman of Regular Baptist, led by the Rev. Toy A. Hodge, hosted the six-day event to commemorate National Black History Month.

The event began with a “Ministry Prayer Breakfast: A Tribute to Educators” on Jan. 15. Present and past educators including civil rights legends, administrators, teachers and school leaders were recognized for their service and dedication to the education of African American youth.

A “Youth and Young Adult Voter Registration Drive” and distribution of historical literature about notable African Americans were the focus of the Feb. 1 and and Feb. 15 events.

On Feb. 19. a “Forum and Panel Discussion: A Conversation on the Plight of Education in the 21st Century” was moderated by Brenda Brent Williams. Williams is interim president of the LA Civil Rights Museum Advisory Board and member of St. Joseph the Worker Catholic Church.

The program listed panelists who included the Rev. Samson “Skip” Alexander, a Civil Rights leader and frontline advocate for social change. He was present during the strike on Montgomery, Selma, and Washington D.C. and was working with AFL-CIO when Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. was assassinated.
