The Chen Story Has Made Headlines Everywhere Else in the World Except China

May 7, 2012 Uncategorized

The amazing story of blind Chinese activist Chen Guangcheng has made headlines around the world and attracted exhaustive coverage on news networks riveted by the international diplomatic standoff forced on China’s communist rulers by one man.

But, because of China’s media censors, few in China know what is happening.
Even Chen’s name — let alone his story — has been mostly kept out of domestic media. As in decades past, the mainland’s traditional and social media must avoid the sensitive topics laid down in regular lists by the ruling Communist Party.
Newspaper vendor Yan Jinzhang said you cannot trust what you read in the papers. “All the news in Chinese media is censored,” he said. “I don’t know what to think of this man Chen Guangcheng. There’s no detail about what he’s done.”
Chen, 40, a self-taught legal activist, was jailed for four years and, after his release, kept under house arrest in his Shandong province home for speaking out on behalf of thousands of women forced to have abortions and be sterilized as part of China’s family planning policy.
Last month, he sneaked past government-paid thugs and managed to make it 300 miles from his home to Beijing. There, he dodged Chinese security and took refuge at the U.S. Embassy.
The affair created a crisis for Secretary of State Hillary Rodham Clinton, who arrived days later for high-level talks.
On Monday, Chen, his wife and two kids remained at a Beijing hospital where he went after U.S. officials said they had struck a deal. Officials later negotiated to allow Chen to leave China to study abroad.
