Tom Brady, Patriots Win Over Peyton Manning and Broncos

October 8, 2012 Uncategorized

In the dozen times they had met before, Peyton Manning and Tom Brady had forged an epic rivalry, the greatest quarterbacks of their generation creating monuments to electric passing and scintillating competition.
Now in the twilight of their careers, they played again Sunday, a bit of gray sprinkled in their hair and questions studding their offenses. Had Manning, recovered from his neck injury, found a rhythm just one month into his first season with his new teammates in Denver? What of the transition the Patriots had seemed to be undergoing early in the season, away from Brady’s favorite target, receiver Wes Welker?
One team got an answer. The other did not have much of a chance. The Patriots, executing what felt like a breathless hurry-up offense much of the game, played keep-away with Manning, mixing short passes with slippery runs to slowly, steadily, inexorably march down the field, suffocate the Bronco defense and drain the clock. While the Patriots stayed on the field for drive after drive — they had 35 first downs — Manning stood on his sideline pacing, stretching, adjusting his socks and putting on and then removing his helmet over and over, as antsy as a child in detention.
An anticipated tit-for-tat passing duel between Brady and Manning never materialized, although Manning kept the Broncos on the cusp of a comeback until the end. But when the game was over, the Patriots had won, 31-21, and the doubts about their offense had been erased as they improved to 3-2.
“Two great, great players,” Coach Bill Belichick said. “There’s nobody I’d rather have than Tom Brady, but Manning is a great player.”
Along with Brady (23 of 31 for 223 yards and one touchdown), the Patriots’ offense is more balanced than it has been in the Belichick era, and for the first time since 1978 the Patriots have had consecutive 200-yard rushing games. And Welker, an afterthought in the early weeks of the season, caught 13 passes for 104 yards, seeming to prove that the Patriots — even bereft of their multidimensional injured tight end Aaron Hernandez — operate most efficiently when Welker is at the center of their offense
“It wasn’t a surprise,” Denver Coach John Fox said of the Patriots’ fast-paced offense. “It looked like it was. It was something we practice. It didn’t translate to the field tonight.”
