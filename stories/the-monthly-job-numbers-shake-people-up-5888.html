The Monthly Job Numbers Shake People Up

May 12, 2012 Uncategorized

I’ve never been a fan of numbers.
I struggled with Algebra II and geometry in ninth grade and, like a lot of 14-year-olds, asked, “Will I ever use this stuff?”
Today I am thankful for my fleeting but mostly enduring education of fractions, percentages and multiplication tables. And I thank Mr. Habaker — our teacher whose name I am not sure how to spell — who stuck with me during tutoring sessions.
I indeed have used that “stuff” in calculating tips, recipe ingredients and budgets and in other ways I can’t think of at this moment.
But these days, some numbers don’t help. They just are making job hunters nervous and distracted.
That would be the U.S. Labor Department figures the first Friday of every month that point out things like unemployment rate; how many are men, women or teens; the number of people out of work because they lost their last job as opposed to quitting; and how many folks are working part time.
It’s hard to know exactly what all the data really means — especially when these estimated numbers get revised, sometimes dramatically, from previous months. But it never fails that when these numbers come out, observers — some more informed than others — react.
They argue it’s good or bad and it means this or that. People tsk, and even when the news is good, when the report shows the job market getting better, job hunters get more down in the dumps.
Though it’s clear we’re still healing from a bad economic downturn and economists have been saying since early on that companies will need years to recover, this short-term snapshot riles job hunters who then declare with defiance: “It’s impossible to find a job.”
