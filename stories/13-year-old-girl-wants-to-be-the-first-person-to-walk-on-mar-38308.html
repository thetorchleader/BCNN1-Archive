13-Year-Old Girl Wants to be the First Person to Walk on Mars

October 9, 2014 Science

Stonehenge #spacecamp (PHOTO CREDIT: Alyssa Carson @NASABlueberry1)

A teenage girl has launched a bid to become the first person to land on Mars.

Alyssa Carson, 13, is looking to follow in the footsteps of legendary astronauts such as Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin and propel her way into the history books.

The youngster, from Baton Rouge, Louisiana, has spent the last nine years in training in the hope of being involved in a proposed mission to the Red Planet in 2033.

Alyssa is the first person to have attended all three of NASA’s world space camps, which put budding young astronauts to the test.

She regularly tweets pictures from the space training sessions.

The five day camps enable youngsters to ride space flight simulators, test their physical ability and experience zero gravity as they would in space,

Alyssa, who speaks a number of languages, said: “I want to go to Mars because it is a place nobody has been.

“It is really deserted right now so I want to take that first step.

“I think that I have a high chance of going to Mars basically because I’ve been training for nine years so far.

She added: “As I get older and continue to do more things the resume will just get longer and hopefully help me stand out and help me look unique compare to other people.
