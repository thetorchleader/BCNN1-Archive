Scientists Discover Supermassive Black Hole Over 2 Billion Light Years Away

February 26, 2015 Science

An artist’s conception of a black hole and the winds that surround it. (PHOTO CREDIT: NASA/JPL-Caltech)

Space lovers, we introduce you to galaxy PDS 456. It’s a quasar — a young, bright galaxy that’s powered by a supermassive black hole. According to NASA, it is more than 2 billion light-years away from Earth.

Just how bright is this quasar, in a galaxy far, far away? Its mass is 12 billion times that of the sun, and it sustains winds that emit more energy per second than a trillion suns.

Whoa.

NASA shared this discovery as part of its broader research on how black holes interact with their host galaxies. For the first time, NASA’s Nuclear Spectroscopic Telescope Array (NuSTAR) and European Space Agency’s XMM-Newton telescope have worked together to prove what astronomers had suspected: that the winds from a black hole blow outward in all directions, creating a spherical shape.
