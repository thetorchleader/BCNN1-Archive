Samsung Says Profits are Expected to Drop 60%

October 8, 2014 Technology

Samsung Galaxy Note 4 tablets are displayed at a launch event in Seoul on Sept. 24, 2014. (PHOTO CREDIT: Kim Dong-Hyun, AFP/Getty Images)

South Korean electronics giant Samsung’s smartphone dominance is taking a hit even before the full marketplace effect from Apple’s latest wave of iPhones kicks in.

Increased competition in the smartphone market is driving the company’s profit level to its lowest in more than three years. In a preview of its upcoming quarterly earnings announcement for the July-September period, Samsung said that its third-quarter operating profit is expected to be 4.1 trillion won ($3.8 billion), a 60% drop from a record-high 10.2 trillion won last year.

Its new Galaxy S5 smartphones aren’t dominating the market as much as expected, in part because competition from Chinese makers such as Huawei and others is undercutting Samsung. “A lot of Chinese brands have come out that are pushing devices that are really decent and of good quality, and they provide an alternative,” said Annette Zimmermann, research director, consumer markets and technology for Gartner.

SHIP MORE, SPEND MORE
Samsung’s market share has slipped slightly, falling to 29.5% in the fourth quarter of 2013 from 31.1% the same period in 2012, Gartner says. Apple share fell also to 17.8%, while Huawei’s has risen to nearly 6%.

Samsung actually shipped more smartphones during the third quarter, but profit declined because the company had to spend more on marketing to promote phones, even as the average price of phones fell. Any loss in market share results has repercussions for the chips and displays that Samsung makes for the smartphones.
