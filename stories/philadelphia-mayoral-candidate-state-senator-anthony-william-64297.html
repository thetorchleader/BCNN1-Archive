Philadelphia Mayoral Candidate State Senator Anthony Williams Receives Support from Local Clergy

March 29, 2015 Black

Mayoral candidate state Sen. Anthony Williams, at podium, receives support of faith-based leaders.— Photo by Abdul R. Sulayman/Chief Tribune Photographer.

After receiving the support of nearly two dozen faith leaders Friday afternoon, mayoral candidate state Sen. Anthony Williams vowed to bring government and spiritual leadership together to create a better Philadelphia.

The religious leaders gathered at the Hilton Hotel on City Line Avenue to endorse Williams. Williams will face opponents Lynne Abraham, Nelson Diaz, Doug Oliver, Jim Kenney and Milton Street in the May 19 Democratic mayoral primary.

The group of ministers included: Earnest Morris, Mt. Airy COGIC; Steven Avinger, Greater St. Matthews; Keith Reed-Sharon Baptist; James Hall, Triumph Baptist; Damon Jones, Bibleway Baptist, and William Hall, regional director, 7th Day Adventists.

And although the assembled leaders do not represent the Black Clergy of Philadelphia and Vicinity, Williams was happy to receive their support and vowed to work with them, should he be elected the city’s next mayor.

“Today is about connecting our places of faith,” Williams said while flanked by the spiritual leaders. “Whether you worship on a Saturday or Sunday, Monday comes, and and Monday comes with problems. I, along with all of us, recognize the separation between communities and families and the decline in their places of worship.

“It is my intent and my design to have the faith-based community organize — not symbolically and not just somebody who has a job in my administration — but someone and some organization to connect places of faith with communities and their government.”

In the brief gathering, Williams also expanded on his efforts to fund school and foster school choice, and and spoke of the seemingly dangerous relationship between someone communities and the police department.

“Yes, I will step body into this conversation about good schools in Philadelphia; I don’t apologize for what I do in that space,” Williams said. “Since 2010, $250 million has come to Philadelphia public schools because of Tony Williams.
