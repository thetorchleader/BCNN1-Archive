How to Protect Your Home and Family from Disaster

October 14, 2014 Finances

Peter LeWando puts plywood sheets over the windows of his apartment in Avon, N.C., on July 3, 2014, as Hurricane Arthur approached the region.
(Photo: Steve Earley, AP)

When a natural disaster hits — from a hurricane to a blizzard — there are ways to prepare to keep your family and home safe.

“We get more high-impact weather than any other country on the planet,” said Sean Potter, a meteorologist and weather historian in New York City.

The USA gets pummeled with tornadoes, droughts, floods, wildfires, heat waves and cold snaps. On Monday, for example, there were tornado warnings in the South, a hurricane barreling toward Puerto Rico and high winds sweeping the West.

And that list doesn’t include geological hazards like earthquakes, volcanoes and tsunamis.

Though disasters can’t be prevented, home and family can be protected beforehand. First, have an emergency plan so all family members know where to go and what to do in case of a disaster, whether a fire or a storm.

Also know how to turn off the gas, water and electricity at home, in case that’s needed. If the power goes out, whether from a blizzard or severe storm, consider getting a generator, but use it safely.

Remember that basic services — such as electricity, water, phones — could be cut off for days, a week or even longer. So families should have enough food, water and other emergency supplies to last for at least 72 hours.

Planning ahead and preparing for the worst-case scenario is the best way to stay safe before a disaster hits.
