WATCH: Second Baptist Church in Doylestown, PA, Continues to Grow in Numbers

April 4, 2014 Uncategorized

Vice chairman of the building committee Jimmy Jones walks to where the The Second Baptist Church of Doylestown’s new fellowship hall will be. The church will break ground Sunday on a new fellowship hall and the ceremony marks the completion of a two-phase building plan for the 107-year-old church, a predominantly African-American congregation. Rick Kintzel/Staff Photographer

Second Baptist Church of Doylestown will begin taking steps this weekend to complete a building project that began almost a decade ago.

Church members will break ground Sunday on a new Fellowship Hall, an addition to the red brick church that sits atop the hill at Route 313 and Stump Road in Plumstead. The ceremony will mark another milestone for the predominantly African-American church, which has doubled in membership over the past 10 years.

“The Lord has blessed me with a vision for the church,” the Rev. Robert E. Hamlin Sr. said. “The Lord gave me a vision that the church would be a church where everyone is welcome, and people would be able to come together as an example as to what can happen when people of all different backgrounds come together to worship.”

The 6,000-square-foot hall will include a greeting area, bathrooms, a full-service commercial kitchen, additional classrooms, a seminar space and a banquet space that can fit 250 people.

Second Baptist traces its roots back more than 100 years to St. Mark C.M.E Church. The congregation, also known as the People’s Colored Church of Doylestown, was chartered on April 29, 1874. Members worshipped in a building on Ashland Street by the railroad tracks.

In 1907, the church was incorporated as the Second Baptist Church of Doylestown. In 1973, the church purchased a building on Route 611 in Plumstead, which served as its home until 2003.

A growing congregation, as well as repeated damage from floods, prompted Second Baptist to purchase 7.5 acres on Route 313 and Stump Road for a new house of worship. The $1.5 million church opened to members in 2007.
