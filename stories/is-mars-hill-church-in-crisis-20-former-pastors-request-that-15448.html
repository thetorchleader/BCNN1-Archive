Is Mars Hill Church in Crisis? 20 Former Pastors Apologize for Becoming ‘Enablers and Enforcers’ and Request that Mark Driscoll Enter Into Mediation

March 26, 2014 Christian

The congregation gathers at the Mars Hill Church downtown campus on Christmas Eve.

A former Mars Hill Church worship leader and musician is urging congregation members to deliver a message to Pastor Mark Driscoll and church leaders by cutting back donations, reducing volunteer work and leaving chairs empty at worship services.

“I personally believe Mark and his executive elders will only respond and repent when their scorecard turns red,” Luke Abrams, who says he gave 20 hours of work per week to the mega-church, wrote at the end of a lengthy mea culpa. It was published on the Patheos website.

“So, if you’re on the fence, think about your noses and nickels and all the things you do that the leaders interpret as support,” Abrams added.  “You can’t get a face to face with Mark but you can talk to him loudly through how you participate.”

The unrest at Mars Hill, which claims 13,000 members, is the biggest Lenten turmoil to strike a major Seattle congregation since 2007-08, when firings and leadership style led to the resignation of St. Mark’s Cathedral Dean Robert Taylor (with a $313,000 severance package).

Driscoll has already done a mea culpa of sorts. The pastor-founder sent out a letter, earlier this month, promising to “reset my life,” and adding:  “I don’t see how I can be both a celebrity and a pastor, and so I am happy to give up the former so I can focus on the latter.”

Driscoll has had to confront allegations of plagiarism over his book “Real Marriage.”  He admitted and apologized for hiring a promotional firm, ResultSource Inc., to manipulate sales of the book — including bulk purchase by Mars Hill — to get it on The New York Times bestseller list.

Driscoll promised to travel less, and reflected on recent high turnover of key staff, writing:  “I am deeply grieved and even depressed by the pain we have caused.”

But 20 former pastors at the church have requested that Driscoll enter into mediation aimed at repentance and reconciliation.

In public statements, they have apologized for becoming enablers and enforcers.
