The Marriage Issue is on the Ballot in Four States this Election Day: Maine, Maryland, Minnesota, Washington

November 6, 2012 Uncategorized
Marriage, a perennial issue on statewide ballots over the past decade, is back before voters again in 2012. It is on the ballot in four states in November 2012. Marriage appeared on the primary ballot in North Carolina, where voters approved a constitutional definition of marriage as between one man and one woman in May 2012. The states voting on marriage in 2012 are:
  • Maine: An initiative on the ballot seeks to legalize same-sex marriage. This is the first time a state’s voters have been directly asked to legalize same-sex marriage, rather than prohibit it.
      
  • Maryland: Voters will consider a popular referendum seeking to overturn a new law legalizing same-sex marriage.
      
  • Minnesota: The state legislature referred a same-sex marriage ban to the ballot. This question is similar to those that have appeared on the ballot in 30 other states since 1998.
      
  • Washington: Like Maryland, Washington has a popular referendum on the ballot that seeks to overturn a new law legalizing same-sex marriage.
      
  • North Carolina: Voters approved a same-sex marriage ban in May 2012. The “yes” vote was 61.1%, the lowest affirmative vote ever received by a same-sex marriage ban in a southern state.
      
Petitions dealing with marriage were circulated in at least five other states–California, Colorado, Florida, Nebraska, and Ohio–but failed to gather sufficient signatures to reach the November 2012 ballot. In a break from the established historical pattern on this issue, all of these petitions sought to either legalize same-sex marriage or repeal an existing ban on it.
SOURCE: National Conference of State Legislatures
