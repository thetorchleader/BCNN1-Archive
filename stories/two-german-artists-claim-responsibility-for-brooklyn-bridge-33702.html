Two German Artists Claim Responsibility for Brooklyn Bridge White-flag Stunt; ‘Not an Anti-American Statement’

August 14, 2014 National

The Berlin-based art duo of Matthias Wermke (left) and Mischa Leinkauf. (DW.DE VIA YOUTUBE)

A pair of German artists have come forward claiming they’re the culprits behind the brazen Brooklyn Bridge flag swap.

Mischa Leinkauf and Matthias Wermke say the stunt was neither a political statement nor an attempt to shame the NYPD.

In an interview with The New York Times, the duo insisted they simply wanted to celebrate “the beauty of public space” and pay homage to the bridge’s German-born engineer.

“This was not an anti-American statement,” Wermke, 35, said.

To bolster their credibility, the artists released a short video clip showing a white flag flapping in the wind, the Verizon building visible in the distance.

NYPD spokesman Stephen Davis released a terse statement.

“The NYPD is aware of the public statements made by the German Artists, Mishcha Leinkauf and Matthias Wermke in regard to the Brooklyn Bridge flag case,” it said. “The investigation is continuing.” The July 22 caper raised concerns over the security of one of the nation’s most iconic spans.

Many New Yorkers wondered how anyone could scale the famed bridge undetected and hoist two bleached-white American flags in place of the normal red-white-and-blue banners.

The Germans say they were taken aback by the reaction.
