Detroit Tigers Honor Lou Whitaker with African American Legacy Award

April 27, 2015 Black, Sports

These days, Lou Whitaker is a golfer.

He lives the retired life in Greensboro, N.C., and the former Detroit Tigers second baseman watches some baseball, works with some baseball players, but mainly, he plays golf.

On Friday, he was in Detroit for the Tigers’ second annual Negro Leagues Weekend Legacy Luncheon at Comerica Park.

Whitaker, 57, was honored as the recipient of the 2015 Tigers African American Legacy Award.

“I’m honored to be here,” Whitaker said. “All those before me, they had an impact to a person like myself and our future, so I can only thank them, all of them.”

Whitaker, who also will be honored before Sunday’s game, spoke in front of past and present Tigers, including Willie Horton and Jake Wood and starting pitcher David Price, and spoke afterward about his career in Detroit.

“My first experience here is we love people who go to work,” he said. “That’s what I did, came to work. Not talk about it. Work. I never thought about going anywhere else because once I put on that uniform, that’s who I played for.”

Whitaker played 19 seasons as a second baseman with the Tigers. He won the 1978 American League Rookie of the Year Award and was a member of the 1984 World Series championship team.
