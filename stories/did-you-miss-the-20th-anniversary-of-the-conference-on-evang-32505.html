Did You Miss the 20th Anniversary of the Conference on Evangelizing Black America (COEBA)? Listen to the Messages Delivered at the Conference Here

August 4, 2014 Christian

COEBA (The Conference On Evangelizing Black America) is an effort to plant New Testament churches to effectively minister in black communities. The modern foreign missionary era has proven that the most effective way to reach a people group is with a national pastor. Similarly, the condition in our country is such that Black America can only be evangelized en masse as God-called black men are burdened, trained and commissioned to plant churches. Though these churches may be predominantly black, they must always champion their God-given mandate to reach all people.

Spreading the gospel in Black America is the responsibility of every New Testament church. This is why COEBA is not a “black” movement, but a New Testament movement. Churches and Christians have joined hands and hearts in one of the most concerted, specific missions endeavors in history.

Lou Baldwin

Great things have happened since COEBA began in 1994. By the grace of God, this effort led by Dr. Lou Baldwin and Crossroads Baptist Church has helped to produce more than twenty-eight New Testament Baptist churches. Millions of dollars have been given for missions through these ministries. Hundreds of men and boys have surrendered to preach. Furthermore, countless racial barriers have been broken as Christians of all races begin to understand that New Testament Christianity is not defined by color but by Christ.

History of the COEBA National Gathering

It was 1978, on the way home from a meeting when God burdened the heart of a young, black businessman. Shortly thereafter, Lou Baldwin would surrender to the call of God, attend Bible college and commit his life to reaching his people.

In 1981, he and his family were commissioned to plant an independent, fundamental Baptist church in the Washington, DC area. The church grew as God would have it, and many men were called to the gospel ministry. It became increasingly clear that God had special plans for Crossroads Baptist Church.

Pastor Baldwin continued to share his burden for his “kinsman according to the flesh” in churches, fellowships, conferences and Bible colleges around the country. His belief that Black America was a figurative “Samaria” in the Great Commission often alienated him from his Christian brethren–black and white. Yet he fervently advanced a deeply-held notion that Black America, though religious, was ripe and ready for spiritual awakening.

In the summer of 1994, several dozen people from Crossroads and other churches gathered in Chester, Virginia for the first “Conference on Evangelizing Black America.” Realizing the great need they committed to building a cross-cultural, independent Baptist effort to plant churches among one of the world’s most neglected people groups. Thus, COEBA was born.

The number in attendance at the National Gathering has increased from a couple of hundred to as many as a couple of thousand. However, the vision that God gave Dr. Lou Baldwin remains unchanged. Pastors and churches gather each summer to show their support and learn what more can be done. Bible colleges and Christian ministries make their resources available. A great choir sings, powerful sermons are delivered and decisions are made at a tear-stained altar.

We hope you’ll plan to join us for the next COEBA National Gathering. Come and witness what God is doing in places like Houston and Birmingham, Memphis and Oakland. Once more, come and be a part of the work that God is accomplishing in the hearts of His people–bringing us together, breaking down barriers, so that together we can spread the gospel in Black America and around the world.
