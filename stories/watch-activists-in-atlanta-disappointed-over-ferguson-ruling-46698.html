WATCH: Activists in Atlanta Disappointed Over Ferguson Ruling; Bernice King says ‘Culture of Death Must Stop’

November 25, 2014 Black

http://c.brightcove.com/services/viewer/federated_f9?isSlim=1

A small group of civil rights leaders gathered outside of the Russell Federal Building in downtown Atlanta on Monday to listen to the live stream of the Ferguson grand jury decision announcement.

A St. Louis County grand jury declined to indict officer Darren Wilson, 28, for firing six shots in an August confrontation that killed 18-year-old Michael Brown, St. Louis County prosecutor Robert McCulloch said Monday night.

People who disagreed with the decision said they were stunned.

The Rev. Markel Hutchins and others called for peaceful protests.

Before the announcement, organizers said they were planning a demonstration on Tuesday at 5 p.m. outside the Five Points MARTA station.

Those organizers said they believe that Brown’s death is a terrible injustice, and they were hoping that the officer involved in the shooting, Darren Wilson, would be indicted.

“Michael Brown deserves better than looting,” said Rev. Markel Hutchins, a civil rights activist. “Michael Brown deserves better than violence. It was violence that killed him. Not only does Michael Brown deserve better, but we as a community deserve better.”

Bernice King, the daughter of Martin Luther King Jr., told 11Alive’s Kevin Rowson at the King Center that it was a somber moment in time.

“We got to find to a way to create a better relationship between our law enforcement officials and the community,” King said. “But more importantly, we’ve got to stop this death culture where so many young people are losing lives.”
