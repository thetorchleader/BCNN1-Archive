California Woman Finds Her Ministry After Surviving Breast Cancer

September 26, 2011 Uncategorized

Tammie Denyse heard the call to serve the church but didn’t know how to answer. Then she was diagnosed with Stage 2 breast cancer and found her ministry.
The Rev. Tammie Denyse, holding a calendar featuring African American women breast cancer survivors, beat the disease herself. The calendar will be unveiled Saturday, as her Carrie’s Touch ministry holds a fundraiser called Unmasking the Big C. 
At St. Andrews African Methodist Episcopal Church on Sunday morning, Denyse sat near the altar with the clergy. She watched ushers wearing black and white greet worshippers at the door, and listened as the pastor delivered a stirring message to the Sacramento congregation.
“Be good, do good,” said the Rev. Tyrone Hicks of the Sacramento church. “If you see someone fall … then pick that person up.”
That is the purpose of Carrie’s Touch, the ministry that Rev. Denyse founded to increase breast cancer awareness in the African American community.
At the Hyatt Regency on Saturday, Carrie’s Touch will celebrate five years of service with a fundraiser called Unmasking the Big C.
A calendar featuring area African American women breast cancer survivors – from their 20s to age 80 and called “Breast Cancer Exposed” – will be unveiled at the event.
“I think people will be surprised, because what an impact this disease has had,” said Denyse, 46. “And seeing the survivors will give them hope.”
African American women are more likely to die from breast cancer than white women, according to the American Cancer Society.
Denyse says fear and a lack of education are two reasons. Also, she said, African American women are less likely to get early detection or know their family history.
