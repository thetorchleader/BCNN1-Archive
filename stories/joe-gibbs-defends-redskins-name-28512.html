Joe Gibbs Defends Redskins’ Name

Date not recorded in the original archive Sports

Joe Gibbs (Peter Casey/USA TODAY Sports)

Joe Gibbs is just fine with the Washington Redskins’ nickname.

The Redskins name has increasingly been viewed as racist over the last year, and the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office canceled six trademark registrations owned by the team last week. But Gibbs, who coached the Redskins to three Super Bowl titles, said the team’s name has never been anything but positive throughout his life and career.

“I grew up in North Carolina and the only games we could get on TV in the late ’40s was the Redskins,” he said Saturday at Kentucky Speedway, where he was preparing to watch his Joe Gibbs Racing team compete. “I pulled for them my whole life. The whole time I was there — 15 years — never once did I hear anybody say anything negative about the name Redskins.”

Gibbs said the name was “always prideful” and evoked “courage.” He emphatically said the name was a good thing.

“We have a song: ‘Hail to the Redskins,’” he said. “So everything — everything​ — about that name has been positive for me and my past.”
