Man Intentionally Ends Nearly 500-Person Pay-it-Forward Chain at Starbucks

August 23, 2014 What is this World Coming To?

Peter Schorsch, customer No. 458, said he broke the pay-it-forward chain on purpose. (Facebook)

The coffee isn’t the only thing that’s bitter.

Two days of pay-it-forward streaks at a St. Petersburg Starbucks ended Thursday when a grouchy customer refused to pay for the next person’s coffee.

And the curmudgeon claimed he did it on purpose.

“I had to put an end to it,” Peter Schorsch wrote on his blog. He claimed customers were socially pressured into the good deeds.

The two days of kindness started on Wednesday, when 378 people paid for the person behind them. That day’s pay-it-forward streak ended with the 379th customer, a woman who didn’t quite understand the concept.

On Thursday, the chain started up again.

But all hopes of the marathon good deed reaching into a third day were crushed when Schorsch, a blogger and political consultant, entered the drive-thru around 2 p.m.
