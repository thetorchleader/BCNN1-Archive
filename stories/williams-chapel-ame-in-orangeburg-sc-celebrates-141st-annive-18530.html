Williams Chapel AME in Orangeburg, SC Celebrates 141st Anniversary

April 18, 2014 Uncategorized

Dr. Caesar Roland Richburg

Williams Chapel African Methodist Episcopal Church celebrated its 141st anniversary on Sunday, March 30. The occasion was marked by a proclamation issued by Orangeburg Mayor Michael Butler naming March 30 as Williams Chapel AME Day.

Williams Chapel African Methodist Episcopal (AME) Church was founded in 1873 with the present building located on Glover Street being erected in 1927.

Following the lighting of the candles, the Praise Team encouraged worshippers to enter into the gates of celebration through their rousing rendition of “Clap Our Hands and Give Him Praise.”

Guest speaker for the service was the Rev. Sterling Dowling, an Orangeburg native who grew up in the Williams Chapel family. Currently, Dowling is the pastor of Fairview AME Church in the Denver community of Anderson. He is also the author of the “Full Fountain” spiritual blog (www.theFUllFountain.blogspot.com).

Dowling’s sermon centered on the idea that everyone is considered royalty in God’s kingdom and, as such, are entitled to sit at the table with the King. He referenced II Samuel 9 and told the story of Saul’s house and the son of Jonathan called Mephibosheth. He was the son of royalty who had been crippled at birth because his nurse had dropped him. Because he was of royal birth, Mephibosheth was cared for his entire life. Had he been a “commoner,” he might not have lived long enough to reach adulthood. Despite his disability, Mephibosheth was always seated at the royal table of David, who had restored his lands and title. Dowling’s point was that every child of God is a member of the royal family and should think of themselves as such.

Also during the service, Michael McKinney, owner of Mello Vision Productions, presented a documentary about the church, its heritage and its congregation. During the presentation, the documentary explained that the reason the AME churches have a blacksmith’s anvil under its cross is because when the church was first founded in 1787, Richard Allen purchased an old blacksmith’s building and held the services in it. The complete documentary can be viewed at http://www.WilliamsChapelAME Church.org, and additional information can be found at the African Methodist Episcopal website.
