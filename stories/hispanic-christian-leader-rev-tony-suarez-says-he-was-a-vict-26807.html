Hispanic Christian Leader, Rev. Tony Suarez, Says He Was a Victim of Racial Profiling

June 16, 2014 Christian

Rev. Tony Suarez

A Virginia pastor of Colombian descent claims he was racially profiled by Des Moines police late Saturday after preaching to a small church that invited him to the city.

The Rev. Tony Suarez, vice president of the National Hispanic Christian Leaders Conference, said he was stopped by two officers in the parking lot of the Quality Inn & Suites on Fleur Drive near the airport.

Suarez said the officers told him “he fit a profile of a drug dealer” and asked to search his car.

“I asked them what I had done wrong,” Suarez told The Des Moines Register on Sunday. “They said they were looking for someone who looked me because I really look like a drug dealer. Being of Colombian descent, that really hurt me.”

Suarez said he left messages about the incident with Des Moines Police Chief Judy Bradshaw, Des Moines Mayor Frank Cownie and U.S. Congressman Tom Latham.

The messages will be forwarded to the police department’s Office of Professional Standards on this morning, said Sgt. Steve Woody, a Des Moines police spokesman who works in the internal investigations office.

“We won’t know if he filed a complaint until (Monday) morning when we open up at about 7:45 a.m.,” Woody said.

The first step in any investigation is to interview the complainant in person or over the phone. Then detectives would pull the video and audio from the stop to review it against what the complainant said. The officers involved in the incident would then be interviewed “if it gets to that point,” Woody said.

“Obviously, this is not the kind of complaint you want to hear and we’ll definitely look into it,” Woody said.

The interaction between Suarez and police took place at about 11:30 p.m. Saturday and lasted for about 45 minutes to an hour.

Suarez said he preached at Inglesia Pentecostal Unida International, a small church of about 140 members at 3504 S.W. Ninth. A member of the church loaned him a car — a white BMW — to get around the city during his visit, Suarez said.

On his way back to hotel, Suarez stopped at a drug store to buy some toiletries, he said. Not long after he left the drug store, he noticed a police vehicle following him. He said the police followed him for about three miles.

Suarez said police had already begun questioning him and asking for ID, and after he realized he was at the wrong hotel, he left to go to the Hampton Inn, where the questioning continued.

“I was outside listening and watching when (the police) actually talked to the guy,” Crawford said. “The cops said they were looking for a car involved in a hit-and-run. They asked to search his car and he said, ‘No, he knows his rights.’ They kind of started harassing him, but I don’t think they were racially profiling him. The guy seemed to be making more problems than not.”
