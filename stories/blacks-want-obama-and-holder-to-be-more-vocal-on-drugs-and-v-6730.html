Blacks Want Obama and Holder to Be More Vocal on Drugs and Violence

June 23, 2012 Uncategorized

TANYA NOLAN: When Barack Obama was elected as the first African-American president nearly four years ago it was hailed as a momentous development for black Americans. 

But now as he campaigns for a second term, there’s a vigorous debate about whether he’s done enough for his own race.
Many African-Americans see him as an inspiration but some argue that he should be speaking out on problems such as the prevalence of guns and drugs in poorer black communities.
Brendan Trembath reports from Washington.
(Music)
BRENDAN TREMBATH: In a hair salon in Charlotte, North Carolina the hairdressers take a break between customers.
(Music)
Angela Rutledge who owns the salon says she’ll be voting for Barack Obama in November.
ANGELA RUTLEDGE: Well, I think he’s doing a great job, you know, getting things done. 
BRENDAN TREMBATH: J’mes Dixon supports Barack Obama too. 
J’MES DIXON: I love that he stands for equality and unity and also he is about giving to all people, not just the one type of person. 
BRENDAN TREMBATH: David McInnis Junior helps out in the salon and also works as a cook. He’s sure of his vote.
DAVID MCINNIS: I’m gonna vote for Obama again. I think he is doing great. I think he is doing a good job. 
BRENDAN TREMBATH: David McInnis Junior says Barack Obama’s election in 2008 was a great moment.
DAVID MCINNIS: I’m 52 years old and I thought that I’d never live to see a black president. Martin Luther King’s dream stands. 
MARTIN LUTHER KING (1963): I have a dream that my four little children will one day live in a nation where they will not be judged by the colour of the skin but by the content of their character. I have a dream today. 
BRENDAN TREMBATH: Martin Luther King in Washington in 1963. Today just over half the city’s population is African American.
Ronald Hanna has an office job and has written more than 11 books but he is disappointed that so many in his area are out of work and on drugs.
RONALD HANNA: You can’t tell a kid to go to McDonalds and flip burgers for like $7 an hour if they can go ahead and sell crack cocaine and make $7,000 a night. The people who process and put the drugs in our community know that. 
BRENDAN TREMBATH: He says the president and the attorney-general as African-Americans should be more vocal on the prevalence of drugs and guns in black communities.
RONALD HANNA: It’s a fact that everybody has a gun now. 
BRENDAN TREMBATH: What should the president do about it then?
RONALD HANNA: We should speak about it. I think that basically if he said that it is a major problem then Eric Holder and law enforcement officials will find it necessary to step up their game. 
BRENDAN TREMBATH: He does plan to vote for Barack Obama but reluctantly.
