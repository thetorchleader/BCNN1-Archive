Robert Griffin III of Baylor May Shock the World and Win the Heisman Trophy

December 10, 2011 Uncategorized

The program isn’t pedigreed. The record isn’t perfect.

Voters for college football’s Heisman Trophy appear to have gotten past that, seizing on Robert Griffin III‘s game-changing capabilities, extraordinary numbers – and perhaps high character in these troubled, lurid-headlined times.

Baylor’s
junior quarterback is expected to be named Saturday night as the 77th
Heisman winner, projected in nearly every straw poll to finish
comfortably ahead of Stanford quarterback Andrew Luck in the balloting. They’re among five finalists invited to ceremonies in New York City.

  • BLOG: Griffin tops USA TODAY’s Heisman Watch

  • PHOTOS: Images of the Heisman finalists

The other three in order of projected finish: Alabama running back Trent Richardson, Wisconsin running back Montee Ball and LSU cornerback Tyrann Mathieu.

“It’s
big for our program,” Griffin said of his place among the top five,
“and shows the direction that we’re going in – that we can get a guy
into the finals for the Heisman Trophy, one of the most prestigious
awards out there. It’s huge. It’ll show recruits that you can come to
Baylor and make a difference.”

He has done just that, in concert with Bears coach Art Briles, elevating a perennial loser into formidable, 9-3 team that beat Oklahoma, overran Texas and finished 12th in the Bowl Championship Series‘ final composite rankings. The nine-win season is Baylor’s first since 1986.

The program’s highest previous Heisman finisher was quarterback Don Trull, who was a distant fourth in the voting when Roger Staubach won in 1963.
