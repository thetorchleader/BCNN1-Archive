James Brown’s Home Church, Trinity CME Church in Augusta, Georgia, Set for Demolition

February 17, 2015 Black

The days are numbered for a church with connections to slavery and even the Godfather of Soul. The original Trinity CME church on Taylor and 8th Streets will be torn down.

Atlanta Gas Light wants to clean up contamination left behind by the gas plant that used to be there, but says they can’t do it without demolishing the church.

Eddie Furse is happy to call Trinity CME church his home away from home. “We love it here, our children are growing up here,” Furse said.

He knows the new Trinity Church, but reminders of where it all started from are around every corner.

It’s history spans one hundred and seventy-five years. “When people were enslaved and they couldn’t pray and worship like they wanted to, they went into that little building and brought about the spirit of God,” Furse said.

But this land wasn’t only home to a church. Atlanta Gas Light operated a gas plant here since the 1850’s and has owned the land for almost twenty years. The company has been cleaning up coal tar in the groundwater, and say the only option is to tear down the church. “A lot of history there, I mean James Brown was a member there, there’s just a lot of history that’s going to be pretty much lost,” he said.
