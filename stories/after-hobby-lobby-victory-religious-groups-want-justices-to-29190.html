After Hobby Lobby Victory, Religious Groups Want Justices to Strike Contraceptive Requirement for Them Too

Date not recorded in the original archive National

The Supreme Court’s Hobby Lobby decision is just days old, but a group of religious schools and institutions is preparing for Part Two.

By fall, the court is expected to have several requests from Catholic schools and other nonprofits that want the justices to strike the contraceptive coverage requirement in Obamacare for them, too.

Monday’s ruling applied only to closely held companies in which the owners have religious objections to providing birth control. The Obama administration has promised to restore coverage, perhaps through new regulations, but hasn’t detailed how it would do that.

The religious groups have to make a different argument because, thanks to an accommodation that the administration devised to try to avert a political backlash, they don’t have to provide contraception directly. So far, they’ve been unsuccessful in lower courts in challenging that special provision.

The Catholic Diocese of Nashville and the Michigan Catholic Conference lost their cases in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 6th Circuit last month and could soon appeal to the Supreme Court. The University of Notre Dame’s petition was rejected by the 7th Circuit, and it is now providing contraceptive coverage, although it says it’s under duress. The school has asked the justices if it can have until October to appeal.

Elsewhere, the 3rd, 5th and 10th Circuit courts are facing similar cases, while the D.C. Court of Appeals is expected to rule soon on a suit brought by the Catholic Church in Washington. The plaintiffs all say their religious beliefs would be violated if they were forced to use the government’s accommodation. They want no role in providing anyone access to contraception, no matter how tenuous the link.

If any of the appellate courts rule against the government, it would create conflicting opinions and give the Supreme Court another reason to look at the issue.
