Springfield College Starts Scholarship Named After African American Pioneer In the Field of Physical Education

August 29, 2014 Education

Springfield College

The first African-American who graduated from Springfield College with a degree in physical education and went on to become a pioneer in his field is the model for a new scholarship.

Springfield College and the Springfield school system have introduced a the four-year, full-tuition scholarship to be awarded to a 2015 city graduate of color who is planning a career in public school teaching.

The scholarship, valued at $132,000, was named for African-American educator William H. J. Beckett who graduated from the college in 1906 and went on to become a pioneer in the field of physical education in St. Louis, Mo.

Springfield College President Mary-Beth Cooper said the scholarship is a continuation of the colleges’ strong relationship with the Springfield public schools and Superintendent Daniel Warwick.

“This new scholarship is an example of our collective work in advancing opportunities for access to education,” Cooper said. “Our intent is that the future recipients of this award will graduate from our institution and return to the Springfield public schools as a teacher to enhance education for Springfield youth.”

In Beckett’s 1954 obituary, published in the former St. Louis Argus, the headline read: “City mourns passing of great athletic leader ‘Pops” Becket: Was idol of thousands of young athletes.”

A June 22 story that appeared in the former Springfield Union when World War I veteran Beckett returned to the college for his 40th reunion, noted that he originally planned to become a social worker until “the late Dr.James H. McCurdy recognized in him a student well qualified for physical education.”

“Beckett became the first African-American to graduate with such a degree,” the account said.
