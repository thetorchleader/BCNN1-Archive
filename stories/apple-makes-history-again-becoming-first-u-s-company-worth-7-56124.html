Apple Makes History Again Becoming First U.S. Company Worth $700B

February 10, 2015 Technology

Getty

Apple (AAPL) – one of the stocks analysts are calling a screaming buy – notched another all-time stock high and closed trading as the first $700 billion U.S. company.

Shares of the gadget maker Tuesday closed up $2.29, or 1.9%, to $122.02 – putting a punctuation on what’s been a breathtaking 60% rise over the past year. Giant moves by a giant company – make earth-shattering valuations. At $122.02 a share, the company is worth $710.7 billion. That means Apple is worth nearly twice the second most valuable company, oil giant Exxon Mobil, at $385.4 billion.

Here’s the math. By closing above $120.18 a share, Apple is the first company in U.S. history to close with a value of $700 billion or more, according to share data from S&P Capital IQ and historical data from S&P Dow Jones Indices. That’s based on the company’s 5.82 billion shares outstanding. Microsoft was the first company to close with a $600 billion valuation in 2000. Apple made a run at $700 billion before, but didn’t close there.

It’s ironic that Apple is named after a fruit associated with the discovery of gravity, since the stock seems to defy the force itself. The stock first broke the $600 billion market value barrier on Aug. 17, 2012, according to data from S&P Capital IQ. That was just 170 calendar days after the company broke the $500 billion barrier on Feb. 29, 2012. And it broke the $500 billion barrier just 35 days after closing over $400 billion.
