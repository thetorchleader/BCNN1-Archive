Florida Pastor John Guns’ Plans for “Operation Save Our Sons Week” Has Support of Jacksonville Mayor Alvin Brown, Officials

May 14, 2014 African-American, Christian

Pastor John Guns (at podium) talks about Operation Save Our Sons Week, an event that aims to start initiatives to help curb black-on-black crime, at a news conference Tuesday St. Paul Missionary Baptist Church.
Will.Dickey@jacksonville.com

Jacksonville Mayor Alvin Brown’s office, prosecutors and school and nonprofit officials threw support Monday behind a pastor’s plans for a week of events in June focused on preventing black youth crime.

“If we don’t fight for them, they will be fighting against us,” the Rev. John Guns said as he outlined plans for Operation Save Our Sons Week, a series of events meant to spark discussion of problems facing young black males.

Save Our Sons is a group founded by Guns, pastor of St. Paul Missionary Baptist Church, to help youths between ages 10 and 18 make better decisions about their lives.

The group held a one-day gathering last year, and Guns said the extended format for the week ending on Father’s Day, June 15, is designed to involve more people.

A surge in gun violence this spring, some involving young people, helped focus public attention on the issue.

In April, 35 people were wounded and seven killed in 28 shooting incidents, Times-Union records reflect. During the first two weeks of May, at least 10 were wounded by shootings or stabbings and two were killed, in addition to a man police shot after he stabbed an officer.

But Guns said he didn’t think police, educators, ministers or any other group held all-purpose answers to young people’s issues.
