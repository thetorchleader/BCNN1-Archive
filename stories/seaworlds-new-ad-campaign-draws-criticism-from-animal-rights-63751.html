SeaWorld’s New Ad Campaign Draws Criticism from Animal Rights Activists

March 26, 2015 Science

SeaWorld’s new ad campaign comes after its negative portrayal in the documentary “Blackfish” and allegations levied by animal rights groups. (PHOTO CREDIT: Getty Images / Jerod Harris)

SeaWorld launched an ad campaign this week in defense of its killer whale program called Ask SeaWorld, drawing ire from animal rights activists.

The campaign, which started March 23, includes TV and print ads. It invites the public to submit questions via Twitter about the company’s treatment of animals. It also features a website with videotaped interviews with veterinarians and trainers.

SeaWorld has been under fire from animal rights groups and the public since the popular 2013 documentary Blackfish explored the impact of the company’s captivity of orcas. This new campaign comes the same time as former Seaworld trainer John Hargrove’s book launch. The book condemns the company’s treatment of killer whales.

The timing of the ad campaign and Hargrove’s book was coincidental, SeaWorld spokesman Fred Jacobs told USA TODAY Network in an e-mail.

Lifespan of whales in SeaWorld parks
The first video ad released features SeaWorld veterinarian Chris Dold disputing a claim that killer whales live shorter lives in captivity than in the wild.

Dold cites a federal report that concluded animals born at SeaWorld have a life expectancy comparable to what is found in the wild, which is about 30 to 50 years old.

Fact-checking website Politifact.com reviewed the ad and concluded that SeaWorld’s version is partially true. Orca lifespans are “quite close to what you might see in the wild,” Douglas DeMaster, science director of the Alaska Fisheries Science Center, told Politifact.com.
