Kirk Franklin Goes on Fearless Tour, Promotes ‘Power to End Stroke’

October 30, 2011 Uncategorized

Kirk Franklin wants to stir souls with his Grammy-winning gospel music. But it’s your life he wants to improve – and lengthen – as he weaves together music and heart health information in his current, 19-city Fearless tour which comes to Houston’s Arena Theater on Nov. 4.

The Dallas-area performer has teamed up with the American Heart Association to use his music and the audience it draws to reach out to a community particularly hard-hit by stroke and heart disease: African Americans.
The AHA notes that blacks have twice the risk for first-time strokes and a higher death rate from strokes, when compared to whites. Overall, the risk of strokes for blacks is four times higher than that for whites.
Franklin took time recently to talk about his music, his health regimen and how he lives his faith.
Q: How did you get involved in the AHA program?
A: I really try to be conscious of my own health and want to help others. Some people have health problems that are genetic, but others can do things like exercise and cook differently. The AHA is very committed to getting that message out.
 
Q: Do you have a history of heart disease or health problems in your family?
A: I don’t know because I was adopted, but in working with the church all my life and living in the African-American community, I’ve had close friends whose parents had diabetes or had strokes. It was something I saw all the time.
