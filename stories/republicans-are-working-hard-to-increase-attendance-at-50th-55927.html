Republicans Are Working Hard to Increase Attendance at 50th Selma March Anniversary

February 9, 2015 Black

Congressional Republicans, traditionally outnumbered by Democrats at the annual commemoration of a historic voting rights march in Selma, Ala., are making an extra effort to boost their attendance at this year’s 50th anniversary.

Rep. Martha Roby of Alabama and Sen. Tim Scott of South Carolina, both Republicans, are busily recruiting colleagues to join the Faith and Politics Institute’s pilgrimage to Alabama in March.

This week’s march-related events in Washington include a Capitol Hill reception and a screening of the movie Selma exclusively for members of Congress. Lawmakers will receive personal, hand-delivered invitations to the march anniversary.

The trip to Alabama will take place March 6-8 and will include stops in Birmingham, Selma and Montgomery. By tradition, the pilgrimage falls on the 50th anniversary of Bloody Sunday, when police violently beat voting rights marchers as they tried to cross the Edmund Pettus Bridge in Selma on their way to Montgomery in 1965.

The Faith and Politics Institute — a bipartisan, interfaith nonprofit focused on racial and political reconciliation in Congress — started the civil rights pilgrimages in 1998. The annual event typically draws many more Democrats than Republicans.
