Florida Church Takes Over Former Strip Club

January 21, 2012 Uncategorized

The church that moved into a closed strip club soon will be open for business after receiving a surprise donation of $12,000.

Donald Registe (from left), Pastor Dumont Pierre, Pastor Pierre’s grandson D’Andre Pierre, 11, and Jacques Sauveur, along with other members of the new congregation, volunteer their time to work on the new Evangelic Assembly of Christ Church in Boynton Beach Thursday Dec. 29, 2011.
“Oh, Lord. I cannot explain it,” Dumont Pierre, pastor of Eglise Assemblee Evangelique de Christ, said Thursday night. “For us, we consider this as a miracle.”
The donor’s a familiar one: Christ Fellowship.
In December, the Palm Beach Gardens megachurch, believed to be Palm Beach County’s largest congregation, revealed plans to buy a closed 126,000-square-foot Dillard’s department store in the stressed Boynton Beach Mall and set up a satellite worship center that would host thousands every week.
In May, Eglise Assemblee, a largely Haitian-American congregation of about 250, paid $600,000 for the former Platinum Showgirls site at 1115 N. Federal Highway in Boynton.
Police had raided the club early Halloween morning in 2009 amid allegations dancers engaged in sex and sold drugs.
Eglise Assemblee had been renting space at a Delray Beach church. But the $2,000-a-month rent was costly, so Christmas Day was its last service. It has been dormant as work is done on the new site.
Volunteers have painted over black walls, removed dancing poles and torn up carpeting still smelling of stale alcohol.
It took all the money the congregation could collect to buy the site and do the remodeling. But there still was more to do before the city would declare it ready.
After a story on the church and its financial struggles appeared Dec. 31 in The Palm Beach Post, Leo Abdella, a pastor and director of community relations for Christ Fellowship, came to the site.
