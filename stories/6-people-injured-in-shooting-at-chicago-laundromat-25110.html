6 People Injured in Shooting at Chicago Laundromat

June 3, 2014 Black

A man lights a cigarette as Chicago Police investigate shooting at a laundromat on 79th Street in Chicago. Seven people were shot, including a 14-year-old. (Scott Strazzante, Chicago Tribune / June 2, 2014)

A gunman fired into a crowd at a South Shore strip mall Monday night, striking two teenagers and four adults, according police.

All six people shot, who range in age from 14 to 41, are expected to survive, as is a woman who was injured trying to run away, police said.

The shooting happened about 8:05 p.m. at a strip mall in the 2600 block of East 79th Street, authorities said. After approaching on foot, a male shooter fired a handgun into a crowd of people, Police News Affairs Officer Ron Gaines said.

The glass window of a coin laundry was shattered in the shooting, and police were working into the morning to determine which of the six people were struck inside the laundromat and which had been standing outside, officials said.

Paramedics found four of the injured inside the laundromat, Chicago Fire Department spokesman Larry Langford said. The three others were outside.

The youngest person shot, a 14-year-old boy, was shot in the leg and taken to University of Chicago Comer Children’s Hospital, authorities said. A 16-year-old boy was also shot in the leg and was taken to Advocate Christ Medical Center in Oak Lawn.

Three men, ages 41, 42 and 52 were also shot, suffering wounds to the elbow, arm and leg, respectively. A 27-year-old woman was shot in the leg.

A 25-year-old woman injured her toe while running away from the gunfire, police said.

The injured adults were treated at South Shore Hospital, Jackson Park Hospital, Northwestern Memorial Hospital and John H. Stroger, Jr. Hospital of Cook County.

Four of the injured were transported in serious-to-critical condition, and three were in fair-to-serious condition, Langford said. Police said later that the conditions of all seven were stabilized.

Police remained outside the laundromat more than hours, blocking 79th Street with yellow tape and directing neighborhood residents to seek alternate routes from one side of the crime scene to another.

For Tamesha Ginn, a lifelong South Shore resident who heard the gunfire from her porch, the shooting reflected changes her neighborhood has gone through in the years since her childhood.

“I just wish it could go back to how the neighborhood used to be,” said Ginn, a 32-year-old property manager. “It wasn’t like this.”
