Kentucky Church Found Liable for Death of Boy Killed After Youth Minister Let Him Drive His Car

May 9, 2012 Uncategorized

A Nelson Circuit Court jury awarded $2.15 million in damages Thursday to the estate of a boy who was killed in 2009 when a youth minister at Big Springs Assembly of God church let him drive his vehicle.
The jury found former youth minister Derek Coulter and the church liable for the death of Jamie Mitchell, 13.
The verdict was returned after a four-day trial and four hours of deliberation by a jury of 11 women and one man, said Thomas Clay, one of the estate’s lawyers.
Clay said Jamie’s parents, Rebecca Coleman and James Mitchell, were pleased with the verdict, as were Clay and his co-counsel, Gary Adams and Kirsten Daniel.
“We felt like the jury paid close attention to the evidence and came back with a substantial award that reflected the seriousness of what happened,” Clay said.
A portion of the verdict — about half — will be reduced to reflect the jury’s finding that Jamie was 20 percent at fault.
J. Dale Golden, a lawyer for the church, now known as the Open Door Christian Center, said it will consider appealing based on what he described as inconsistencies in the verdict. He said the jury found that Coulter wasn’t acting in the scope of his employment but that the church was negligent in supervising him.
Attorneys for Coulter did not respond to requests for comment.
