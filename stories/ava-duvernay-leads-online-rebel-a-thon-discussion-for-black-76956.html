Ava DuVernay Leads Online ‘Rebel-a-Thon’ Discussion for Black Film-makers

May 31, 2015 Entertainment

Rebel with a cause: Ava DuVernay. Photograph: Action Press/Rex

The Selma director curated an online discussion with black directors and actors, including John Singleton and Tyler Perry, who shared advice and affirmation

For 12 hours yesterday, Ava DuVernay – the Golden Globe-nominated director of Selma – wielded both her increasing influence and the connective power of social media to host a Twitter conversation in which more than 40 black directors dispensed advice to fans and fledgling film-makers.

The online event, entitled the ‘Rebel-a-Thon’, was also intended to raise awareness of a membership drive for DuVernay’s AFFRM, an organisation dedicated to releasing independent work by black film-makers (DuVernay released her first two films, I Will Follow (2010) and Middle of Nowhere (2012), through the company).

Each participating film-maker had a short slot in which to respond to questions asked via Twitter, after users were encouraged to submit thoughts and questions using the hashtag #ARRAY.

Amid a wide range of talent, there were galvanising contributions from big names such as Tyler Perry, who lauded DuVernay for her ability to mobilise.

There was even a light-hearted cameo from Oprah Winfrey, who recently starred as activist Annie Lee Cooper in Selma.

Up-and-comers such as Ryan Coogler (Fruitvale Station) and Justin Simien (Dear White People) offered thoughtful contributions, while the more experimentally inclined film-maker Terence Nance spoke about the process of making his debut, An Oversimplification of Her Beauty, and also shed light on issues like the troubling paucity of black female cinematographers.

In an email, Nance expanded on why DuVernay’s drive is so crucial:

“Black film-makers acting collectively is the only way for us to break through the white supremacy that permeates the film industry and [will allow us to] get our films made and distributed consistently,” he wrote.

“Our collective talent and numbers as creatives and producers is far outpacing our collective output (less than 10 movies a year from ‘art first’ black film-makers). We can’t increase our output without pooling our resources and strategizing and acting collectively.”

He also took time to pay tribute to DuVernay’s vision, saying “Ava is spearheading this effort and her skill, talent, bravery and generosity are unprecedented. What she is doing is very necessary and the rest of us should feel a responsibility to build institutions with each other and act in our collective interest.”
