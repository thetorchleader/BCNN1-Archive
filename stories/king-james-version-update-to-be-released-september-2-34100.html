King James Version Update to Be Released September 2

August 17, 2014 Books

KJV/MEV Parallel Bible

An Orlando-based publishing house will soon release the first Bible translation in the King James tradition in more than 30 years. The Modern English Version (MEV) uses the same original source texts as the King James Version (KJV), but contains updated English.

Passio, an imprint of Charisma House Book Group, will release the MEV Thinline Reference Bible on Sept. 2. It will be followed by the KJV/MEV Parallel Bible on Oct. 7 and the SpiritLed Woman Bible on Nov. 4.

The most recent KJV edition was The New King James Version, released in 1982 by Thomas Nelson Publishers. “Since early 2000 various Bible scholars have felt the KJV needed to be updated,” said Jason McMullen, publishing director for the Modern English Version.

“The translation committee’s goal was to create a readable but accurate translation,” McMullen said. “The King James has been such a large part of culture that the translators wanted to preserve as much of the original wording, rhythm and flow as possible and preserve it for a new generation.”

Retired Army chaplain James Linzey, a graduate of Fuller Theological Seminary, served as chief editor of the translation. Linzey originally conceived the project as a way to provide a New Testament that military chaplains could use with troops who struggled to grasp the KJV’s language and phrasing.

The inter-denominational translation committee included 47 chaplains and scholars from the United States and Great Britain. The late Stanley Horton, professor emeritus at the Assemblies of God Theological Seminary, served as senior editorial advisor.

Before his death in July, Horton—the author or co-author of nearly 20 books—called the MEV a clear, concise translation, as well as the most scholarly, modern update of the King James Version.

“Our version is a King James for a new generation,” said Linzey, an ordained Southern Baptist evangelist. “The MEV is a more accurate translation of the Textus Receptus (the source for the KJV New Testament), based on a more modern English vernacular.”

“Not only will the new version impact the entire English-speaking world, the work of translating Scripture has always played an important role in missions,” Linzey said.

He noted that because of the work of missionary translators, the Bible is available in more than 400 languages. However, missionaries traditionally have relied on the King James rather than ancient Hebrew, Greek or Aramaic texts.

“The MEV is a translators Bible for missions work to provide the Word of God to all English-speaking people,” Linzey said.
