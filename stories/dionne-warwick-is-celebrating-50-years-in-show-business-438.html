Dionne Warwick Is Celebrating 50 Years In Show Business

October 15, 2012 Uncategorized

Two years later, she won her second Grammy, for “I’ll Never Fall in Love Again,” and joined Ella Fitzgerald as the second African-American solo female artist to win the prestigious award for Best Contemporary Female Vocalist Performance.
Entering her second decade as a successful performer and recording artist, the 1970s saw Dionne labeled as the artist who “bridged the gap” between gospel, pop and R&B. She earned a contract with Warner Bros. Records, for whom she recorded six albums, and hit the charts with a 1974 duet she did with the Spinners, “Then Came You.”
The latter part of the ’70s saw another change for Dionne as she was signed with Arista Records. He first album with Arista, “Dionne,” which included hits, “I’ll Never Love This Way Again,” and “Déj? vu,” was produced by Barry Manilow and became her first platinum-selling offering, earning her two more Grammy Awards.
Dionne entered the 1980s in a collaborative way, working with the many friends she had made within the industry including Barry Gibb, Johnny Mathis and Smokey Robinson. Her friendly collaboration was topped off in 1985, when she teamed up with Bacharach, his wife, Carole Bayer Sager, Gladys Knight, Elton John and Stevie Wonder to record “That’s What Friends Are For,” which became a number one hit and the first recording that raised awareness of AIDS and over $3 million for The Foundation for Aids Research (AMFAR).
“When I think of ‘That’s What Friends Are For,” I think of the beautiful friendships I have with the people who are represented on that song,” said Dionne. “Every one of those people is very dear to my heart.”
