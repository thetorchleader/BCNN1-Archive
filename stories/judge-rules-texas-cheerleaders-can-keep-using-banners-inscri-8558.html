Judge Rules Texas Cheerleaders Can Keep Using Banners Inscribed with Bible Verses

October 5, 2012 Uncategorized

Cheerleaders in a small southeast Texas town can continue using their controversial biblical banners at football games, at least for now, a judge has ruled.
Pictured: Kountze High School cheerleaders and other children work on a large sign.
The polarizing posters were temporarily banned at the cheer squad’s public high school last month, but a judge reinstated their use and granted another two week extension Thursday as he decides whether they violate the First Amendment.
“We’re fighting for our freedom of religion and freedom to display our religious beliefs at our football games,” cheerleader Macy Matthews, 15, told the Daily News Friday.
Players for Kountze High School are expected to bolt through one of the 20-foot homemade scriptural signs at a game Friday night.
“And let us run with endurance the race God has set before us. Hebrews 12:1b,” the red sign with black writing reads.
“But thanks be to God, which gives us Victory through our Lord Jesus Christ. 1 Cor. 15:57,” another recent run-through banner said.
“We were sitting around a table at cheerleading camp this summer when we got the idea. The messages are all very positive. I don’t think it’s very fair to say we can’t do it,” Macy Matthews said.
“These signs are more encouraging and more positive to both sides — not just our team, but the other side and the coaching staff and everyone,” her mom Coti Matthews, 31, told The News.
“The girls don’t shed their individual rights just because they walk on a football field,” she continued. “The school doesn’t provide the uniforms or pay for the supplies to make the banners.”
The mom, a former cheerleader herself, was one of scores of supporters who flocked to a courthouse Thursday to hear testimony in the case that’s quickly gaining national attention.
