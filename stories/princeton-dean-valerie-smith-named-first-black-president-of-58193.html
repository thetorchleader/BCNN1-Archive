Princeton Dean Valerie Smith Named First Black President of Swarthmore College

February 23, 2015 Education

Valerie Smith will become Swarthmore College’s 15th president on July 1. SUBMITTED PHOTO

A dean at Princeton University has been named the new president at Swarthmore College, becoming the first African-American to lead the Delaware County campus.

The choice of Valerie Smith as Swarthmore’s 15th president was made by a unanimous agreement of the school’s board of managers, the college announced Saturday.

Smith, dean of the College at Princeton University and a scholar of African-American literature and culture, will replace Interim President Constance Cain Hungerford, who will resume her position as the Mari S. Michener Professor of Art History when Smith assumes her role July 1.

“Swarthmore’s values very much align with my own,” Smith said, “ensuring that a quality liberal arts education is accessible and affordable and that students’ academic experiences prepare them to make a difference not just in their chosen field — whether it be in medicine, Wall Street, the arts or the classroom – but in the world.”

She said she’s anticipating being a part of the Swarthmore community and listening and learning from the students, faculty, staff and alumni while maintaining the college’s reputation.

“Specifically, I hope to extend the college’s efforts to build a more inclusive environment for students, staff and faculty; to invest in more innovative pedagogical practices; and to cultivate national and international research and conversation about the value of a liberal arts education,” Smith said, adding that she wanted to find ways to deepen connections between students’ intellectual and extra-curricular lives.

“I relish the opportunity to forge new opportunities for academic and service opportunities beyond the campus,” she said.

The board of managers commissioned a 12-member search committee of faculty, staff, student and alumni to look for a president.
