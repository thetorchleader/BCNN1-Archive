Kevin Costner Talks Investing in Race Relations Drama, ‘Black and White’

August 6, 2014 Entertainment

Costner and Octavia Spencer battle in court over a child-custody case in ‘Black and White.’ (Photo: Tracy Bennett, Treehouse Films)

Kevin Costner knows he took the hard road starring in and helping to finance Black and White, a drama that travels through tough racial issues.

But the actor has already converted one of his most important and initially skeptical critics — his wife Christine Baumgartner.

“I had to explain this all to my wife who looked at me and said, ‘Why are we doing this?’ ” Costner says of the financing decision. “I had to tell her, ‘We have to put our business hat on, honey, since it’s our money. But I think it will come back to us. The movie is that good.’ ”

Costner, 59, admits it was his heart, not his business mind, that grabbed him when he received screenwriter/director Mike Binder’s script. The two have been looking to collaborate on a project since 2005’s The Upside of Anger. But it was the role of hard-drinking, grieving widower Elliott Anderson — engaged in an escalating custody battle over his biracial granddaughter — that immediately captured Costner’s imagination. It led to Costner heading the financing for the $9 million production budget, half of it with his own family’s cash.

“I have reminded (Binder) that he could trust me when something really rings my bell, I don’t need a lot of counsel,” says Costner. “When I read this script, I promised I would get it made. So when no one would, I said I would write this check.”

Costner says he traveled to Hollywood studios and “all of the billionaires in town” to fund the project. But the idea of dealing with the racial issues in what becomes a heated courtroom drama proved daunting.

“They would say that it’s kind of scary. I would say, ‘Wait a second. You have the chance to make something really cool here and get your money back.’ But it even scares billionaires,” says Costner.
