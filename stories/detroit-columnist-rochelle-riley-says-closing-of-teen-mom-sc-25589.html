Detroit Columnist Rochelle Riley says Closing of Teen Mom School is Chance to Teach Young People to Just Say No to Sex

June 6, 2014 Black

I will make some folks angry, but when has that ever stopped me?

With great loss sometimes comes great opportunity. And in Detroit, that opportunity comes with the loss of the celebrated Catherine Ferguson Academy for Young Women, which served pregnant and parenting teens and provided child care so they could attend class.

Truth is, Detroit lost the academy in 2011, when the former Detroit Public Schools nationally celebrated program, which once had 288 students, saw its enrollment drop by more than half to 119 last fall, according to a Free Press report. The enrollment decline came as students and teachers filed a lawsuit claiming students were receiving an inferior education. If the district, as officials say, plans a replacement, officials should learn lessons from that alleged failure.

Since our girls, particularly black girls, are in trouble in Detroit and America, the greatest lesson is this: Just Say No to sex.

Most babies in Detroit — 79% — were born to single mothers and every fifth child to a teenager, according to 2012 U.S. Census data updates. Data Driven Detroit, in its landmark 2012 study, State of the Detroit Child, reported that the majority of children were born to women who did not have any post high school education. And while the number of births to teenage mothers dropped by 9.2% between 2008 and 2010, teen mothers still accounted for one of every five Detroit births.

Moreover, black girls and black women are getting short shrift in the national public policy discussion. The Obama administration announced to much fanfare “My Brother’s Keeper,” the $200-million public- and private-sector effort directing resources to black and Hispanic boys. And two Detroit City Council members — James Tate and Andre Spivey — are co-chairing a new Task Force on Black Male Engagement to improve the lives of black men in Detroit.

But with the statistics cited above and black girls needing just as much attention, we cannot afford to discriminate.

The Washington Post reported last week that 210 prominent black men have written to the president decrying his decision to focus solely on black boys. The Post reported that “no woman is more likely to be murdered in this country today than a black woman. No woman is more likely to be raped than a black woman. And no woman is more likely to be beaten, either by a stranger or by someone she loves and trusts, than a black woman.” The story also reported that only 60% of young black women graduate high school in four years, compared with 78% of young white women.

Combine those traumas with our city’s birth rate and it’s clear we have work to do.
