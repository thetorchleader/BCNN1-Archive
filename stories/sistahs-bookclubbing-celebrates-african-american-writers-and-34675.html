Sistahs BookClubbing Celebrates African-American Writers and Their Books

August 21, 2014 Books

(Photo: Special to The Times )

Tannie Lewis Bradley describes herself as a confirmed bookaholic. She’s also a promoter of literacy and the joy of reading.

Her love of books shines in Sistahs BookClubbing, a local book club she organized in 2000. The group of about two dozen members meets monthly to discuss mostly African-American literature.

Bradley, who grew up in North Louisiana, started the club after retiring from Hughes Space and Communications in California. Her mission: To increase the awareness and appreciation of African-American literature and build a sisterhood of women who could candidly discuss books.

“I like to read biographies, fiction, Southern fiction, nonfiction, history and African-American literature,” she said. “I have, in my library, books autographed by Maya Angelou, James Baldwin, Nikki Giovanni, Ernest Gaines, Ernest Hill and other African-American writers.”

She co-hosted the August meeting with Carrie Belle, a retired counselor from Byrd High School, and they chose to read “The Pecan Man,” a self-published debut novel. Bradley discovered the story online and loved it so much that she contacted author Cassie Dandridge Selleck, who drove all the way from north Florida to visit with the club over lunch at J’Shanae’s Magnolia Restaurant on Pines Road.

The meeting brought together about 25 enthusiastic book lovers. Mothers and daughters, friends and neighbors joined a lively discussion of the novel, talking about subjects ranging from race to marriage and the workplace.

The enthusiasm for this book was almost palpable with members jumping in with comments about characters and themes, reading passages aloud and asking the author why certain things happened the way they did. The group applauded when the author said she is considering a sequel.

“When I read the book, I felt like I was sitting on the porch and Ora Lee (the main character) and I were chatting,” one member said.
