Alabama Church Holds Gun Buyback Campaign In Response to Shootings

Date not recorded in the original archive Black

The Rev. Dr. Wendell O. Davis, left, with Madison County Sheriff’s Department Lt. J.R. Bulluck at the church Saturday, July 5, 2014. The church organized a gun buyback that show a number of people turn in guns Saturday. (Blawson@al./AL.com photo)

Laid on the table in an annex room of Union Chapel Missionary Baptist Church were 12 guns, revolvers, automatics, a shotgun and a rifle.

It’s an uncommon sight at most churches, but the guns on display late this morning, represented the fruits of a campaign led by Union Chapel and joined by other churches in Northwest Huntsville to buy back guns for $50 and get them off the streets.

The campaign was funded by member of Union Chapel’s congregation and other donations, said the Rev. Dr. Wendell O. Davis, the church’s pastor. Davis said the larger goal is send evangelism teams into the community, to rebuild trust and the stop the violence.

The gun buyback was in response to a recent spate of shootings , including a June 18 shooting that claimed the life of Ashia Raquel Oliver, 18, who was an innocent bystander.

Oliver’s mother, a lifelong Union Chapel member spoke Friday and said “It’s time to put down the guns.”

The buyback began July 4 and only one gun was brought in, but this morning saw a number of people bring in guns and give them up in exchange for a $50 prepaid credit card.

Davis said they were pleased with the response, even it was just a relative handful of guns.

“I know we’re not even scratching the surface in terms of getting guns off the street,” he said. “But if one life is saved as a result, then it will be worth it.”
