Vanderbilt University Names Dorm In Honor Of Civil Rights Leader Rev. Kelly Miller Smith

August 14, 2014 Black

Fisk University student Diane Nash and the Rev. Kelly Miller Smith confer before releasing a statement to reporters on April 9, 1960.
(Photo: File / The Tennessean)

Vanderbilt University has named one of its newest residence halls for the late Rev. Kelly Miller Smith, a Nashville preacher who played a key role in the civil rights movement.

Smith Hall honors a man who also was the assistant dean of Vanderbilt Divinity School from 1969 until he died in 1984.

“Kelly was one of the premier prophetic voices as it relates to justice and freedom for African-American people,” Forrest Harris, director of Vanderbilt’s Kelly Miller Smith Institute on Black Church Studies, said in a news release.

“Kelly was committed to nonviolent work, through love, truth and treating people with dignity,” said the Rev. James Lawson, another central figure in the Nashville civil rights movement. “Kelly was one of those individuals who felt that you cannot remove racism by imitating it.”
