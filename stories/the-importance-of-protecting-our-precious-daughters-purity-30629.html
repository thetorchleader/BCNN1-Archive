The Importance of Protecting Our Precious Daughters’ Purity

July 17, 2014 God & Family

When I was a little girl, my mother bought me an Easy Bake Oven, Legos, Play Dough, cabbage patch kids and a baby grand piano. She introduced my sisters and I to the great outdoors through camping, youth retreats, and enrolled us in ballet, tap dance, and baton lessons.

It saddens me that many young girls today are being introduced to twerk dancing and other sexually provocative dances, pornographic music, sex icons, TV shows and movies containing sexually explicit content, immodest clothing, and adultified children’s toys — some that are outright racy and gothic.

What on earth is going on???

Girls are being sexualized through increasing exposure to ungodly sexual influences in various forms of media and advertising.

These damaging and destructive influences can be linked to harmful consequences such as teen STDs, teen pregnancy, sexual promiscuity, negative self-image, body dissatisfaction, low self-esteem, depression and depressed moods, cognitive issues, and eating disorders.

I do not believe that these harmful consequences are a mere by-product of consumerism. Nor do I believe that the root cause is liberalism, communism, feminism, sexism or any other -ism. I believe that it is the spirit of this age (the mystery of iniquity) seeking to violate the innocence of children, pervert them, and ultimately lead them to destruction.

“. . . Be strong in the Lord and in his mighty power. Put on all of God’s armor so that you will be able to stand firm against all strategies of the devil. For we are not fighting against flesh-and-blood enemies, but against evil rulers and authorities of the unseen world, against mighty powers in this dark world, and against evil spirits in the heavenly places.” (Ephesians 6:10-12)

In 2007, the APA (the American Psychological Association) Task Force on the Sexualization of Girls published a report offering examples of the sexualization of girls in U.S. culture:

“Toy manufacturers produce dolls wearing black leather miniskirts, feather boas, and thigh-high boots and market them to 8-to 12- year-old girls (LaFerla, 2003). Clothing stores sell thongs sized for 7– to 10-year-old girls (R. Brooks, 2006; Cook & Kaiser,
2004), some printed with slogans such as “eye candy” or “wink wink” (Cook & Kaiser, 2004; Haynes, 2005; Levy, 2005a; Merskin, 2004); other thongs sized for women and late adolescent girls are imprinted with characters from Dr. Seuss and the Muppets (e.g.,seehttp://www.princesscassie.com/children/cat.shtml) (Levy, 2005a; Pollett & Hurwitz, 2004). In the world of child beauty pageants, 5-year-old girls wear fake teeth, hair extensions, and makeup and are encouraged to “flirt” onstage by batting their long, false eyelashes (Cookson, 2001). On prime-time television, girls can watch fashion shows in which models made to resemble little girls wear sexy lingerie (e.g., the CBS broadcast of Victoria’s Secret Fashion Show on December 6, 2005)”

I don’t agree with every report by the APA, but I do believe that this particular report provides some helpful insight into the sexualization of girls.
