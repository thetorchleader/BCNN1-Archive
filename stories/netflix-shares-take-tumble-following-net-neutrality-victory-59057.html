Netflix Shares Take Tumble Following Net Neutrality Victory

February 27, 2015 Business

Robin Wright and Kevin Spacey in Season 3 of Netflix’s “House of Cards.” Credit: David Giesbrecht, Netflix

Less than one day after government regulators dealt Netflix a huge win with new Internet rules, investors are already on the lookout for the next phase in what is sure to be a long and drawn out battle.

Shares of Netflix stumbled on Friday, while shares of cable companies — which stand to be the biggest losers of the Federal Communications Commission’s proposed new rules — ticked higher.

Netflix stock traded down 0.5% on Friday to $480.50 a share. Shares of broadband provider Comcast, by contrast, rose 0.14% to $59.23, while Time Warner Cable, which together with Comcast controls 40% of wired broadband connections, moved up 0.5% to $153.17.

That’s a reversal of how stocks traded Thursday afternoon after federal regulators OK’d the FCC’s plans to prohibit cable companies from charging more for Internet fast lanes. After the 3-2 vote was announced Thursday, Netflix’s stock was charging higher while Comcast and TWC saw their shares move lower.

The reversal demonstrates how little faith investors have that the FCC proposal will become the law of the land.

Indeed, Internet service providers (ISPs) have already made it clear they will challenge the FCC’s proposal in court.

And they have a history of winning those battles.
