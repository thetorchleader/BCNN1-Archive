Rory McIlroy Wins PGA Championship Again, Tiger Woods Finishes 11th

August 13, 2012 Uncategorized

Much like the daily slog to and from this barrier island, Rory McIlroy’s road back to major championship glory was chock-full of congestion.
After the Boy Wonder from Northern Ireland was anointed the game’s next superstar at 22 following his crushing victory in the 2011 U.S. Open, his every move seemingly came under scrutiny, such as switching managers, deciding to play both the PGA and European Tours and his renting of a Florida home. A recent slump led to criticism from the British tabloids, who said he was canoodling too much with his girlfriend, tennis star Caroline Wozniacki.
Stung a bit by the criticism, McIlroy nonetheless remained calm and confident in his play on the course. Knowing it had been only 14 months since winning a major, he never thought about pulling hairs out of his unruly curly mop. And on Sunday, at the penal Ocean Course at Kiawah Island bordering the Atlantic Ocean, he was rewarded for his composure.
Contending in a major for the first time since winning the U.S. Open by eight strokes, McIlroy overpowered the sun-baked and windless course here with abundant drives of more than 350 yards and rolled to a tournament-record 8-shot victory in the PGA Championship, the year’s last major.
McIlroy also ended the streak of 16 consecutive majors with different winners, and he became the first 54-hole leader to win a major this year, after the fades by Peter Hanson at the Masters, Graeme McDowell and Jim Furyk at the U.S. Open and Adam Scott at the British Open.
Finishing at 13-under-par 275 on a course Golf Digest rated the toughest in the country and one where two tee box microphones were gobbled up by alligators this week, McIlroy topped David Lynn by eight and a group of four players, including defending champion Keegan Bradley and Ian Poulter, by nine.
Tiger Woods had another major weekend to forget and finished in a tie for 11th.
“I was a little frustrated with how I was playing earlier in the year. And there were a few people pushing the panic button for no good reason,” said McIlroy, who won the Honda Classic in March. “To be honest, I wanted to go out there and prove people wrong. That’s what I did. It took me all of four weeks to get my game back in shape and get out of my mini-slump, and this is the result.”
