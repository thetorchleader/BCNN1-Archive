Many African-Americans Brought In the New Year at Church

January 1, 2013 Uncategorized

While many people tonight are going to parties, nightclubs and restaurants to celebrate the new year, many African Americans will be in church.


Rev. Ray Turner leads a Watch Night service at Temple Missionary Baptist Church in San Bernardino in 2010. Terry Pierson, The Press-Enterprise
New Year’s Eve is when many predominantly black churches attract among their biggest crowds of the year.
The custom of holding “Watch Night” New Year’s Eve services may have started with Methodists as early as the 1700s, but it gained special meaning among many African-Americans in the 1800s. Many freed slaves and abolitionists gathered on Dec. 31, 1862, to await the Emancipation Proclamation, the declaration ending slavery in Confederate states that President Abraham Lincoln issued on Jan. 1, 1863.
Some churches begin services at 9 or 10 p.m., concluding them shortly after midnight.
Two years ago, I attended and wrote about a Watch Night service at San Bernardino’s Temple Missionary Baptist Church.
