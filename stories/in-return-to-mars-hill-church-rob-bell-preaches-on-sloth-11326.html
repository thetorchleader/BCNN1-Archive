In Return to Mars Hill Church, Rob Bell Preaches on Sloth

March 11, 2013 Uncategorized

Rob Bell had a confession for thousands of his former congregants at Mars Hill Bible Church, the Grandville megachurch that the charismatic pastor and author left more than a year ago for life and work in Los Angeles.
His revelation, which opened his first remarks to the church since his high-profile departure in January 2012, was something someone unlike Bell might not freely admit.
“I did flunk my California driver’s test the first time, so I could use a little more help there,” said Bell, to laughter, during Mars Hill’s 11 a.m. service Sunday, March 10.
“My problem with the test is I started questioning the questions,” he joked, “like, ‘That could have been worded better.'”
Bell delivered Sunday’s sermons at the 9 a.m. and 11 a.m. services at Mars Hill, which he founded in 1999. As part of a series of sermons on the seven deadly sins, Bell was tasked by his successor, Kent Dobson, with talking about “sloth.”
It was, perhaps, an appropriate subject for Bell, whose controversial book “Love Wins” caused a stir in the Christian evangelical community upon its publication in 2011 for suggesting nonbelievers were not doomed to eternal damnation.
His new book, “What We Talk About When We Talk About God,” also has led to some criticism by evangelical leaders after Bell posted excerpts on his website.
Sloth, Bell said, is not defined as “stillness, rest, play, Sabbath, or reflection.” It is, rather, “saying ‘no’ to the endless potentials and possibilities of the life God has given you.”
Along that vein, Bell relocated his family to Southern California last year to pursue other ministry work and begin development of a talk show with Carlton Cuse, a former writer and producer of the popular show “Lost.”
Speculation had swirled upon Bell’s departure that congregants’ displeasure with “Love Wins” and its subsequent blowback from many Christian leaders forced his resignation.
That was, Bell said, not the case. Instead, he said he’d been mulling his accomplishments at Mars Hill, how the church had grown and what it had taught him about the human condition.
“We want to talk about the things that matter most,” Bell said. “We want to talk about our brokenness and our struggle, we want to be inspired. We want to hear stories.
“In our culture, what are the two things that get the discussion started the fastest at Thanksgiving? Religion and politics. And in politics, we have every variation of discussion. Everything from Rachel Maddow to Fox News to everything in between.”
Unlike politics, spirituality has few outlets in mainstream culture, Bell said.
