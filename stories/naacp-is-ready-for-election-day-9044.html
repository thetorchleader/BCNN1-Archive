NAACP Is Ready for Election Day

November 6, 2012 Uncategorized

NAACP representatives said they are determined not to allow any type of voter suppression, including intimidation, tricks or false information, to keep minorities from voting today.


North Carolina NAACP President Rev. William Barber speaks to reporters in Durham on Nov. 5, 2012, about reports of voter intimidation at early-polling sites.
The Rev. William Barber II, president of the North Carolina NAACP, and Benjamin Todd Jealous, president of the National NAACP, spoke to a group of reporters Monday morning at the Durham headquarters of the NAACP, saying they’ve already heard reports of voter suppression and intimidation during the early voting process.
“It is of great concern that Gary Bartlett, the director of the State Board of Elections, commented last week that the number of voter intimidation and suppression incidents during the early voting was at the highest level he has seen in 20 years of monitoring North Carolina’s voting activities,” Barber said.
Barber claimed that more than 50 percent of African American voters used early voting, and that their voter protection hotline had received more than 600 calls during the early voting period.
Barber called it personal. He remembers the stories his parents told of struggling against voter intimidation and suppression.
“I never thought in 2012, we would see what we are seeing today,” he said.
Barber showed a photograph of a trailer that contained what looked like an open shed, and a number of mannequins, including one representing Barack Obama, Gov. Bev Perdue and judges, hanging from the rafters of the shed.
The silver truck towing the trailer pulled up to an early voting site in Goldsboro, and according to published news account, owner V.R. Phipps has driven the trailer through Manhattan and in other states. In those accounts, Phipps claims he’s trying to get justice for a family member killed in 1995. In YouTube videos, Phipps claims Democrats killed his family member.
Barber said that driving the trailer to polling sites was beyond troubling and is designed to intimidate and discourage black voters.
“But it should be known clearly we are not going to allow this to stand unchallenged,” Barber said.
African Americans worked and died for the right to vote and they will not turn back now, Barber said.
