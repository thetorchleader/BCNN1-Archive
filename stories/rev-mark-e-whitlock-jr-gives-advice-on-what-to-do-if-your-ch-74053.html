Rev. Mark E. Whitlock, Jr. Gives Advice on What to Do If Your Church Is Being Ripped Off

May 17, 2015 Black

Pastor Mark E. Whitlock, Jr.

In bad economic times, churches are the targets of professional con artists committed to rip the church off.  Some rip off artists hit fifty churches a day. 

Con artists are good at creating compelling stories for financial gain. Pastors and the benevolent committee are tasked with making donations to people in need and weeding out the rip off artist.

That How should we determine who should receive a financial donation? Helping the poor and people who have a temporary need for financial help is a never-ending ministry of the church. The church is called to serve the needy. The church is the place people go for help, but what method is used to determine value of the gift? How does the church know the difference in a person in need and a rip off artist?

One method is to establish a set of metrics for giving funds. The church board may wish to consider factors such as helping people who are members of the church, a person’s age, mothers with children, family size, unemployed, and homeless families.

The metric should determine the degree of problems. For example, a senior needing medicine may receive priority over a person needing a telephone bill paid. A church should decide whom not to give money to. Many churches don’t donate money for rent because there is a strong reality that the person will return for another donation in 30 days.
