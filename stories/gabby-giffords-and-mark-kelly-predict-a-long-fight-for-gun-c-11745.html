Gabby Giffords and Mark Kelly Predict a Long Fight for Gun Control Laws

April 16, 2013 Uncategorized

Mark Kelly, the astronaut turned activist lobbying for stricter gun rules, said Tuesday the gun-control movement is closer to victory than it was a week or two ago — but that it might take months or even years to win changes in the law.
Pictured: Pia Carusone and Mark Kelly of Americans for Responsible Solutions meet with reporters at a breakfast April 16 sponsored by the Christian Science Monitor. (Photo: Kateland Oakes, The Christian Science Monitor)
The National Rifle Association, he said, is a “formidable organization” that still has a powerful hold on many members of Congress.
“They had a hundred-year head start. This is going to take a little bit of time,” he said at a breakfast with reporters sponsored by the Christian Science Monitor. “It might take some election cycles.”
Kelly founded Americans for Responsible Solutions with his wife, former congresswoman Gabby Giffords, D-Ariz., after she was shot in the head while meeting with constituents at a grocery store in Tucson in 2011. In addition to lobbying on Capitol Hill, the group is recruiting and supporting candidates who support gun safety legislation.
Gun legislation being considered in the Senate seems to be losing steam after an apparent breakthrough last week. “I’m not completely pessimistic. Two weeks ago, this legislation was dead,” Kelly said. But he acknowledged setbacks since then.
Kelly singled out home state Sen. Jeff Flake, R-Ariz., who voted against a filibuster of the gun bill but then said he would oppose the compromise background check amendment sponsored by Sens. Joe Manchin, D-W.Va., and Pat Toomey, R-Pa.
“Manchin-Toomey would expand background checks far beyond commercial sales to include almost all private transfers – including between friends and neighbors – if the posting or display of the ad for a firearm was made public,” Flake said on his Facebook page Monday. “It simply goes too far.”
Kelly called Flake “a friend of Gabby’s,” and said “it appears to me he hasn’t read the bill.”
“There are a lot of senators who are just looking for an excuse to get to no,” Kelly said.
Senate Majority Leader Harry Reid, D-Nev., said Tuesday morning that he hopes to hold votes on amendments as soon as the afternoon. Kelly said he and Giffords would be on Capitol Hill with families of gun-violence victims during the day in an effort to swing votes.
“There’s no one path to victory in this,” said Pia Carusone, Giffords’ former chief of staff who serves as executive director of the group. “I think this is a pendulum. We’re going to lose a few more senators before we gain some.”
And in the House? “We would not want the vote in the House to be in the next week or so,” because there clearly isn’t enough support for gun control there, Kelly said.
