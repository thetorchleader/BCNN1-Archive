University of Missouri Student Rose Schmidt Gives 3 Reasons Why She Will Never Watch Another Episode of “19 Kids and Counting”

May 29, 2015 Opinion

Jill gushes about Derick. Jessa Lauren laughs at everything Ben says. Michelle calls Jim Bob her “wonderful husband.”

These are the moments that make up just about every episode of TLC’s 19 Kids and Counting, a show detailing the life of the Duggar clan – a family lionized for their size and their conservative Protestant background.

I will never experience these moments again.

After more than a decade, reports have resurfaced, exposing Josh Duggar’s involvement in a sexual molestation investigation involving five minor girls.

I did not want to believe the allegations at first. I have spent many days sitting in my apartment watching TLC, fascinated by the family’s lifestyle. The Duggars live sheltered away from society in a world many people do not know is in existence, which is one of the major things that drew me to their show. But after hearing about the probe and how the Duggars’ radical lifestyle fostered a culture of silence, I have decided that I, a Netflix-crazed college student, will never watch the show again. This is some of my reasoning:

1. THE PARENTS STOOD BY THEIR SON – NOT THE VICTIMS.

If you’ve watched the show, you know that what Jim Bob says is law in the Duggar house; I can’t think of any situations where Michelle’s word would trump his. This is the same model applied to the allegations against their son. Instead of focusing on how the female victims have coped with what happened to them, their media statements have usually highlighted Josh’s side, with him looking tragically sorry.

2. THEY TRIED TO COVER IT UP.

In a move that invokes thoughts of the Quiverfull teachings, a movement that declares problems be solved within the home, The Duggars sided against outside counseling, believing the best way to deal with this issue was to make sure it never left the home. But if you do something wrong, why make excuses and pretend like you haven’t hurt anyone? Josh Duggar’s actions affected many people, and failing to address the issue after it happened may have scarred these women for life.

3. THEY MAKE IT SEEM OK TO BLAME THE VICTIMS.

It’s never OK to blame the victims, and the idea that “they were asking for it” is utterly ridiculous. The Duggar family is known for dressing conservatively, and the family’s TV show even pokes fun at their restrictions on physical contact. The children are only allowed to hold hands with someone once they’re engaged, kissing is only acceptable when they’re married, and most outrageously, siblings and potential spouses are only allowed to give each other “side hugs.” Yet they still tried to spin the allegations to blame the victims for tempting Josh. Yay for patriarchy!

While it is likely that TLC will pull 19 Kids and Counting for good, this might not be the last we see of the Duggars. There is speculation that TLC could film a spinoff about the two married daughters, Jill and Jessa, and their respective husbands, Derick Dillard and Ben Seewald.

All I can tell you is that I won’t be watching that either.

SOURCE: USA Today
Rose Schmidt is a student at the University of Missouri and a summer 2015 USA TODAY Collegiate Correspondent.
