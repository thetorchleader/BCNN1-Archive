LISTEN: The National Prayer Breakfast Controversy: President Obama is Smart, but is he Wise? (The Torch Leadership Foundation’s Cross in Culture Podcast #15 with Daniel Whyte III)

February 14, 2015 Christian

President Obama at the National Prayer Breakfast

Daniel Whyte III

The Torch Leadership Foundation’s Cross in Culture Podcast #15

On last Thursday, President Obama spoke at the National Prayer Breakfast. During his speech, he outlined three things he believed everyone should keep in mind as we face the ongoing war that is being waged by Islamic extremists in the Middle East and North Africa who are also targeting Europe (in fact, another Paris, France-like terrorist attack took place at the time of this recording in Copenhagen, Denmark) and these same Islamic extremists have threatened to carry out attacks in America. Those three things are: humility about matters of faith, maintaining the separation between religion and government; and a commitment to the Golden Rule — treating others as we wish to be treated.

However, it was his remarks which seemed to draw a comparison between Christianity and ISIS that caused the most controversy. President Obama said, “Lest we get on our high horse and think this is unique to some other place, remember that during the Crusades and the Inquisition, people committed terrible deeds in the name of Christ. In our home country, slavery and Jim Crow all too often was justified in the name of Christ.” Many Christians and conservatives took issue with this comparison. Some believe that President Obama was comparing Christianity to ISIS which they contend is inherently violent.

To be sure, the cases of misguided people using the Christian faith for wrong purposes which President Obama cited are historically accurate.

According to Biblical Archaeology magazine, the Crusades were an attempt by the Catholic Church (also known then as the Holy Roman Empire) to take back the Holy Land from its Muslim conquerors. Atrocities were committed during these conquests. For example, after a two-week siege of Jerusalem, “the city’s Muslim rulers surrendered without a fight. The next morning, however, Jerusalem became a killing field as the conquerors slaughtered nearly every Muslim in the city and burned down a synagogue in which Jews had sought refuge.”

According to the Encyclopedia Brittanica, the Inquisition was a procedure of the church that often allowed alleged “heretics to be tortured by lay henchmen… On rare occasions, large public executions did take place, such as at Verona in 1278, when some 200 Cathars were burned.”

Regarding the issue of some people using the Bible and the Christian faith to condone American slavery: According to research by the University of Richmond, “Such extremist beliefs were common in the slavery/antislavery debate. Slaveholders believed that slavery would liberate Africans from their savage-like ways, especially if they were infused with Christianity. As religion ran deep through slavery, white Christian slaveholders argued that slavery was a necessary evil because it would control the sinful, less humane, black race.”

Regarding Jim Crow and segregation: there were white Christians on both sides of the issue. Some stood with Dr. King and other civil rights leaders to protest segregation, while others defended the status quo. For example, the powerful Southern politician, Theodore Bilbo, was trained as a Southern Baptist minister, yet he also was a member of the Ku Klux Klan and wrote a book which used Scripture to defend segregation. On Easter Sunday in 1960, Dr. Bob Jones Sr. preached a sermon in defense of segregation. However, on the other hand, evangelist Billy Graham released a statement in 1960 urging Southern Christians to pursue racial reconciliation. He said, “Racial prejudice must be ended, and Christian love must prevail.” Two years after the publication of Bilbo’s book, the Federal Council of Churches officially denounced segregation, stating that Jim Crow was “a violation of the gospel of love and human brotherhood.”

With these historical facts in mind, it is disingenuous for anyone to suggest that people have not done wrong things in the name of Christ in history. Let’s not forget that we are all sinners, for the Bible makes it very clear that we have all sinned and come short of the glory of God. We may be Christians, but we are not Jesus Christ. Historical facts are historical facts. What is up for debate, however, is whether or not President Obama was wise to have brought up these “facts” at a National Prayer Breakfast that is Christian based the present time when ISIS which claims to be a Muslim group recently burned alive a Jordanian pilot and put it on video for the world to see. They have carried out numerous beheadings, expelled thousands of Christians from their homelands in Iraq, killed an estimated 20,000 people in Syria and Iraq (who, by the way, were mostly Muslim), and (according to a new UN report) they are selling and crucifying children as well as forcing them to serve as human shields and suicide bombers. Was it wise for President Obama to bring up those who used Christianity to promote wrong agendas in light of all this? No, it was not.

Not only that, but it was not wise for President Obama to bring this up particularly in light of the fact that he already has a rocky relationship with mainstream Christians in America due to his promotion of abortion, promotion of homosexuality and homosexual marriage, lack of action on religious liberty issues, and slow action on addressing the issue of the persecution of Christians around the world. Nuns, of all people, have had to go to court to prevent his administration from fining them because they do not want to participate in his healthcare law which covers contraception.

Furthermore, it was not wise for President Obama to compare Christianity to ISIS when he refuses to state clearly that the war America and its allies are in is a war against Islamic extremism.

President Obama was not wise to make this comparison when some believe that part of the reason why ISIS, al-Qaeda, and other Muslim extremists are gunning for America is because this president accepts and embraces things that they see as abominations — such as homosexuality.

President Obama is obviously a smart man, but he has once again proven that he is not wise.

In closing, let me leave you with this Scripture passage and quote.

Daniel 2:20-21 says, “Blessed be the name of God for ever and ever: for wisdom and might are his: And he changeth the times and the seasons: he removeth kings, and setteth up kings: he giveth wisdom unto the wise, and knowledge to them that know understanding.”

The Greek philosopher Sophocles said, “The kind of man who always thinks that he is right, that his opinions, his pronouncements, are the final word, when once exposed shows nothing there.”

In light of what we have discussed today, I want to remind you that in our ever-changing world, there is one Person who never changes. That person is Jesus Christ. The Bible says that He is “the same yesterday, today, and forever.” He is not subject to the whims of society or popular opinion. He has an unchanging, eternal love for you. If you do not know Him as your Savior, I encourage you to get to know Him today. John 3:16 says, “For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.” Just believe in your heart that Jesus Christ died for your sins, was buried, and rose from the dead by the power of God for you so that you can live eternally with Him. Pray and ask Him to come into your heart today, and He will. Romans 10:13 says, “For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.”

Thank you for listening to this Torch Leadership Cross in Culture Podcast.
