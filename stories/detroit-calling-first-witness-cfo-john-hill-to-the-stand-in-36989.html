Detroit Calling First Witness, CFO John Hill, to the Stand In Bankruptcy Trial

September 4, 2014 National

Detroit chief financial officer John Hill is scheduled to be the city’s first witness in its bankruptcy proceedings Sept. 4, 2014. (Photo: City of Detroit)

The trial on Detroit’s plan for exiting bankruptcy resumes Thursday with opening arguments stretching into a third day in what could be seven weeks of hearings on what city lawyers have billed as the city’s future.

Oakland County’s lawyer, Jaye Quadrozzi, will kick off the day’s arguments, attacking the city’s plans for the Detroit Water and Sewerage Department. She argued Wednesday that Detroit’s plans for trying to pay off unfunded DWSD pension obligations amount to transferring onto the suburbs the legacy costs a poorly run public utility with “dilapidated” infrastructure.

Lawyers for the United Auto Workers, Wayne County and Detroit’s largest municipal union, the American Federation of State, County and Municipal Employees, also are expected to present their arguments Thursday.

On Wednesday, lawyers lined up to take shots at Detroit’s plan of adjustment, calling it illegal, unfair and dead on arrival. Creditor lawyers were highly critical of the city’s bankruptcy grand bargain that eases pension cuts by the equivalent of $816 million and shields the Detroit Institute of Arts from selling its masterpieces.

Among the highlights: Rhodes at one point forced a lawyer for the city’s most outspoken opponent in bankruptcy, bond holder Syncora, to say in court exactly what level of recovery the company would consider reasonable. The answer: 75 cents on the dollar.
