Pastor Antonio Stewart of The Reformation Faith Ministries Church in Kokomo, Indiana Seeks to Help Disadvantaged Youth Succeed

February 23, 2015 Black

STANDING UP — Pastor Antonio Stewart preaches at Indiana Wesleyan University, where Reformations Faith Ministries got its start before moving to its permanent home in the former Courtland Avenue Friends Church.

When Pastor Antonio and Dr. Dana Stewart launched The Reformation Faith Ministries church in January 2014, Pastor Stewart already had in mind what one of his biggest goals would be—reaching out to underprivileged youth.

As an African American child who grew up in an inner-city, underdeveloped neighborhood, Stewart said he knows what kind of disadvantage that poses to a child, especially a minority child.

“I grew up in the projects, and it is an environment that sometimes, when you don’t grow up in it, you don’t see the outcome of where these kids end up,” he said.

With an impoverished environment and dark skin color, the odds seemed stacked against him, as racially-charged situations came his way. But Stewart also said he’s just as aware that his behavior wasn’t always the best in response to the challenges he faced that later led him to lose a basketball scholarship and paved the way for him to spend nine months in a boys’ school.

“Sometimes how I acted wasn’t appropriate, and I know that. There were things I had to do to get my life right and act right and don’t do things I have no business doing,” he said. “And then there are people who have to check themselves and say, ‘I’m not looking at this person the right way. I have some racially-motivated tendencies to look at people who are not the same color as me, and I’m prejudice against these people for something that was embedded in me when I was younger.’”

While his youth was tough at times, Stewart credits a man named Dusty Stevens to helping him get his life on the right path and having faith in his ability to succeed. It was also Stevens who gave Stewart faith that not all white men were against him.

“He happened to be a white Caucasian man. He’s still in my life now. He was the best man at my wedding. He was here when I opened the church. I still talk to him to this day, so when I see a lot of racial stuff going on, I know that there are these people who are prejudice. But I also know I look at a character of a person. I know there are people who are white who care about me and love me and my daughters and do a lot of good things for me. You have to be able to separate the two,” he said.

That mentor-mentee relationship is what Stewart hopes to bestow among children in the community.
