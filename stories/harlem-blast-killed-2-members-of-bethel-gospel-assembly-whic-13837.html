Harlem Blast Killed 2 Members of Bethel Gospel Assembly Which Is Blocks from Where Explosion Took Place

March 14, 2014 Christian

Bishop Carlton Brown of Bethel Gospel Assembly had the grim task of telling his congregation about the deaths of two fellow members. Adrienne Grunwald for The Wall Street Journal

A sprawling East Harlem church with roots that date back a century is facing a painful toll: Two of its members died in Wednesday’s explosion that leveled two buildings and upended a community.

Griselde Camacho and Carmen Tanco were faithful members of Bethel Gospel Assembly, often volunteering or taking leadership roles, church members and officials said.

Wednesday night, church members cried when Bishop Carlton Brown delivered the news. Tributes poured in on the church’s Facebook page.

“Our hearts are heavy as we will truly miss these two beautiful women,” the bishop wrote on the church’s Facebook page. “…There is no need for you to go through these moments alone. Deacons and Elders are here for you.”

The church, which describes itself as a “loving, learning, launching church,” has a vibrant presence in the community, hosting dance concerts and singles ministry mixers, serving large dinners and traveling abroad for mission trips.

Housed in a five-story brick building just blocks from where the explosion occurred, the church has several lively services a week and a weekly radio broadcast.

Bishop Brown described Wednesday as one of both mission and mourning for the congregation, which provided food, water, clothing and shelter to community residents who were forced out of four adjacent buildings shut down by the explosion. He added that the church is working closely with the American Red Cross, which is providing relief to displaced residents.

“They got out of there fast,” Bishop Brown said, referring to the fleeing residents. “They came in slippers and pajamas and came up here. And we took care of them.”
