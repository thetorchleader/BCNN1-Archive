Sherwood Baptist Church Improving Facilities Through “Meet the Need” Campaign

September 1, 2014 Christian

A new youth worship center, which can hold up to 450-500 people, is among the recent improvements at Sherwood Baptist Church as part of the “Meet the Need” campaign. (Staff Photo: Jennifer Parks)

As part of a vision to expand so that the 21st century needs could be met in the church, in the community and worldwide, Sherwood Baptist Church just recently opened up its doors to its new Jerusalem by bringing in its congregation into the completed first phase of its improvements to its Whispering Pines Road facilities — brought to reality as a result of the “2020 Vision: Meet the Need” campaign.

Michael Catt, senior pastor at Sherwood, said the process of addressing those needs started in 2010.

“A vision planning team worked with me to establish priorities … on how we could have a strong home base and reach out to the world,” he said.

The campaign was announced by officials at Sherwood in early 2012, and has since been funded through annual giving campaigns with the overall goal in mind to raise $10 million for improvements at the church and Sherwood Christian Academy, maintaining ministries at the former Coca-Cola bottling plant on Pine Avenue and expanding church planting along with area and international missions.

On the church campus, that primarily meant the replacement or improvement of buildings that had been there since the formation of the church in 1956.

“We had 60-year-old facilities that were outdated and inadequate,” Catt said. “We needed to provide facilities where people would want to come.

“We serve Dougherty County and 29 other counties. We are not a community church; we are a regional church. (The improvements) will help us impact the next generation. We didn’t need this just for us; we needed this for the folks who will come after us.”

Considering the financial circumstances that many members of Sherwood’s congregation face, Catt called the response to the campaign “phenomenal.”

“(The congregation has been) serving, working, praying and have stepped up to the plate,” he said. “You could describe members of this church as: ‘Whatever it takes.’”
