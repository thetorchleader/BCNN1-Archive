NAACP Tennessee Chapter President, Dr. Elenora Woods, Calls Black Communities ‘Toxic’ for Pervasive Crime and Violence

January 2, 2015 Black

The new president of the Chattanooga-Hamilton County Chapter of the NAACP said it’s past time to speak out about youth violence in the city’s African American communities.

Dr. Elenora Woods gave a passionate speech at the “All Lives Matter” Jubilee Ceremony. From the podium at First Baptist Church on 8th St., Woods asked, “We have a serious problem in Chattanooga. African American communities are toxic. Toxic. When you wake up in the morning and you hear that three of your people are dead and nobody says anything?”

Actually only two people have died from Thursday morning’s shootings, but you get Woods’ point.

It’s personal and passionate. “Our schools are dilapidated. We don’t have teachers that really care about our kids. They don’t even try to work with our children. They send them straight to S.R.O. And from there, to juvenile. And that starts the vicious cycle. And the next thing you know, they’re in prison and nobody says anything?”

She said it’s past time to say something.Thursday morning, James Mapp was honored for his 28 years — off and on — as president of the Chattanooga-Hamilton County chapter of the NAACP.

Mapp said life for African Americans in Chattanooga has regressed.”Here we got 479 sworn (police) positions, only 83 blacks and we’re nearly 40 percent of the population. So you see, those are things that would bring crime down.”
