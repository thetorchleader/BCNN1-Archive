FBI Director’s Chief of Staff, Chuck Rosenberg, Picked as Acting Head of Troubled Drug Enforcement Administration

May 14, 2015 Politics

embattled DEA Administrator Michele Leonhart

Chuck Rosenberg, the FBI director’s chief of staff, has been appointed as the acting head of the Drug Enforcement Administration, Attorney General Loretta Lynch announced Wednesday. 

Rosenberg, a long-time Justice Department official who also served as two-time U.S. attorney, replaces embattled DEA Administrator Michele Leonhart who announced her retirement last month in wake of an internal Justice investigation which found that agents participated in sex parties with prostitutes supplied by drug cartels in Colombia.

Leonhart, a career DEA official who was confirmed as the agency’s top administrator in 2010, was expected to depart by the middle of the month. Rosenberg is set to take over Monday.

“He has proven himself as an exceptional leader, a skilled problem-solver and a consummate public servant of unshakable integrity,” Lynch said. “I can think of no better individual to lead this storied agency.”

FBI Director James Comey called Rosenberg “one of the finest people and public servants I have ever known.”

Rosenberg takes over a $2 billion enforcement operation that was deeply shaken by a Justice Department inspector general’s report last month which found that agents posted in Colombia engaged in the sex parties where a local Colombian police officer often stood guard, protecting the agents’ firearms and other property.
