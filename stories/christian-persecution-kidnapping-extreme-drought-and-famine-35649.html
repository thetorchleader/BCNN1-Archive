Christian Persecution, Kidnapping, Extreme Drought and Famine: Much of Africa Remains Mired In Evil

August 27, 2014 Eye on Africa

Christians of East Africa face increased levels of religious persecution as radical Islam spreads.

Christian persecution and other humanitarian issues continue on the African continent – most recently just off Somalia.

Four people traveling recently from Kenya to a popular island off the coast of Somalia near the border with Kenya were kidnapped by members of Al-Shabaab. Cameron Thomas with International Christian Concern provides some details about the militants’ actions.

“The Al-Shabaab is a radical Islamic insurgency that has control of large swaths of southern Somalia especially,” he describes. “[These four individuals] were kidnapped by this group of militants [and] held in the forest for a number of days.

“Three of the four who were kidnapped were Muslim; the fourth was a Christian. The three Muslims were eventually released and the fourth was summarily executed,” Thomas says. “So there’s another example of an active battle or struggle, if you will, between radical Islam and Christian and other religious minorities in Africa.”

In some cases, Thomas says groups are converting people by force and/or enslaving those who don’t agree with their ideology.

“Another great example – and it’s not so much a persecution issue but something that needs to be spoken about because it is under-reported in the news at the moment – is the ongoing crisis in South Sudan,” he shares.

“There are millions who have been displaced from their homes [in South Sudan]. They’re facing an extreme drought and famine, which is impending. Humanitarian aid is unable to get into afflicted areas because of the ongoing conflict.”
