5 Executives In Line to become the Next CEO of Procter & Gamble

August 23, 2014 Business

David Taylor, 56, is group president of global health and grooming at Procter & Gamble.(Photo: Provided to The Cincinnati Enquirer)

Tucked away in the fine print of just-delivered annual financial reports are clues that may help answer a tantalizing question: Who might become Procter & Gamble’s next CEO?

Chief executive A.G. Lafley returned to the big office from retirement last year, charged with restoring company growth and goosing a stagnant stock price out of the doldrums. He immediately shook up the management ranks and drew five key executives into his inner circle, signaling that one of them might one day succeed him.

Now, a year’s results are in for the five executives, each of whom supervises operations accounting for more than $20 billion in annual sales.

P&G insiders and analysts say whoever succeeds Lafley will do so on the strength of proven career results – not any single year. And no one’s saying when Lafley, 67, may decide to leave and retire for a second time.

Still, P&G has never chosen an outsider to lead the company. It’s always groomed chief executives who’ve run major brands or territories, and achieved success in different parts of the world.

As P&G starts a new fiscal year, here’s how the executive race looks for the five frontrunners. Our assessment is based on the track records that made the five executives serious contenders in the first place, and their latest results when the boss was watching closest.

On the rise: David Taylor

Taylor, 56, is group president of global health and grooming. The North Carolina native started early in manufacturing – even running P&G’s largest factory in the world in Mehoopany, Pennsylvania – before taking several management jobs in baby care, beauty and family care. He’s been stationed in Asia, Europe and North America.

• Key role: He headed P&G’s stalwart home care business for six years before being tapped in 2013 to oversee the company’s highly profitable, but sputtering, razor business.

• Bragging rights: As soon as he took over, the razor business started to rebound. Organic sales climbed 3 percent and surged 7 percent in the fourth quarter last fiscal year as Gillette unveiled new, badly needed product innovations: the Gillette Body and Flex Ball shaving systems.

• Obstacles: Health care growth slowed last year, but nobody really blames Taylor for a slow cold and flu season.
