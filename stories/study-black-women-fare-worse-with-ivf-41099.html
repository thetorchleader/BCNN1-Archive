Study: Black Women Fare Worse With IVF

October 23, 2014 Health

Doting mother Kim Fields, 45, poses for a snap with her two sons Sebastian Alexander, (L) and newest addition, Quincy Xavier (Credit: Twitter)

After miscarrying twice, Christian actress Kim Fields welcomed her “miracle baby” Quincy Xavier last December at age 44.

For others, their miracle is facilitated by in vitro fertilization. But a new study says black women fare worse with IVF.

Presented Monday at the American Society for Reproductive Medicine (ASRM) annual meeting in Honolulu, research revealed black women are only about half as likely as white women to become pregnant using the popular assisted reproduction technique.

HealthDay News reports, the study, which examined more than 4,000 IVF cycles over the span of two years found that about 31 percent of white patients became pregnant after IVF, compared to about 17 percent of black patients.

University of Chicago researchers also discovered that miscarriage after IVF (when eggs and sperm are implanted in a woman’s uterus), occurred twice as often among blacks than whites.

Fields, who has never shared whether she achieved motherhood for the second time post-40 through fertility drugs, artificial insemination, IVF or exclusively natural measures, does make one thing clear.

“If I’m encouraging to anybody … it’s not like I’m the poster child for over 40 and pregnant,” said the Facts of Life and Living Single star in an interview with PEOPLE.
