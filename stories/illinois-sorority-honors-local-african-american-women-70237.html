Illinois Sorority Honors Local African-American Women

April 27, 2015 Black

A local group is honoring African-American women who they feel are making a difference. Members of Zeta Phi Beta Sorority, Inc. say the BET program, Black Girls Rock, inspired them to create their own version, Black Girls Who Rock. The fourth annual event was held at the Martin Luther King Junior Center on Sunday.

Organizer Lachelle Guy says the women in attendance know that they “rock” and they want to make everyone else aware, too.

“I mean you can’t say it any other way,” she said. “I’m a black girl who rocks, my sorority sisters are black girls who rock, my mother is a black girl who rocks.”

She and her sisters have invited the community to celebrate local role models who excel in all aspects of their lives. They awarded four women for their work in education, religion and community service.

“They’re very intelligent, they’re very active in the community, they’re active in their schools, in their churches,” Guy said.

The purpose of the event is to applaud their accomplishments, which Guy says is lacking in society in general.

“I think black girls and black women are kind of pushed to the side,” she said. “They aren’t recognized. They’re looked at maybe as a minority and I think that we need to constantly honor them, constantly recognize them, especially our young girls.”

Eight high school seniors took home awards, including Pleasant Valley senior Shirlya Bacchus.
