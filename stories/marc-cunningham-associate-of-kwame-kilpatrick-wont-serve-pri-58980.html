Marc Cunningham, Associate of Kwame Kilpatrick, Won’t Serve Prison Time in Corruption Probe

February 27, 2015 Black

Marc Cunningham is shown in his booking mugshot on Nov. 5, 2010. He helped the government in its prosecution of former Detroit mayor Kwame Kilpatrick and his father, Bernard Kilpatrick. Cunningham pleaded guilty to conspiracy in 2010.
(Photo: U.S. Marshal Service)

The man who admitted giving kickbacks to the father of former Detroit Mayor Kwame Kilpatrick said he shouldn’t go to prison for his crime because he’s truly remorseful and has accepted responsibility for his actions. 

The prosecution agreed. So did the judge.

Marc Cunningham, Kilpatrick’s former fraternity brother who testified against Kwame Kilpatrick and his father, Bernard Kilpatrick, during the 2012 corruption trial, was spared a prison sentence today.

U.S. District Judge Nancy Edmunds instead gave him a one-day sentence, which has already been served and two years supervised release.

“Your cooperation … has been extraordinary,” Edmunds said. “I can only imagine the strain that this has put on you and your family.”

Before learning of his fate, Cunningham addressed the court and apologized for his actions.

“I do believe I’m a good person. I just made a bad decision, a couple of bad decisions,” Cunningham said, adding he learned his lesson.

“I just hope you allow me to still raise my children.”
