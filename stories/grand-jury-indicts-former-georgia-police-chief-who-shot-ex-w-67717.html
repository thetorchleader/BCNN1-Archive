Grand Jury Indicts Former Georgia Police Chief Who Shot Ex-Wife

April 15, 2015 National

In this undated image released by the Peachtree City Police Department, Chief William McCollom poses for a photograph. McCollom dialed 911 on New Year’s Day to report accidentally shooting his wife as she slept in their suburban Atlanta home.
(Photo: Peachtree City Police Department via AP)

An Atlanta-area grand jury indicted a former police chief Wednesday on misdemeanor charges in the shooting of his former wife.

William McCollom was indicted on charges of misdemeanor reckless conduct. McCollom was the police chief in the Atlanta suburb of Peachtree City at the time of the shooting.

Fayette County District Attorney Scott Ballard argued before the grand jury that McCollom acted with reckless conduct when he shot his former wife, Margaret McCollom, in their bed on New Year’s Day.

The shooting has left Margaret McCollom paralyzed below the waist.

Under Georgia law, reckless conduct occurs when “a person who causes bodily harm to or endangers the bodily safety of another person by consciously disregarding a substantial and unjustifiable risk.”

“There is no evidence to suggest that Chief McCollom intentionally shot Mrs. McCollom, nor did the investigation reveal any motive to cause her harm,” Ballard said in a statement.

Investigators found that William McCollom went to bed with a loaded gun after drinking and taking sleeping medication. He was awakened by the gunshot.

McCollom resigned as police chief in March.

McCollom’s defense attorney, Thomas Cook Jr., did not immediately return a message seeking comment.

SOURCE: WXIA-TV, Atlanta – Julie Wolfe
