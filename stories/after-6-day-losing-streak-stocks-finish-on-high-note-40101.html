After 6 Day Losing Streak, Stocks Finish on High Note

October 17, 2014 Finances

Traders Tommy in a booth on the floor of the New York Stock Exchange.
(Photo: Richard Drew, AP)

Stocks rose sharply Friday amid a dip in investor fear levels driven by strong earnings from a big Wall Street bank, a rebound rally in Europe, a strong reading on September housing starts and a sense the nation’s central bank will support the markets if needed.

The Dow Jones industrial average ended up 263.17 points, 1.6%, to close at 16,380.41, ending a six-session losing streak. The Nasdaq composite closed up 41.05 points, 1%, to end at 4258.44. The Standard & Poor’s 500 finished up 24 points, 1.3%, to 1886.76. All 10 S&P sectors finished higher.

European markets saw strong gains, with Germany’s DAX index up 3.1% and the CAC 40 of France shooting 2.9% higher.

On Thursday, the Dow ended off a scant 24.5 points to 16,117. The S&P 500 added 0.27 points to 1,862, while the Nasdaq composite rose 2 points to 4,217.

The investment community, which has been gripped by fear the past two weeks, is finding comfort in continued signs that U.S. companies are still earning good money and CEOs remain confident that the U.S. economy can keep its steady yet unspectacular growth rate intact despite slowdowns in Europe and China.

And consumers appear to be shrugging off the financial markets’ turmoil. The Thomson Reuters/University of Michigan’s consumer sentiment index rose to a seven-year high of 86.4 this month, according to the preliminary reading released Friday.

Rising stock prices and home values are giving confidence a lift, but Action Economics predicted the index’s final reading due Oct. 31 will be revised down due to worries over the Ebola outbreak and the world economy.
