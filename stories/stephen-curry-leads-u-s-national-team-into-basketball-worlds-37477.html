Stephen Curry Leads U.S. National Team Into Basketball Worlds Quarters

September 7, 2014 Sports

Stephen Curry was 6-of-9 behind the arc on Saturday against Mexico.
Jesse D. Garrabrant/Getty Images

They have a gold medal to chase and a golden U.S. basketball legacy to protect, so the Americans knew they had to bring their best to Barcelona.

Stephen Curry sure did.

Curry made six 3-pointers and scored 20 points, leading the U.S. national team into the quarterfinals of the Basketball World Cup with an 86-63 victory over Mexico on Saturday.

The Golden State Warriors All-Star was 7 of 10 from the field and 6 of 9 behind the arc, further evidence that one of the game’s best shooters was moving past his dismal start to the tournament.

He said his team is improving with every game, just as a similarly young U.S. squad did four years ago in winning gold in Turkey.

“In 2010, we did that,” Curry said. “Every game was fun, it was energetic, just enjoying the ride and I think now we’re here in Barcelona and got our first medal game under our belt, we got the wheels going and excited to get back to work on Tuesday.”

Warriors teammate Klay Thompson added 15 points for the Americans, who next play Slovenia after they defeated Dominican Republic. The U.S. beat both easily in exhibition play this summer, then handled the Dominicans again during the group stage in Bilbao.

Slovenia beat the Dominican Republic 71-61 in a thrilling contest of contrasting styles and scoring runs. Zoran Dragic, the younger brother of Phoenix Suns guard Goran, led Slovenia with 18 points.

Also advancing to the quarterfinals was France, who had to rally and then hold on to eliminate Croatia 68-64. Nicolas Batum and Evan Fournier scored 13 points each for European champion France.

Curry was just 4 for 17 overall in his first two games, though he shot the ball better over the next three. He saved his best performance for Barcelona, where the Americans are used to them.

He made consecutive 3s early in the third quarter when the Americans put away the game, the second coming as he was knocked down by a defender who was lucky not to be called for a foul.
