5 YOUNG MINISTERS, 5 STORIES: 25-Year-Old Arnold Woods III Says Young People Are “Starving” for the Bible

April 20, 2014 Christian

Associate minister Arnold Woods III leads a Bible study group at Faith Missionary Baptist Church in Des Moines.
(Photo: Bryon Houlgrave/The Register)

“We’re going to talk about stress this evening,” Arnold Woods III says as he opens his Bible at a table at Faith Missionary Baptist Church, where people in their 20s meet weekly to discuss life’s challenges through the lens of the New Testament.

“How do you deal with stress?” Woods, 25, asked.

“Netflix,” said Tess Montgomery, 21.

Montgomery had been unaffiliated with religion until she met Woods. Joining his class, she became more interested in faith, which helped her deal with issues of self-esteem, feeling alone and mourning for a relative.

“He really introduced me to a lot of this,” Montgomery said.

Woods says reaching out to young people through the Bible is his calling.

“People in our age group are starving for it,” he said.

Woods himself grew up deeply connected to church and community. (His father, Arnold Woods Jr., is a former president of the Iowa and Nebraska NAACP chapter, and Woods III led the youth and college division.)

But while an undergraduate student at Iowa State University, Woods became disenchanted with the leaders of his home church, which no longer operates in Des Moines.

“I felt lost,” he said. “I felt directionless.”
