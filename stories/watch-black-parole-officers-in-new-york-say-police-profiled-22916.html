WATCH: Black Parole Officers in New York say Police Profiled Them at Gunpoint

May 20, 2014 African-American

Four New York state parole officers press their case and lawsuit that Ramapo police racially profiled them in April 2014 and detained them for close to an hour at gunpoint in Airmont. (Photo: Steve Lieberman/The Journal News)

Four black state parole officers said Monday they were racially profiled by Rockland police who detained them at gunpoint for nearly an hour during an Airmont car stop last month while the officers were on the job.

At a news conference announcing plans for filing a civil rights lawsuit, the officers said they thought they were going to be killed as a group of Ramapo officers and a Suffern cop pointed their guns and an assault rifle at them on April 21.

The parole officers said they were wearing bulletproof vest and wearing law enforcement shields around their necks. Despite identifying themselves, they said the Rockland officers continued to treat them like criminals.

“I believed my life was going to end,” Parole Officer Sheila Penister said on Monday, standing with her fellow officers, more than 20 supporters and their civil rights lawyer outside the U.S. District Courthouse in White Plains.

The officers were driving an unmarked car on Airmont Road checking on parolees from state prisons when they said the local police stopped their car, pulled them out and surrounded them. The parole officers said police unlawfully detained them, with one cop pointing an assault rifle at them.

Parole Officer Mario Alexandre said one of the police officers recognized him, yet the detainment continued. The incident ended with no arrests or charges.

Alexandre called the car stop “an attack on my life and integrity.”
