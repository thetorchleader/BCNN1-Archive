Activists Want the FBI to Create a National Database that Tracks Police Misconduct

October 24, 2014 Black

Demonstrators face off with police in a Walgreen’s parking lot as protests continue in the wake of 18-year-old Michael Brown’s death on October 22, 2014 in Ferguson, Mo.
SCOTT OLSON/GETTY IMAGES

A coalition of activists want the FBI to create a national database that delineates every time an officer uses excessive force against a person, and they want that data broken down by race.Activists that have been protesting over incident where law enforcement officers have killed unarmed black men are calling on the FBI to do a better job of keeping track of police brutality and are now asking that those numbers be logged into a national database and broken down by race, Al Jazeera reports.

Activists and researchers believe the current system is not comprehensive enough. “The way the FBI collects data under its Uniform Crime Reporting (UCR) Program makes it impossible to know exactly how many Americans are killed by law enforcement officials each year. Local and state departments only report such information on a voluntary basis, according to an emailed statement from FBI Relief Media Liaison Billy Estok,” the report explains.

In order to further shed light on the disparities that exist between the rates at which black people are killed by law enforcement versus white people, the activists want the data to be broken down by race and other factors.

“Activists have called on federal agencies to create a national public database of police shootings, use of excessive force, misconduct complaints, arrests and more — all broken down by race and other demographic factors, Nelson told Al Jazeera.”
