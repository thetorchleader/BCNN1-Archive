Over 2,000 Miles of U.S. Hit With Snow, Icy Rain, and Sleet

March 5, 2015 National

New Yorkers woke up to snow, icy rain and sleet Thursday as a cross-county winter storm stretched from Texas to New England — but forecasters say this could be winter’s last hurrah.

New York City is set for eight inches of snow throughout Thursday, and governors in Alabama, Mississippi, West Virginia and New Jersey declared states of emergency in advance of the storm.

Altogether, about 92 million Americans will be under a winter weather advisory by the time they wake up Thursday.

The cold front is headed through a 2,000-mile territory between New Mexico and Massachusetts, with temperatures all through that area expected to drop at least 20 degrees.

Early Thursday morning, an onslaught of snow was already reported in the region between Jonesboro, Ark., and northwest Tennessee, according to the National Weather Service. The service predicted blizzard conditions in that region through 6 a.m., with snowfall and sleet totals expected to reach 12 inches.

Kentucky is likely to get 6 to 10 inches of snow. Lexington Mayor Jim Gray tweeted a picture of Weather Channel correspondents in the city Wednesday night and wrote: “You know something’s going on when the @weatherchannel is in town! Stay home if you can!”

Boston — which is a little more than 2 inches shy of its all-time snowfall record — could get 1 to 2 inches by storm’s end Thursday evening.
