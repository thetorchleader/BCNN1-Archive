Milton W. Hinton Jr. Says, “Young Black Men Are Carrying Out Genocide On Their Peers”; Asks Where Are All the Black Churches In Helping With This Crisis

August 10, 2014 Black

Left: Milton W. Hinton Jr.
Right: Tiya Hudson,13, was hit by a stray bullet early Monday morning as she slept in the bedroom of her Winslow Township home, Monday, July 7, 2014. (Staff Photo by Tim Hawk/South Jersey Times)

The homicide rate in the black community remains high; yet, astoundingly, neither individuals, organizations, nor influential institutions within the community appear willing to intervene.

It is so bad I no longer see it as homicide. It can only be viewed as genocide being practiced by young black men upon their peers on a seemingly daily basis. Too many sit by meekly as young black men continue to shoot up the very neighborhoods in which they reside, killing innocent people who have absolutely nothing to do with their being disrespected, with their turf wars, or with their capitalistic disputes over drugs and money.

In many neighborhoods, some people — mostly the friends and relatives of the innocent victims — briefly mourn, but those not directly impacted quickly return to business as usual.

It is time for the cover to be pulled off of this disgraceful behavior that diminishes the entire community in a society where relationships with other ethnic groups is often a prerequisite for securing and maintaining employment, business and other beneficial relationships. It is past time for the men in the black community to stand up and be men, and take control of the neighborhoods where they live, mentor their younger male relatives, and stop acting like scared little boys.

Continuing to place blame for black kids killing other black kids on law enforcement, or poverty and other social ills, is an attempt to justify the lack of community action or any attempt at intervention. History will not be kind to a community that stands by while allowing its children to be shot down in the street time and time again.

Last week, two men in Salem City were shot, reportedly in retaliation for another shooting two days earlier in which four teens were wounded by a shotgun blast. A 12-year-old girl in Paterson was gunned down while riding her scooter after three men opened fire from a van. A 13-year-old girl in Winslow Township was struck by a stray bullet while sleeping in her bedroom — an innocent child not even safe in her own bed.

In South Philadelphia, a 3-year old girl sitting on her porch getting her hair braided was killed and two others were wounded when two men arguing nearby pulled out guns and opened fire. There have been so many homicides in Camden that the city has a reputation as being one of the most dangerous in the nation.

The lives of black men and women in this nation have always been undervalued, but it is frightening to see so many believing and acting as if life has no value at all. I already hear the pundits, those who know nothing about the black community, repeating that Rush Limbaugh, Sean Hannity, nonsense that the pregnancy rate, or the number of single-parent households and absentee fathers, are the reasons behind this escalation of violence. Those sentiments are inaccurate, as evidenced by the fact that there has always been an overrepresentation of these social ills in black households, some of which also impact white households. The social conditions are not new. The shooting and killing is.

One of the criticisms emanating from the acolytes of Hannity and Limbaugh is that if a white person were shooting and killing so many young black men, the black community would be infuriated and mobilized. It pains me to admit they are correct, so much so that I have to question my own community and ask: “Where is your anger and concern about so many young people slaughtering each other? Where are the men? Where is the leadership?”

I know this will not be popular, but where are all of the black churches that have magnificent buildings in or near communities where this carnage is taking place?
