Newt Gingrich’s Outreach to Religious Voters Rolls Out in Iowa

December 19, 2011 Uncategorized

Many political observers have been scratching their heads over Newt Gingrich‘s
perch atop the polls in Iowa, where the caucuses are dominated by
evangelical voters who generally eschew candidates with messy private
lives.

Mid-November, a previously unknown group called
Iowans for Christian Leaders in Government dropped a flier that attacked
Gingrich’s sordid trail of affairs and divorces, asking, “If Newt
Gingrich can’t be faithful to his wife, how can we trust him to be
faithful to conservative voters?”

Great question. In fact, wasn’t this basically the conservative argument for why Bill Clinton shouldn’t be trusted as president, after the Monica Lewinsky fiasco?

“The evangelicals I talk to have made peace with (the marital issues). Everybody has screwed up,” David Lane, of Iowa’s Pastors and Pews, told me. “In 2007, Newt went on Focus on the Family radio and confessed (to James Dobson) and got on his knees.”

Gingrich
was “confessing” to something that was already very much public
knowledge. But to many evangelicals, the fact that he prostrated himself
before an evangelical leader to receive absolution means something. A
tenet of Christianity, after all, is redemption.

Of
course, most people who want to engage in Christian confession do it
directly to God, or privately to a priest, pastor or accountability
partner.

So, why the public display?
