Mount Emory Baptist Church, Second Oldest Black Baptist Church in Illinois, Marks Milestone

August 18, 2014 Black

One of the oldest black Baptist churches in the state of Illinois has marked a milestone.

Mount Emory Baptist Church turned 177 yesterday and the church members had a visiting pastor attend its service.

The Rev. Barry West from the Holy City Missionary Baptist Church attended the service at 4 p.m.

The church was established in 1837 as the Second Baptist Church until it was renamed Mount Emory in 1867.

Mount Emory is the second-oldest black church in Illinois and one of the oldest in the country, said Ruth Linear, a church member and historian.

“For at least 147 years, we were led to believe that Mount Emory was the first black Baptist church in the state of Illinois. On further research, we have discovered that the actual first Baptist church in Illinois is the Union Baptist Church of Alton,” Linear said.

The church was registered with the Wood River Baptist District Association on Sept. 13, 1839.

Under the guidance of John Livingston, who served as the first pastor, and Samuel Ball, the church was established and began to expand. As Livingston traveled to help establish other churches, several members gave sermons for the church. Eventually, Andrew Jackson took over as pastor of the church in the 1860s, teaching himself in the ways of the ministry.

The church has moved several times, starting out on South Church Street before moving to a building at Anna and Rockwell streets. In 1851, the church again moved to the north side of Anna Street between South Church and South West streets.
