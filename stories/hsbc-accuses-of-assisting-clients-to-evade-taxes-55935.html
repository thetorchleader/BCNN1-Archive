HSBC Accused of Assisting Clients to Evade Taxes

February 9, 2015 Business

HSBC, the U.K.’s biggest bank, helped rich customers to dodge paying tax by allowing them to set up secret accounts in Switzerland, according to a report.

BBC Panorama said it had seen thousands of documents from the company’s private Swiss bank that showed that some clients were able to hide money from HM Revenue and Customs.

As a result of investigations by the HMRC, some $190 million in unpaid tax, interest and penalties have since been handed over by the British citizens involved, it is claimed.

The documents, which were stolen by a computer expert in 2007 and have now been leaked to the BBC and other European media, detail the Swiss accounts of more than 100,000 people from around the world, including just under 7,000 British citizens.

According to the program, a French investigation concluded that 99.8% of the French people on the list had probably avoided paying tax.

It said HMRC was given the information in 2010 and subsequently decided that 1,100 British people had not paid enough tax. However, only one tax evader was prosecuted in the U.K.
