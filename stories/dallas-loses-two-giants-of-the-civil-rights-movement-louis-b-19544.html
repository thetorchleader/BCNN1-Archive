Dallas Loses Two Giants of the Civil Rights Movement: Louis Bedford Jr. and William “Bill” Blair Jr.

April 25, 2014 African-American

The Lincoln High School Marching Band performs in this iconic 2001 picture of the Martin Luther King Jr. parade in Dallas. Elite News founder William “Bill” Blair Jr. was a driving force behind the parade. Blair and another civil rights icon, Louis Bedford, the city’s first African-American judge, passed away this month. (Chris Hamilton)

As the last lions of the civil rights era pass away, the magnitude of their accomplishments sometimes is lost in the fog of time. If you didn’t live in their heyday or in their skin, it’s difficult to really appreciate what they meant to the voiceless in their communities.

This month, Dallas has lost two groundbreaking civil rights figures, Louis Bedford Jr., 88, and William “Bill” Blair Jr., 92, whose roles as change agents in the city should never be forgotten.

Bedford was Dallas County’s first black judge; Blair was the founder of Elite News, a paper that has been part of Dallas’ black community for decades. Blair’s funeral services will be held today.

Both men came of age when Dallas was a far different city, when overt racism barred African-Americans from the judiciary, news media and many other walks of life. Opening padlocked doors and mentoring those who followed became their missions.

A consummate lawyer who practiced with grace, Bedford quietly worked for change even as he weathered racial taunts and abuses in and around the court. Many young black attorneys drew inspiration from him. “He was a person we could talk to about different issues in the community,” recalls state Sen. Royce West, D-Dallas.

Bedford picketed against segregated lunch counters in downtown Dallas, provided legal support to civil rights campaigns, worked on cases with Thurgood Marshall and sought to desegregate Dallas schools. He helped organize the precursor to the J.L. Turner Legal Association, an African-American bar association named after the fabled Dallas lawyer, and, in the mid-1960s, became the first black judge on Dallas’ municipal court. Later, he prodded the Dallas Bar Association to make the presidents of the Turner group and the then-Mexican American Bar Association of Dallas voting members of the DBA’s board.

As Bedford blazed legal trails, Blair, a former Negro league baseball pitcher, built media pathways, establishing Southwest Sports News in 1949 to publish scores from African-American college games and news that didn’t appear in the mainstream press. Over the next decade, it evolved into Elite News, a powerful voice of the black church and community in Dallas.
