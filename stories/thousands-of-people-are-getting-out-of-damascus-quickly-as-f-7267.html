Thousands of People Are Getting Out of Damascus Quickly as Fighting Surges in Syria

July 20, 2012 Uncategorized

Thousands of people were fleeing Damascus and heading into Lebanon on Friday, according to the United Nations refugee agency, as rebels described the country’s bloodiest fighting in the 17-month uprising.


Lebanese immigration officers check a Syrian’s documents at the Masnaa border on Friday, where thousands have fled to, in the last 48 hours.
Between 8,500 and 30,000 Syrians have crossed into Lebanon in the last 48 hours, an agency spokeswoman, Melissa Fleming, said at a news briefing in Geneva. The new flood adds to an exodus of more than 112,000 who have already registered as refugees in Lebanon, Turkey, Iraq and Jordan, and many thousands more who have fled but not registered. United Nations relief agencies say three-quarters of them are women and children, often arriving in a desperate state with no more than the clothes they are wearing. Internally, as many as a million people have been displaced, according to the Syrian Arab Red Crescent.
The government forces in Damascus, the Syrian capital, claimed on Friday to have retaken a pocket of the city a day after rebel fighters, building on the momentum gained by their brazen assassination of top security officials, seized all four border crossings with Iraq and one into Turkey. Syrian state television reported that a fourth member of President Bashar al-Assad’s elite circle, Hisham Ikhtiar, the head of general security, had died of wounds sustained in Wednesday’s rebel bombing at a security headquarters buildings.
The attack also killed Asef Shawkat, who was the president’s brother-in-law and a senior security official; the defense minister, Gen. Dawoud A. Rajha; and Maj. Gen. Hassan Turkmani, a former defense minister. Maher al-Assad, President Assad’s brother who commands the praetorian guard protecting the family, and Vice President Farouk al-Sharaa were said to have presided over a ceremony honoring them, and there were reports that they were to be buried Friday.
Also on Friday, a last-ditch compromise at the Security Council authorized a 30-day extension for the 300 international observers who suspended their work on June 16 because of the heavy violence. The compromise was reached only hours before the mandate of the monitors was due to expire, giving what amounted to a thin lifeline for efforts by Kofi Annan, the United Nations and Arab League special envoy, to bring about an end to the violence. The departing officer in charge of the United Nations observers, Gen. Robert Mood, said at a news conference in Damascus on Thursday that the monitors were “irrelevant” without the will for peace on both sides.
Syrian state news media and the Syrian Observatory for Human Rights, based in Britain, said government forces, fighting back with a superior military machine pitted against an opposition still working predominantly with small-caliber weapons, had retaken a section of the Midan neighborhood on Friday, while the number of dead in fighting across Syria on Thursday was reported to have risen above 300. Of the dead, 98 were soldiers, 139 civilians and 65 rebels, rebels said.
The figures updated earlier estimates by the Syrian Observatory for Thursday’s death toll, claiming that 155 civilians and 93 government soldiers had been killed, including nearly 60 civilians in and around Damascus.
The latest tally was among the highest — if not the highest — claimed in a single day since the revolt began in March 2011. It could not be independently corroborated because of the difficulties and restrictions on independent news gathering in Syria.
Claiming victory in Midan, Syrian state television declared, “Our brave army forces have completely cleaned the area of Midan in Damascus of the remaining mercenary terrorists and have re-established security.” Rebels said they had staged a “tactical withdrawal” from Midan to avert civilian casualties.
Conditions across Damascus were growing more difficult, Ms. Fleming said, with shops running out of supplies and closing. She said that relief agency workers were hearing that state and private banks have run out of funds.
“Many Syrians in general are running low on resources and increasingly turning to the Syrian Arab Red Crescent and other organizations for help,” she added.
