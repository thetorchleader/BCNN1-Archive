Pasadena Public Health Director Dr. Eric Walsh Is In Trouble for Not Disclosing how Much he Gets Paid as an Associate Pastor

May 6, 2014 African-American

Eric Walsh

Pasadena Public Health Director Dr. Eric Walsh did not disclose his associate pastor salary on a required public statement of economic interest, according to documents exclusively obtained by this news organization.

In the required California Form 700 Walsh submitted to the city for 2013, the embattled city employee indicated that he had not received income from any investments, real property, gifts, travel payments or income, loans or business positions for last year.

But, according to the Southern California Conference for the national Seventh-day Adventist Church organization, Walsh is paid to be an associate pastor at the Altadena Seventh-day Adventist Church.

The form is signed under penalty of perjury.

Attorney Terry Francke of Californians Aware, an open-government advocacy group, said Walsh should have disclosed the income he earned on the pulpit.

His local counterpart Gil Aguirre agreed.

“If he is a salaried employee there, I would think that would be reportable income,” Aguirre said. “The 700 form is really designed to alert both the office and the public that there might be a conflict of interest.”

Walsh has been placed on administrative leave from his post at the city health department after recordings of his sermons surfaced on the Internet. In those talks, Walsh condemned homosexuality, Muslims and popular culture. In the sermons, many of which were given before Walsh was hired as public health director in 2010, he also describes Darwin’s theory of evolution as a satanic belief and condemns other denominations of Christianity, including Catholicism.

Links to the sermons were distributed to the media last week after Walsh was announced as the commencement speaker for Pasadena City College.

That announcement followed a scandal in which the college uninvited screenwriter, actor and gay-rights advocate Dustin Lance Black. Walshbacked out last week, and Black has accepted a renewed invitation and will speak at the commencement this weekend.

In contrast to his statements at the pulpit, at public events Walsh has spoken about the negative effects of discrimination and has championed progressive causes at the Public Health Department, including opening the state’s first city-run dental clinic for HIV patients.

“Dr. Walsh is a part-time pastor, but he is paid,” SDA spokeswoman Betty Cooney said. She declined to disclose how much Walsh makes in a year.

Before the issue with Walsh’s lack of financial disclosure surfaced, City Manager Michael Beck said his investigation would focus solely on whether Walsh’s sentiments affected his job performance. Beck said Walsh has the right to freedom of speech and religion outside city functions.

As an at-will employee, Walsh can legally be terminated from his position at any time with or without cause, according to Walsh’s employment contract with the city.
