Former Virginia Governor Bob McDonnell’s Corruption Case Goes to Jury

August 29, 2014 National

Former Gov. Bob McDonnell, center, faced his accusers Monday, Aug. 25, 2014, fielding the prosecution’s questions with a set jaw, a narrowed stare and careful pauses before short yes and no answers during day 21 of his corruption trial. ( Joe Fudge / Daily Press / August 25, 2014 )

Bob and Maureen McDonnell both rested their cases Wednesday, leaving just a few steps to go before a jury decides their fate.

Closing arguments will likely come Friday, federal Judge James R. Spencer said, wrapping up a five-week case.

There’s one more prosecution witness planned for Thursday, to rebut testimony given by defense witnesses. Then attorneys will debate jury instructions – the list of things Spencer will tell jurors about the complex federal laws involved in this 14-count case.

Wednesday was largely about wrapping up loose ends for the defense. Bob McDonnell’s legal team rested its case in the morning without calling his former secretary, Pam Watts, back to the stand as a character witness, as once planned.

Likewise, witnesses from the sealed subpoenas requested by the McDonnell defense never materialized. A number of named potential witnesses, including former Attorney General Ken Cuccinelli and current Speaker of the House Williams Howell, were never called to the stand.

Maureen McDonnell’s attorneys put on an abbreviated defense Wednesday, but her team seems to have worked closely with the governor’s throughout this case. Jurors heard Wednesday from a close friend, the McDonnell’s oldest daughter – an Iraq war veteran pregnant with their first grandchild – a former family attorney and a private investigator who served as a summary witness for the defense.

They didn’t hear from Mrs. McDonnell. She rose at the end of the day for three questions from the judge, who made sure she understand her right to testify, and her right not to.

“Yes, your honor,” she said three times.

Daughter Jeanine McDonnell Zubowsky’s testimony captivated the courtroom as she delved back into the ugly details of her parents’ marriage, essentially confirming what her father and other witnesses had testified to already. She also took a swing at the government’s star witness Jonnie R. Williams Sr., calling him a crook from the stand.

Bob and Maureen McDonnell are accused of accepting gifts and loans from Williams in return for their help promoting Anatabloc, a dietary supplement his company, Star Scientific, brought to market in 2011.
