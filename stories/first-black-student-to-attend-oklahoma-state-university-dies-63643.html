First Black Student to Attend Oklahoma State University Dies at 88

March 25, 2015 Black, Education

Nancy Randolph Davis, the first African-American woman to be enrolled at Oklahoma State University, died Monday, March 23, 2015. She was 88-years-old.

Davis was denied twice by OSU when she first sought to enroll at what was then called Oklahoma A&M College. With persistence and assistance from prominent NAACP leaders, Davis was admitted to the university in 1949.

A high school teacher at the time, Davis took classes during the summers and graduated in 1952 with a master’s degree in Home Economics (now known as Human Sciences).

Remaining closely connected with OSU, the university named the residential facility Davis Hall in her honor in 2001. She received the OSU Alumni Association’s Distinguished Alumni Award. There are also three scholarships bearing her name, and “Nancy Randolph Davis Day” is celebrated during Black History Month.

About Nancy Randolph Davis

Nancy Davis grew up in Sapulpa with four brothers and one sister. While education was a priority in the Davis family, Nancy also grew up in Sapulpa’s Mount Olive Baptist Church, where she developed an unwavering faith in God and a solid spiritual foundation.

After graduating high school in 1944, Davis enrolled at Langston University, where she was active in the Young Women’s Christian Association and the Alpha Kappa Alpha Sorority. Nancy accepted a teaching position at Dunjee High School after graduating from Langston University in 1948.

“I had a one room building where I had to teach 60 students, and there were five sewing machines, and [only] three of them worked.”
