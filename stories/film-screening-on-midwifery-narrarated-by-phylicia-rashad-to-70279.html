Film Screening on Midwifery Narrarated by Phylicia Rashad to Raise Money for African American Midwives

April 27, 2015 Black

Shafia Monroe makes a house call to provide prenatal midwifery care. (The Oregonian/OregonLive file photo)

Midwifery has been part of African American history for centuries, yet today African American women make up less than 2 percent of U.S. midwives. Shafia Monroe wants to change that, starting by raising awareness about African American midwives.

Monroe is founder, president and chief executive officer of the Portland-based International Center for Traditional Childbearing, a 24-year-old nonprofit that works to train, support and advocate for midwives and doulas. The center is holding a May 1 fundraising screening of “Bringin’ in Da Spirit,” an award-winning 2003 documentary about African American midwives. Actress Phylicia Rashad narrates the hour-long film, which splices together first-person narrative and archival images.

Following the film screening at 5:30 p.m. at the Portland Community College Cascade Campus, 705 N. Killingsworth St., Monroe will moderate a 30-minute panel discussion with E.D. Mondaine, pastor of the Celebration Tabernacle in Kenton; Willie Poinsette, a longtime Portland Public Schools employee; and Mariah Taylor, founder of the North Portland Nurse Practitioner Community Health Clinic, now the Children’s Community Clinic, and winner of a 2000 “Use Your Life Award” from Oprah’s Angel Network.

Tickets for the fundraiser start at $25 per person; reservations should be made by Tuesday, April 28. Proceeds will go partly to the ICTC and partly to scholarships for attending the center’s annual International Black Midwives and Healers Conference, which is returning in October to Portland for the first time since 2004.
