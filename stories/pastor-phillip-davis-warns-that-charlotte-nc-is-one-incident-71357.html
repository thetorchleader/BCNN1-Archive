Pastor Phillip Davis Warns that Charlotte, NC, is ‘One Incident Away’ from Seeing Baltimore-style Flare-Up; Says City’s Churches Must Come Together Now

May 1, 2015 Christian

Dr. Phillip M. Davis

by Dr. Phillip M. Davis

The Church is in the closet hiding from the reality, while the city burns. This is the sense I get when I look at what’s happened in Ferguson and what is happening in Baltimore. While I am thankful and proud that the clergy, the Church Leaders and Faith Community reacted to the events at hand, I would suggest that we must be proactive and respond rather than react.

Charlotte is but one incident, one event away from seeing the same kind of lawlessness, looting and rioting by those who feel disenfranchised, disrespected, and disillusioned by the so-called “American Dream.” I believe that as racial tensions simmer beneath the polite political and religious surface, it is time for the Clergy, Faith Community and Religious Leaders of Charlotte-Mecklenburg to act now and engage in meaningful dialogue aimed at producing genuine sustainable solutions and healing to the racial divide in our city.

How can the Church help heal the land? How can the Church help heal the Charlotte community?

How can we balance the tension between lawful peaceful and appropriate protest and lawless looting and rioting?

How can we balance the tension between the faithful, family oriented community servants who work as Police Officers and those few who are bigots with badges?

How can we balance the tension between the marginalized, the least, last and the left-out who face a culture of double-digit unemployment; who are looked upon as second-class citizens, with those who are good people but who have been raised with a sense of entitlement and superiority?

I believe it is past time for white evangelicals and white Christian and Faith leaders to come out of the closet and quit hiding behind the doors of institutional racism that has long been the culture of our nation and take action to solve this moral problem.

I believe it is past time for Black Christian and Faith Leaders to lay down our egos and logos and our distrust of one another and seek to collectively clean up, sure up, and build up the walls of our communities.

Now is the time for the church to not simply react because reactions are emotional and temporary, but we must respond with a sustainable strategic plan that is both engaging and empowering. It begins with those who are willing to be honest and transparent coming to the table of brotherhood and talking to one another, rather than talking at and about one another.

I am offering to host a “Reconciled Church” Summit as a starting point, this Thursday on the National Day of Prayer, at 11:30am until 1:30pm at the Nations Ford Community Church, 5901 Nations Ford Road. I am inviting every Senior Pastor, every Parish Priest, every Bishop, and every Rabbi, every Religious Leader of every faith. I am inviting every evangelical, conservative, liberal and moderate Faith Leader to join me to begin a community dialogue about a Charlotte Faith Community Response to the Racial Divide and let’s see, “What Can The Reconciled Church Do?”
