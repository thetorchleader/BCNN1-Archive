To Combat Crime, Indianapolis Needs a Rescue Mission for Young Black Males

November 18, 2014 Black

Crime scene tape surrounds the exhibit room for the traveling art exhibit, “Kin Killin’ Kin,” at Central Library. Spent gun shells litter the floor, as well.
(Photo: Kelly Wilkinson/The Star

Black on black crime.

After months of reading news stories about it and watching the murder rate skyrocket in Indianapolis, many of us think we know a lot about this subject. But a new report from the Your Life Matters Task Force, commissioned last spring by Mayor Greg Ballard, shows that we don’t know the half of it.

The report, being released today at a summit at Bankers Life Fieldhouse, is 102 pages of the most depressing, most troubling words I’ve ever read.

It’s filled with paragraphs like this: “Nearly 40 percent of black children in Indianapolis live in poverty and 60 percent of them live in single-parent households. The unemployment rate for black youth between the ages of 16-19 in Marion County is nearly triple the average youth unemployment rate for the state of Indiana.”

And this: “Indiana’s percentage of black male students who experience out-of-school suspensions was 27 percent and was tied for the second highest percentage among all states.” In Marion County, “the number of out-of-school suspensions represented more than 60 percent of the student population at various schools… ‘Attendance’ was the fifth most common reason for students being thrown out of school.”

And this: “Black males in Indiana are sentenced and incarcerated longer than any other ethnic group. In fact, they are about six times more likely to be incarcerated than their white male counterparts. Though blacks make up only 9.5 percent of the population in Indiana, 37 percent of inmates in the Indiana Department of Correction in 2012 were black males.”

As I said, this is depressing, troubling stuff.
