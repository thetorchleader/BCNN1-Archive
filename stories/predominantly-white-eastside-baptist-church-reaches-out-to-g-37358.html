Predominantly White Eastside Baptist Church Reaches Out to Growing Black Community With Black Youth Pastor

September 6, 2014 Christian

Mike Johnson, left, is the new pastor at Eastside Baptist Church; Brandon Knight, right, is the new youth pastor. (Eastside Baptist Church)

Eastside Baptist Church on U.S. 11 in Roebuck has hired two staff people, a white pastor and a black youth pastor, as it attempts to rebound from lost membership and reach a growing black population in the surrounding neighborhood.

The Rev. Mike Johnson, 60, who started as interim pastor on June 1 and was hired as full-time pastor on Aug. 24, thinks the church is poised for a turnaround.

“I’ve been in the ministry for 40 years,” he said. “I have pastored churches in Alabama, Wyoming, Louisiana. We’ve taken churches that were in decline or had plateaued and turn them around.”

On Aug. 17, the church hired a black youth pastor, Samford University student Brandon Knight.

“It’s been a changing community,” Johnson said. “The church showed some great faith and promise to call an African-American as youth pastor. He’s a history major at Samford, a very sharp young man. He’s connecting with kids and hopefully with parents.”

They make a great team, Johnson said. “We just have a mutual respect for each other,” he said. “We don’t take ourselves too seriously, but we take the Lord very seriously. We have a lot more in common than we have differences.”

Knight leads a youth service on Wednesday nights and helps with Bible studies on Sunday morning. “It’s been exciting,” Knight said. “The kids are great. Pastor Mike is great.”

Knight has been a licensed minister since 2008 and previously was on staff at New Zion Bible Way Church. After taking a class on North and South Korea, he started a Korean relief fund at Samford that aids North Korean refugees.

Eastside Baptist is not targeting just one demographic, but his presence may make blacks feel more welcome, Knight said. “There’s a different response from the people because they have someone they can relate to,” Knight said. “We’re trying to draw anybody and everybody we can.”

Johnson said that when he arrived at Eastside Baptist, the congregation had declined to about 80 from almost 1,000 members two decades ago.

“We’re starting our way back up,” Johnson said. “We’ve seen nine baptisms, including an 83-year-old man.”

The church has about 135 people at Sunday worship services and more than 90 in Sunday school, Johnson said.

“I led the church through a visioning session to capture a vision of how to better reach the community,” Johnson said. “We’re trying to shift from an inward focus to an outward focus.”

The church wants to reach out to Roebuck, said Johnson, a native of Albertville.

“We are a diverse congregation,” Johnson said. “We have five deacons and one is African-American. He’s been serving in that capacity for years. As far as students, it’s predominantly black. The congregation is 15 percent to 20 percent black.”

There was no more resistance to hiring Knight than to hiring him, Johnson said.
