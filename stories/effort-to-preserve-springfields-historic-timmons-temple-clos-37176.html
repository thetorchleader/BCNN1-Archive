Effort to Preserve Springfield’s Historic Timmons Temple Closes In On Goal

September 5, 2014 Christian

Supporters of an effort to move the historic rock Timmons Temple, which was slated for demolition, now have only $20,000 to go. Organizers say they need $15,000 more, following two large donations made in recent days.
(Photo: News-Leader file photo)

A group trying to save Timmons Temple from demolition received another “substantial” donation this week from a local business.

The exact amount of the donation from the Rice Family Foundation, authorized by Mr. and Mrs. Edwin Rice of Ozarks Coca-Cola Bottling, was not revealed.

“Timmons Temple was our neighbor for many years when the Ozarks’ Coca-Cola Bottling plant was located on Clay St., just a few blocks southwest of the church,” Edwin Rice said, according to a news release announcing the donation.

“Those are our roots, and we are pleased to help save a piece of history for our hometown. We encourage others who are financially able to contribute to this worthy preservation effort.”

Whatever the donation, organizers say $15,000 more is needed to move the rock-walled church from its current location, 934 E. Webster St., to nearby Silver Springs Park.

This donation comes days after a $70,000 pledge from Greenway Studios LLC, which is developing an apartment building where the church currently stands.

The total cost to move the structure is estimated at $105,000.

Organizers of the preservation effort are also working to secure in-kind donations to help with bathrooms, plumbing, electrical and other expenses still needed to have the building properly placed at the park.

The Springfield-Greene County Park Board will have the final approval for the building to be placed in Silver Springs Park. The next park board meeting is Sept. 12, when the Timmons Temple proposal will be discussed.

The church building was built by black citizens in 1932 and many say the temple has historic importance for Springfield’s black community.

Greenway Studios is expected to start construction on the Greenway Studio Apartments this fall and be completed in early spring. The apartments will be the first micro-efficiency apartments in Springfield.
