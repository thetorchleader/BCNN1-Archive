After 20+ Hurricane-free Years, Hawaii Feels the Force of Iselle with Another Storm on the Horizon

August 8, 2014 National

Large swells caused by the approach of Hurricane Iselle in Pohoiki, on the Big Island of Hawaii, on Thursday. (Credit: Bruce Omori/European Pressphoto Agency)

After more than two decades without a hurricane hitting the state, two major storms are now laying siege to Hawaii. But early on Friday, the damage from the first storm did not appear to be severe.

Tropical Storm Iselle made landfall on the Big Island early Friday morning, bringing high surf, strong winds and more than a foot of rain to the east side of the island. The storm was downgraded from a hurricane late Thursday night, and it continued to lose power as it moved west toward the other islands.

Hurricane Julio, with winds of more than 100 miles per hour, was close behind and could reach the islands over the weekend. Meteorologists expected it to go north of the islands, bringing more rain but sparing the islands of major damage.’

Storm surges and high winds on Thursday night forced more than 1,000 people on the Big Island into evacuation shelters. The heavy rain and high winds downed trees and power lines, cutting electricity to thousands across the island and blocking several highways.

At a geothermal power plant, an uncontrolled release of hydrogen sulfide, a poisonous gas, prompted county officials to warn nearby residents to stay indoors and evacuate the area if they experienced discomfort.

No severe damage had been reported Friday morning.

Mike Cantin, a meteorologist with the Central Pacific Hurricane Center, warned that heavy rain continued on the Big Island, while Maui and Oahu would receive strong winds and rain later Friday as the storm moved west.

“Folks need to stay vigilant, stay out of harm’s way,” Mr. Cantin said. “Don’t go running outside. Don’t jump in the ocean. Take it easy today and keep an eye on Julio.”

In addition to the havoc that Tropical Storm Iselle was creating on the Big Island, the storms also threatened to disrupt the primary election here on Saturday, when two Democratic incumbents, Gov. Neil Abercrombie and Senator Brian Schatz, face close, bitterly contested primary challenges.
