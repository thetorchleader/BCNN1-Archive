Michigan Police Chief Resigns After Video Showing Police Beating Unarmed Black Man During Traffic Stop Incites Outrage

April 23, 2015 Black, National

The police chief of Inkster, Mich., resigned Wednesday, three months after the beating of an autoworker in her city sparked national outrage.

Chief Vicki Yost’s resignation is effective immediately, the city of Inkster said in a statement.

“The city would like to thank Chief Yost for her services and wish her the best in future endeavors,” the statement said.

The resignation comes after the Jan. 28 beating of Floyd Dent by Inkster police officers led to protests and anger among civil rights advocates. Dent, who is African-American, was pulled over by police in Inkster, yanked out of his car, tasered and assaulted — including repeated punches to his head — by several white police officers.

Protesters said it was a case of racial profiling, linking it to the Black Lives Matter movement that came about after a police shooting death in Ferguson, Mo.

Dent said he was “treated like a dog.”

He had been slapped with drug charges, but he said police planted cocaine in his car. The charges were dropped Wednesday.

Videos showed the beating of Dent and the planting of drugs by police, said Dent’s attorney. The videos circulated on social media, leading to anger nationwide.

Wayne County Prosecutor Kym Worthy this week dropped the drug charge against Dent and charged one of the officers involved, William Melendez, 46, with misconduct in office for mistreatment of a prisoner, a five-year felony, and assault with intent to do great bodily harm less than murder, a 10-year felony.

Melendez has been hit with 12 lawsuits since 1996, accusing him of planting evidence, assaulting people, making up police reports, and wrongly arresting people. In 2003, he was charged along with other officers by federal authorities but was acquitted.

A message left Wednesday afternoon with Chief Yost’s assistant was not immediately returned.
