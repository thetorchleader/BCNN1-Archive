Bobby Jindal to Meet With Iowa Pastors to Help Decide 2016 Campaign

January 5, 2015 Christian

Governor of Louisiana Bobby Jindal speaks to the press on Tuesday, Dec. 16, 2014 before his appearance at the Polk County Republican’s Holiday Party at the West Des Moines Marriott Hotel. Jindal told the press that he will decide early next year if he’ll run for president. (Photo: Kelsey Kremer/The Register)

Bobby Jindal’s Iowa caucus campaign test drive continues this week with events in front of a key demographic: conservative religious leaders.

Jindal, the two-term Republican governor of Louisiana, is scheduled to meet politically active pastors in closed-door meetings in Des Moines and Cedar Rapids on Tuesday. Candidates in previous Iowa caucus cycles have used such meetings to introduce themselves to influential conservatives and gauge their prospects for a full-blown campaign.

The private lunch in Cedar Rapids and off-the-record dinner in Des Moines will allow pastors from conservative congregations to quiz the potential presidential candidate and sound out his views without the press and public present.

The socially conservative American Renewal Project is sponsoring the get-togethers. It has held similar events in recent years with Republican presidential candidates in Iowa, including Newt Gingrich, Michele Bachmann and Mike Huckabee.

The Cedar Rapids event will be held at 12:30 p.m. Tuesday at the Hotel at Kirkwood Center. The Des Moines event will take place at 7 p.m. at the Airport Holiday Inn.
