March 21st Basketball Game Event between Madison, Wisconsin, Police and Black High School Players Is Still On despite Recent Shooting

March 14, 2015 Black

Madison police officer Caleb Johnson helps kids set up basketball hoops on Leland Drive last summer for a street basketball game. Madison police are hoping basketball will provide another opportunity for community building when they meet a group of African-American high school players for a tournament at West High School.

A basketball game between Madison Police Department officers and African-American players from each of Madison’s four high schools is still on in the wake of the shooting last week of an unarmed black teen by a white police officer.

The March 6 shooting of Tony Robinson by officer Matt Kenny has sparked angry but peaceful protests across the city over the past week. Organizers of the basketball game are pressing on.

Called “Play it Forward,” the event is slated for March 21 at West High School between 1 p.m. and 3 p.m. Tickets are $10 for adults and $5 for students. They’re available at West High School, located at 30 Ash St., Madison’s South Police District or online.

“We still want to do the event, and part of me just thinks maybe even talking about the decision to go forward with the game is almost as cool as the story behind the game,” said Rev. Alex Gee.

Gee approached Madison Police Chief Mike Koval last August about holding the event as a way to raise money to help African-American students tour college campuses and foster goodwill between black youths and the police.

All the money raised will go to Black Student Unions at the high schools to pay transportation costs for the college visits.

Robinson’s shooting and the ensuing protests briefly put the brakes on promoting the event. But Gee said the event could provide a way to break the ice.

“We will always have discussions, we will always have demonstrations, we’re going to have sit-downs and summits,” he said.
