WATCH: Despite Recent Shootings and Protests, 11-Year-Old Minnesota Boy Wants To Be a Police Officer

December 25, 2014 Black

The nation is mourning the execution style murders of two New York City police officers over the weekend. And with civil rights protests against police brutality, it’s been a challenging past few months for law enforcement.

Sometimes being an officer can feel like a thankless job, especially when some people see cops as the bad guys. But in Brooklyn Park, Minn. one young man still wants to be a cop, because of an unlikely mentor — a relationship that could teach others about race relations.

“I mean it’s just part of the job,” Steve Nelson, detective with Brooklyn Park police said. “Sometimes we’re portrayed in a negative light.”

Nelson is trying to change that perception. Along with his usual patrols, he works at Oxbow Creek Elementary, connecting with students like 11-year-old Joshua Addou, a precocious fifth-grader.

The two didn’t meet until last month, when Joshua wrote a letter only addressed to Brooklyn Park Police Department asking how to be a cop.

“What is minimum wage over at your police department? I want to be ready for whatever you throw at me,” Joshua wrote in the letter.

Nelson said the letter ended up on his desk and it moved him, so he decided to respond in person. However during this time, racial tensions started to mount across the country. Unarmed black men in Ferguson and New York City died at the hands of white police officers.
