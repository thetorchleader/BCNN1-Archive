Miami Heat Power Past Celtics In Game 1

May 29, 2012 Uncategorized

The refrain is on repeat right now for the Miami Heat and any opponent they play.


Too much LeBron James, too much Dwyane Wade. It is, until proven otherwise, an unstoppable combination.
It was that way in the final three games of the Heat-Indiana Pacers series in the Eastern Conference semifinals.
It was that way in Game 1 of the Eastern Conference finals between the Heat and Boston Celtics.
Miami defeated Boston 93-79 Tuesday, taking a 1-0 lead in the best-of-seven series.
Picking up where he left off against the Pacers, James scored 32 points, grabbed 13 rebounds and shot 13-for-22 from the field.
Wade had 22 points and seven assists, 16 points and four assists coming in the second half as the Heat blew open a 46-46 halftime score with a dominant third quarter.
“We let Wade (and) we let LeBron play in extreme comfort tonight,” Boston Celtics coach Doc Rivers said, adding later in his postgame news conference, “They’re cat quick. That’s who they are. You know that going into the series. As much as you can watch it and prepare for it, I do think you need to face it once to see and get used to it. I thought their speed at times overwhelmed us for sure.”
In his last four games, all Miami victories, James — the league’s most valuable player — has totaled 130 points (32.5 per game), 47 rebounds (11.8 per game) and 27 assists (6.8 per game).
Wade is averaging 30.3 points, 6.3 rebounds and 3.5 assists.
There is mesmerizing beauty coupled with methodical determination in their assault on opponents. It is the kind of symbiotic give-and-take that was expected from the start of the James-Wade-Chris Bosh union.
“We’re doing a better job of playing off each other — both being aggressive at the same time and also understanding when another guy has certain matchups that he can expose, whether it’s to get his own shot or whether it’s to get a double-team,” Wade said.
Playing without Bosh (out indefinitely with a lower abdominal strain), Heat coach Erik Spoelstra has put the onus on James and Wade to carry the offensive load. They have delivered — with opponents knowing exactly how the Heat are going to try and win.
It’s no secret. James and Wade need to score a significant amount of points. Last year, that wasn’t possible. The comfort level wasn’t there, a fact Wade admitted.
“We’re not having as much hero ball as we played last year,” Wade said. “We’re trusting each other a lot more. It comes with time. In the fourth quarter, we believe in each other, and that’s been a success for us.”
