Deadly Storms Tear Through the South

November 18, 2011 Uncategorized

A severe weather front that ripped through five Southeastern states on Wednesday, killing at least six people and injuring scores, marked the beginning a fall storm season that is likely to bring more trouble to a region that has barely recovered from an unusually deadly string of spring tornadoes.

The storms, which began in Texas on Tuesday, rode a strong jet stream through the Mississippi Valley and into Louisiana, Alabama, Georgia and the Carolinas before trailing off through Florida on Thursday, leaving in its wake drops in temperature of as much as 15 degrees and serious structural damage.
Severe weather in the South is common in November, when warm, humid air gets pushed out by cold fronts.
“Most people are aware of the strong spring systems, but we always expect a secondary severe weather system this time in the fall,” said Mark Wiley, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service.
The most deaths occurred in a rural community just south of Rock Hill, S.C., where wind shears were brief, contained and powerful. A tornado that appeared to hit an area less than a mile long killed three people, including a married couple in their 60s who died when their house collapsed, said Lt. Mike Baker of the York County Sheriff’s Department.
On Thursday, people who had lost their homes were trying to make sense of the damage, said Katharine Correll, the executive director of the Upper Palmetto chapter of the Red Cross.
“People’s homes don’t even look like homes anymore,” she said. “The odd thing is because it’s farmland, you see all of these homes collapsed but cows grazing in the pastures like nothing happened.”
