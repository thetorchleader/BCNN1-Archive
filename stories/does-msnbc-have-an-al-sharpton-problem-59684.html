Does MSNBC Have an Al Sharpton Problem?

March 3, 2015 Black

GETTY JAHI CHIKWENDIU/THE WASHINGTON POST

The presence of the Reverend Al Sharpton on MSNBC every night always has been problematic. More than any other host on the network, even more than Ed Schultz, Sharpton comes closes to obliterating the line between observation and participation, in the fashion of Sean Hannity during last weekend’s CPAC, or everybody on Fox when the Tea Party was a’borning. That I tend to agree with most of the stances Sharpton takes is immaterial. He’s a terrible host on his merits, and he’s such a walking conflict of interest that it’s come to bother me considerably. However, comes now Byron Allen, who is suing Comcast, with more serious charges — namely, that the Reverend is a wholly owned subsidiary of the mega-corporation Allen is suing.

The lawsuit, seeking $20 billion, was filed in Los Angeles federal court Friday by Entertainment Studios, a television company founded by black producer and comedian Byron Allen and the National Association of African-American Owned Media (NAAAOM). The complaint, which comes as regulators mull a $45-billion merger between Comcast and TWC, alleges that Comcast has refused to do business with Allen and other black media executives. “Comcast has engaged in, and is engaging in, pernicious, intentional racial discrimination in contracting,” it reads. Whether or not it gets anywhere is another question. Anyone can file a lawsuit alleging anything and claiming any amount of money.

This strikes me as just a tad glib on the part of the writer. It’s not as though Sharpton’s past is spotless, and it’s not as though in the past, he’s been averse to taking corporate dough for his own activities. Also, Sharpton’s response to the lawsuit does not fill me with confidence.
