Jury Gives No Verdict on First Day of Kwame Kilpatrick Deliberations

February 25, 2013 Uncategorized

Day One of deliberations was uneventful for the jury — at least publicly — in the corruption trial of former Detroit Mayor Kwame Kilpatrick and two others.


Former Detroit Mayor Kwame Kilpatrick makes his way into federal court in Detroit on Feb. 11. He faces 30 charges and up to 20 years in prison if convicted. / ANDRE J. JACKSON/Detroit Free Press
The nine women and three men of jury met for about 7 1/2 hours Tuesday, including over lunch, and did not reach a verdict in the racketeering case against Kilpatrick; his father, Bernard Kilpatrick, and contractor Bobby Ferguson, a friend of the ex-mayor.
The jury did not ask any questions that merited a public hearing before Judge Nancy Edmunds. All deliberations were conducted behind a closed door in the U.S. District Court in downtown Detroit.
The jurors will reconvene today at 9 a.m. and go until they reach a verdict, or the end of the day at 4:30 p.m. That’s the schedule for each weekday until a decision is rendered.
Kwame Kilpatrick faces 30 charges. His father faces four, and Ferguson, 11.
If convicted, they each face up to 20 years in prison.
