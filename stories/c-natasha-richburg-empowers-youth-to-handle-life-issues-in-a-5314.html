C. NaTasha Richburg Empowers Youth to Handle Life Issues in A Hoodie Ain’t a Coat

April 18, 2012 Uncategorized

Over the last month and a half, the nation has been impacted by the tragic shooting death of Trayvon Martin, a 17-year-old African-American Florida teenager.
As the case is ongoing and many calls are being made for the shooter to be put in jail, the issue of the hoodie came up. Trayvon was wearing a hoodie while walking home from the store when he was shot. The hoodie has been the source of much contention as one side seems to think that young black men who wear hoodies are stereotyped as ‘dangerous’, while the other side says there is nothing wrong with wearing a hoodie and that it should not be the reason behind the death of Trayvon Martin.
In her book, A Hoodie Ain’t a Coat, C. Natasha Richburg, shows readers how the hoodie plays an important role in the life of her main character, Jez. The hoodie is where he thinks, where he contemplates life, where he expresses his emotions, and where he retains his memory of the world and his experiences in it. For Jez, a hoodie represents a protected zone where he can deal comfortably with his private thoughts.
A Hoodie Ain’t a Coat is a coming of age story about Jez. Jez is a young man looking for a place to fit in. The street corner appeared to be the perfect place to gain a rep as Jez developed a swagger that reigned within his circle of friends. Pressed to become part of a family unit, Jez sought to be affiliated with the local Lee Street Gang to become the understudy of its leader. The unconventional nature of Jez’s relationship with the leader was never openly discussed with his boys even after Jez received the devastating news.
Filled with 12 full color illustrations, A Hoodie Ain’t a Coat will encourage youth who are growing into adulthood on how to handle relationships with their friends and how to discern when one’s friends are real or fake. “Trial by fire will let you know who your real friends are.”

C. NaTasha Richburg has been married to her husband, Melvin, for over 26 years. She is the mother of four children. Mrs. Richburg is a preacher, teacher, and speaker positioned to bring forth illustrations in her short stories that allows us to find a reflection of peace in the middle of life’s storms. 
Mrs. Richburg has a Masters of Business Administration from Morgan State University and a Masters in Information Systems from Johns Hopkins University. She has attended, and completed many courses at St. Mary’s Seminary in Baltimore.  Mrs. Richburg is currently attending Morgan State University in pursuit of a Doctorate in Urban Leadership Education. Mrs. Richburg professes Proverbs 24:5 (KJV) which says: “[a] wise man is strong; yea a man of knowledge increaseth strength.”
