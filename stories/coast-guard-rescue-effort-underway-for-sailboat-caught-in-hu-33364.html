Coast Guard Rescue Effort Underway for Sailboat Caught in Hurricane Julio 400 Miles Off Hawaii

August 11, 2014 National

(Photo: U.S. Coast Guard)

Authorities have launched an operation to rescue three people stranded on a sailboat that got caught in Hurricane Julio approximately 400 miles northeast of the Hawaiian island of Oahu. 

KHON reported that the 42-inch vessel Walkabout was disabled and taking on water amid 30-foot high seas and winds reported between 92 and 115 miles per hour. The Coast Guard said in a statement that one of the boat’s hatches had blown away, as had the life raft.

The initial message from the walkabout requesting Coast Guard assistance was received at 7:15 a.m. local time (1:15 p.m. Eastern Time) Sunday. Approximately three-and-a-half hours later, an aircraft sent to establish contact with those on board detected Mayday calls from the boat.

Another aircraft that reached the sailboat dropped a life raft and pump into the water, but the sailors couldn’t retrieve the equipment because of the winds and swells, Coast Guard spokeswoman Petty Officer Melissa McKenzie said.

Another aircraft was sent to attempt another drop, she said. A 661-foot container ship, Manukai, was diverted from its route to the location of the sailboat was still on its way late Sunday.

The Associated Press contributed to this report.
