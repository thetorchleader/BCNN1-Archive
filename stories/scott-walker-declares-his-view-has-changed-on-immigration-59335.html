Scott Walker Declares his ‘View Has Changed’ On Immigration

March 1, 2015 National

Wisconsin Gov. Scott Walker speaks at the Conservative Political Action Conference on Feb. 26, 2015. (H. Darr Beiser, USA TODAY)

Wisconsin Gov. Scott Walker acknowledged he has changed his position on immigration, addressing charges that he has flip flopped on issues as he moves toward an all-but-declared presidential race.

“My view has changed. I’m flat out saying it,” Walker said on Fox News Sunday, as he stressed that he’d like to see stronger border security and opposes “amnesty” for undocumented workers who came to the United States illegally.

In his Fox News interview with Chris Wallace, Walker also defended his support for right-to-work legislation making its way to his desk and finally indicated he believes President Obama loves his country. Wallace’s questions are a result of Walker’s rise as the currently hot GOP contender, following the governor’s well-received appearances at the Conservative Political Action Conference and an Iowa forum.

Support for a path to citizenship as part of an immigration overhaul is considered anathema to conservatives, and is a major reason why Jeb Bush is viewed with skepticism among GOP hard-liners. Walker noted Sunday that Wisconsin was among the states joining the lawsuit initiated by Texas to block President Obama’s executive actions on immigration.
