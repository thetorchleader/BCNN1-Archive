Police Arrest Richard Burgin Jr., the Suspect Accused of Killing Two Brothers at West Huntsville United Methodist Church in Birmingham

August 8, 2014 Black

Richard Burgin Jr. (Madison County jail)

Police arrested Richard Burgin in last year’s brutal slaying of two brothers at a church food pantry after the state crime lab matched his DNA to a cup found near the crime scene.

Huntsville Police Department investigator Charlie Gray said the cup was found near a bloody butcher knife and other items in some bushes near the West Huntsville United Methodist Church where the two men were killed.

Burgin faces capital murder charges in the May 21, 2013 stabbing deaths of Anthony Jackson, 76, and Terry Jackson, 69, at the food pantry at West Huntsville United Methodist Church, where the brothers volunteered to clean up and help distribute food.

Gray testified during Burgin’s preliminary hearing this morning. He said police had taken DNA samples from several transients in the area around the church following the slayings but none matched the DNA found on the cup.

In December the Alabama Department of Forensic Sciences informed police they had found a match with Burgin, who was in prison at the Draper Correctional Facility.

Gray said investigators went to Draper, took a DNA sample from Burgin and tried to interview him. Gray said he described the church killings and showed Burgin some photos. Burgin said he thought he needed a lawyer, Gray said, and the interview ended.

The new DNA sample matched the cup and the DNA on file, Gray said.

A witness who lived across the street from the church said on the day of the stabbings he saw a black male come to a door of the church, speak to a white male then enter the building. A few minutes later the black man emerged carrying a number of items, including a red cup.

The witness said the man moved at a steady pace on foot down 6th Street then turned onto 8th Avenue.

Other witnesses told police they saw a black male running down 8th Avenue, looking back while he ran.

A K-9 unit was called and found a church bulletin, the cup, a bloody knife and bloody rag in some bushes.

Gray testified the director of the church food pantry arrived a few minutes after the attack and found Anthony Jackson dead on the floor and Terry Jackson barely alive. There were two pools of blood at the crime scene and blood spatters on more than one wall and a door, Gray said.
