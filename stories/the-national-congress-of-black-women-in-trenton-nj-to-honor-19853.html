The National Congress of Black Women in Trenton, NJ to Honor 5 Men for Their Service

April 27, 2014 African-American

Freeholder Pat Colavita, pictured in a file photo, is among the five men being honored by Trenton’s National Congress of Black Women. (Mary Iuvone/For The Times)

The National Congress of Black Women, Trenton-Mercer Chapter, will honor five men for their service to the community today.

More than 250 people are expected to attend the group’s annual spring luncheon at the Trenton Country Club. The theme of the event is “Working Together — Moving Forward.”

“We honor men in the community every year for the sacrifices and contributions they have made to the people of the greater Trenton area,” said Jerlene “Cookie” Worthy, NCBW chairwoman.

“This year’s nominees are outstanding individuals who set a high standard for men in our city. We are thrilled to give them the recognition they deserve.”

The honorees are:

Allen J. Cannon, who is in his second term as deputy mayor of Hopewell Township and was the first African-American ever elected to office in the township. He is a medical laboratory technician and served in the U.S. Army for 25 years, earning more than 25 medals of distinction. He is married to NCBW member Melody Crawford-Cannon. They have two children.

Pasquale “Pat” A. Colavita Jr. is a lifelong resident of Lawrence. He served on the Mercer County Board of Chosen Freeholders, the Lawrence Township Board of Education and the Lawrence Township Council. He worked for 37 years as a speech language pathologist for the Hamilton Township Board of Education. He is married to Marianne and they have two children.
