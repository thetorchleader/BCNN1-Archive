Gov. Pat Quinn Tells Chicago Church, “We’re Not Going to Let the Gangbangers Take Over!”

March 11, 2013 Uncategorized

With the gusto of a stump speech, Gov. Pat Quinn told congregants of a prominent Chicago church Sunday that combating street violence is a priority and his proposed budget would help address the problem.
“We’re never going to give up,” he shouted from the pulpit before congregants at Greater St. John Bible Church. “We’re not going to let the gangbangers take over!”
The stop wasn’t listed on Quinn’s public schedule, but comes days after the Chicago Democrat delivered what he called the most difficult budget the state has ever faced and as Republicans and some fellow Democrats are talking of challenging him in 2014.
The budget contains a bit more money in some areas of public safety — new cadet classes for Illinois State Police and $25 million for mental health care — along with huge cuts in education. Quinn played up his efforts to retain funding for early education and increases for mental health.
The stop was likely a preview of Quinn’s coming months as he gears up for a possible primary challenge and offered a glimpse of his spirited campaign persona. He quoted the Bible frequently, spoke without notes and played to the crowd, made up largely of black families on Chicago’s West Side.
He referenced last year’s slaying of 7-year-old Heaven Sutton, who was killed by a stray bullet as she was selling snow cones in the neighborhood, which isn’t far from Quinn’s Chicago home. Her death was among the more than 500 murders in 2012, an increase from years past.
“Little Heaven’s up in heaven today,” Quinn said. “We better take good care of our babies.”
Several Republicans — including state senators Bill Brady and Kirk Dillard, Treasurer Dan Rutherford and U.S. Rep. Aaron Schock — are mulling gubernatorial run. Former White House chief of staff Bill Daley and Illinois Attorney General Lisa Madigan, both Democrats, haven’t made up their minds yet.
Quinn dismissed the stop as campaigning.
“It is important for people of faith, whatever your faith, to understand that we as a faith community need to take on violence,” he told The Associated Press.
He said the Rev. Ira Acree –one of Chicago’s most vocal activist pastors — is a friend and he had promised to stop by the church. Quinn said he’d been too busy with the budget to do so until now.
The governor proposed his $35.6 billion spending plan last week. It calls for $400 million in cuts to education, along with a proposal to pay down the state’s massive backlog of bills. Quinn said he had to make the cuts because of the strain pensions has on other spending. Illinois’ pension problem is the worst in the nation with nearly $100 billion in unfunded liability.
While neither Quinn nor Acree mentioned the 2014 race directly, the pastor had high praise for Quinn. Just seconds after Quinn walked off stage, Acree praised him and his knowledge of scripture.
“We need Christians in office,” he told congregants.
SOURCE: The Associated Press
