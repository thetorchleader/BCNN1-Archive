Kenyan Born Scholar, Ali Mazrui, Dies at 81

October 14, 2014 Eye on Africa

Ali Mazrui
YOUTUBE

Kenya’s president Uhuru Kenyatta called Mazrui a “towering” academic whose “intellectual contributions played a major role in shaping African scholarship,” BBC notes. Professor, political writer, and one of Kenya’s brightest son’s, Ali Mazrui died Monday at the age of 81.

“Death has robbed us of one of Kenya’s greatest scholars,” Kenya’s President Uhuru Kenyatta told BBC News about the Kenyan born scholar.

President Kenyatta called Mazrui a “towering” academic whose “intellectual contributions played a major role in shaping African scholarship,” BBC notes.

Mazrui, studied in Kenya, Great Britain, and Columbia University in New York during his early education, was a world-renowned scholar who wrote extensively about colonialism and lead academic departments throughout Africa and North America.

Mazrui was an Albert Schweitzer professor in the humanities and the director of the Institute of Global Cultural Studies at Binghamton University in New York, at the time of his death, the BBC reports.

Mazuri, a leading pan-Africanist, was the author or co-author to more than 20 books and wrote about varied topics including: African politics, international political culture and political Islam according to BBC.

“In one of his books, Islam between Globalization and Counter Terrorism, he explained how the religion was entrapped in the danger of rising extremism,” BBC reports.
