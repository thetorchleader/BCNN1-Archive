Mother of Released U.S. Journalist, Peter Theo Curtis, Reaches Out to Mother of James Foley

August 26, 2014 National

Peter Theo Curtis

The mother of journalist Peter Theo Curtis said Monday that she contacted the mother of slain reporter James Foley when she learned that her own son had been freed from captivity by Islamic militants.

Speaking to ABC World News, Nancy Curtis said from her Cambridge home that she immediately reached out to Diane Foley upon learning that her own son had been released.

“I sat down and sent an e-mail to Diane Foley,” Nancy Curtis said. “We’ve been through so much together. I didn’t want her to hear it from the media first.”

Curtis also told the network she plans to embrace her son as soon as he returns to the US.

“I’ll give him a big hug,” she said. “And I’ll probably cry, and he’’ll probably cry.”

Meanwhile, a man who escaped from an Islamic militant group operating in Syria with the help of Peter Theo Curtis today hailed Curtis’s release.

“This day will go down as one of the happiest of my life,” Matthew Schrier said in a statement. “On this day my thoughts, prayers, and tears of joy go out to the entire Curtis family. Hopefully, this is just the beginning regarding our captives and their long journeys home.”

Schrier was able to escape from their captors in July 2013 by standing on Curtis’s back and squeezing through an opening in the walls of their cell, the New York Times has reported.

“After living through the tragic events of this past week with the death of James Foley and hearing that the life of Theo Curtis has been spared, it gives me great hope for the safe return of all the other hostages,” Schrier said.

Curtis, 45, a writer and scholar with ties to the Boston area, was held captive for nearly two years.
