Autopsy on Michael Brown Reveals Police Shot him at Close Range

October 23, 2014 Black

An image of Michael Brown is next to his casket St Louis, Mo., on Aug. 25.
(Photo: Robert Cohen, Pool via european pressphoto agency)

Michael Brown, the unarmed black man who was killed by a white police officer in Ferguson, Mo., in August, was shot in the hand at close range and had marijuana in his system, the St. Louis Post-Dispatch reports, citing analysis of an autopsy report.

The Post-Dispatch says it obtained the St. Louis County medical examiner’s autopsy and toxicology reports and had them analyzed by experts not directly involved in the case.

The Post-Dispatch said the autopsy report provides the “most detailed description to date of the wounds Brown sustained” in the confrontation with Officer Darren Wilson.

Police say Brown, 18, struggled with Wilson inside his police car and reached for the officer’s weapon. Brown’s family says the teen was killed as he raised his hands to surrender to the officer. The St. Louis medical examiner, Michael Graham, who is not part of the official investigation, reviewed the autopsy report and said it “does support that there was a significant altercation at the car.”

Graham told the Post-Dispatch that his examination indicated a shot traveled from the tip of Brown’s right thumb toward his wrist. The report says there is no stippling — powder burns around a wound that indicate a shot fired at relatively short range. But Graham said stippling does not always appear “when it’s really close, such as within an inch or so.”
