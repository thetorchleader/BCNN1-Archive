Spirit of Faith Christian Center in Ohio to Focus on Ministering to Local Families

August 9, 2014 Black

Former Alliance residents the Revs. Raymont and Brenda Johnson have returned to the Carnation City to pursue a calling from God to plant and pastor a church, Spirit of Faith Christian Center of Ohio.

The two, who each have a background in teaching, said they felt burdened for their hometown and were inspired to do something about it last summer while on vacation in the Bahamas. They sought the support of their family, friends and pastor before making the move, ensuring appropriate foundational support would be in place.

“We have a clear vision of what we’re here to do, and it’s about having an impact on the community. We want to see people win in every area of their lives,” said Raymont, who retired from the U.S. Air Force and earned an associate’s degree from the Community College of the Air Force, a Bachelor of Science in business management from Central Texas College, a Master of Science in organizational leadership from Nyack College and a certificate in Christian family counseling from Capital Bible Seminary.

Brenda earned an associate’s degree from Howard University, a Bachelor of Arts from Prince George’s County Community College and a certificate of ministerial studies from Spirit of Faith Bible Institute. A former music educator, she will serve as the associate pastor, sharing administrative and teaching responsibilities with Raymont, and leading the music ministry. She plans to launch “Women Transformed,” a women’s fellowship ministry, in January to encourage area women through the Scriptures and activities they enjoy.

“People often think that going to church is the work of the ministry, but it’s not; it’s the place where you go to receive training so that you’ll have the confidence, when you do meet somebody in the grocery store, to minister to them … lift them up … or encourage them to find their purpose,” she said.

Raymont described Spirit of Faith Christian Center of Ohio, an offshoot of their home church in Maryland, as a principle-driven teaching ministry that also focuses on counseling and serving. “Everything that goes on when you come in that door is meant to help clear your mind of all your issues and prepare your mind and heart so that you can hear instruction for life,” he said, adding he plans to encourage attendees to “discover their gifting and callings” and to “start right, stay right and finish right.”

In addition to weekly corporate teaching and discipleship, such encouragement will come in the form of programs from Family Empowerment Ministries, a 501(c)3 nonprofit in existence since 2007 that serves as the outreach arm of the church.

Programs will include free walk-in counseling for families and individuals beginning in September; “Marriage Matters: Couples’ Night Out” the fourth Friday of every month starting Sept. 25 (the Johnsons’ 32nd wedding anniversary), for those who are already married or intended; “24/7 Dad,” through the National Fatherhood Initiative; and Youth and Young Adult Leadership Institute, to help instill leadership and management skills in young adults.

“We’re here to minister to the whole community,” Raymont emphasized. “It’s not a black church, or a white church, or a Hispanic church; it’s a church to minister. And we are a teaching ministry — that’s our focus.”
