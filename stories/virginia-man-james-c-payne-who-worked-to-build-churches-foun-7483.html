Virginia Man, James C. Payne, Who Worked to Build Churches Found Dead at 89

August 5, 2012 Uncategorized

James C. Payne started his first business when he was a teenager, growing up in segregated Roanoke before World War II. He ran a masonry business, which had a dozen or more employees, while attending Lucy Addison High School.

He went on to start a prominent construction company that built well-known predominantly black churches in Roanoke, as well as worked on major projects at the Veterans Affairs Medical Center in Salem, Langley Air Force Base, the University of Virginia and many others over 40 years.
Payne, 89, was found dead at his home in Rustburg on Friday. The Campbell County Sheriff’s Office is investigating his death as a homicide.
Payne was remembered on Saturday as a hard-working, honest businessman, whose perfectionism was a trademark and whose word was his bond. He helped other businesses owned by blacks, but he often told his employees at Payne Construction Co. that it was the quality of their work that mattered, not the color of their skin.
“He always told us that we are general contractors who happen to be black and not black general contractors,” said Charles Price, Payne’s nephew and a vice president of the company.
Payne left Roanoke as a young man because he believed he would have better business prospects outside of the segregated South. He worked as a contractor before founding his own company in Teaneck, N.J., in 1971. He moved the company to Virginia in 1978, first to Vinton, then Bedford and finally to Rustburg, where his great-grandparents had lived.
His first Virginia project was the Smyth County Library in Marion. The company became well-known for its work on churches in the Roanoke Valley, most of them historically black churches.
Payne Construction built First Baptist Church near the Hotel Roanoke, Macedonia Baptist and Hill Street Baptist. A short list of churches for which the company built additions includes Garden of Prayer No. 7, Loudon Avenue Christian, St. Gerard Catholic, Morning Star Baptist and Jerusalem Baptist. Payne also worked on historic churches in Baltimore, Norfolk and Newport News.
