Newark Mayor Cory Booker says 'I Used to be Disgusted with Homosexuals but My Mind Was Changed by a Homosexual Guidance Counselor'

January 12, 2013 Uncategorized

‘I was writing about my teenage struggle for integrity,’ Booker explained Thursday on Twitter. The piece was written as Booker pursued a master’s degree in sociology at Stanford. He said a gay counselor’s accounts of prejudice and violence changed his mind.

Newark Mayor Cory Booker said a gay counselor’s stories of violence and prejudice reminded him of the racism faced by his grandparents. Previously, “the thought of two men kissing each other was about as appealing as a frontal lobotomy,” he said. 
Newark Mayor Cory Booker admitted that he “hated gays” as a young man in a 1992 opinion piece republished by The Stanford Daily, in which he spoke frankly about his evolution on homosexuality. 
The column was published as Booker was pursuing a master’s degree in sociology at Stanford and reprinted by the newspaper on Wednesday.  
 
The high-profile mayor recently said he is planning to run for New Jersey Sen. Frank Lautenberg’s seat in 2014. 
 
“I was writing about my teenage struggle for integrity,” Booker explained Thursday on Twitter.
 
In the column, Booker wrote that he was “disgusted by gays” as a teenager, even as he put on a show of acceptance. 
 
“The thought of two men kissing each other was about as appealing as a frontal lobotomy,” he said. 
 
“Allow me to be more direct, escaping the euphemisms of my past – I hated gays,” he continued. “The disgust and latent hostility I felt toward gays were subcategories of hatred, plain and simple.”
 
Booker said he had a change of heart as a freshman after a gay counselor shared his story about enduring prejudice and violence because of his homosexuality.
