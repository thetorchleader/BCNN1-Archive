Research Shows Working Out With a Partner Enhances Your Time of Exercise

October 16, 2014 Health

Exercising with a partner can help keep you motivated, and research shows that you may work out harder. Shared exercise time can also double as social time, making you even more likely to show up for workouts. So recruit a friend or relative. Here’s more advice:

Team up with a slightly stronger partner. You want someone who is like-minded and shares similar fitness goals, say experts at the American Council on Exercise. If you are just starting a running program, for example, don’t pick a seasoned marathon runner as an exercise companion. But if your buddy runs a little faster than you, that may actually work in your favor. Research from Michigan State University suggests that working out with someone you perceive as moderately more capable than you can as much as double your own workout time and overall performance.

Make sure your schedules mesh. If you like to exercise early in the morning before heading to the office, don’t partner up with someone who works night shifts, for example, or prefers to head to the gym during his lunch hour.
