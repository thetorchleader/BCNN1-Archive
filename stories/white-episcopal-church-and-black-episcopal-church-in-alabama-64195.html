White Episcopal Church and Black Episcopal Church in Alabama Will Hold Joint Palm Sunday Service to Commemorate 1965 Events that Led to Integration of White Church

March 28, 2015 Black

St. Paul’s Episcopal Church in Selma will welcome members of St. Mark’s Episcopal Church in Birmingham on Palm Sunday. (File)

Palm Sunday in 1965 left an emotional scar on St. Paul’s Episcopal Church in Selma.

A group of church members from the predominantly black St. Mark’s Episcopal Church in Birmingham showed up for worship at St. Paul’s in Selma, and were turned away, called “outside demonstrators,” even though they were fellow Episcopalians.

On Sunday, St. Paul’s and St. Mark’s will join for a commemoration of the events of 1965 that led to the integration of St. Paul’s.

They will share a Palm Sunday service at 10:30 a.m.

Members of St. Paul’s, most of whom are white, will join with members of St. Mark’s Episcopal Church, Birmingham, most of whom are black, and members of Brown Chapel AME Church for the service. Those attending will include Lou Willie of St. Mark’s, who was among the group of worshippers that was turned away from St. Paul’s before integration was finally accomplished on March 28, 1965.

“Our service will revisit the steps where the delegation was blocked from worship,” said the Rev. Jack Alvey, rector of St. Paul’s. “Clergy and worship leaders alike will all kneel before the doors of the parish.  We will name and recognize that we are all responsible for the systems of injustice and hate in our world.”
